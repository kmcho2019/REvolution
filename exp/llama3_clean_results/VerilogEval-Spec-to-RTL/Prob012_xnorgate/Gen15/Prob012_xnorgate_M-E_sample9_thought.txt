A novel architectural approach to implement an XNOR gate could involve utilizing a lookup table (LUT) or a small ROM (Read-Only Memory) that stores the XNOR truth table. This method is based on the principle that the XNOR operation's output can be directly determined by the input combinations. By using a LUT or ROM, the design leverages the existing digital logic infrastructure to perform the XNOR operation, potentially offering a unique optimization path in terms of area and power, especially in FPGAs or other programmable devices where LUTs are a fundamental component. This approach might be more beneficial when integrating the XNOR gate into a larger digital circuit where LUTs or ROMs are already being utilized, thereby potentially reducing overall area usage and power consumption by leveraging existing resources.
