A novel approach to implementing the finite-state machine (FSM) could involve leveraging a hybrid encoding scheme that combines the benefits of both binary and one-hot encoding. This scheme would encode the states in a way that minimizes the number of transitions between states, thus reducing power consumption. Additionally, instead of using a traditional next-state logic and output logic combination, we could employ a lookup table (LUT) based approach. The LUT would store the next states and outputs for each current state and input combination, allowing for a more direct and potentially more efficient implementation.

To further optimize the design, we could also incorporate a dynamic clock gating technique that not only disables the clock when the state doesn't change but also takes into account the input signals to predict when a state change is likely to occur. This predictive clock gating could potentially reduce power consumption even further by minimizing unnecessary clock cycles.

Lastly, to enhance maintainability and scalability, the design could be structured into smaller, more manageable modules, each responsible for a specific aspect of the FSM's functionality. This modular approach would facilitate easier updates and modifications to the FSM's logic without affecting the overall architecture.
