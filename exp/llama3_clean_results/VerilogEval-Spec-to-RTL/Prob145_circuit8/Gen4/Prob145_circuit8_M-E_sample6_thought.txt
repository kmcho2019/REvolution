The novel approach here is to recognize that the circuit's behavior can be represented as a simple state machine that updates its state based on the input 'a' and the current state of the system, but only when the clock is in a certain phase (either rising or falling edge). The key observation from the simulation waveforms is that 'q' is updated to match 'a' on the falling edge of the clock, and 'p' seems to be high whenever 'clock' is high and 'a' is high. However, the logic behind 'p' also seems to be influenced by the state of 'q'. Given this, a straightforward state machine or a simple sequential logic that directly updates 'q' based on 'a' at the falling edge of the clock and determines 'p' based on 'clock', 'a', and possibly the current state of 'q' could work. The main idea is to use a simpler, more direct approach to update 'q' and determine 'p', leveraging the clock and input 'a' directly.
