To address the issues in the original code and provide a novel architectural idea, I propose a modular and structured approach. This involves breaking down the clock into its fundamental components: hours, minutes, and seconds, each handled by a separate module. The `TopModule` will serve as a controller, managing the overall state and interactions between these components.

The idea is to use three separate modules: `HoursModule`, `MinutesModule`, and `SecondsModule`. Each module will have its own counter and will handle its own logic for incrementing and resetting. The `TopModule` will then manage the outputs and the enable signal (`ena`) to these modules, ensuring that the clock increments correctly and resets as required.

To handle the 12-hour format, the `HoursModule` will need to manage the AM/PM logic and ensure that the hours wrap around correctly. The `MinutesModule` and `SecondsModule` will handle the 60-second/60-minute logic, resetting to 0 when they reach 60.

This modular approach simplifies the code, making it easier to understand, maintain, and debug. It also allows for easier expansion or modification of the clock's functionality in the future.

For BCD encoding, each module will output its value in binary, and then the outputs will be converted to BCD format as needed for display. This approach keeps the logic within each module simple and focused on its core functionality.

The reset logic will be simplified by having each module handle its own reset, ensuring that all components of the clock are correctly reset to their initial states when the reset signal is asserted.
