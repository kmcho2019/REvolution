The new architectural idea is to implement the 12-hour clock using separate counters for hours, minutes, and seconds. Each counter will be clocked by the fast-running 'clk' signal and will increment when the 'ena' signal is high. This approach ensures that the counters are synchronized with the clock signal and can be easily controlled by the enable signal.

The main components of the design will be:

1. A seconds counter that increments every second (when 'ena' is high) and wraps around from 59 to 0.
2. A minutes counter that increments every minute (when the seconds counter wraps around) and wraps around from 59 to 0.
3. An hours counter that increments every hour (when the minutes counter wraps around) and wraps around from 12 to 1 (for AM) or from 12 to 1 (for PM).
4. A reset mechanism that resets all counters to their initial values when 'reset' is high.
5. A module that determines the AM/PM indicator based on the hour and the number of hours passed since 12:00 AM.

The benefits of this approach include:

* Simplified logic: The design only needs to increment separate counters and perform simple conditional checks to determine the AM/PM indicator.
* Improved performance: The design can operate at a higher frequency since the logic is simpler and the critical path is shorter.
* Reduced area: The design only needs separate counters for hours, minutes, and seconds, which reduces the area required.

The code will be written in Verilog and will use synthesizable constructs to ensure that the design can be efficiently implemented on an FPGA or ASIC.
