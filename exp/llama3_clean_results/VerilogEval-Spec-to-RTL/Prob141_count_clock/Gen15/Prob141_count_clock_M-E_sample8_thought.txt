To create a novel and efficient 12-hour clock design in Verilog, we will adopt a more modular and hierarchical approach. The core idea revolves around dividing the clock functionality into smaller, more manageable modules, each responsible for a specific aspect of the clock's operation. This includes separate modules for handling seconds, minutes, hours, and the AM/PM indicator.

The clock's operation will be centered around a main controller module, which will oversee the interaction between these smaller modules and ensure that the clock updates correctly. Each time-keeping module (seconds, minutes, hours) will have its own logic for incrementing and resetting, based on the enable signal and the current state of the other modules.

To implement the 12-hour clock display, we will use a combination of arithmetic and conditional logic to convert the binary representations of hours, minutes, and seconds into their respective BCD formats. This conversion will be done in a way that minimizes complexity and ensures accurate representation of time in the 12-hour format.

The AM/PM indicator will be updated based on the hour value, with the clock toggling between AM and PM at noon and midnight, respectively. The reset functionality will be implemented such that upon assertion of the reset signal, the clock resets to "12:00 AM," ensuring a clean and predictable initial state.

By structuring the design in this modular fashion, we aim to improve code readability, simplify the debugging process, and enhance the overall maintainability of the Verilog code. This approach also allows for easier optimization and extension of the design in the future, should additional features be required.
