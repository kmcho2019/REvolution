Given that the current design is functionally correct but redundant, our primary strategy is to eliminate the redundancy. The existing design uses both an assign statement and a combinational always block to implement the AND gate, which results in two separate outputs for the same logical operation. This redundancy can increase power consumption due to potential switching activity on both outputs. To optimize for power, we can remove one of the methods, either the assign statement or the always block. Since the assign statement is more straightforward and typically more efficient for simple combinational logic, we will remove the always block. This simplification not only reduces power consumption but also minimally impacts area and performance, as the removed logic is redundant and not on the critical path for timing.

Moreover, to maintain the original functionality and interface, we will keep the module structure but simplify the internal implementation. This approach ensures that the optimization does not alter the module's behavior or interface, making it a drop-in replacement in larger designs.
