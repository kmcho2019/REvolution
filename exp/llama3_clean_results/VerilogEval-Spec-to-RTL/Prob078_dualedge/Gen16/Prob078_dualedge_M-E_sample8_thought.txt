To implement a novel architectural idea for a dual-edge triggered flip-flop, we can leverage the concept of time-division multiplexing (TDM) and phase-shifted clocks. Instead of using two separate flip-flops for positive and negative edges, we can utilize a single flip-flop with a phase-shifted clock signal. This approach involves generating two clock signals that are 180 degrees out of phase with each other. The original clock signal serves as one phase, and a phase-shifted version of it, which can be obtained through a simple XOR operation with a delayed version of the clock, acts as the other phase.

By doing so, we effectively capture both the rising and falling edges of the original clock within a single clock period of the phase-shifted signals. This method allows us to use a single flip-flop to mimic the behavior of a dual-edge triggered flip-flop, potentially reducing area and improving power efficiency. The key challenge lies in generating the phase-shifted clock signals efficiently and ensuring that the flip-flop is triggered correctly on both edges of the original clock signal.
