A novel approach to implementing a dual-edge triggered flip-flop involves using a single flip-flop and manipulating the clock signal to achieve the dual-edge triggering effect. This can be accomplished by creating a delayed version of the clock signal and using it to clock the flip-flop on the opposite edge of the original clock. By controlling the data input based on the current state of the clock and its delayed version, the flip-flop can effectively be triggered on both the rising and falling edges of the clock signal. This method reduces the number of flip-flops required and potentially simplifies the overall design, which could lead to improvements in power consumption and area usage.
