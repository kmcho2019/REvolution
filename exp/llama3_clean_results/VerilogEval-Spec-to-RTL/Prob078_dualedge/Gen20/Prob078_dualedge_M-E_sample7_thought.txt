A novel approach to implementing a dual-edge triggered flip-flop without using an always @(posedge clk or negedge clk) sensitivity list involves utilizing a single flip-flop and a clock edge detector circuit. The clock edge detector will identify the rising and falling edges of the clock signal and use these edges to trigger the flip-flop. This can be achieved by comparing the current clock state with its previous state, allowing the detection of both rising and falling edges. The flip-flop can then be triggered on the detected edges, effectively behaving as a dual-edge triggered flip-flop.

The main components of this solution include:
1. A clock edge detector circuit to identify rising and falling edges of the clock signal.
2. A single flip-flop that will be triggered based on the output of the clock edge detector.
3. Combinational logic to determine when the flip-flop should capture the input data based on the detected edges.

This approach aims to minimize the use of resources by leveraging the capability of a single flip-flop, potentially leading to area savings compared to designs that use multiple flip-flops. Additionally, by focusing on edge detection and using a simple flip-flop, this design could offer improvements in power consumption due to reduced switching activity.
