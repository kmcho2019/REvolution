To implement a dual-edge triggered flip-flop on an FPGA, we can use a single-edge triggered flip-flop and a delayed version of the clock signal. The input to the flip-flop is the data signal 'd' and the clock signal is the delayed clock 'clk_d'. The delayed clock is generated by passing the original clock signal through a buffer chain or a series of inverters, introducing a delay that is less than the period of the clock signal. This delayed clock signal is then used as the clock input to the flip-flop. By carefully controlling the delay of the clock signal, we can effectively create a dual-edge triggered behavior where the flip-flop captures the data on both the rising and falling edges of the original clock signal.
