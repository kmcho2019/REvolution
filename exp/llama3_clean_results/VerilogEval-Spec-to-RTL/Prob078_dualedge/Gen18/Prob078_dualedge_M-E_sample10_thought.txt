A novel approach to implement a dual-edge triggered flip-flop in an FPGA, which does not natively support dual-edge triggered flip-flops, involves utilizing a single flip-flop and a clever clocking scheme. The idea is to use a single flip-flop that is triggered on the positive edge of a clock, but the input to this flip-flop is selectively delayed or not delayed based on the current state of the system clock. This can be achieved by using a multiplexer to choose between the immediate input data and the data delayed by one clock cycle. The decision to use the immediate or delayed data is made based on the phase of the clock, effectively allowing the flip-flop to capture data on both the rising and falling edges of the clock. This approach simplifies the circuit by reducing the number of flip-flops required, potentially improving area and power efficiency.
