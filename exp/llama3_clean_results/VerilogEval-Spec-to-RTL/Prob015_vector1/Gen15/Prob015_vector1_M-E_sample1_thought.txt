To create a novel architectural solution for the TopModule, we will introduce a pipeline-based approach. This involves breaking down the combinational logic into multiple stages, allowing the inputs to be processed in a sequential manner. Although the original problem statement does not explicitly require a clocked system, utilizing a pipelined architecture can offer several advantages, including improved timing performance, reduced power consumption due to lower switching activity, and potentially better area efficiency.

Our new solution, named PipelinedSplitter, will consist of two stages: an input register stage and a splitter stage. The input register stage will latch the 16-bit input, in, on the rising edge of the clock. The splitter stage will then split the latched input into the upper and lower bytes and output them.

This approach not only provides a more complex and interesting design but also demonstrates how a seemingly simple combinational problem can be reimagined using a sequential, pipelined architecture. It's worth noting that for a real-world implementation, the choice between a combinational and a pipelined approach would depend on the specific requirements of the system, including timing constraints, power consumption targets, and area limitations.
