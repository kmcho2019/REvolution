To simplify the solution, we can use a simpler state encoding scheme, such as binary encoding, and minimize the number of state transitions. We can also combine the next state logic and the 'done' signal generation into a single always_comb block. Additionally, we can remove the unnecessary 'done_sync' signal and directly assign the 'done' signal based on the current state.

We can define three states: IDLE, BYTE1, and BYTE2. In the IDLE state, we wait for a byte with in[3]=1. Once we receive such a byte, we transition to BYTE1. In the BYTE1 state, we wait for the next byte, regardless of its value. Once we receive the next byte, we transition to BYTE2. In the BYTE2 state, we wait for the next byte, and once we receive it, we assert the 'done' signal and transition back to IDLE.

This simplified approach reduces the number of state transitions and combinational logic gates, making the design more efficient and easier to understand.
