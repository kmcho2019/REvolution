To simplify the existing solution, we can leverage Verilog's built-in `$countones` function directly on the input vector without dividing it into chunks. This approach eliminates the need for the chunking logic, the array of registers to hold the chunk counts, and the compressor tree to sum these counts. By using `$countones` on the entire 255-bit input vector, we can significantly reduce the complexity of the design. This simplified approach may also lead to improvements in area usage and potentially power consumption, as it involves less logic. However, it's essential to verify that the performance (timing) meets the requirements, as the operation now involves a single, potentially complex, function call.
