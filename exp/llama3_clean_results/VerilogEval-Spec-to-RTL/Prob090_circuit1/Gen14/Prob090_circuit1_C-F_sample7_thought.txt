However, since the LUT approach is more suited to complex combinational logic and might be considered over-engineering for a simple AND gate, we'll focus on optimizing the straightforward `assign` statement method.

Our strategy involves recognizing that for simple combinational logic like an AND gate, the most efficient implementation in terms of Power, Performance, and Area (PPA) is typically a direct logical operation. Thus, we'll use the `assign` statement to directly specify the relationship between inputs and outputs, similar to Example 2, but we'll also consider the implications of this choice on PPA metrics, as discussed in the feedback sections of both examples.

The key insight is that for minimalistic combinational logic, simplicity and directness are often the best strategies. This approach minimizes the critical path, reduces switching activity (and thus power consumption), and results in a small area footprint. By using `assign` for a simple AND operation, we achieve a balance between performance, power efficiency, and area usage without unnecessarily complicating the design.
