A novel architectural idea for the 256-to-1 multiplexer involves utilizing a cascade of 16-to-1 multiplexers, but instead of dividing the select signal into two parts, the design employs a decoder to generate enable signals for each multiplexer. The input vector is divided into 16 groups of 16 bits, and each group is fed into a 16-to-1 multiplexer. The decoder takes the 8-bit select signal and generates a one-hot encoded signal, where each bit corresponds to one of the 16 multiplexers. The one-hot encoded signal is then used to enable the corresponding multiplexer, and the output of each multiplexer is fed into an OR gate. The OR gate produces the final output, which is the selected bit from the input vector. This approach allows for a more straightforward and efficient design, as it eliminates the need for a second level of multiplexers.
