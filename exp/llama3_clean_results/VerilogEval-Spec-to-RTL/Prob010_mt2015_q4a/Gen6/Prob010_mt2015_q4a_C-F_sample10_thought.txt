To create a superior solution, we will fuse the best ideas from both examples. The first example utilized a hierarchical design approach by separating the XOR operation into a distinct module, which can be beneficial for readability, maintainability, and potentially for PPA metrics if the separation allows for more efficient technology mapping during synthesis. However, this approach may introduce additional delay due to the module interface and potentially increase power consumption due to the intermediate signal. The second example simplified the boolean function z = (x^y) & x to z = x & ~y, reducing the complexity of the operations and potentially leading to area and power reductions. Our fusion strategy will involve directly implementing the simplified expression z = x & ~y within the TopModule, thus combining the benefits of a simplified logic structure with the efficiency of a direct implementation. This approach minimizes the number of operations, avoids the potential delay of a separate module, and reduces the number of signals, which can contribute to better PPA metrics.
