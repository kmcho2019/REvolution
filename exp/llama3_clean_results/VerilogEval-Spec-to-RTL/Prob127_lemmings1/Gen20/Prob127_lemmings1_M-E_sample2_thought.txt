To create a novel architectural solution for the Lemmings game logic, we can leverage a different approach to finite state machine (FSM) design, focusing on the behavioral aspects of the Lemmings' movement. Instead of directly encoding the states as walking left or right, we can design the FSM to respond to the bump signals by toggling the direction of the Lemmings. This approach simplifies the state machine's logic and focuses on the action (toggling direction) rather than the state (walking left or right).

The new idea involves using a single state variable that indicates the current direction of the Lemmings. When a bump signal is received, the FSM toggles this direction variable, effectively switching the Lemmings' direction. This design eliminates the need for explicit encoding of the walking states, reducing complexity and potentially improving the overall efficiency of the design.

Furthermore, to make the design more scalable and adaptable to potential future additions (like more complex behaviors or additional inputs), we can implement the FSM using a more modular approach, separating the next state logic from the output logic. This modularity allows for easier modification or extension of the design without affecting the core functionality.

By adopting this novel architectural approach, we aim to create a design that is not only efficient in terms of area and power consumption but also highly flexible and adaptable to future requirements.
