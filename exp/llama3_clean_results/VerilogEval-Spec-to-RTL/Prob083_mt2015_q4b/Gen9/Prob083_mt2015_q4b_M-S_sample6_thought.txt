The existing solution is already quite minimal, utilizing a basic XOR operation followed by an inversion. To simplify further, we recognize that the XOR operation (`^`) followed by a negation (`!`) can be directly replaced with an XNOR operation, which is logically equivalent but might offer a slight simplification in terms of understanding the intent behind the code. The XNOR operation directly gives the result of `z` being high when both inputs are the same (both 0 or both 1), which aligns with the desired behavior observed in the simulation waveform. This approach reduces the cognitive complexity by directly expressing the intended logic without needing to understand the intermediate step of XOR followed by inversion.
