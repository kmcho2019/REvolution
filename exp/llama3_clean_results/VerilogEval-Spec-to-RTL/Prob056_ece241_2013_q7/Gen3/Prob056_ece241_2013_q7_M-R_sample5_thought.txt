To refactor the existing JK flip-flop implementation, we can leverage a more concise and expressive way of describing the sequential logic using a single `always` block with a case statement. The core idea is to explicitly handle each combination of J and K inputs as per the given truth table. This approach simplifies the conditional statements and improves readability. Additionally, the use of a non-blocking assignment (`<=`) within the `always` block at the positive edge of the clock ensures that the output Q is updated correctly without any race conditions or dependency on the current value of Q during the execution of the block.
