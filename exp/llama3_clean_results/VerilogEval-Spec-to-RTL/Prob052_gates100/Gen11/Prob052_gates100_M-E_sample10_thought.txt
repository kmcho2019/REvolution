To improve the performance, power, and area (PPA) metrics of the design, I propose a novel architectural idea that leverages a hierarchical, pipelined approach. The main concept is to divide the 100-bit input into smaller segments, but instead of performing the AND, OR, and XOR operations on each segment separately, we can use a pipelined architecture to perform these operations in a staged manner.

The new design will consist of a series of pipeline stages, each responsible for a portion of the operations. Each stage will take the output from the previous stage and perform a portion of the required operations, allowing for a more efficient and area-friendly design.

To further optimize the design, we can use a technique called "carry-save addition" to reduce the number of operations required for the XOR operation. This involves breaking down the XOR operation into smaller components and reusing intermediate results to minimize the number of gates required.

By using a pipelined architecture and carry-save addition, we can significantly reduce the area required for the design, improve performance by reducing the critical path, and lower power consumption by minimizing switching activity.
