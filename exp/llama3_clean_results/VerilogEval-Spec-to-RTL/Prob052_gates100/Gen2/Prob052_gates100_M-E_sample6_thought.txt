A novel approach to implementing the TopModule with 100 inputs and three outputs for AND, OR, and XOR operations is to utilize a hierarchical design strategy. This involves breaking down the large 100-input problem into smaller, manageable chunks, processing each chunk, and then combining the results. Specifically, we can divide the 100 inputs into groups of 4 (since 2^4 = 16, which is close to the sweet spot for gate fan-in in digital design) and perform the AND, OR, and XOR operations within each group. The results from each group can then be further combined using the same operations to obtain the final outputs. This hierarchical approach can potentially reduce the combinational delay, minimize the number of logic gates, and lower power consumption compared to a flat, loop-based implementation.

To further optimize the design, we can leverage Verilog's built-in reduction operators for AND, OR, and XOR, which can simplify the implementation within each group and between groups. The reduction operators (&, |, and ^) applied to a vector (e.g., in[3:0]) will perform the respective operation across all bits of the vector, producing a single-bit result.
