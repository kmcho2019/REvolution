A novel architectural idea for the gshare branch predictor is to use a hybrid approach that combines the benefits of both gshare and bimodal predictors. The proposed architecture includes two separate prediction tables: one for gshare prediction and another for bimodal prediction. The gshare table is used to predict the branch direction based on the global branch history, while the bimodal table is used to predict the branch direction based on the local branch history. 

The predictor uses a selector to choose between the two prediction tables based on the confidence of the predictions. If the confidence of the gshare prediction is high, the predictor uses the gshare table; otherwise, it uses the bimodal table. The confidence of the predictions is determined by the saturating counters in the prediction tables.

The predictor also includes a mechanism to update the global branch history register and the prediction tables based on the training requests. The predictor prioritizes the training requests over the prediction requests and updates the global branch history register accordingly. If a misprediction occurs, the predictor recovers the global branch history register to the state after the mispredicting branch completes execution.

The proposed architecture provides better prediction accuracy and adaptability to different branch behavior, as it combines the benefits of both gshare and bimodal predictors.
