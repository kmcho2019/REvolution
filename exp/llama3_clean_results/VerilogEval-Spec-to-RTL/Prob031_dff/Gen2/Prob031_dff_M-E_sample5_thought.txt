To create a novel architectural idea for a D flip-flop, we can leverage the concept of a latch-based design. Instead of using a traditional edge-triggered flip-flop, we can implement the D flip-flop using two back-to-back latches, a master latch and a slave latch. The master latch is transparent when the clock is high, allowing the input 'd' to be captured, and the slave latch captures the output of the master latch when the clock goes low. This design inherently creates an edge-triggered behavior at the positive edge of the clock, as the slave latch only updates when the clock transitions from high to low, effectively capturing the state of the master latch at the positive edge of the clock. This approach provides a unique perspective on implementing a D flip-flop, differing from the traditional single-clock-edge triggered design.
