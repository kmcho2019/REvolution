To create a novel architectural idea for a D flip-flop, consider using a different approach that still captures the essence of a sequential element but utilizes inherent properties of digital circuits. One such approach is to use a latch-based structure. A D latch is essentially a device that captures its input when enabled and retains that value when disabled. By cleverly combining two such latches with complementary enable signals derived from the clock, we can mimic the behavior of a D flip-flop that captures its input on the positive edge of the clock. This approach is known as a master-slave flip-flop configuration. The first latch (master) captures the input when the clock is low, and then the second latch (slave) captures the output of the master latch when the clock goes high, effectively transferring the data on the positive edge of the clock.
