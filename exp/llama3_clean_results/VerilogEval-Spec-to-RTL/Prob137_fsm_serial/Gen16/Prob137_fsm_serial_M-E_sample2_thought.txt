A novel approach to implementing the finite state machine (FSM) for serial byte detection would be to utilize a hybrid architecture that combines the benefits of both sequential and combinational logic. The new idea involves using a ring counter to keep track of the states and a separate module for data collection and stop bit verification. This modular approach allows for easier modification, testing, and optimization of individual components.

The ring counter will be implemented using a 4-bit register, where each bit represents a different state: IDLE, START, RECEIVE, and STOP. This counter will be designed to cycle through these states based on the input bits and the current state.

The data collection module will be responsible for shifting in the incoming bits and storing them in an 8-bit register. Once all 8 bits have been collected, the module will verify the stop bit and generate a 'done' signal if it is correct.

To improve the PPA metrics, the design will incorporate power-saving techniques such as clock gating and reducing unnecessary switching activity. Additionally, the use of a modular architecture will facilitate easier optimization and modification of individual components, allowing for better area utilization and reduced power consumption.

The new design will also include a synchronous reset mechanism to ensure that the FSM is properly initialized and reset when necessary.
