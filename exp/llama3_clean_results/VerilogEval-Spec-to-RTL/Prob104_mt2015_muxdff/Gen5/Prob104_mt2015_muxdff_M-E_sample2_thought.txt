A novel approach to designing the `TopModule` involves treating it as a basic cell that can be connected in a specific way to replicate the behavior of the `full_module`. This cell will contain a flip-flop and a 2:1 multiplexer. The key insight here is to realize that the operation `{q[1] ^ q[2], q[0], q[2]}` can be decomposed into simpler operations that can be performed in parallel across multiple instances of `TopModule`. Each instance of `TopModule` will handle one bit of the output `Q`. When `L` is asserted, `TopModule` loads the corresponding bit from `r_in`. When `L` is not asserted, it performs the required shift and XOR operation based on inputs from neighboring instances.

The architectural novelty comes from how these instances are connected. Specifically, the output of one instance (`Q`) becomes the input to the next instance (`q_in`), facilitating the shift operation. Additionally, each instance needs to receive the XOR result of the next two instances, which can be achieved through additional wiring and logic outside the `TopModule` itself, or by incorporating this logic within `TopModule` if the interface allows for it.

This approach simplifies the internal logic of `TopModule` while achieving the desired functionality through the connectivity between instances. It emphasizes modularity and the ability to scale the design by adding more instances of `TopModule`, making it suitable for a hierarchical design.
