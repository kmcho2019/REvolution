1.  Store the input bits in a shift register, shifting the bits one position to the right on each clock cycle.
2.  Monitor the input bits to detect the first '1' encountered, which marks the beginning of the conversion.
3.  Once the first '1' is detected, invert the subsequent bits as they are shifted into the register.
4.  After the conversion is complete (i.e., when the reset signal is asserted), shift the converted bits out of the register, starting from the most significant bit.

This approach ensures that the 2's complement conversion is accurately performed on the input sequence, regardless of its length or the position of the first '1'.

The shift register-based architecture offers several advantages, including simplicity, low power consumption, and efficient area usage.
