The new solution involves a novel architectural approach that leverages a single, wide D flip-flop to store the entire shift register state, rather than using multiple D flip-flops in series. This approach can reduce the power consumption and area of the design, as fewer flip-flops are required. The shift operation is performed through bitwise operations on the wide D flip-flop's output, allowing for efficient implementation of the shift register functionality. This approach also simplifies the clock tree and reduces the number of sequential elements, which can improve performance and reduce power consumption.
