To implement a novel architectural solution for the TopModule, we can leverage the concept of a single, wide D flip-flop that can selectively update its bits based on the byte enable signals. This approach involves using a single, 16-bit register and implementing the byte-enable logic within a single always block. The idea is to decode the byte enable signals and then use bitwise operations to selectively update the corresponding bytes of the register.

This approach differs significantly from the original modular approach, as it consolidates the logic into a single module, potentially reducing area usage and improving performance due to reduced overhead from module instantiation. The key to this design is the efficient decoding and manipulation of the byte enable signals to control the register updates.

The advantages of this consolidated approach include potential improvements in area efficiency, as the overhead of multiple module instantiations is avoided, and possible enhancements in performance, due to the reduction in combinatorial logic depth. However, the complexity of the always block and the decoding logic must be carefully managed to ensure that the design remains scalable and maintainable.
