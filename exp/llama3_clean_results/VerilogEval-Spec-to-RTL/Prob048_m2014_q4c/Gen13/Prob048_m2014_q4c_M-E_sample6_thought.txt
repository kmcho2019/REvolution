A novel architectural idea for implementing a D flip-flop with an active high synchronous reset is to use a clock-gated approach. In this design, the clock signal is gated (i.e., enabled or disabled) based on the state of the reset signal. When the reset signal is high, the clock is gated, preventing the flip-flop from clocking and thus holding its output at zero. When the reset signal is low, the clock is enabled, allowing the flip-flop to operate normally. This approach can be more power-efficient in scenarios where the reset signal is frequently asserted, as it reduces the switching activity within the flip-flop. However, it requires careful consideration of the clock tree and potential impact on timing.

To implement this, we can use a simple AND gate to gate the clock signal based on the inverse of the reset signal. The gated clock signal is then used to drive the D flip-flop. The D flip-flop itself can be implemented using a standard always block with a non-blocking assignment to update its output based on the input data at each positive edge of the gated clock.

This approach not only provides a power-efficient way to implement the D flip-flop with synchronous reset but also demonstrates a clock-gating technique that can be applied to more complex digital designs to reduce power consumption.
