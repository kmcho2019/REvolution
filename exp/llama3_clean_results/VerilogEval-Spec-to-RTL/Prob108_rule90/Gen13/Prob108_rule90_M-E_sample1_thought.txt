The novel architectural idea is to implement the Rule 90 cellular automaton using a pipeline architecture. Instead of calculating the next state of all cells in a single clock cycle, the design will be divided into stages, where each stage calculates the next state of a subset of cells. This approach can improve the timing performance by reducing the combinational logic depth and potentially reducing the area and power consumption.

The pipeline will consist of multiple stages, each responsible for calculating the next state of a group of cells. The number of stages and the number of cells per stage will be determined based on the desired trade-off between area, power, and timing performance.

To further optimize the design, the pipeline stages will be designed to reuse the same combinational logic for calculating the next state of each cell. This will reduce the area utilization and power consumption. Additionally, the design will use clock gating to minimize the switching activity and reduce power consumption.

The pipeline will be designed to operate in a synchronous manner, with each stage clocked by the same clock signal. The output of each stage will be registered to ensure that the data is properly synchronized and to reduce the switching activity.
