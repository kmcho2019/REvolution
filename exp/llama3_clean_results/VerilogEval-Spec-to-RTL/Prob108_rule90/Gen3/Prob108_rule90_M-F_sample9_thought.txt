To address the synthesizability issues and optimize the performance, power, and area of the Rule 90 cellular automaton implementation, a revised architectural approach is necessary. The key idea is to minimize the use of explicit shift registers and instead utilize a more straightforward and synthesis-friendly method to calculate the next state of each cell. By directly using the current state of the cells and applying the Rule 90 logic, we can simplify the implementation and reduce potential issues related to synthesizability. Specifically, the approach involves directly calculating the next state of each cell based on its neighbors without explicitly shifting the entire array, which can lead to more efficient synthesis and reduced area consumption. This strategy should also help in minimizing power consumption by reducing unnecessary switching activity. Furthermore, ensuring that all sequential logic is properly synchronized and that the implementation is amenable to optimization by synthesis tools will be crucial.
