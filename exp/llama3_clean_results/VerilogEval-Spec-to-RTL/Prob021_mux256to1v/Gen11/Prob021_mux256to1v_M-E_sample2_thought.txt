The new idea involves organizing the 1024-bit input vector into a tree-like structure, where each level of the tree corresponds to a certain number of bits in the selector. The top level corresponds to the most significant bits of the selector, and each subsequent level corresponds to the next most significant bits, down to the least significant bits.

This approach allows for a more scalable and potentially area-efficient design, as it breaks down the complex selection process into simpler, more manageable parts. The tree-like structure also facilitates the use of shared logic and reduces the number of required multiplexers.

The implementation will utilize a combination of multiplexers and decoders to navigate the tree structure and select the desired 4-bit value. The use of decoders can help in simplifying the logic and reducing the area requirements.

Overall, this novel approach aims to achieve a better balance between area, power, and performance by leveraging the inherent structure of the input data and the selector, and by breaking down the selection process into more manageable stages.
