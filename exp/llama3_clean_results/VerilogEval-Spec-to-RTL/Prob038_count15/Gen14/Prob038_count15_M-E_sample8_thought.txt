To implement a novel architectural idea for the 4-bit binary counter, we can utilize a Johnson counter (also known as a twisted ring counter or switch-tail ring counter) instead of a traditional binary counter. A Johnson counter is a type of shift register where the output of the last stage is inverted and fed back to the first stage. This approach allows us to generate a sequence of binary numbers by shifting the bits. For a 4-bit counter, we can design a 4-stage Johnson counter. Each stage will be a D flip-flop, and the output of the last stage will be inverted and connected to the input of the first stage. The counter will count from 0 to 15 and then wrap around. This design is different from the traditional binary counter and provides an alternative solution to the problem.
