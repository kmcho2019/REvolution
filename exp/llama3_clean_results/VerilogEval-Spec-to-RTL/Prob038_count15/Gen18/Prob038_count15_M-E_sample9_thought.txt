The novel approach involves designing the 4-bit binary counter as a Johnson counter (also known as a twisted ring counter or switch-tail counter), which is a simple and efficient counter design that uses a ring structure to generate the binary count sequence. This approach differs significantly from the traditional direct incrementation method, as it relies on the circular nature of the counter to generate the next state.

In this design, we will utilize a 4-bit shift register to implement the Johnson counter. The basic principle of operation is as follows: the counter's least significant bit (LSB) is inverted and fed back to the most significant bit (MSB) after passing through all the stages, thus creating a circular dependency that allows the counter to cycle through all 16 possible states (0 to 15) before resetting to 0.

To implement this concept, we need to use a combination of logical operations to manage the feedback and shifting of bits within the counter. The reset functionality can be incorporated by setting all bits of the counter to 0 when the reset signal is asserted.

This Johnson counter-based approach offers a unique perspective on the binary counter problem, leveraging the inherent properties of digital circuits to create a compact and potentially power-efficient design.
