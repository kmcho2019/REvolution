To create a novel 4-bit adder architecture, we'll explore the concept of a "split-digit" adder, which breaks down each 4-bit number into two parts: a high 2-bit and a low 2-bit segment. This approach allows us to use smaller, more efficient adders for each segment, potentially reducing area and power consumption. We'll use two 2-bit adders to compute the sum of the high and low segments separately, then combine the results, handling any carry between the segments. This design could offer benefits in terms of reduced power consumption and area, as it avoids the need for a full ripple-carry chain across all 4 bits.

Key components of this design include:
1. Two 2-bit adders for the high and low segments.
2. A mechanism to handle carry between the segments.
3. Logic to combine the segment sums and the carry into a final 5-bit result.

This split-digit approach simplifies the carry propagation, as we only need to handle carry between two segments rather than across each bit position individually.
