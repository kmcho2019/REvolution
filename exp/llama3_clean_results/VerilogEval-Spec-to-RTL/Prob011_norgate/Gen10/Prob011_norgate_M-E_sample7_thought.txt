To create a novel architectural idea for implementing a NOR gate, we can leverage the concept of using two NOT gates and an AND gate. This approach, while not as straightforward as the direct implementation of a NOR gate using the `~` and `|` operators, demonstrates an alternative method for achieving the same logical operation.

The idea involves first inverting the inputs `a` and `b` using two NOT gates. The outputs of these NOT gates are then fed into an AND gate. The AND gate will produce an output of `1` only if both of its inputs are `1`, which corresponds to the condition where both `a` and `b` are `0` (since we inverted them). This effectively implements the NOR logic, where the output is `1` if and only if both inputs are `0`.

This architectural idea can be beneficial in scenarios where the direct implementation of a NOR gate is not feasible or desirable due to specific constraints or requirements of the design environment. It showcases the flexibility and versatility of digital logic design in achieving the same functionality through different combinations of basic logical operations.
