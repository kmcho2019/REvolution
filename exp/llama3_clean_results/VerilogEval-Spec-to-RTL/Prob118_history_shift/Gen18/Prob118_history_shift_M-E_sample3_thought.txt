The new architectural idea involves utilizing a FIFO (First-In-First-Out) structure to implement the 32-bit global history shift register. The concept is to enqueue new branch predictions at the end of the FIFO and dequeue the oldest branch prediction when the FIFO is full. This approach simplifies the shift operation and eliminates the need for a complex combinational logic block.

When a branch misprediction occurs, the FIFO can be loaded with the new history by enqueueing the train_history bits and the actual result of the branch (train_taken). The predict_history output can be obtained by concatenating the bits from the FIFO, ensuring that the youngest branch prediction is at the LSB side.

This approach can be more efficient in terms of area and power consumption, as it avoids the complex logic generated by the ternary operator and the shifting operation. Additionally, the FIFO structure can be easily implemented using a RAM or a shift register, making it a more scalable and flexible solution.
