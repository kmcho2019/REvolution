A novel approach to implementing the 32-bit global history shift register could involve utilizing a FIFO (First-In-First-Out) structure to manage the branch history. This design would treat the history as a queue, where new predictions are added to the end and, when a misprediction occurs, the queue is loaded with the corrected history. The predict_history output would be the current state of the queue, where the least significant bit (LSB) represents the most recent branch direction. By using a FIFO, the design could inherently support rolling back state in response to a pipeline flush caused by a branch misprediction, as the corrected history can be directly loaded into the queue. This approach could potentially offer a different trade-off in terms of area, power, and performance compared to the straightforward register-based implementation.
