To implement a novel architectural solution for the 32-bit global history shift register, I propose a design that incorporates a hybrid approach combining the benefits of synchronous and asynchronous design elements. The idea revolves around using a pair of registers: one for storing the current history (`history`) and another for temporarily holding the updated history (`next_history`) before it is committed to `history`. This allows for a more flexible and scalable design, especially when considering the requirement to roll back the state in response to a pipeline flush due to a branch misprediction.

The key innovation in this approach is the introduction of a "commit" signal (`commit_update`) that controls when the updated history from `next_history` is loaded into `history`. This commit signal is generated based on the input conditions, ensuring that the update is only committed when necessary, thus minimizing unnecessary switching activity and potentially improving power efficiency.

Furthermore, the design will utilize a conditional clocking technique for the `history` register, where the clock is only enabled when an update is required. This is achieved through a clock enable signal (`clk_en`) that is derived from the input conditions. By gating the clock in this manner, power consumption can be reduced, as the register is only clocked when an update is necessary.

The `next_history` register will be updated based on the current inputs (`predict_taken`, `train_mispredicted`, `train_history`, and `train_taken`), and its value will be loaded into `history` when the `commit_update` signal is asserted. This two-stage approach allows for a clean separation of the update logic from the commit logic, making the design more modular and easier to optimize for performance and area.

Overall, this novel architectural idea focuses on enhancing the efficiency and flexibility of the history shift register by leveraging a combination of synchronous and asynchronous design techniques, along with conditional clocking to minimize power consumption.
