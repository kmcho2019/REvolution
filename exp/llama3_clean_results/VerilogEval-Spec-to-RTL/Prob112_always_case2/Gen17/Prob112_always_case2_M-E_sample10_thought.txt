My novel approach involves utilizing a hierarchical and sequential bit comparison strategy. The idea is to use a combination of logical operations to compare bits from most significant to least significant, determining the position of the first '1' bit in the input vector. This strategy reduces the complexity of the combinational logic and avoids the need for an extensive case statement. The architecture consists of a series of conditional checks that evaluate the input bits in a descending order of significance, leveraging the property that in a priority encoder, the first '1' bit encountered from the most significant bit position dictates the output. This method allows for a more scalable and potentially area-efficient design, as it avoids the exponential growth of a case statement with increasing input vector size.
