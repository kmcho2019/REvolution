A novel approach to implementing a decade counter could involve utilizing a ring counter structure. In a ring counter, each stage of the counter is connected in a circular manner, and the output of each stage is fed back into the input of the previous stage after a certain number of clock cycles. This creates a circulating pattern of states that can be used to generate a sequence of counts. However, instead of using a traditional ring counter with flip-flops, we can leverage the properties of a Johnson counter (a type of ring counter) and simplify it for a decade counter application. We will use a 4-bit shift register where each bit represents a state in the sequence. The output of the most significant bit (MSB) is fed back into the least significant bit (LSB) after passing through a NOT gate to ensure the sequence restarts after reaching '1001' (9 in decimal). This approach allows for a simple and area-efficient implementation of a decade counter.
