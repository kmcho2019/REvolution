A novel architectural approach to designing a decade counter involves utilizing a Johnson counter (also known as a twisted-ring counter or switch-tail ring counter) with a slight modification to accommodate the decade counting requirement. The Johnson counter is typically used for generating a sequence of binary numbers in a circular manner. By using a 4-bit Johnson counter and adding a control logic to limit the count to 10 (from 0 to 9), we can achieve the decade counter functionality. This approach is distinct from the straightforward binary counter implementation and offers an interesting alternative perspective on digital counter design.

In this design, the Johnson counter will be used to generate the counting sequence, and an additional control logic will be responsible for detecting when the count reaches 10 and resetting it to 0. This solution requires careful consideration of the counter's behavior and the control logic to ensure proper operation.

The advantage of using a Johnson counter in this context is its inherent ability to generate a circular sequence, which can be leveraged to create the decade counter with minimal additional logic. However, it's essential to note that this approach might introduce additional complexity compared to a straightforward binary counter, and its suitability depends on the specific requirements and constraints of the project.
