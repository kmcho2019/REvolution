To implement the decade counter with a novel architectural idea, we will use a Johnson counter (also known as a twisted ring counter or switch-tail ring counter) and modify it to meet the decade counter requirements. A Johnson counter is a type of ring counter that generates a sequence of binary numbers by shifting the bits in a circular manner. We can use a 4-bit Johnson counter to generate a sequence of 10 unique states, which can be decoded to produce the desired decade counter output. This approach eliminates the need for explicit conditional statements and increment operations, potentially reducing power consumption and area usage.
