A novel approach to finding the minimum of four input values involves utilizing a tree-like structure that compares inputs in a hierarchical manner. This structure, known as a tournament tree or a comparison tree, allows for the simultaneous comparison of multiple inputs, reducing the overall number of comparison operations required. The key idea is to arrange the comparisons in a way that each node in the tree represents a comparison between two inputs, with the winner (the smaller value) progressing to the next level of the tree. The final output of the tree will be the minimum value among all inputs. This approach can be particularly efficient in digital circuits due to its ability to parallelize comparisons, potentially leading to faster execution times compared to sequential comparison methods.
