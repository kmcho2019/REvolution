To introduce a novel architectural idea, we can leverage the concept of hierarchical state machines, where we have an outer state machine that manages the Lemming's overall state (walking or falling) and an inner state machine that handles the walking direction (left or right). This approach allows for a cleaner separation of concerns and potentially simplifies the logic for state transitions and output generation.

The outer state machine will primarily be responsible for determining whether the Lemming is walking or falling, based on the `ground` signal. The inner state machine will handle the direction of walking based on the `bump_left` and `bump_right` signals. When the Lemming is falling, the inner state machine's state (direction) will be frozen until the Lemming starts walking again. This way, we maintain the previous walking direction during a fall and resume it correctly when the ground reappears.

By structuring the state machine in this hierarchical manner, we can reduce the complexity of the state transition logic and potentially improve the design's overall readability and maintainability. Additionally, this structure might offer opportunities for further optimization, especially in terms of reducing the number of state transitions and thus potentially lowering power consumption.
