To implement a novel architectural idea for the cellphone's ringer and vibration motor control, we can use a finite state machine (FSM) approach. The FSM will have two states: 'idle' and 'ringing'. When the phone is in 'idle' state and receives an incoming call (ring input is high), it transitions to the 'ringing' state. In the 'ringing' state, it checks the vibrate_mode input to decide whether to turn on the ringer or the motor. This approach allows for easy extension to more complex states or modes in the future, such as silent mode or do-not-disturb mode, by simply adding more states and transitions to the FSM.
