A novel architectural idea to implement the cellphone's ringer and vibration motor control circuit involves utilizing a finite state machine (FSM) to manage the different states of the phone. Although the current design is purely combinational and straightforward, introducing an FSM can provide a structured approach to handling more complex behaviors if the requirements evolve. In this scenario, the FSM can have two main states: 'idle' and 'ringing'. When the phone is in the 'idle' state, both the ringer and motor are off. Upon receiving an incoming call (input 'ring' goes high), the FSM transitions to the 'ringing' state. In the 'ringing' state, the FSM checks the 'vibrate_mode' input and decides whether to turn on the ringer or the motor. This approach allows for easier expansion of the phone's behavior in the future, such as adding more states for different types of notifications or integrating additional features.
