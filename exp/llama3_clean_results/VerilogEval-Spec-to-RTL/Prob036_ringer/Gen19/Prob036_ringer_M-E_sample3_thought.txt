To implement a novel architectural idea for the cellphone's ringer and vibration motor control, we can leverage a finite state machine (FSM) approach. The FSM will have two states: Idle and Ringing. When the phone is not ringing, it will be in the Idle state. Upon receiving a ring signal, it will transition to the Ringing state. In the Ringing state, the FSM will check the vibrate_mode input. If vibrate_mode is 1, it will turn on the motor; otherwise, it will turn on the ringer. This approach allows for a more structured and scalable design, making it easier to add more features in the future, such as different ring modes or silent modes.
