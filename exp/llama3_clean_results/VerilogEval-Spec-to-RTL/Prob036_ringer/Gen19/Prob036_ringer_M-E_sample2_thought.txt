A novel architectural idea for this problem is to use a finite state machine (FSM) to control the cellphone's ringer and vibration motor. The FSM can have two states: an idle state and a ringing state. When the phone is in the idle state and an incoming call is received (input ring = 1), it transitions to the ringing state. In the ringing state, the FSM checks the vibrate mode (input vibrate_mode) and turns on either the ringer or the motor accordingly. This approach provides a clear and structured way to manage the different states and transitions of the cellphone's ringer and vibration motor.
