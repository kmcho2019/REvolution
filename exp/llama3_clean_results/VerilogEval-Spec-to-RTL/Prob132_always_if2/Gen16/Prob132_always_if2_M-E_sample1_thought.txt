A novel architectural idea to refactor the given code is to utilize a Finite State Machine (FSM) that can efficiently manage the different states of the system, such as shutting off the computer due to overheating and deciding whether to keep driving based on arrival and gas tank status. This approach can simplify the design and reduce the number of logic gates required, leading to potential improvements in area and power consumption.

We can define two main states: 'normal' and 'overheated'. In the 'normal' state, the system checks if the computer has arrived and if the gas tank is not empty, and decides whether to keep driving accordingly. In the 'overheated' state, the system shuts off the computer and does not consider the 'keep driving' decision.

This FSM-based approach allows for a more structured and scalable design, making it easier to add or modify states and transitions in the future. Additionally, the use of a single FSM module can reduce the overhead of instantiating multiple separate modules, potentially improving the overall area efficiency.
