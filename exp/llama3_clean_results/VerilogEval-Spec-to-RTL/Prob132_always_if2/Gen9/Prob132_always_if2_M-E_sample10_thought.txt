The existing code implements a simple decision-making logic for shutting off a computer due to overheating and deciding whether to keep driving based on arrival status and gas tank level. A novel architectural idea would be to introduce a finite state machine (FSM) that can handle these decisions in a more structured and scalable way. The FSM can have distinct states for the computer's power status and the driving status, and it can transition between these states based on the input signals. This approach allows for easier addition of new features or conditions in the future, as it provides a clear and organized framework for managing the different states and transitions.

The proposed FSM will have four states: IDLE (initial state), COMPUTER_ON (computer is running), DRIVING (vehicle is moving), and COMPUTER_OFF (computer is shut off). The transitions between these states will be controlled by the input signals cpu_overheated, arrived, and gas_tank_empty. For example, if the computer is currently on (COMPUTER_ON state) and it overheats, the FSM will transition to the COMPUTER_OFF state. Similarly, if the vehicle has not arrived (DRIVING state) and the gas tank is not empty, the FSM will remain in the DRIVING state.
