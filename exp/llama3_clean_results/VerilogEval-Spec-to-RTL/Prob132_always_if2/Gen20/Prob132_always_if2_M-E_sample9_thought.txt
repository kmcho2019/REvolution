The existing module has a straightforward approach to determining when to shut off the computer and when to keep driving. However, it does not handle cases where multiple conditions are met simultaneously, such as when the CPU is overheated and the car has not arrived but the gas tank is empty. To improve this, we can introduce a state machine that evaluates all conditions and takes appropriate actions based on the current state of the system.

This novel architectural idea involves implementing a finite state machine (FSM) with states that correspond to different operating conditions of the system. The FSM will evaluate the inputs (cpu_overheated, arrived, gas_tank_empty) and transition between states accordingly, ensuring that the system responds correctly to all possible combinations of input conditions.

For example, the states could be defined as follows:
- Idle: Initial state, waiting for input conditions to change.
- ShutDown: CPU is overheated, shut down the computer.
- Drive: Car has not arrived and gas tank is not empty, keep driving.
- Refuel: Car has not arrived but gas tank is empty, stop to refuel.
- ArrivedState: Car has arrived, stop driving.

By using an FSM, we can ensure that the system behaves predictably and correctly under all possible input conditions, and we can easily add more states or transitions if needed to handle additional scenarios.
