The existing solution involves separate modules for determining whether to shut off the computer and whether to keep driving. This approach is modular and easy to understand. However, a novel architectural idea could be to use a finite state machine (FSM) that encapsulates both the logic for shutting off the computer and the decision to keep driving. This FSM would have states that correspond to the various conditions (e.g., computer overheated, arrived, gas tank empty) and would transition between these states based on the inputs. The outputs (shut_off_computer and keep_driving) would be determined by the current state of the FSM. This approach could potentially reduce the area requirement by minimizing the amount of logic needed, as the same state machine could handle both decisions. It also offers a structured way to manage complex logic, especially if the requirements were to become more intricate in the future.
