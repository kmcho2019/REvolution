To implement a novel architectural idea for the sequence detection problem, we can utilize a shift register to store the recent history of the input data stream. This approach allows us to continuously compare the contents of the shift register with the target sequence '1101' without the need for explicit state machines. This method simplifies the logic and potentially reduces the area and power consumption by minimizing the number of states and conditional checks required.

The shift register approach also offers a scalable solution if the sequence length needs to be adjusted in the future, as it primarily involves modifying the length of the shift register and the comparison logic. Additionally, this method is more intuitive for sequence detection problems, as it directly implements the concept of examining a window of recent input data for a match.
