To approach this problem with a novel architectural idea, I plan to utilize a lookup table (LUT) based design. The LUT will store the output values for each possible combination of inputs `c[1:0]`. This method allows for a more straightforward and potentially area-efficient implementation compared to the traditional multiplexer approach.

The idea is to use the two least significant bits of `c` (`c[1:0]`) as an index into the LUT. For each index value (00, 01, 10, 11), the corresponding output will be `b`, `e`, `a`, and `d`, respectively. Additionally, I will implement a condition to check if the two most significant bits of `c` (`c[3:2]`) are not both zero, and if so, override the output to `4'b1111`.

This LUT-based design simplifies the logic and could lead to improvements in area and power consumption, as it reduces the number of conditional statements and bitwise operations required.
