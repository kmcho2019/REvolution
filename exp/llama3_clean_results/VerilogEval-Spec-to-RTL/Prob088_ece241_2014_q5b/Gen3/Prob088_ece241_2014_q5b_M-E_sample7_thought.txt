A novel architectural approach to implement the given Mealy finite-state machine as a 2's complementer is to utilize a toggle-based design. The idea is to maintain a single-bit register that toggles (inverts) its state whenever the input `x` is high. This register's state will directly determine the output `z`. When the register is in its initial state (reset), `z` will be equal to `x`, and when the register is in its toggled state, `z` will be the inverse of `x`. This design inherently implements the state transitions of the Mealy machine without explicitly defining states A and B. The toggle operation serves as a proxy for the state transitions, simplifying the logic and potentially reducing area and power consumption.
