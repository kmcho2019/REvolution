To provide a completely new and different solution, we can approach the problem by utilizing a shift register to reverse the bit order of the input. The idea involves shifting the input bits into a shift register, and then shifting them out in the reverse order to form the output. This method is novel compared to the direct assignment approach or the use of a generate block with a for loop. The shift register can be implemented using a series of D flip-flops connected in a chain, where each flip-flop represents a bit in the register.

Our strategy will focus on:

1. **Shift Register Implementation:** Create a shift register that can hold all 100 bits of the input. This can be achieved by instantiating 100 D flip-flops and connecting them in a chain.
2. **Input Shift:** Shift the input bits into the shift register, one bit at a time, from the least significant bit to the most significant bit.
3. **Output Shift:** After all input bits have been shifted into the register, shift the bits out of the register, from the most significant bit to the least significant bit, to form the output.

This approach, while more complex than the direct assignment, offers a unique perspective on the problem and can be useful in scenarios where the input bits need to be processed sequentially or where the reversal needs to be done in a pipeline fashion.
