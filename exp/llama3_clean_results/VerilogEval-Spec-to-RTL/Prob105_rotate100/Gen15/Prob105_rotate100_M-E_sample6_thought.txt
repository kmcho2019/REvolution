Instead of performing the rotation by concatenating bits as in the previous examples, this approach involves dividing the 100-bit register into smaller segments and using a Barrel Shifter to rotate these segments. The basic idea is to use a Barrel Shifter as a component that can shift the bits of the register in a circular manner, thus achieving rotation. This architecture is more modular and can potentially be more efficient in terms of area and power consumption, especially for larger register sizes. 

The main components of this design include the Barrel Shifter, a control unit to handle the load and rotation enable signals, and a multiplexer to select between the loaded data, the rotated register content, or the unmodified register content based on the control signals. The Barrel Shifter can be designed to rotate the register content by one position in either direction (left or right) based on the enable signals.

By dividing the register into smaller segments, the design can potentially achieve better timing and area efficiency. Moreover, the use of a Barrel Shifter allows for a more scalable design that can easily be adapted to larger or smaller register sizes by adjusting the number of segments and the control logic accordingly.
