To introduce a novel architectural idea, let's consider utilizing a ring counter-based approach for the 100-bit left/right rotator. This concept involves designing a circular shift register where each bit is connected to its adjacent bit, forming a ring. The rotation direction is controlled by selecting which bit to use as the input for the next clock cycle.

In this approach, we will implement a 100-bit ring counter with a load mechanism. When the load signal is high, the ring counter is loaded with the input data. The rotation direction and enable are controlled by the ena signal, where ena[1:0] = 2'b01 rotates right, ena[1:0] = 2'b10 rotates left, and other values do not rotate.

This design takes advantage of the inherent properties of a ring counter to efficiently rotate bits without needing explicit barrel shifter logic or complex case statements. It simplifies the rotation logic and potentially reduces the area and power consumption compared to previous implementations.

To further optimize this design, clock gating can be applied to reduce power consumption when the rotation is not enabled. Additionally, the ring counter structure can be optimized for the specific use case, considering factors like the number of bits and the required rotation directions.

Overall, the ring counter-based approach offers a unique architectural perspective on implementing the 100-bit left/right rotator, focusing on simplicity, efficiency, and potential power savings.
