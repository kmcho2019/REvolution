To simplify the solution, we can analyze the Karnaugh map again. The output is 1 whenever `a` is 1, regardless of `b` and `c`. When `a` is 0, the output is 1 if either `b` or `c` is 1. This can be directly expressed as a logical OR operation between `a` and the result of `(b | c)`, since when `a` is 1, the OR operation will always yield 1, and when `a` is 0, the output depends solely on `(b | c)`.
