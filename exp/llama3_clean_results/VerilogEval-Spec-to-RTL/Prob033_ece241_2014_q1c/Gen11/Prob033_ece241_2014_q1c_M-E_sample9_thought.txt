To create a novel solution, let's consider an alternative approach that incorporates a more streamlined and integrated design. Instead of separating the addition and overflow detection into distinct modules, we can merge these functionalities into a single, unified module. This approach leverages the arithmetic logic unit (ALU) concept, where a single unit performs multiple related operations. 

The key insight here is to recognize that the overflow detection is inherently linked to the addition process, especially when dealing with 2's complement arithmetic. By embedding the overflow detection directly within the addition logic, we can potentially reduce the overall area and power consumption, as well as simplify the design by minimizing the number of module interfaces and signal routing.

The new design, dubbed "UnifiedAdder", will perform the 8-bit addition and simultaneously detect any overflow condition. This is achieved by monitoring the carry-out from the most significant bit (MSB) addition, which indicates an overflow when the signs of the operands are the same and the result's sign differs from the operands' signs.

This unified approach aims to optimize the design in terms of Power, Performance, and Area (PPA) metrics by reducing unnecessary signal routing, minimizing the number of logic gates required, and potentially lowering power consumption by reducing switching activity on internal nets.
