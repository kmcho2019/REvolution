To introduce a novel architectural idea, we will employ a residue number system (RNS) to perform the 8-bit addition and overflow detection. The RNS is a non-weighted, non-positional numeral system that can provide faster arithmetic operations by utilizing the properties of modular arithmetic. This approach can potentially offer advantages in terms of performance and area efficiency.

The idea involves decomposing the 8-bit numbers into smaller residues, performing the addition in the residue domain, and then converting the result back to the conventional binary representation. The overflow detection can be integrated into this process by monitoring the residue values and their relationship to the modulus.

By leveraging the residue number system, we can create a design that is potentially more efficient in terms of area and power consumption, as the operations in the residue domain can be simpler and more parallelizable compared to traditional binary arithmetic. This could be particularly beneficial in applications where high-speed arithmetic operations are required.

To implement this idea, we will define a module for residue number system conversion, another for addition within the residue domain, and a final module for converting the result back to binary and detecting overflow.
