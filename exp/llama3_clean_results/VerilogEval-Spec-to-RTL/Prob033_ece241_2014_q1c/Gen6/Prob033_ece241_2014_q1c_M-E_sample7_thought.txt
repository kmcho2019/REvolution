A novel approach to implement the 8-bit adder with signed overflow detection is to use a parallel prefix adder architecture. This architecture is based on the concept of calculating the carry bits in parallel using a prefix tree structure, which reduces the delay of the addition operation compared to the ripple-carry adder.

The parallel prefix adder consists of three stages: generate, propagate, and sum. In the generate stage, the generate signals (g) are calculated based on the input bits. The propagate signals (p) are calculated in the same stage. In the propagate stage, the prefix signals (p:) are calculated using the propagate and generate signals. Finally, in the sum stage, the sum bits are calculated using the prefix signals and the input bits.

For signed overflow detection, we can observe the carry into the MSB and the carry out of the MSB. If the carry into the MSB is 1 and the carry out of the MSB is 1, it means that the sum is greater than 2^7 - 1 (the maximum positive value for 8-bit 2's complement), resulting in a signed overflow. Similarly, if the carry into the MSB is 0 and the carry out of the MSB is 0, it means that the sum is less than -2^7 (the minimum negative value for 8-bit 2's complement), resulting in a signed overflow.
