A novel approach to implementing a 2-1 multiplexer could involve using a combination of bitwise operations and a barrel shifter. However, a more innovative and potentially efficient method, especially for large input vectors like 100 bits, could be to utilize a lookup table (LUT) based architecture. The idea is to precompute and store the output for all possible combinations of the select line and the corresponding bits of the input vectors 'a' and 'b'. This approach can be highly efficient in terms of area and power, as it avoids the need for complex logic and directly maps inputs to outputs. However, due to the nature of the problem, which involves selecting between two large vectors based on a single bit, a direct implementation using LUTs for each bit position would be impractical due to the sheer size of the LUTs required. Instead, a more feasible novel approach could involve using a series of smaller multiplexers that are cascaded or arranged in a tree-like structure to select bits or groups of bits from 'a' or 'b' based on the 'sel' input. This could potentially reduce the complexity and improve the scalability of the design. Nonetheless, for simplicity and adherence to conventional digital design practices, leveraging the existing ternary operator or case statement for a straightforward multiplexer implementation remains a preferred approach due to its simplicity, readability, and synthesizability.
