To solve this problem, I propose a novel architectural idea that utilizes a finite state machine (FSM) with a separate counter to track the number of clock cycles. The FSM will have two main states: an IDLE state and a SHIFT_ENABLE state. When the reset signal is asserted, the FSM transitions to the SHIFT_ENABLE state and initializes the counter to 4. The counter then decrements every clock cycle, and when it reaches 0, the FSM transitions back to the IDLE state. During the SHIFT_ENABLE state, the shift_ena signal is asserted. This approach ensures that the shift_ena signal is asserted for exactly 4 clock cycles after reset and remains low until the next reset.

The main advantage of this approach is its simplicity and ease of implementation. The FSM logic is straightforward, and the counter is used to accurately track the number of clock cycles. This design also meets the requirements of being synchronous and triggered on the positive edge of the clock.
