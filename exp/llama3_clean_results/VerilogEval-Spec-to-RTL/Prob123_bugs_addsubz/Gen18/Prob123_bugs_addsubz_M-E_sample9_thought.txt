A novel architectural idea for an adder-subtractor with a zero flag is to use a hybrid approach that combines the benefits of a ripple-carry adder (RCA) and a parallel prefix adder (PPA). This hybrid architecture can take advantage of the simplicity and low area of RCAs for smaller operands while leveraging the high-speed capabilities of PPAs for larger operands. Additionally, the design can incorporate a separate zero detection circuit that utilizes a tree of AND gates to efficiently determine if the output is zero.

The hybrid adder can be designed to dynamically switch between RCA and PPA modes based on the size of the operands or the clock frequency. For example, when the operands are small or the clock frequency is low, the RCA mode can be used to reduce area and power consumption. Conversely, when the operands are large or the clock frequency is high, the PPA mode can be enabled to minimize latency and maximize throughput.

To further enhance the design, the zero detection circuit can be optimized using a hierarchical approach, where the output is divided into smaller segments, and each segment is checked for zero using a smaller AND tree. This hierarchical approach can reduce the number of gates required for zero detection, resulting in improved area efficiency and reduced power consumption.
