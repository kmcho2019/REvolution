The new solution, dubbed "Neighbourhood Processor", approaches the problem by designing a modular, pipelined architecture that processes each bit and its neighbours in a serial manner, rather than in parallel as seen in the original solution. This novel approach involves three primary stages: 
1. Bit Shift and Alignment: Each bit of the input vector is shifted and aligned with its neighbour to the left (for 'both' and 'different' operations) and to the right (for 'any' operation) in separate registers. 
2. Operation Execution: The aligned bits are then processed in combinational logic to produce the desired outputs. For 'both', an AND operation is performed; for 'any', an OR operation; and for 'different', an XOR operation is used.
3. Output Alignment: The processed results are then aligned back to their original positions within the output vectors.

This pipelined approach allows for a more linear and scalable design, potentially reducing area usage and power consumption by minimizing the complexity of the combinational logic at each stage. Additionally, it enables easier extension to larger input vectors without a significant increase in complexity, making it a more flexible solution for various applications.
