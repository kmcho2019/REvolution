The novel architectural idea involves designing the TopModule as a finite state machine (FSM) with two primary states: Idle and Counting. In the Idle state, the module waits for the load signal to become high, at which point it transitions to the Counting state. Once in the Counting state, it starts decrementing the counter until it reaches zero, at which point it asserts the terminal count signal (tc) and remains in the Counting state, preventing further decrements until the load signal becomes high again.

This approach simplifies the logic and potentially reduces the area required, as it eliminates the need for a separate down-counter module and directly integrates the counter logic within the state machine. Additionally, by explicitly defining the states and transitions, the design becomes more modular and easier to understand, potentially leading to improved maintainability and scalability.

To further optimize the design, the FSM can be implemented using a one-hot encoding scheme, where each state is represented by a unique binary vector. This encoding method can simplify the logic and improve the performance of the FSM by reducing the complexity of the next-state logic and the output logic.
