To implement a countdown timer with a novel architectural idea, we can utilize a shift register-based approach. This method involves loading the countdown value into a shift register and then shifting the bits out one by one at each clock cycle. When the last bit is shifted out, it indicates that the countdown has reached 0. This approach eliminates the need for a traditional counter and can potentially reduce area and power consumption.

The key components of this design include a shift register to hold the countdown value, a load signal to load the new value into the shift register, and a terminal count (tc) signal to indicate when the countdown has reached 0. The shift register can be implemented using D flip-flops, and the tc signal can be generated by checking if the shift register is empty.

This approach offers several advantages, including simplicity, low power consumption, and minimal area usage. Additionally, it can be easily scaled up or down depending on the required countdown range.
