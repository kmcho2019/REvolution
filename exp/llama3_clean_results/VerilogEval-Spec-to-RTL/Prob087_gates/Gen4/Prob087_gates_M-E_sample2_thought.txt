To create a novel architectural solution, we can leverage the concept of lookup tables (LUTs) to implement the combinational logic. This approach involves using a single entity (in this case, a 2-to-1 multiplexer tree or a single large LUT) to generate all the output values based on the inputs a and b. However, Verilog natively supports a more straightforward method to achieve similar efficiency and flexibility: using always blocks and case statements or if-else conditions to generate outputs. The idea is to map the input combinations of a and b to their respective output values for each gate type. This method can offer a clean and unified way to express the logic, although it might be less efficient in terms of area and power compared to direct gate implementations, depending on the synthesis tools and target technology. The focus here is on showcasing an alternative, high-level design approach rather than optimizing for PPA metrics directly.
