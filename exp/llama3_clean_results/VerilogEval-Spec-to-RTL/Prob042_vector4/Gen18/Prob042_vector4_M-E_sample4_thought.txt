To create a novel architectural solution for sign-extending an 8-bit number to 32 bits, we will leverage the capabilities of bitwise shift operations and conditional statements in Verilog. The idea revolves around using a single always block to handle the sign extension based on the most significant bit (MSB) of the input. If the MSB is '1', indicating a negative number in two's complement representation, we shift a vector of ones 24 bits to the right and then bitwise OR it with the input shifted 24 bits to the left. This effectively fills the most significant 24 bits with '1's (sign extension for negative numbers) and keeps the original 8 bits. If the MSB is '0', indicating a positive number, we simply shift the input 24 bits to the left, filling the most significant 24 bits with '0's.

This approach eliminates the need for explicit replication of the MSB and utilizes the inherent properties of binary numbers and bitwise operations, potentially leading to a more efficient implementation in terms of area and power consumption.
