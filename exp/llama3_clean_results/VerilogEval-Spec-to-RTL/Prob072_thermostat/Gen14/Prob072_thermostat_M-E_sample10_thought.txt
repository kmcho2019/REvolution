To implement a novel architectural idea for the thermostat controller, we can design a finite state machine (FSM) that captures the modes and conditions of the thermostat. The FSM will have four states: Idle, Heating, Cooling, and FanOnly. The transitions between these states will be based on the input signals mode, too_cold, too_hot, and fan_on. This approach allows for a clear and structured way to manage the different conditions and modes of the thermostat, potentially making the code more readable and maintainable. 

The Idle state will be the default state where none of the heater, air conditioner, or fan are on. When the mode is 1 (heating mode) and it's too cold, the FSM will transition to the Heating state, turning on the heater and fan. When the mode is 0 (cooling mode) and it's too hot, the FSM will transition to the Cooling state, turning on the air conditioner and fan. If fan_on is asserted at any time, the FSM will transition to the FanOnly state, turning on the fan regardless of the other conditions. 

This FSM-based approach provides a systematic way to handle the different scenarios and conditions of the thermostat, potentially making the design more robust and easier to understand.
