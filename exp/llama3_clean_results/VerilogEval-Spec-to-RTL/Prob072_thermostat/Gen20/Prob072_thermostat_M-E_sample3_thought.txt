To provide a novel architectural solution for the thermostat controller, we can introduce a finite state machine (FSM) that encapsulates the different states of the thermostat based on the mode and temperature conditions. This approach allows for a clear and structured implementation of the control logic, making it easier to manage different scenarios and transitions between them.

The FSM can have four main states: 
1. Idle - The initial state where no heating or cooling is required.
2. Heating - The state where the heater is turned on due to it being too cold.
3. Cooling - The state where the air conditioner is turned on due to it being too hot.
4. FanOnly - A state where only the fan is turned on as requested by the user.

Transitions between these states can be triggered by changes in the `mode`, `too_cold`, `too_hot`, and `fan_on` signals. This FSM-based approach not only provides a clear structure but also allows for easy extension or modification of the thermostat's behavior in the future.

By using an FSM, we can simplify the combinational logic and potentially reduce the area and power consumption compared to a purely combinational implementation. Additionally, this approach makes the design more modular and easier to understand, which can be beneficial for maintenance and future modifications.
