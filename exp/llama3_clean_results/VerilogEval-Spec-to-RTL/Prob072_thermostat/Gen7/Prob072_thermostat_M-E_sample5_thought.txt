To create a novel architectural solution for the thermostat controller, we can leverage a state machine approach. This design will define specific states for the system (e.g., idle, heating, cooling) and use these states to determine the outputs. The transitions between states will be based on the inputs (mode, too_cold, too_hot, fan_on). By organizing the logic around states and transitions, we can achieve a clean and scalable design. This approach allows for easy addition of more complex logic or states in the future if needed.

The state machine will have an idle state where the heater, aircon, and fan are off. It will transition to a heating state when mode is high and too_cold is high, turning on the heater and fan. It will transition to a cooling state when mode is low and too_hot is high, turning on the aircon and fan. The fan can also be turned on independently when fan_on is high, without changing the state related to heating or cooling. This design ensures that the system's behavior is well-structured and predictable.
