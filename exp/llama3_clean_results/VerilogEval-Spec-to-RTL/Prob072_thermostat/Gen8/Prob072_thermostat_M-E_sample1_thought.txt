To create a novel architectural solution for the thermostat controller, we can leverage a finite state machine (FSM) approach. The FSM can have distinct states for heating, cooling, and idle (or off) modes, allowing for a more structured and scalable design. This approach enables easy extension of the thermostat's functionality in the future, such as adding more states for different operating conditions or incorporating additional control logic.

The FSM will be controlled by the `mode` input, switching between heating and cooling states. Within each mode, the FSM will check the temperature conditions (`too_cold` or `too_hot`) to determine whether to activate the heater or air conditioner. The fan's operation will be tied to the activation of the heater or air conditioner, with the additional condition of `fan_on` allowing manual override.

This design provides a clear and modular implementation of the thermostat's logic, making it easier to understand, modify, and expand upon. By using an FSM, we can also more easily incorporate additional features or complexities in the thermostat's operation, such as adaptive temperature control or energy-saving modes, by simply adding new states or transitions.
