To introduce a novel architectural idea, let's consider a finite state machine (FSM) approach for the thermostat controller. The FSM can have states that reflect the current mode (heating or cooling) and the status of the heater, air conditioner, and fan. This approach allows for a more structured and modular implementation, where each state transition and output is clearly defined based on the current state and inputs.

The FSM will have two main states: `HEATING` and `COOLING`, which correspond to the `mode` input. Within each state, the FSM will check the `too_cold` or `too_hot` signals to decide whether to turn on the heater or air conditioner, respectively. The fan will be turned on whenever the heater or air conditioner is on, or if the `fan_on` signal is high.

This FSM-based approach provides a clean and scalable way to manage the thermostat's behavior, making it easier to add more features or modify existing ones without complicating the logic.
