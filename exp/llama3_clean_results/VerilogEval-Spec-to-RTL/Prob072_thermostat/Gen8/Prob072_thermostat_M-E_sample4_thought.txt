A novel approach to implementing the thermostat controller could involve using a finite state machine (FSM) to manage the different operating modes and conditions of the system. This approach could provide a more structured and scalable way to handle complex control logic, especially if the requirements were to expand in the future.

The FSM could have states corresponding to the primary operating modes: Heating, Cooling, and Off. Transitions between these states could be triggered by changes in the `mode` input, `too_cold`, `too_hot`, and `fan_on` signals. Within each state, the FSM could assert the appropriate control signals for the heater, air conditioner, and fan based on the current state and input conditions.

This FSM-based design would allow for easy extension to more complex control scenarios, such as incorporating additional inputs (like a temperature sensor) or adding more states for different operating conditions (e.g., a "maintenance" mode). The use of an FSM could also make the code more readable and maintainable, as the control logic would be explicitly defined in terms of states and transitions rather than implicit in combinational logic.
