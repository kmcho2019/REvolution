To approach this problem with a novel architectural idea, let's consider implementing a finite state machine (FSM) that encapsulates the control logic for the thermostat. The FSM can have states that correspond to the different operational modes of the system: idle, heating, cooling, and fan only. This approach allows for a clear and structured way to manage the different conditions and transitions between them. 

The FSM can be designed with the following states:
- Idle: The initial state where the heater, air conditioner, and fan are all off.
- Heating: The state where the heater and fan are on, and the air conditioner is off.
- Cooling: The state where the air conditioner and fan are on, and the heater is off.
- Fan Only: The state where only the fan is on.

Transitions between these states can be based on the inputs `mode`, `too_cold`, `too_hot`, and `fan_on`. For example, if `mode` is 1 (heating mode) and `too_cold` is 1, the FSM transitions from Idle to Heating. If `fan_on` is 1, it can transition to Fan Only from any state where the fan is not already on.

This approach provides a clean, modular way to manage the complex logic and can be beneficial for scalability and maintainability if the system's requirements become more complex in the future.
