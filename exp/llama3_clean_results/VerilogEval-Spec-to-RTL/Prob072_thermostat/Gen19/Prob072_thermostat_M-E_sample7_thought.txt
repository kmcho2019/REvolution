The novel architectural idea here is to implement the thermostat controller using a finite state machine (FSM) approach. This involves defining states for the different operating conditions of the thermostat (heating, cooling, idle) and transitions between these states based on the input conditions (mode, too_cold, too_hot, fan_on). The FSM can be designed to minimize the number of states and transitions, thus potentially reducing the area and power consumption. By explicitly managing the states and transitions, the FSM approach can also provide clearer insights into the system's behavior and facilitate easier debugging and modification.

The states can be defined as follows:
- `HEATING`: The system is in heating mode, and the heater is on if it's too cold.
- `COOLING`: The system is in cooling mode, and the air conditioner is on if it's too hot.
- `IDLE`: The system is neither heating nor cooling, but the fan can still be on if requested.

The transitions between states are based on the `mode` signal, and within each state, the actions (heater on/off, aircon on/off, fan on/off) are determined by the current state and the input conditions (too_cold, too_hot, fan_on).

This approach allows for a structured and systematic implementation of the thermostat's logic, potentially leading to a more efficient and scalable design.
