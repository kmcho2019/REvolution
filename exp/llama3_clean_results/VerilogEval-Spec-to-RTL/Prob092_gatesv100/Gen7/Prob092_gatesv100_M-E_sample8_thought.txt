A novel architectural idea for this problem is to utilize a combination of look-up tables (LUTs) and multiplexers to efficiently compute the output vectors. The concept involves creating LUTs for each of the three output vectors, where each LUT corresponds to a specific input bit and its neighbor. The LUTs can be populated with pre-computed values based on the possible combinations of input bits and their neighbors. Then, using multiplexers, the design can select the appropriate output value from the LUTs based on the actual input bits. This approach can lead to significant improvements in area and power consumption, as it minimizes the number of logic gates required and reduces the switching activity in the design.
