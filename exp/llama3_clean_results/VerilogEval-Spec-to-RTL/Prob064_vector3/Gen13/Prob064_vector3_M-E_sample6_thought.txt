The novel architectural idea for this problem involves using a shift register-based approach. Instead of concatenating and splitting the input vectors directly, we can utilize a shift register to store the input vectors sequentially and then shift them out to form the desired output vectors. This approach can potentially reduce the area and power consumption by minimizing the amount of combinational logic required. The shift register can be designed to shift the input vectors in a sequential manner, allowing us to easily form the output vectors w, x, y, and z.

The design will consist of a single module, TopModule, which will contain the shift register and the necessary logic to control the shifting process. The input vectors a, b, c, d, e, and f will be stored in the shift register one by one, and then the register will be shifted to form the output vectors. The two additional '1' bits required at the end of the output will be appended during the shifting process.

This approach not only simplifies the design but also reduces the complexity of the combinational logic, potentially leading to better performance and lower power consumption.
