The novel architectural idea involves creating a single module, TopModule, that directly concatenates the input vectors and appends two '1' bits to form a 32-bit vector. This 32-bit vector is then split into four 8-bit output vectors using direct bit assignment. The design leverages Verilog's bit slicing and concatenation capabilities to achieve a compact and efficient implementation.

This approach eliminates the need for intermediate modules and wires, potentially reducing area consumption and improving performance. By directly assigning bits from the concatenated and appended vector to the output vectors, the design minimizes the use of combinational logic, which could lead to better timing and power consumption.
