A novel architectural approach to model the Lemmings' behavior is to utilize a hierarchical finite state machine (HFSM) structure. This design leverages the inherent modularity of HFSMs to manage complex behaviors and interactions between different states. The top-level state machine will handle the overall Lemming state (walking, falling, digging), while sub-state machines will manage more specific behaviors, such as walking direction and digging status.

This hierarchical design enables better scalability, maintainability, and readability of the code, as each sub-state machine can be developed and tested independently. Additionally, the HFSM structure allows for easier integration of new behaviors or states, should the design requirements evolve.

The top-level state machine will have three main states: `walking`, `falling`, and `digging`. The `walking` state will be further divided into two sub-states: `walking_left` and `walking_right`, managed by a sub-state machine. The `digging` state will also have a sub-state machine to track the digging status.

This hierarchical approach simplifies the design and reduces the complexity of the state transition logic, making it easier to understand, implement, and verify the correctness of the Lemmings' behavior model.
