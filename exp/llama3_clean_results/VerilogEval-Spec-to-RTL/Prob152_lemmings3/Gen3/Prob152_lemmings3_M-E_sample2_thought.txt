The original solution uses a finite state machine (FSM) with four states: WALK_LEFT, WALK_RIGHT, FALLING, and DIGGING. However, this approach has limitations in handling the complex behavior of the Lemming, particularly when it comes to state transitions and signal assignments.

A novel architectural idea is to use a hierarchical state machine (HSM) instead of a flat FSM. The HSM consists of two levels: the outer level handles the high-level states (WALKING, FALLING, and DIGGING), and the inner level handles the walking direction (LEFT and RIGHT). This approach allows for more modular and scalable design, making it easier to manage the complex behavior of the Lemming.

At the outer level, the HSM has three states: WALKING, FALLING, and DIGGING. The WALKING state has two sub-states: LEFT and RIGHT, which represent the walking direction. The FALLING and DIGGING states do not have sub-states.

The HSM transitions between states based on the input signals. For example, when the ground disappears, the HSM transitions from the WALKING state to the FALLING state. When the ground reappears, the HSM transitions from the FALLING state back to the WALKING state, and the walking direction is restored to its previous value.

The inner level handles the walking direction based on the input signals bump_left and bump_right. When the Lemming is bumped, the inner level transitions to the opposite walking direction.

This hierarchical approach allows for more flexibility and scalability in managing the complex behavior of the Lemming, making it easier to add new features or modify existing behavior.
