The novel architectural idea is to design a hierarchical finite state machine (FSM) that separates the control of the Lemming's walking direction and its actions (falling, digging). This approach allows for a more modular and scalable design.

The top-level FSM will manage the overall state of the Lemming, including walking, falling, and digging. It will receive inputs from the environment (bump_left, bump_right, ground, dig) and control the walking direction and actions.

A separate, lower-level FSM will be responsible for managing the walking direction. It will receive inputs from the top-level FSM and the environment (bump_left, bump_right) and control the walk_left and walk_right outputs.

This hierarchical approach enables a more organized and maintainable design, as each FSM has a specific and well-defined responsibility. It also allows for easier extension or modification of the Lemming's behavior, as new states or actions can be added to the top-level or lower-level FSMs without affecting the overall architecture.

The use of a hierarchical FSM also enables the application of optimization techniques, such as state encoding and minimization, to each FSM separately, which can lead to improved performance and area efficiency.
