To create a novel architectural solution for the 16-bit full adder, I propose a design that leverages a tree-like structure for the carry propagation, inspired by the concept of binary trees. This approach aims to reduce the critical path delay associated with ripple-carry adders and improve the overall performance.

The idea is to split the 16-bit operands into four 4-bit segments. Each segment will have its own 4-bit adder, and the carry output from each segment will be connected to a binary tree-like structure. This structure will propagate the carry signals up the tree, allowing the carries to be computed in parallel. The final result will be constructed by combining the outputs of the 4-bit adders and the carry tree.

This design takes advantage of the properties of binary trees to reduce the height of the carry propagation path, thus decreasing the critical path delay. Additionally, by using 4-bit adders as the basic building blocks, the design maintains modularity and scalability.

To further optimize the design, we can consider using a more efficient adder architecture for the 4-bit segments, such as a carry-lookahead adder. We can also explore techniques to reduce power consumption, such as operand isolation or clock gating, and apply area optimization techniques, like resource sharing or time-multiplexing.
