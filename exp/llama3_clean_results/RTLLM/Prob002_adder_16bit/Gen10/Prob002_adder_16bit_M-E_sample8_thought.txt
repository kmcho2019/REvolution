To create a novel architectural solution for the 16-bit full adder, we can explore the concept of a carry-save adder. A carry-save adder is a type of digital adder that reduces the critical path delay by avoiding the ripple-carry chain. Instead, it uses a parallel approach to calculate the sum and carry bits simultaneously. This approach can significantly improve the performance of the adder.

The idea is to divide the 16-bit input operands into four 4-bit segments. Then, we use four 4-bit carry-save adders to calculate the sum and carry bits for each segment in parallel. The outputs of these adders are then combined using a final 4-bit adder to produce the 16-bit result.

This architecture takes advantage of the parallelism in the calculation, reducing the critical path delay and improving the overall performance of the adder. Additionally, by avoiding the ripple-carry chain, we can reduce the power consumption and area requirements of the design.
