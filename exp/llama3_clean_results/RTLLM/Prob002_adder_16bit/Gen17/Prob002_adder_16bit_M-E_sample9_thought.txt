To create a novel solution for the 16-bit full adder, we can consider a design that uses a hybrid approach combining the strengths of both ripple-carry and carry-lookahead adders. The carry outputs from each 4-bit segment will be used to generate the carry inputs for the subsequent segments. This approach aims to balance the trade-off between area, power, and performance by reducing the propagation delay associated with the carry chain while maintaining a relatively simple and modular design.

The use of 4-bit segments allows for a more efficient carry-lookahead mechanism within each segment, reducing the overall number of logic gates required compared to a straightforward 16-bit carry-lookahead adder. Furthermore, dividing the adder into smaller segments can help in reducing the switching activity, as each segment operates somewhat independently, potentially leading to power savings.

The new architecture will involve designing a 4-bit carry-lookahead adder as the building block, which will be instantiated four times to form the 16-bit adder.
