A novel approach to implementing a 32-bit floating-point multiplier for the IEEE-754 standard could involve utilizing a pipelined architecture with dedicated stages for each operation in the multiplication process. This would allow for a more streamlined and efficient execution of the floating-point multiplication, improving overall performance.

The architectural idea involves dividing the multiplication process into several stages, each handling a specific part of the operation:
1. **Input Stage:** Extraction of mantissas, exponents, and sign bits from the input floating-point numbers.
2. **Special Cases Handling Stage:** Identification and handling of special cases like NaN and infinity.
3. **Exponent Adjustment Stage:** Calculation of the new exponent for the result.
4. **Mantissa Multiplication Stage:** Multiplication of the mantissas.
5. **Rounding Stage:** Application of the appropriate rounding mode (e.g., round to nearest even) to the product of the mantissas.
6. **Normalization and Output Stage:** Normalization of the result (if necessary) and formatting it according to the IEEE-754 standard.

Each stage would be designed to operate independently, with the output of one stage feeding into the next. This pipelined approach can significantly improve the throughput of the floating-point multiplier by allowing multiple operations to be processed simultaneously, each at a different stage of the pipeline.

Furthermore, to enhance the rounding logic and handle underflow and overflow conditions more accurately, the rounding stage could be implemented using a combination of combinatorial and sequential logic, ensuring that all rounding modes are correctly supported and that edge cases are properly handled.

In terms of improving Power, Performance, and Area (PPA) metrics, the pipelined architecture allows for:
- **Performance (Timing) Improvement:** By processing multiple operations in parallel across different stages, the overall throughput is increased.
- **Power Reduction:** Although the pipelined architecture might initially seem to increase power consumption due to the additional registers and logic, careful design and optimization can lead to power savings by reducing the need for complex combinatorial logic and minimizing switching activity.
- **Area Efficiency:** While the pipelined approach might increase the area due to the addition of registers and separate logic for each stage, optimizations in the design of each stage and the use of area-efficient architectures for the multiplier and rounding logic can help mitigate this increase.
