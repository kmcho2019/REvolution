To improve the floating-point multiplier, we'll adopt a novel architectural approach that leverages a combination of fixed-point and floating-point operations. This involves breaking down the floating-point multiplication into two main stages: a fixed-point multiplier for the mantissas and an arithmetic logic unit (ALU) for exponent calculation and rounding. This design aims to enhance performance, reduce area, and improve power efficiency.

The architecture includes the following components:

1. **Fixed-Point Multiplier**: A high-performance, pipelined fixed-point multiplier will be used to multiply the mantissas of the two input floating-point numbers. This will produce a 49-bit product, which will then be processed for rounding and normalization.

2. **Exponent Calculation Unit**: A dedicated ALU will handle exponent calculation, including addition, subtraction, and adjustment for bias. This unit will also perform checks for overflow and underflow conditions.

3. **Rounding and Normalization Unit**: A specialized unit will be designed to perform rounding to nearest even (RNE) and handle normalization of the result. This unit will take into account the guard, round, and sticky bits to determine the correct rounding direction.

4. **Control Unit**: A finite state machine (FSM) will serve as the control unit, orchestrating the operations across different components, managing the data flow, and ensuring that the multiplier adheres to the IEEE 754 standard for floating-point operations.

By modularizing the floating-point multiplier into these components, we can optimize each stage independently, leading to improvements in overall performance, power consumption, and area utilization.

Additionally, to further enhance the design, we will incorporate the following features:

- **Pipelining**: Each stage of the multiplier will be pipelined to increase throughput and reduce the critical path delay.
- **Clock Gating**: Clock signals to unused components will be gated to minimize power consumption.
- **Logic Optimization**: Advanced logic optimization techniques will be applied to reduce the number of logic gates and minimize switching activity.

This novel architectural approach is expected to yield a high-performance, low-power, and area-efficient floating-point multiplier that complies with the IEEE 754 standard.
