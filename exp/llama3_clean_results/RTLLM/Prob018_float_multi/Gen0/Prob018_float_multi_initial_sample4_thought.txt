To implement the 32-bit floating-point multiplier for IEEE-754 standard single-precision arithmetic, we first need to understand the format and requirements of the IEEE 754 standard. The standard specifies a 32-bit floating-point number as having 1 bit for the sign, 8 bits for the exponent, and 23 bits for the mantissa.

Our design approach involves the following steps:
- Extract the sign, exponent, and mantissa from the input numbers during the first clock cycle.
- Handle special cases such as NaN (Not a Number) and infinity based on the input values.
- Normalize the mantissas if necessary, ensuring they adhere to the standard.
- Multiply the mantissas, combining their signs and adjusting the exponents accordingly.
- Perform rounding and adjustment to ensure the result is accurately represented according to the IEEE 754 standard.
- Generate the final output in the IEEE 754 format, handling overflow and underflow conditions appropriately.

We will use a finite state machine to control the operation sequence, utilizing a counter to keep track of the current cycle and perform actions based on the cycle number.
