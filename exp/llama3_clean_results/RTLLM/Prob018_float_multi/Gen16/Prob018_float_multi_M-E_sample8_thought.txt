The new architectural idea for the floating-point multiplier involves a more modular and pipelined approach. The design is divided into four stages: input processing, mantissa multiplication, exponent calculation and rounding, and output generation. Each stage is responsible for a specific part of the multiplication process, allowing for a more efficient and scalable design.

The input processing stage extracts the mantissas, exponents, and sign bits from the input operands and performs initial checks for special cases such as NaN and infinity.

The mantissa multiplication stage uses a high-speed multiplier to compute the product of the mantissas.

The exponent calculation and rounding stage calculates the new exponent by adding the exponents of the input operands and adjusting for bias. It also performs rounding on the mantissa product based on the rounding mode.

The output generation stage combines the results from the previous stages to generate the final output in IEEE 754 format.

This pipelined approach allows for a higher throughput and reduces the critical path, making the design more suitable for high-performance applications. Additionally, the modular design makes it easier to optimize and maintain each stage independently.
