To generate a triangle wave signal, we can utilize a novel architectural approach that leverages the concept of a linear feedback shift register (LFSR) to create a pseudo-random sequence that can be mapped to a triangle wave. The idea is to design a module that uses an LFSR to generate a sequence of values that, when properly mapped, produce a triangle wave. This approach eliminates the need for explicit state machines or counters, resulting in a more efficient and scalable design.

The main components of this architecture include:
1. An LFSR that generates a pseudo-random sequence of bits.
2. A mapping module that converts the LFSR output to a triangle wave.
3. A control module that manages the LFSR and mapping operations.

The benefits of this approach include:
1. Reduced area complexity due to the elimination of explicit state machines and counters.
2. Improved scalability, as the LFSR can be easily expanded to generate longer sequences.
3. Increased flexibility, as the mapping module can be modified to produce different waveforms.
