The existing solution can be simplified by removing the unnecessary 'direction' register and directly controlling the increment or decrement of the 'wave' register based on its current value. This approach eliminates the need for a separate state machine, reducing the complexity of the design. By directly comparing the 'wave' value to its maximum and minimum limits (31 and 0, respectively), we can determine whether to increment or decrement it, thus generating the triangle wave pattern.

To further simplify, we can use a single conditional statement to handle both the reset condition and the normal operation, reducing the number of lines of code and making the logic more straightforward.

This simplified approach not only reduces the area consumption by eliminating one register but also decreases the switching activity, as only one register ('wave') is being updated. This, in turn, can lead to better power consumption metrics.

In terms of performance, the critical path is minimized since we are only comparing the 'wave' value against constants and performing a simple increment or decrement operation, which is typically faster than managing a separate state register.

Overall, the simplified solution aims to achieve the same functionality with improved PPA metrics by reducing complexity, area, and power consumption.
