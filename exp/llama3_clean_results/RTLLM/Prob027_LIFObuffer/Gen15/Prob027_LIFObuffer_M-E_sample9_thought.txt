The buffer's operations (push and pop) would then be implemented by adding or removing nodes from the list, respectively. 

This linked list approach offers several advantages, including the ability to easily implement the LIFO behavior without needing a fixed-size array and the potential for more efficient memory usage, especially in scenarios where the buffer is not always fully utilized. 

However, implementing a linked list in hardware requires careful consideration of the node structure, the management of the list (including adding and removing nodes), and ensuring that the design can meet the required performance and power consumption targets.

Given the complexity of managing a dynamic data structure like a linked list in hardware, especially considering issues like node allocation and deallocation, synchronization, and ensuring correct operation under all scenarios, this approach might be more challenging to implement and verify than a traditional array-based buffer.
