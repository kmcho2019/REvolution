To develop a novel solution for the LIFO buffer, I will introduce a modular, hierarchical design approach. The new architecture, termed "Hybrid LIFO," incorporates a combination of shift registers and a small, dual-port RAM to optimize performance, power, and area utilization.

The Hybrid LIFO buffer is divided into two main components: a Shift Register Unit (SRU) and a Dual-Port RAM Unit (DPRU). The SRU is responsible for handling the most recent data entries, utilizing a chain of shift registers to efficiently store and retrieve the latest data. This approach minimizes the need for complex address management and reduces the power consumption associated with accessing and updating the stack pointer.

The DPRU serves as a secondary storage component, utilizing a small, dual-port RAM to store older data entries. This RAM is accessed using a simplified address generation mechanism, which takes advantage of the dual-port capability to read and write data in parallel. By decoupling the data storage from the shift register chain, the DPRU allows for more efficient management of the LIFO buffer's capacity.

To manage data flow between the SRU and DPRU, a control unit is introduced. This unit monitors the occupancy of the SRU and DPRU, directing data flow and triggering data transfers between the two units as needed. The control unit also generates the EMPTY and FULL flags based on the occupancy of the SRU and DPRU.

This modular architecture enables a more efficient use of resources, as each component can be optimized independently for performance, power, and area. The Hybrid LIFO buffer's design allows for scalability and adaptability, making it suitable for a wide range of applications and configurations.
