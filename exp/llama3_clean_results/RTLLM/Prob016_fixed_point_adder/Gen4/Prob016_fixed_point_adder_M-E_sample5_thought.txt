To improve the fixed-point adder module, a novel architectural idea is to implement a hybrid approach that combines the benefits of both serial and parallel arithmetic operations. This hybrid approach, termed "Serial-Parallel Fixed-Point Adder" (SPFPA), leverages the advantages of serial arithmetic for precision and parallel arithmetic for performance.

The SPFPA architecture consists of two primary components:
1. **Serial Arithmetic Unit (SAU):** This unit performs serial arithmetic operations, such as addition and subtraction, on the fractional parts of the input operands. The SAU is optimized for precision and can handle complex arithmetic operations.
2. **Parallel Arithmetic Unit (PAU):** This unit performs parallel arithmetic operations, such as addition and subtraction, on the integer parts of the input operands. The PAU is optimized for performance and can handle large integer values.

The SPFPA architecture also includes a **Control Unit (CU)** that coordinates the operation of the SAU and PAU. The CU determines the sign of the result, manages the precision of the arithmetic operations, and handles overflow conditions.

The SPFPA architecture offers several advantages, including:
* Improved precision: The SAU ensures accurate arithmetic operations on the fractional parts of the input operands.
* High performance: The PAU enables fast arithmetic operations on the integer parts of the input operands.
* Efficient overflow handling: The CU manages overflow conditions and ensures that the result is correctly represented.

Overall, the SPFPA architecture provides a novel and efficient approach to implementing fixed-point arithmetic operations.
