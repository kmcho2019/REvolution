A novel approach to designing the fixed-point adder involves splitting the input operands into their integer and fractional parts and then handling these parts separately. This approach can simplify the addition and subtraction logic, especially when dealing with the sign bits and fractional parts. Here's a high-level overview of the thought process:

1. **Split Input Operands**: Divide each input operand (`a` and `b`) into its integer and fractional parts. This can be done based on the parameter `Q`, which specifies the number of fractional bits.

2. **Sign Bit Handling**: Extract the sign bits of both `a` and `b`. If the signs are the same, perform an addition. If the signs are different, perform a subtraction.

3. **Integer Part Addition/Subtraction**: Handle the integer parts of `a` and `b` separately based on the operation determined by the sign bits.

4. **Fractional Part Addition/Subtraction**: Perform the addition or subtraction of the fractional parts. This step requires careful handling to ensure that the result's fractional part is correctly calculated and that any carry from the fractional part addition is properly propagated to the integer part.

5. **Combine Results**: Combine the results of the integer and fractional part operations, ensuring that the final result is a fixed-point number with the correct sign and fractional part precision.

6. **Overflow Handling**: Implement overflow detection and handling. If the result exceeds the maximum value that can be represented by `N` bits, handle the overflow according to the desired strategy (e.g., saturation, wrapping).

7. **Parameterization**: Ensure that the design remains parameterized to allow for flexible specification of `N` and `Q`.

This approach can potentially simplify the logic and improve the readability of the code, making it easier to understand and verify the operation of the fixed-point adder.
