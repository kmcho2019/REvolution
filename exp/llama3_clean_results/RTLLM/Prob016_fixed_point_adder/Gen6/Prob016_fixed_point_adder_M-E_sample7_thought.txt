The novel architectural idea for the fixed-point adder involves a two-stage approach. The first stage handles the sign and magnitude of the operands separately, using a sign bit detector to determine whether to perform addition or subtraction. The second stage uses a combination of arithmetic and logic operations to compute the result while managing overflow and underflow conditions. This approach simplifies the logic, reduces the complexity of conditional statements, and enhances synthesizability by breaking down the operation into more straightforward steps.

The new idea includes:
1. Separate sign detection: Use a dedicated logic block to detect the signs of the input operands and determine whether to add or subtract their magnitudes.
2. Magnitude computation: Compute the absolute values of the operands and perform the required arithmetic operation based on the sign detection.
3. Overflow and underflow management: Implement a dedicated overflow and underflow detection mechanism to ensure the result fits within the N-bit range.
4. Result construction: Construct the final result by combining the sign and magnitude information, ensuring that the result's precision is consistent with the input operands.

This two-stage architecture allows for a more modular design, where each stage can be optimized independently, potentially leading to improved performance and area efficiency.
