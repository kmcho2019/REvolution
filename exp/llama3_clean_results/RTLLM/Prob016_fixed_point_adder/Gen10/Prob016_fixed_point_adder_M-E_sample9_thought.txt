To address the requirements of a parameterized fixed-point adder, we introduce a novel architectural idea called "Flexible and Modular Fixed-Point Arithmetic (FMFPA)". This approach involves the following key components:

1. **Fractional Part Alignment Unit (FPAU)**: A dedicated unit responsible for aligning the fractional parts of the input operands based on their exponents (Q). This unit ensures accurate alignment and simplifies subsequent arithmetic operations.

2. **Modular Arithmetic Unit (MAU)**: A modular, pipelined arithmetic unit that handles the integer and fractional parts of the input operands separately. This unit is designed to minimize latency and maximize throughput.

3. **Sign-Bit Management Unit (SBMU)**: A unit dedicated to managing the sign bits of the input operands and the result. This unit ensures correct sign propagation and handling of overflow conditions.

4. **Overflow Detection and Handling Unit (ODHU)**: A unit responsible for detecting overflows and handling saturation and wrap-around scenarios. This unit works in conjunction with the SBMU to ensure accurate results.

The FMFPA architecture involves the following steps:

- The input operands `a` and `b` are first processed by the FPAU to align their fractional parts.
- The aligned operands are then passed to the MAU, which performs the arithmetic operations on the integer and fractional parts separately.
- The results from the MAU are then processed by the SBMU to determine the final sign of the result.
- The SBMU output is then passed to the ODHU, which detects overflows and handles saturation and wrap-around scenarios.
- The final result is then generated based on the output from the ODHU.

This novel architectural idea offers improved flexibility, modularity, and performance, making it suitable for a wide range of applications.
