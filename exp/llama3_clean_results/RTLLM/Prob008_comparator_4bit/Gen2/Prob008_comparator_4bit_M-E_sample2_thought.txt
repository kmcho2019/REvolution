A novel architectural idea for the 4-bit comparator involves utilizing a tree-like structure of XOR gates to compare the bits of the two operands A and B. This approach leverages the properties of XOR operations to detect mismatches between corresponding bits of A and B.

The idea is to compare the bits from most significant to least significant using XOR gates. If a mismatch is detected (i.e., the XOR result is 1), the comparator then checks the current bit positions of A and B to determine whether A is greater than, equal to, or less than B.

This approach can be implemented using a combination of XOR gates and simple logical operations, potentially leading to a more efficient and scalable comparator design. By avoiding explicit subtraction or direct comparison, this novel architecture explores an alternative path to achieving the desired comparison functionality.

The key insight is to use the XOR operation to identify the first mismatch between A and B, and then use this information to determine the relationship between the two operands. This method may offer advantages in terms of reduced switching activity, simplified logic, and improved PPA metrics, especially for larger operand sizes or more complex comparator designs.
