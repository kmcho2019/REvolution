To improve upon the existing comparator design, a novel approach can be taken by utilizing a hierarchical comparison structure. This involves comparing the most significant bits (MSBs) of the input operands first and then progressively comparing less significant bits only when necessary. The idea is to minimize the number of comparisons required to determine the relationship between the two operands, thus potentially reducing the area and power consumption. By structuring the comparison logic in a tree-like fashion, with each level of the tree handling a specific bit position, we can create a more efficient comparator. This approach takes advantage of the fact that in binary numbers, the most significant bits have the greatest influence on the overall value, allowing for early determination of the comparison outcome in many cases. The outputs (A_greater, A_equal, A_less) can still be generated based on the final comparison result, ensuring mutual exclusivity.
