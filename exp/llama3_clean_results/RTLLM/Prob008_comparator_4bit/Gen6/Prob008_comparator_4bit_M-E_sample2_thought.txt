A novel approach to designing a 4-bit comparator module involves utilizing a combination of bitwise operations and a priority encoder. This method deviates from the traditional direct comparison or subtraction-based methods by leveraging the properties of binary numbers and the efficiency of bitwise operations in digital circuits.

The idea is to perform bitwise comparisons between the corresponding bits of the two input operands, A and B. This comparison can be done using XOR and AND operations to detect mismatches and determine the relationship between A and B. The results of these bitwise operations are then encoded using a priority encoder to generate the final output signals (A_greater, A_equal, A_less).

This approach offers a unique perspective on comparator design, potentially leading to efficient implementations in certain technologies or contexts where bitwise operations are favored over direct comparisons. Moreover, it demonstrates the versatility of digital design principles and the importance of exploring diverse architectural ideas to meet specific design constraints or optimization goals.
