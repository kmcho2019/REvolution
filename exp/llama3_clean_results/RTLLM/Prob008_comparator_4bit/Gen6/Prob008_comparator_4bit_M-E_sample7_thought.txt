To create a novel solution, we will leverage the concept of a cascaded comparator architecture. This approach involves breaking down the comparison into smaller, more manageable parts, allowing for a hierarchical and modular design. Specifically, we will use a combination of 1-bit comparators and logic gates to create a 4-bit comparator.

The idea is to compare the most significant bits (MSBs) of the two input numbers first. If they are equal, we then compare the next most significant bits, and so on. This process continues until we find a pair of bits that are not equal or until we have compared all bits. By using this cascaded approach, we can simplify the comparison logic and potentially reduce the overall area and power consumption of the design.

The comparator will use a combination of AND, OR, and NOT gates to generate the output signals (A_greater, A_equal, A_less) based on the results of the bit-level comparisons. This approach allows for a high degree of flexibility and modularity, making it easier to extend the comparator to larger input sizes if needed.
