A novel approach to designing the 4-bit comparator module involves utilizing a ripple-carry subtractor to perform the comparison between the two input operands A and B. This subtractor can be designed using full subtractors as the basic building blocks. Each full subtractor compares two corresponding bits of A and B, along with a borrow bit from the previous subtraction, to determine the difference and the new borrow. The final borrow and the difference bits can then be used to determine whether A is greater than, equal to, or less than B.

The architectural idea here is to leverage the properties of binary subtraction to simplify the comparison logic. By performing a subtraction operation, we can inherently determine the relationship between A and B based on the result (difference) and the borrow. If the final borrow is 1, it indicates that A is less than B. If the borrow is 0 and the difference is all zeros, A equals B. Otherwise, if the borrow is 0 and the difference is not all zeros, A is greater than B.

This approach not only simplifies the comparison logic but also provides a clear and systematic way to design the comparator module, potentially leading to better area efficiency and reduced power consumption due to the minimized number of logic gates required.
