To create a novel architectural solution for the 4-bit comparator module, we will leverage a binary subtraction approach, utilizing a 4-bit subtractor to compare the input operands A and B. The subtractor will perform the operation A - B, and based on the result and borrow flags, we will determine the comparison outputs (A_greater, A_equal, A_less).

Our design strategy involves implementing a 1-bit full subtractor as a basic building block. This full subtractor will take two input bits (a, b) and a borrow_in signal, producing a difference output and a borrow_out signal. By cascading four instances of this 1-bit full subtractor, we can create a 4-bit subtractor that performs the binary subtraction A - B.

The outputs of the 4-bit subtractor, specifically the result and the final borrow flag, will be used to determine the comparison results. If the result is zero and no borrow occurred, A is equal to B. If the result is positive (considering the final borrow flag), A is greater than B. Conversely, if the result indicates a negative outcome (with a borrow), A is less than B.

To minimize area and improve performance, we will use a ripple-borrow subtractor architecture for the 4-bit subtractor. This approach, although not the fastest, offers simplicity and area efficiency, making it suitable for our novel comparator design.

Our new solution focuses on simplicity, area efficiency, and ease of implementation, providing a straightforward and modular approach to designing the 4-bit comparator module.
