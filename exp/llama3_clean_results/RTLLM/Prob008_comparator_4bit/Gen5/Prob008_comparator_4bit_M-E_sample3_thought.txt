The proposed solution introduces a novel architectural idea that leverages a combination of bitwise XOR and AND operations to determine the relationship between the two 4-bit input operands A and B. This approach avoids the need for explicit subtraction or comparison operations, potentially reducing the number of gates required and improving the overall efficiency of the comparator.

The idea is to use bitwise XOR operations to identify the bits where A and B differ, and then use AND operations to determine the significance of these differences. By analyzing the results of these operations, we can infer whether A is greater than, equal to, or less than B.

The key insight is that if A and B are equal, the XOR of their corresponding bits will be zero. If A and B differ, the XOR will be non-zero, and the AND operation can help determine the direction of the difference. This approach enables a compact and efficient implementation of the comparator, potentially leading to improved Power, Performance, and Area (PPA) metrics.
