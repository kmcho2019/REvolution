To create a novel architectural solution for the 4-bit comparator, we can utilize a tree-like structure with bitwise comparison at each node, culminating in a final decision based on the results of these comparisons. 

The concept is to break down the comparison into smaller, manageable parts, comparing bits from the most significant to the least significant, and using the results to determine the final output. This approach can lead to a more modular and potentially more area-efficient design compared to a straightforward comparison.

Each node in the tree will compare corresponding bits from `A` and `B`, with the results guiding the decision-making process. The output will be determined based on the first bit position where `A` and `B` differ, as this directly indicates their relationship (greater, equal, or less).
