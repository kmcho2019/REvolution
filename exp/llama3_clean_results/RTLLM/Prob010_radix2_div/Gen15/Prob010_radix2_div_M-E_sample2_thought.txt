To design a simplified radix-2 divider, we can adopt a novel architectural approach that leverages a hybrid of sequential and combinational logic. This approach focuses on breaking down the division process into stages that can be efficiently pipelined, allowing for improved performance and reduced complexity. 

The key idea is to implement a radix-2 division algorithm that utilizes a non-restoring division method, which eliminates the need for restoring the partial remainder after each subtraction. This can be achieved by using a combinational logic block to determine the next bit of the quotient based on the current remainder and divisor.

The architecture consists of the following stages:

1.  **Initialization Stage**: This stage captures the input operands (dividend and divisor), detects the sign of the operands, and initializes the necessary registers (remainder and quotient) based on the radix-2 division algorithm's requirements.
2.  **Pipelined Division Stage**: A series of pipelined stages that perform the non-restoring division. Each stage consists of a combinational logic block that calculates the next bit of the quotient and updates the remainder based on the current values.
3.  **Finalization Stage**: After the division process is complete, this stage constructs the final result by combining the remainder and quotient, taking into account the signs of the original operands.

This approach offers improved performance due to pipelining, reduced complexity by simplifying the division logic, and flexibility in handling both signed and unsigned division operations.

To further enhance this design, we can incorporate additional features such as:

*   **Clock Gating**: To reduce power consumption by disabling unused parts of the circuit during operation.
*   **Pipelining Optimization**: By balancing the number of stages and the logic within each stage to minimize latency and maximize throughput.
*   **Error Detection**: Implementing checks for division by zero and overflow conditions to ensure the divider behaves predictably under all scenarios.

By implementing this novel architectural idea, we can create a high-performance, low-power, and area-efficient radix-2 divider that meets the requirements for both signed and unsigned division operations.
