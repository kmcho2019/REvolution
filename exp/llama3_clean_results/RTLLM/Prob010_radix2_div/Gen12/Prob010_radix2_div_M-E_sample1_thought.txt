To create a novel architectural solution for the radix-2 divider, we can employ a hybrid approach that combines the benefits of both restoring and non-restoring division methods. This design, dubbed "Hybrid Radix-2 Divider," utilizes a Look-Up Table (LUT) to pre-compute and store the results of subtraction operations between possible dividend values and the divisor. By leveraging the LUT, the divider can quickly determine the quotient and remainder without the need for iterative subtraction operations.

The key components of this design include:

1. **Pre-computation and LUT Generation**: Before the division process begins, the module generates a LUT that stores the results of subtraction operations between all possible 8-bit dividend values and the divisor.

2. **Dividend Alignment and LUT Indexing**: The dividend is aligned with the divisor, and the corresponding LUT index is determined based on the most significant bits of the dividend and divisor.

3. **Quotient and Remainder Calculation**: The LUT is used to retrieve the pre-computed result of the subtraction operation, and the quotient and remainder are calculated accordingly.

4. **Result Formatting and Output**: The quotient and remainder are formatted into the final 16-bit result, with the remainder in the upper 8 bits and the quotient in the lower 8 bits.

By utilizing the LUT, this hybrid approach can significantly reduce the number of clock cycles required for division, resulting in improved performance and reduced power consumption.
