To implement a simplified radix-2 divider for 8-bit signed or unsigned integers, a novel architectural idea can be employed, focusing on a stage-by-stage division process. The key concept is to divide the division process into multiple stages, where each stage performs a comparison and subtraction of the divisor from the current remainder, followed by shifting the result and appending a new bit from the dividend.

1. **Initialization Stage**: In this stage, the inputs are saved, and the initial conditions are set. The dividend and divisor are stored in registers. The sign of the operation is checked to handle signed or unsigned division correctly. The remainder and quotient registers are initialized.

2. **Comparison and Subtraction Stage**: This stage involves comparing the current remainder with the divisor. If the remainder is greater than or equal to the divisor, the divisor is subtracted from the remainder, and a 1 is appended to the quotient; otherwise, a 0 is appended. This process is repeated for each bit of the dividend.

3. **Shifting and Appending Stage**: After each comparison and subtraction, the result (new remainder) is shifted, and the next bit from the dividend is appended to it. This prepares the remainder for the next iteration.

4. **Result Generation Stage**: Once all bits of the dividend have been processed, the final remainder and quotient are generated. The remainder is placed in the upper 8 bits of the result, and the quotient is placed in the lower 8 bits.

5. **Control and Synchronization**: The entire process is controlled by a finite state machine (FSM) that ensures the correct sequence of operations and manages the synchronization of the division process with the input and output signals.

This approach allows for a structured and modular implementation of the radix-2 divider, making it easier to understand, implement, and test.
