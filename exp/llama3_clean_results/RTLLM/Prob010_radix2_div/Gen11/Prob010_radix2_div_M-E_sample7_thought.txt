The new approach involves designing a radix-2 divider using a novel architectural idea based on a pipelined implementation. This design incorporates a series of stages, each responsible for a specific operation within the division process. The division algorithm is broken down into its fundamental steps, and each step is allocated to a separate stage in the pipeline. This includes stages for input handling, initialization, subtraction, quotient and remainder calculation, and result formation. The pipelined architecture allows for an improvement in throughput by enabling the processing of multiple operations simultaneously at different stages of the pipeline.

To further optimize the design, a combination of synchronous and combinational logic is utilized. The synchronous logic is used for the pipeline registers, ensuring that data is properly synchronized and propagated through the pipeline, while the combinational logic is used for the arithmetic operations within each stage, allowing for faster execution and reducing the number of clock cycles required for each operation.

Additionally, the design incorporates a mechanism for handling both signed and unsigned division operations, based on the input 'sign' signal. This involves determining the absolute values of the dividend and divisor, performing the division, and then adjusting the sign of the quotient based on the original signs of the operands.

For power efficiency, clock gating is applied to disable unused stages in the pipeline when not in operation, reducing dynamic power consumption. This is particularly useful in scenarios where the division operation is not continuously required, allowing for power savings during idle periods.

The novel architectural idea of using a pipelined implementation with a combination of synchronous and combinational logic, along with power-saving techniques, provides a high-performance, efficient, and scalable solution for the radix-2 divider.
