The new architecture, dubbed "staged radix-2 division," consists of the following stages:

1.  **Normalization Stage**: This stage prepares the input operands for division by normalizing the divisor to a value between 1 and 2. This step simplifies the subsequent division process and ensures that the divisor is always in a consistent range.
2.  **Multiplexing Stage**: In this stage, we use a multiplexer to select either the normalized divisor or its complement, depending on the sign of the dividend and divisor. This allows us to handle both signed and unsigned division operations using the same hardware.
3.  **Radix-2 Division Stage**: This stage performs the actual radix-2 division using a standard algorithm. The dividend is repeatedly shifted and subtracted by the normalized divisor, and the remainder is updated accordingly.
4.  **Result Formatting Stage**: The final stage formats the result, which consists of the quotient and remainder. Depending on the operation type (signed or unsigned), the result is either left unchanged or adjusted to match the expected output format.

By breaking down the division process into these distinct stages, we can simplify the design, reduce the number of conditional statements, and improve the overall performance of the radix-2 divider.
