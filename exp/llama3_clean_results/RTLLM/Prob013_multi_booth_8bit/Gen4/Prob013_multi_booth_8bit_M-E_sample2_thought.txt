To implement an 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier, we will utilize a novel architectural approach that involves pipelining the multiplication process. The multiplier will be divided into stages, each handling a specific part of the Booth algorithm. This will allow for a more efficient and scalable design. We will also incorporate a control unit to manage the flow of data through the pipeline and ensure correct operation.

The design will consist of a series of registers to store the multiplicand, the multiplier, and the product. The control unit will generate control signals based on the current state of the multiplier and the Booth encoding of the multiplier bits. The multiplicand will be shifted and added or subtracted from the product based on the Booth encoding.

This pipelined approach will enable the multiplier to process one bit of the multiplier per clock cycle, resulting in a total of 16 clock cycles for an 8-bit multiplier. The ready signal will be set high after the 16th clock cycle, indicating the completion of the multiplication process.
