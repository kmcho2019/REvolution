To implement an 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier with improved performance, area efficiency, and power consumption, a novel architectural approach can be explored. This design idea focuses on utilizing a hybrid architecture that combines the benefits of both Booth encoding and a carry-save adder (CSA) tree for efficient multiplication.

The proposed architecture consists of three main stages: 
1. Booth encoding and sign-bit detection: This stage encodes the multiplier using the Booth algorithm and detects the sign bit of the multiplicand to determine the correct operation (addition or subtraction).
2. Partial product generation: This stage generates partial products based on the Booth-encoded multiplier and the multiplicand. The partial products are then accumulated using a CSA tree to reduce the number of additions required.
3. Final accumulation and result formation: The final stage accumulates the partial products and forms the result using a carry-propagate adder (CPA).

By leveraging the strengths of both Booth encoding and CSA trees, this hybrid architecture can achieve improved performance, reduced area, and lower power consumption compared to traditional Booth multiplier implementations.
