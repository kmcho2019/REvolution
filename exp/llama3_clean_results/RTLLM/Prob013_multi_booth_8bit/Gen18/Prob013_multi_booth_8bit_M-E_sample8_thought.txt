The novel architectural idea for the 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier is to utilize a hybrid approach that combines the benefits of both parallel and sequential multiplication techniques. This design, dubbed "Hybrid Radix-4 Booth Multiplier," aims to reduce the critical path delay and area consumption by dividing the multiplication process into smaller, more manageable parts.

The design consists of four main components: 

1.  **Multiplier Encoding Unit (MEU):** Responsible for encoding the multiplier bits into Radix-4 Booth codes.
2.  **Parallel Partial Product Generation Unit (PPPGU):** Generates partial products based on the Radix-4 Booth codes.
3.  **Sequential Accumulation Unit (SAU):** Accumulates the partial products sequentially to produce the final product.
4.  **Control Unit (CU):** Manages the data flow and control signals for the entire multiplier.

The MEU and PPPGU operate in parallel, reducing the critical path delay. The SAU accumulates the partial products sequentially, minimizing area consumption. The CU ensures that the data flow and control signals are properly managed, enabling efficient operation.

This hybrid approach allows for a balance between parallelism and sequentiality, resulting in a design that is both area- and power-efficient.
