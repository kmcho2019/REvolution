To develop a novel architectural idea for the 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier, we will focus on a hybrid approach that combines the benefits of the Booth algorithm with the efficiency of a pipelined design. The new idea involves dividing the multiplication process into four stages: 
1. Initialization: In this stage, the multiplicand and multiplier are loaded into registers, and the product register is reset.
2. Booth Encoding: In this stage, the multiplier is encoded using the Booth algorithm, which involves determining the sign of the current bit and the next bit to decide whether to add, subtract, or do nothing.
3. Multiplication: In this stage, the encoded multiplier is used to perform the multiplication. The multiplicand is shifted left or right based on the Booth encoding, and the product is accumulated.
4. Finalization: In this stage, the final product is stored in the output register, and the ready signal is set.

The pipelined design will allow for a significant reduction in the critical path delay, enabling the design to operate at a higher clock frequency. Additionally, the use of the Booth algorithm will reduce the number of multiplication operations required, resulting in improved performance and power efficiency.

The new design will also incorporate a novel clock gating technique to reduce power consumption. The clock signal will be gated for each stage of the pipeline, ensuring that only the necessary components are clocked, reducing switching activity and power consumption.
