The novel architectural idea for the sequence detector is based on a shift register approach. The main points of this design include:

1. **Shift Register Implementation**: Use a 4-bit shift register to store the last four bits of the input data stream. On each clock cycle, the shift register shifts the bits to the right and loads the new input bit into the leftmost position.
2. **Sequence Comparison**: Compare the contents of the shift register with the target sequence (1001) and generate the `sequence_detected` signal if they match.
3. **Reset Mechanism**: Include a reset mechanism to clear the shift register and reset the `sequence_detected` signal when the `reset_n` signal is low.

The shift register approach simplifies the state machine logic and reduces the number of states required to detect the sequence. The output is generated by comparing the contents of the shift register with the target sequence, making the design more intuitive and easier to understand.

The FSM will be updated on each clock cycle, and the output will be generated based on the comparison of the shift register contents with the target sequence.
