To achieve accurate 3.5x frequency division using the double-edge clocking technique, I propose a novel architecture that utilizes a Time-Domain Multiplexing (TDM) approach. This approach involves dividing the clock cycle into smaller time slots and assigning different phase-shifted clocks to each time slot. The TDM approach can be implemented using a modular design consisting of three main components:

1.  **Clock Phase Generator**: This module generates multiple phase-shifted clocks with a 90-degree phase difference between each clock. These clocks will be used to divide the clock cycle into smaller time slots.
2.  **Time-Domain Multiplexer**: This module assigns the phase-shifted clocks to different time slots based on a predefined sequence. The sequence is designed such that the average frequency of the output clock is 3.5 times the input clock frequency.
3.  **Output Clock Generator**: This module generates the final output clock by logically OR-ing the phase-shifted clocks assigned to each time slot.

The TDM approach offers several advantages, including flexibility, simplicity, and low power consumption. By dividing the clock cycle into smaller time slots and assigning phase-shifted clocks to each time slot, the TDM approach can achieve accurate fractional frequency division without the need for complex PLLs or digital frequency dividers.
