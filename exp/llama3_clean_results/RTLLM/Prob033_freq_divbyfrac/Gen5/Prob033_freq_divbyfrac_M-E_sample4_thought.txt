To implement a frequency divider that divides the input clock frequency by fractional values (3.5x), we will utilize a novel architectural approach involving a combination of counters, phase-shifting logic, and a look-up table (LUT) to generate the fractional clock output. The design will incorporate a dual-edge clocking technique to achieve half-integer division while adjusting for duty cycle imbalance.

The key components of the new architecture include:

1.  A counter to track the clock cycles and generate the uneven clock periods (4 and 3 clock cycles).
2.  A phase-shifting mechanism using a combination of flip-flops and logical operations to delay and advance the clock signals by half a clock period.
3.  A LUT to store the pre-computed phase-shifted clock values for each state of the counter.
4.  A final logic stage to combine the phase-shifted clock signals and produce the smooth fractional clock output.

By leveraging the LUT to store the pre-computed phase-shifted clock values, we can simplify the phase-shifting logic and reduce the overall complexity of the design. The dual-edge clocking technique will be implemented using a combination of rising and falling edge-triggered flip-flops to achieve the desired half-integer division.
