To achieve accurate 3.5x frequency division using the double-edge clocking technique, I propose a novel architecture that utilizes a hybrid approach combining a phase-locked loop (PLL) and a digital frequency divider. The proposed architecture, named "Hybrid Fractional Clock Generator" (HFCG), consists of two main components:

1.  **Phase-Locked Loop (PLL)**: This module generates a high-frequency clock signal that is phase-locked to the input clock signal. The PLL ensures that the generated clock signal has a stable phase relationship with the input clock, which is crucial for achieving accurate fractional frequency division.
2.  **Digital Frequency Divider**: This unit takes the high-frequency clock signal from the PLL and performs digital frequency division to generate the desired fractional clock output. The digital frequency divider uses a combination of counters and logic gates to achieve the 3.5x division ratio.

The HFCG architecture will utilize a combination of analog and digital components to implement the PLL and digital frequency divider. By employing this hybrid approach, we can achieve accurate and efficient 3.5x frequency division using the double-edge clocking technique.

To implement the HFCG architecture, we will use a Verilog module with the following components:

*   A PLL comprising a voltage-controlled oscillator (VCO), a phase detector, and a loop filter to generate the high-frequency clock signal.
*   A digital frequency divider consisting of counters, logic gates, and a state machine to perform the 3.5x frequency division.
*   A control unit to manage the interaction between the PLL and the digital frequency divider, ensuring proper synchronization and phase alignment.

The Verilog code for the HFCG module will be designed to ensure correct timing and synchronization of the clock signals, as well as accurate implementation of the PLL and digital frequency divider mechanisms.
