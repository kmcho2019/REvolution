To achieve a frequency divider that divides the input clock frequency by fractional values using the double-edge clocking technique, we propose a novel architecture based on a hybrid approach combining digital signal processing with clever utilization of clock cycle counting and phase adjustment. 

Our idea is centered around creating an architecture that accurately generates phase-shifted clocks and then combines them in a way that ensures a smooth, fractionally divided clock output with a uniform period. This is achieved by implementing the following components:

1. **Clock Cycle Counter and Phase Controller**: This module accurately counts the input clock cycles and generates control signals to manage the generation of phase-shifted clocks. It ensures that the fractional division ratio is maintained by appropriately adjusting the phase of the output clock.

2. **Phase-Shifting Clock Generators**: Two clock generators are used to produce phase-shifted versions of the divided clock. One generator produces a clock with a phase delay, and the other produces a clock with a phase advance, relative to a reference divided clock.

3. **Weighted Combiner and Duty Cycle Adjuster**: This unit combines the phase-shifted clocks using weighted sums to produce the final fractionally divided clock output. The weights are adjusted to ensure that the output clock has a uniform period and duty cycle, regardless of the fractional division ratio.

By carefully designing these components and ensuring their seamless interaction, our architecture achieves accurate fractional frequency division while maintaining a uniform clock period, thus addressing the challenges of duty cycle imbalance and phase shift management in clock division.

This approach offers improved flexibility and accuracy over traditional methods by allowing for precise control over the phase and duty cycle of the output clock, making it suitable for a wide range of applications requiring fractional frequency division.
