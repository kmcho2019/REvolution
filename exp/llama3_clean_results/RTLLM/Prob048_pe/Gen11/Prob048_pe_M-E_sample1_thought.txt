To create a novel architectural idea for the Multiplying Accumulator (MAC), let's explore a hybrid approach that leverages both parallel and pipelined processing to optimize performance, power, and area.

The new design, named "MAC_CE" (MAC with Controlled Execution), incorporates a two-stage pipeline: one for multiplication and another for accumulation. This pipelining allows for improved throughput and reduced critical path delay, making the design more suitable for high-frequency operations.

To further enhance efficiency, the design includes a control logic that dynamically decides whether to perform the multiplication based on the input operands. If either operand is zero, the multiplication is skipped, and the accumulator is either reset or remains unchanged, depending on the reset signal. This controlled execution reduces unnecessary computations and their associated power consumption.

Additionally, the MAC_CE design utilizes a multiplier with a Booth encoding scheme, which is known for its efficiency in terms of area and power consumption for certain types of multiplications. The accumulator is implemented as a simple register, with the addition operation being performed using a carry-ripple adder for its simplicity and area efficiency.

This novel approach aims to strike a balance between performance, power, and area, making it suitable for applications where these metrics are crucial.
