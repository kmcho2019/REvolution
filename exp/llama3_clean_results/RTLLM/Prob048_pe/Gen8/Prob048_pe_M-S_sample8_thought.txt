To simplify the Multiplying Accumulator (MAC) design, we can utilize a more straightforward approach that minimizes complexity while maintaining functionality. The primary concept is to employ a single register to store the accumulated result and update it by adding the product of the input operands in each clock cycle. This approach eliminates unnecessary complexity, focusing on the essential components necessary for the MAC operation.

The key components of this simplified design include:

1.  **Accumulation Register:** A 32-bit register, `c`, to store the accumulated result.
2.  **Reset Mechanism:** A reset signal `rst` that, when high, resets the register `c` to zero.
3.  **Multiplication and Accumulation:** An always block that performs the multiplication of the input operands `a` and `b`, and accumulates the result in the register `c`.

By leveraging a simple register and always block, we can significantly reduce the design complexity while maintaining its functionality. The always block will be sensitive to the positive edge of the clock signal `clk` and the reset signal `rst`. If the reset signal is high, the register will be reset to zero. Otherwise, the register will be updated by adding the product of `a` and `b` to its current value.

This design simplification aims to minimize the area and power consumption of the MAC while maintaining its functionality and performance.
