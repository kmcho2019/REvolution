To simplify the Multiplying Accumulator (MAC) design, we can eliminate unnecessary complexity while maintaining the core functionality of multiplying two 32-bit integers and accumulating the result.

The main idea is to directly multiply the two input operands (a and b) and accumulate the result in a register. This approach eliminates the need for segmentation, parallel multipliers, and alignment logic, significantly reducing the overall complexity of the design.

The simplified design includes a register to store the accumulated result and an always block to perform the multiplication and accumulation operation. The always block is triggered by the positive edge of the clock signal (clk) or the reset signal (rst), ensuring that the register is updated correctly based on the current state of the reset signal.

By simplifying the design, we can improve the PPA metrics, including reducing the area consumption, power consumption, and potentially improving the effective clock period.
