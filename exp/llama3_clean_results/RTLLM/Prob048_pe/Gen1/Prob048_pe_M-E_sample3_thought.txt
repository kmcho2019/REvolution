To improve the Multiplying Accumulator (MAC) design, a novel architectural approach can be explored. Instead of using a traditional multiplier, a sequential multiplication strategy can be employed. This strategy breaks down the multiplication operation into smaller, manageable parts, reducing the complexity and potentially the area and power consumption of the design. 
The idea is to use a shift-and-add approach for multiplication, where one of the operands (e.g., 'b') is shifted and added to a running total based on the bits of the other operand ('a'). This sequential process can be pipelined, allowing for a more efficient use of resources and potentially improved timing performance.
By breaking the multiplication into stages and using registers to store intermediate results, the critical path can be shortened, leading to a faster and more efficient MAC operation.
