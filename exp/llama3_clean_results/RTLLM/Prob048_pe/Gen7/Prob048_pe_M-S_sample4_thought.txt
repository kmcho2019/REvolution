To simplify the Multiplying Accumulator (MAC) design, we can eliminate unnecessary complexity while maintaining its functionality. The previous solution involved segmenting the input operands, performing parallel multiplications, and then accumulating the partial products. However, this approach can be simplified by directly multiplying the input operands and accumulating the result.

A straightforward implementation would involve using a single register to store the accumulated result and updating this register on each clock cycle by adding the product of the input operands to its current value. This approach eliminates the need for segmentation, parallel multipliers, and a complex adder tree, thereby reducing the overall complexity of the design.

The key components of this simplified design include:

1.  **Accumulator Register:** A single register to store the accumulated result.
2.  **Multiplier:** A single multiplier to compute the product of the input operands.
3.  **Adder:** A simple adder to accumulate the product into the register.

By using this simplified architecture, we can reduce the complexity of the MAC while maintaining its functionality, potentially leading to improvements in area and power consumption.
