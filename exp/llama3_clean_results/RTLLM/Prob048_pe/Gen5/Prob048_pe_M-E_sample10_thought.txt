The new architectural idea is to implement a Multiplying Accumulator (MAC) using a combination of pipelining and a Booth multiplier. Pipelining will allow for improved performance by breaking down the multiplication and accumulation operations into stages, reducing the critical path delay. The Booth multiplier is a more efficient multiplication algorithm that reduces the number of operations required, leading to a decrease in area and power consumption.

The proposed design will consist of three stages:

1. **Stage 1: Operand Fetch and Booth Encoding** - In this stage, the input operands are fetched and prepared for multiplication. The Booth encoder will encode the multiplier (b) into a form that can be used by the Booth multiplier.

2. **Stage 2: Booth Multiplication** - This stage will perform the multiplication using the Booth algorithm. The partial products will be generated and accumulated in this stage.

3. **Stage 3: Accumulation and Result Generation** - In the final stage, the partial products from the Booth multiplier will be accumulated and added to the current value of the accumulator register (c). The result will then be registered and outputted as the new value of c.

By using pipelining and the Booth multiplier, the design aims to achieve improved performance, reduced area consumption, and lower power consumption compared to the original implementation.
