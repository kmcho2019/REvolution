To develop a novel architectural idea for the Multiplying Accumulator (MAC) module, we will introduce a hierarchical and pipelined approach. The new design, named "pe_pipelined", will segment the 32-bit operands into smaller parts, perform multiplications in parallel, and then accumulate the results through a pipelined adder structure. This method aims to balance performance, power efficiency, and area utilization by exploiting the benefits of pipelining and hierarchical design.

The key components of the "pe_pipelined" module include:

1.  **Hierarchical Segmentation:** Divide the 32-bit operands into four 8-bit segments. This allows for parallel multiplication operations, reducing the critical path and improving performance.
2.  **Parallel Multipliers:** Employ four parallel multipliers, each operating on an 8-bit segment of the operands. This enables concurrent multiplication operations, enhancing throughput.
3.  **Pipelined Adder Structure:** Implement a pipelined adder to accumulate the multiplication results. The adder will be divided into stages, with each stage responsible for adding a pair of partial products. This pipelined approach reduces the combinational logic depth, improving the maximum achievable clock frequency.
4.  **Robust Reset Handling:** Incorporate a robust reset mechanism to ensure the accumulator register is properly reset when the reset signal is high.

By adopting this hierarchical and pipelined architecture, the "pe_pipelined" module aims to achieve improved PPA metrics compared to previous designs.

The benefits of this approach include:

*   **Improved Performance:** Pipelining the adder structure and using parallel multipliers reduce the critical path, enabling higher clock frequencies.
*   **Enhanced Power Efficiency:** The hierarchical segmentation and pipelined adder reduce the switching activity and power consumption.
*   **Optimized Area Utilization:** The use of parallel multipliers and pipelined adder structure can lead to more efficient area utilization, as the combinational logic depth is reduced.
