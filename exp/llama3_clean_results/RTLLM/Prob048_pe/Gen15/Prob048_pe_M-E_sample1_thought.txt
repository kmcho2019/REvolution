To create a novel architectural solution for the Multiplying Accumulator (MAC) module, we will explore a pipelined approach that leverages a combination of shifters, adders, and a single multiplier to reduce area and power consumption while maintaining performance. 

The key idea is to break down the multiplication operation into smaller, more manageable parts, and then pipeline these operations to increase throughput and reduce latency.

Here's a high-level overview of the proposed architecture:

1.  **Input Processing:** The input operands (a and b) are first processed to extract the most significant bits that will contribute to the final result. This is done using a combination of shifters and adders.
2.  **Partial Product Generation:** The processed inputs are then fed into a single multiplier to generate partial products. The multiplier is designed to be area-efficient and optimized for low power consumption.
3.  **Pipelined Accumulation:** The partial products are then accumulated in a pipelined fashion using a series of adders and registers. This allows for high throughput and reduces the critical path delay.
4.  **Final Result:** The final accumulated result is then outputted as the result of the MAC operation.

The benefits of this approach include:

*   Reduced area consumption due to the use of a single multiplier and pipelined accumulation.
*   Improved power efficiency resulting from the reduced switching activity in the combinational logic.
*   High throughput and low latency due to the pipelined architecture.

Overall, this novel architectural approach aims to provide a balanced solution that optimizes Power, Performance, and Area (PPA) metrics for the Multiplying Accumulator module.
