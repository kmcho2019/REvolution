The new architectural idea involves utilizing a hierarchical approach to implement the unsigned 16-bit multiplier. This approach will divide the multiplication operation into smaller sub-operations, leveraging the concept of partial products and their summation to achieve the final product.

The novel idea, termed "Hierarchical Partial Product Summation" (HPPS), involves the following key steps:

1. **Partial Product Generation**: Divide the 16-bit multiplicand (a) into four 4-bit segments (a3, a2, a1, a0) and the 16-bit multiplier (b) into four 4-bit segments (b3, b2, b1, b0). Generate partial products for each combination of segments from 'a' and 'b', totaling 16 partial products.

2. **Segmented Multiplication**: Perform the multiplication of each 4-bit segment from 'a' with each 4-bit segment from 'b' using smaller, more efficient multipliers. This step reduces the complexity of the multiplication operation.

3. **Partial Product Alignment and Summation**: Align the partial products according to their positional value and sum them up. This step involves shifting the partial products appropriately to their correct positions before summation.

4. **Final Product Formation**: The final product (yout) is formed by summing up all the aligned partial products.

This hierarchical approach allows for a more modular and scalable design, potentially improving area efficiency and reducing the complexity of the combinational logic.
