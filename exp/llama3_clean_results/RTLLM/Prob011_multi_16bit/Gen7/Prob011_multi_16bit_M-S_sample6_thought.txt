To simplify the existing solution, we will analyze its logic and identify opportunities to reduce complexity while maintaining functionality. The main goals are to minimize the number of registers, simplify the logic for the shift and accumulate operations, and optimize the control signals.

1. **Register Minimization**: We can directly use the input signals `ain` and `bin` in the multiplication process instead of storing them in separate registers `areg` and `breg`. This simplification can help reduce area usage.

2. **Logic Simplification**: The logic for generating the `done` signal and updating the shift count register `i` can be simplified. The `done` signal can be set to 1 when the shift count `i` reaches 16, and reset to 0 when `i` exceeds 16 or when the `start` signal is inactive.

3. **Control Signal Optimization**: The control signals, such as `clock_enable`, can be eliminated as they do not provide significant benefits in this simplified design.

By applying these simplification strategies, we aim to reduce the complexity of the design while maintaining its functionality.
