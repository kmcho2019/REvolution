A novel approach to designing the multi-bit MUX-based synchronizer is to utilize a hybrid synchronization scheme that combines the benefits of both synchronous and asynchronous design techniques. The key idea is to implement a double-flop synchronizer for the enable signal (data_en) and use a handshaking protocol to ensure data synchronization between the two clock domains.

The proposed design consists of two main components: a data capture module and a synchronization controller. The data capture module is responsible for capturing the input data signal (data_in) and generating a request signal when the enable signal (data_en) is high. The synchronization controller, on the other hand, is responsible for synchronizing the request signal and generating an acknowledge signal to indicate when the data has been successfully synchronized.

To optimize the design for PPA (Power, Performance, and Area) metrics, the proposed solution employs several techniques:

1.  **Clock Gating**: The design uses clock gating to minimize power consumption by disabling the clock signal to unused modules.
2.  **Asynchronous Design**: The handshaking protocol used in the design allows for asynchronous communication between the two clock domains, reducing the need for complex synchronization logic.
3.  **Pipelining**: The design uses pipelining to improve performance by breaking down the synchronization process into multiple stages.

The proposed solution has several advantages over the existing design, including improved performance, reduced power consumption, and increased area efficiency.
