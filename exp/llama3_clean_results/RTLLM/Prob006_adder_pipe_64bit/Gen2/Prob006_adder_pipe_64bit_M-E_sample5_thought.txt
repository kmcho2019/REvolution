To design a novel 64-bit ripple carry adder with pipeline stages, I propose a segmented architecture that divides the 64-bit addition into smaller segments, each handled by a separate adder stage. This approach allows for improved pipeline efficiency and reduced critical path delay.

The new design will consist of 16 segments, each 4 bits wide, allowing for a balance between the number of pipeline stages and the complexity of each stage. Each segment will have its own carry propagation, enabling a ripple carry effect within each segment.

To further optimize the design, I will implement a pre-carry generation circuit that predicts the carry-out of each segment, enabling the next stage to begin its addition earlier. This technique is known as "carry-save" addition.

Additionally, to synchronize the output enable signal (o_en) with the pipeline stages, I will introduce a separate control unit that tracks the progression of the addition operation through the pipeline. This unit will assert o_en only when the result is available at the output of the final pipeline stage.

The proposed architecture will lead to a more efficient and scalable 64-bit ripple carry adder with pipeline stages, suitable for high-performance applications.
