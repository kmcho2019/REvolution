To design a novel 64-bit ripple carry adder with pipeline stages, we can utilize a combination of parallel adders and shift registers to improve the overall performance and reduce power consumption. 

The new idea involves dividing the 64-bit operands into four 16-bit segments, each processed by a separate adder stage. This approach reduces the critical path of the adder and allows for more efficient pipelining. 

The pipeline stages are designed to handle the propagation of carry signals between the adder stages, ensuring that the final result is accurate and available at the output after the required number of clock cycles.

The main components of this design include:
1. Segment adders: Four 16-bit adders that process the corresponding segments of the input operands.
2. Carry propagation units: Logic circuits that handle the propagation of carry signals between the segment adders.
3. Shift registers: Used to pipeline the input operands and intermediate results, ensuring that the data is properly synchronized with the clock signal.
4. Output stage: Combines the results from the segment adders and generates the final 65-bit output, including the carry bit.

This architecture allows for a more efficient use of resources, reduces the critical path, and improves the overall performance of the adder.
