To create a novel architectural solution for the traffic light controller, I propose a finite state machine (FSM) design with a hybrid approach, combining the benefits of both Mealy and Moore machines. This design will ensure that the traffic light sequence is correct, and the pass_request signal is handled efficiently.

The key idea is to implement a two-stage FSM, where the first stage handles the traffic light sequence (red, green, yellow), and the second stage handles the pass_request signal and its effect on the green light duration. This approach will allow for a more modular and scalable design.

The first stage of the FSM will have three states: red, green, and yellow. The transition logic will be based on a counter that decrements every clock cycle. When the counter reaches zero, the FSM transitions to the next state in the sequence.

The second stage of the FSM will handle the pass_request signal. When the pass_request signal is active during the green phase, the FSM will shorten the green light duration to 10 clock cycles. If the pass_request signal is inactive, the green light duration will remain at 60 clock cycles.

To optimize the design for power, performance, and area (PPA), I will use a single always block to handle both the state transition logic and the counter management. This will reduce the number of always blocks and minimize the number of signal assignments.

The output signals (red, yellow, green) will be generated based on the current state of the FSM. The clock output will be generated by assigning the value of the internal counter to the output clock signal.

This novel architectural solution will provide a more efficient, scalable, and modular design for the traffic light controller, while ensuring correct functionality and optimizing PPA metrics.
