The novel architectural idea for the edge detection module involves using a finite state machine (FSM) to track the current state of the input signal 'a' and generate the 'rise' and 'down' signals accordingly. This approach is more modular and scalable, allowing for easy extension to detect edges on multiple signals or adding more complex logic for edge handling. The FSM will have two states: 'low' and 'high', representing the current state of the input signal 'a'. On the rising edge of 'a', the FSM transitions from 'low' to 'high' and sets the 'rise' signal. On the falling edge of 'a', it transitions from 'high' to 'low' and sets the 'down' signal. This design ensures that the 'rise' and 'down' signals are synchronized with the clock and are asserted for one clock cycle after the detection of the corresponding edge.

The FSM will be implemented using a single always block, reducing the complexity and improving the efficiency of the design. The 'prev_a' register will be eliminated, and the current state of 'a' will be directly used in the FSM logic. This simplification reduces the number of registers and combinatorial logic gates, leading to area and power savings.

To further optimize the design, the 'rise' and 'down' signals will be generated using a single conditional statement within the FSM, eliminating the need for separate logic gates. This approach minimizes the number of transitions in the circuit, reducing power consumption.

The design will also incorporate a reset mechanism to initialize the FSM to the 'low' state and clear the 'rise' and 'down' signals on reset. This ensures that the module starts in a known state and can recover from any potential errors.
