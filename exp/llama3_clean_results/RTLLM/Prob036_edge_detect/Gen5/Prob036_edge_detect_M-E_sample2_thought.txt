A novel architectural approach to the edge detection problem could involve using a finite state machine (FSM) to track the state of the input signal 'a' and generate the 'rise' and 'down' signals accordingly. This approach could potentially offer advantages in terms of area and power efficiency, as well as flexibility, by explicitly controlling the states and transitions of the edge detection process.

The basic idea is to design a simple FSM with two states: IDLE and EDGE. The IDLE state corresponds to the condition where no edge has been detected, and the EDGE state is entered when an edge (either rising or falling) is detected. The FSM can then use the current state and the input signal 'a' to determine whether a rising or falling edge has occurred and set the 'rise' and 'down' signals appropriately.

This approach could be particularly useful in situations where the input signal 'a' is subject to noise or glitches, as the FSM could be designed to filter out such irregularities by requiring a certain number of consecutive samples of the input signal to confirm an edge.

Moreover, by explicitly controlling the state transitions, the FSM could be designed to minimize the power consumption by reducing the switching activity of the output signals 'rise' and 'down', and potentially reducing the area by minimizing the number of logic gates required.
