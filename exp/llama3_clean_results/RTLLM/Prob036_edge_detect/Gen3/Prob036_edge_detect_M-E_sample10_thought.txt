A novel approach to edge detection can be achieved by utilizing a finite state machine (FSM) that tracks the current and previous states of the input signal `a`. This FSM can be designed to have two states: `IDLE` and `EDGE_DETECTED`. When the input signal `a` changes from 0 to 1 or from 1 to 0, the FSM transitions to the `EDGE_DETECTED` state and sets the corresponding output signal (`rise` or `down`) to 1. On the next clock cycle, the FSM returns to the `IDLE` state, resetting the output signals to 0. This approach ensures that the output signals are synchronized with the clock and only remain high for one clock cycle after an edge is detected.

The benefits of this approach include a clean and modular design, ease of understanding and maintenance, and the potential for optimization in terms of area and power consumption. By using an FSM, the design becomes more scalable and can be easily extended to detect edges on multiple input signals or to implement more complex edge detection logic.
