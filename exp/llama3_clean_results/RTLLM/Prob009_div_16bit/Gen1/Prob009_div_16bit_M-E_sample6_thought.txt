To implement a 16-bit divider module using a novel architectural idea, we can utilize a shift-and-subtract method in a sequential manner. The idea is to shift the divisor to the left until it is greater than or equal to the current value of the dividend's higher bits. The number of shifts required to achieve this is used to update the quotient. Then, the divisor (shifted to its current position) is subtracted from the dividend, and the result becomes the new dividend. This process is repeated until all bits of the dividend have been processed.

We will use a state machine to control the process, ensuring that each step of the division is executed in sequence. The state machine will manage the shifting of the divisor, the subtraction, and the update of the quotient and remainder.

The use of a sequential approach (as opposed to a purely combinational one) allows for a more efficient implementation in terms of area and power consumption, especially for larger inputs. However, it requires a clock signal to synchronize the operation.

This novel approach simplifies the logic required for the division operation and makes it easier to extend the design to handle larger inputs or more complex division algorithms.
