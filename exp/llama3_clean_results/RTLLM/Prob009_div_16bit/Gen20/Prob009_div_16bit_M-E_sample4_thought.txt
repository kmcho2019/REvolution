To implement a 16-bit divider module with an 8-bit divisor in combinational logic, we can leverage a novel architectural idea based on a shift-and-subtract algorithm. The core concept involves shifting the dividend to the left and comparing it with the divisor. If the shifted dividend is greater than or equal to the divisor, we subtract the divisor from the shifted dividend and set the corresponding quotient bit to 1. This process is repeated for all bits of the dividend. We will implement this logic using two always blocks, one for updating the internal registers with the input values and the other for performing the division operation. The novel aspect of this approach lies in the efficient use of shift operations to align the dividend with the divisor for comparison and subtraction, reducing the complexity of the combinational logic.
