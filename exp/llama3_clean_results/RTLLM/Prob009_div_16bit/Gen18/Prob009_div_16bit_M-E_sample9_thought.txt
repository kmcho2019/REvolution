To improve the performance and reduce the area of the divider module, we can implement a novel architectural idea using a combination of adders, multiplexers, and shift registers. The main concept is to use a shift-and-add approach, where the dividend is shifted and added to a register, and the result is compared with the divisor. If the result is greater than or equal to the divisor, the corresponding bit in the quotient is set, and the remainder is updated. This process is repeated until all bits of the dividend have been processed.

The novelty of this approach lies in the use of a barrel shifter to shift the dividend and a Wallace tree adder to add the shifted dividend to the register. The barrel shifter allows for efficient shifting of the dividend, while the Wallace tree adder reduces the critical path and improves the overall performance.

Additionally, we can use a multiplexer to select between the divisor and the result of the addition, depending on the comparison result. This approach eliminates the need for a subtraction operation, reducing the overall complexity and area of the design.
