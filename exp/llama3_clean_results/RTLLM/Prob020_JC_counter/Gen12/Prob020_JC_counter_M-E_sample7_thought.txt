To create a novel solution, we will introduce a new architectural idea that focuses on modularizing the 64-bit Johnson counter into smaller, manageable blocks, leveraging the principle of divide and conquer. This approach allows for easier verification, testing, and potential optimization of each block independently.

Our new design concept involves breaking down the 64-bit Johnson counter into 8 blocks of 8-bit Johnson counters each. Each 8-bit block operates similarly to the original 64-bit design but with a reduced bit width. The output of each block is then concatenated to form the final 64-bit output.

This modular approach offers several benefits:
1. **Simplified Design and Verification**: Each 8-bit block can be designed, tested, and verified independently, reducing the complexity of the overall system.
2. **Scalability**: The modular design makes it easier to scale up or down depending on the bit width requirements, by simply adding or removing blocks.
3. **Potential for Parallel Processing**: In certain implementations, the 8-bit blocks could potentially be operated in parallel, offering a path to improve performance.

The core idea is to maintain the operational principles of the Johnson counter (i.e., the shifting and bit manipulation based on the least significant bit) within each smaller block, while the overall architecture is modularized for better manageability and scalability.
