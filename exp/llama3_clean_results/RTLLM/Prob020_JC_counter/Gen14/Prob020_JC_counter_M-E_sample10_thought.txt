To implement a novel 64-bit Johnson counter architecture, we can leverage a circular buffer approach. The idea is to use a 64-bit register as a circular buffer, where each bit position represents a state in the Johnson counter sequence. By maintaining a pointer to the current state and updating it based on the least significant bit, we can efficiently generate the sequence.

The key insight is to recognize that the Johnson counter sequence can be viewed as a circular rotation of bits, where the least significant bit determines the direction of rotation. By using a circular buffer and updating the pointer accordingly, we can avoid the need for explicit bit-shifting and concatenation operations, potentially reducing the area and power consumption of the design.
