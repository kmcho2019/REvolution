A novel architectural idea for implementing a 64-bit Johnson counter involves utilizing a combination of barrel shifters and multiplexers to minimize the area and improve performance. The basic concept revolves around the fact that the Johnson counter's operation can be represented as a circular shift of its current state, with the direction of the shift determined by the least significant bit.

The new idea involves dividing the 64-bit counter into smaller segments, each with its own barrel shifter and multiplexer. This approach allows for a more efficient implementation by reducing the number of bits that need to be shifted in each operation. Additionally, the use of multiplexers enables the selection of the correct shift direction based on the least significant bit, eliminating the need for complex conditional logic.

To further optimize the design, the segments can be structured in a hierarchical manner, with each segment's output being used as the input for the next segment. This hierarchical approach enables a more efficient use of resources, as the same barrel shifter and multiplexer can be reused across multiple segments.

By leveraging this novel architecture, the 64-bit Johnson counter can be implemented with improved performance, reduced area, and lower power consumption.
