To create a novel architectural solution for the 64-bit Johnson counter, we can leverage the concept of pipelining to improve performance and reduce power consumption. Our idea involves breaking down the counter's operation into stages, where each stage handles a specific part of the update logic. This approach can lead to a more efficient and scalable design.

The key stages in our pipelined architecture will be:
1. **Reset Detection**: A stage dedicated to detecting the reset condition and initializing the counter when necessary.
2. **Most Significant Bit (MSB) Determination**: A stage that determines the value of the MSB based on the least significant bit (LSB) of the current count.
3. **Shift and Append**: A stage responsible for shifting the current count and appending the determined MSB.

By separating the logic into these stages, we can potentially reduce the combinational logic depth, improve timing, and minimize switching activity. Additionally, this approach allows for easier integration of clock gating techniques to further reduce power consumption.

To implement the pipelined architecture, we will use a combination of always blocks and registers to store the intermediate results between stages. This will enable us to pipeline the counter's operation, improving overall efficiency and performance.
