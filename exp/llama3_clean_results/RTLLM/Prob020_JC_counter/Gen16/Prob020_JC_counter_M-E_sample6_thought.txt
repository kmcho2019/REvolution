To implement a 64-bit Johnson counter with a novel architectural approach, we will utilize a modular and hierarchical design. The idea involves breaking down the 64-bit counter into smaller, manageable modules, each responsible for a portion of the counter's functionality. This modular approach can simplify the design, improve readability, and potentially offer advantages in terms of area and power efficiency.

The key novelty in this design is the use of a tree-like structure, where the counter is divided into smaller segments, and each segment is responsible for updating a part of the counter value. The update logic for each segment is simplified, as it only needs to consider its local state and the state of its immediate neighbor, rather than the entire counter value.

This hierarchical design can also facilitate the implementation of more complex counters or the integration of additional features, such as multiple reset levels or advanced clock management, in a more structured and maintainable way.

Furthermore, to optimize for PPA metrics, we will focus on minimizing the number of flip-flops required, simplifying the combinational logic, and ensuring that the design can operate efficiently at the desired clock frequency. By adopting a modular and hierarchical architecture, we can potentially reduce the area and power consumption of the counter while maintaining its performance.
