To create a novel solution for the 64-bit Johnson counter, we can leverage a different architectural approach that focuses on utilizing a combination of shift registers and a control logic to manage the counter's state. This approach involves dividing the 64-bit counter into smaller segments, each managed by a separate shift register. The control logic will then be responsible for orchestrating the shifting and updating of these segments based on the current state of the counter.

The key idea here is to use a hierarchical structure, where each segment of the counter is updated based on the state of its least significant bit and the most significant bit of the previous segment. This allows for a more distributed and modular design, potentially leading to improvements in area efficiency and reducing the complexity of the update logic.

Furthermore, to optimize the design for power and performance, we can incorporate techniques such as clock gating for the shift registers and using a pipelined approach for updating the counter segments. This can help in reducing the switching activity and the critical path delay, respectively.

By adopting this novel architectural approach, we aim to create a 64-bit Johnson counter that not only meets the functional requirements but also offers improvements in terms of power consumption, performance, and area utilization.
