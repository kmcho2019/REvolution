This can be achieved by dividing the 64-bit counter into multiple 8-bit or 16-bit sub-counters, each operating independently but synchronously to form the overall 64-bit Johnson counter.

The key insight here is to recognize that the Johnson counter's behavior, which shifts and appends bits based on the least significant bit, can be parallelized and modularized. By using multiple smaller counters, we can potentially simplify the logic, reduce the propagation delay, and improve the overall performance and area efficiency of the design.

This modular approach allows for a more distributed and parallelized implementation, potentially leading to improvements in timing and area utilization.
