To develop a novel architectural idea for the ROM module, we'll introduce a hybrid approach that combines the efficiency of a lookup table with the flexibility of a programmable memory. This new architecture, dubbed "Hierarchical ROM," organizes the memory into a hierarchical structure, where the upper levels of the hierarchy contain pointers to the locations of frequently accessed data, and the lower levels store the actual data. This approach aims to reduce the number of memory accesses, thus improving performance and potentially reducing power consumption. Additionally, we'll incorporate a simple caching mechanism to further enhance performance by storing recently accessed data in a small, fast-access cache.

The key components of this new architecture include:
1. A cache layer with a small number of entries (e.g., 8-16 entries) to store recently accessed data. This cache will be implemented as a small, fully associative cache for simplicity.
2. A hierarchical memory structure, where the top level contains pointers to the locations of frequently accessed data, and subsequent levels store the actual data.
3. A control unit that manages cache hits and misses, updates the cache, and navigates the hierarchical memory structure.

By leveraging the principles of locality and frequency of access, this hierarchical ROM aims to provide improved performance, reduced power consumption, and a more efficient use of area compared to traditional ROM architectures.
