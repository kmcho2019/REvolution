A novel architectural idea for a 4-bit unsigned pipeline multiplier is to utilize a linear array of adders to perform the multiplication in a pipelined manner. This design takes advantage of the fact that the multiplication of two numbers can be represented as a series of additions.

The idea involves the following steps:

1.  **Generate Partial Products**: Calculate the partial products for each bit of the multiplier.
2.  **Pipelined Addition**: Use a linear array of adders to add the partial products in a pipelined manner. Each adder in the array adds two partial products, and the result is passed to the next adder in the pipeline.
3.  **Final Product**: The final product is obtained at the end of the pipeline, which represents the last stage of addition.

By using a linear array of adders, the design can efficiently handle the addition of multiple partial products, reducing the critical path delay and improving the overall performance. Additionally, this approach allows for easy scalability to larger multiplier sizes by simply adding more adders to the pipeline.

To further improve the design, we can incorporate registers at each stage of the pipeline to store the intermediate results. This enables the multiplier to operate at a higher clock frequency, increasing the overall throughput.
