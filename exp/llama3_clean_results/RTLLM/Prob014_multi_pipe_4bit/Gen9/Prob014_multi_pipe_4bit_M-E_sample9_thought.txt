To implement the design of a 4-bit unsigned number pipeline multiplier, I propose a novel architectural idea that utilizes a combination of shift registers and adders to perform the multiplication operation. The design consists of two levels of registers to store intermediate values and control the multiplication process.

The first level of registers, `reg1`, will store the extended input signal `mul_a` with "size" number of zero bits at the most significant bit positions. The second level of registers, `reg2`, will store the sum of the partial products.

The multiplication operation will be performed using a shift register to left-shift the multiplicand `mul_a` by the current bit position `i` of the multiplier `mul_b`. If the current bit of `mul_b` is 1, the shifted `mul_a` will be added to `reg2`. If the current bit of `mul_b` is 0, the shifted `mul_a` will not be added to `reg2`.

The final product will be calculated by accumulating the sum of the partial products in `reg2` and assigning it to the output register `mul_out`.

This design will handle the reset condition by setting the registers to zero when the reset signal `rst_n` is low. When `rst_n` is high, the design will perform the multiplication operation and update the output register with the final product.

The use of shift registers and adders will simplify the multiplication logic and reduce the complexity of the design.
