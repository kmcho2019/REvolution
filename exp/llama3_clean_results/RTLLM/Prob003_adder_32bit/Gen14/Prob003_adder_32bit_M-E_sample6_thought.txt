To create a novel architectural solution for the 32-bit carry-lookahead adder, we will explore the concept of a hybrid adder that leverages both the ripple-carry adder (RCA) and carry-lookahead adder (CLA) architectures. The main idea is to divide the 32-bit input into four segments of 8 bits each and use a combination of RCA and CLA blocks to optimize the performance and area utilization.

Our approach involves the following steps:

1.  Divide the 32-bit input into four 8-bit segments.
2.  Use a CLA block for each 8-bit segment to generate the sum and carry-out signals.
3.  Employ a hierarchical approach by using a higher-level CLA block to combine the carry-out signals from the individual 8-bit CLA blocks, effectively creating a 32-bit CLA adder.
4.  Optimize the design by minimizing the number of wires and intermediate signals, reducing the combinational logic depth and improving the overall performance.

By implementing this hybrid approach, we aim to create a 32-bit carry-lookahead adder design that offers a better balance between area, power, and speed compared to traditional architectures.
