To address the task of designing a frequency divider that divides the input clock frequency by even numbers, I will introduce a novel architectural idea that leverages a combination of modular arithmetic and a phase-locked loop (PLL) principle, but implemented in a simplified manner to suit the requirements of the frequency divider. The core concept involves using a counter to track the input clock cycles and a comparator to determine when to toggle the output clock. The division factor will be based on a parameterizable value, ensuring the design is flexible and reusable. Unlike the original approach, this design will incorporate a more dynamic method for handling the division factor, allowing for easier modification and extension of the division ratio without altering the fundamental architecture of the module.

The key components of this design include:
1. **Parameterizable Division Factor (NUM_DIV)**: This allows the user to specify the desired division factor, which must be an even number.
2. **Clock Cycle Counter**: A counter that increments on each input clock cycle.
3. **Comparator**: A logic block that compares the counter value with the division factor and decides when to toggle the output clock.
4. **Output Clock Generator**: A simple flip-flop or a basic digital logic circuit that generates the divided clock signal based on the comparator's output.

This approach simplifies the logic required for the frequency division and makes it easier to understand and modify the division factor without delving into complex arithmetic or needing extensive knowledge of digital design principles beyond basic counters and comparators.
