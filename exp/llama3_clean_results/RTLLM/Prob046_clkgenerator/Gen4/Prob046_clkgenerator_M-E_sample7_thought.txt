To create a clock generator module that produces a periodic clock signal, I propose an architectural idea that utilizes a phase accumulator to generate the clock. This approach involves incrementing a phase accumulator at every time unit and checking if the accumulator has reached a threshold value corresponding to half of the desired clock period. When the threshold is reached, the clock signal is toggled, ensuring a square wave clock with the specified period.

This approach is novel because it leverages the concept of a phase accumulator, commonly used in digital signal processing and modulation, to generate a clock signal. The phase accumulator acts as a counter that increments at every time unit, and when it reaches the threshold, it resets and toggles the clock signal, thus creating a periodic square wave.

The design includes an `initial` block to set the initial state of the clock and phase accumulator. The phase accumulator is incremented every time unit, and the clock signal is toggled when the accumulator reaches the threshold value, which is calculated as half of the specified period. This approach ensures a 50% duty cycle for the clock signal and avoids the use of conditional delayed assignments that might pose synthesizability issues.

To improve the design further, a reset mechanism can be added to initialize the clock and phase accumulator to a known state at the start of the simulation or in a system reset scenario, ensuring predictability and robustness.

Overall, the proposed architectural idea offers a novel, efficient, and synthesis-friendly approach to generating a periodic clock signal.
