A novel approach to designing a clock generator module involves utilizing a ring oscillator to produce a periodic clock signal. This method exploits the inherent delays within the digital circuit to create a self-sustaining oscillation, thus generating a clock signal.

The ring oscillator consists of an odd number of inverters connected in a loop, where the output of the last inverter is fed back to the input of the first inverter. This configuration creates a feedback loop that produces a continuous oscillation.

To control the frequency of the oscillation, we can introduce a variable delay element within the loop. This delay element can be implemented using a chain of buffers or a programmable delay line. By adjusting the delay, we can tune the frequency of the clock signal to the desired value.

In this implementation, we will use a parameterized ring oscillator with a variable delay to generate the clock signal. The `PERIOD` parameter will determine the target frequency of the clock signal, and the delay element will be adjusted accordingly.

This approach offers several advantages, including low power consumption, high frequency stability, and minimal area usage. Additionally, the ring oscillator can be easily integrated with other digital circuits, making it a versatile and efficient clock generation solution.
