To simplify the clock generator module, we will utilize a single always block that is sensitive to the posedge of a virtual clock (which could be a real clock input in a more complex system) and implement a straightforward counter-based approach to generate the clock signal. The idea is to increment a counter at every clock cycle and toggle the output clock signal when the counter reaches half of the specified period. This approach ensures a clean and efficient design.

Key points of simplification include:

1. **Single always block**: Use one always block to handle both the counter increment and the clock signal toggle, reducing complexity.

2. **Proper clock sensitivity**: Make the always block sensitive to a clock signal (in this case, a virtual one) to ensure proper timing and synthesizability.

3. **Standard counter logic**: Implement a simple counter that increments with each clock cycle and resets when it reaches the desired threshold, ensuring the output clock toggles at the correct frequency.

4. **Efficient data type**: Use an integer for the counter to avoid potential issues with bit width, especially if the period is variable or could exceed the range of smaller data types.

By focusing on these simplifications, the design becomes more straightforward, efficient, and easy to understand and maintain.
