To develop a novel 64-bit subtractor with overflow checking, we can leverage a parallel and speculative approach. The idea is to perform the subtraction operation speculatively, assuming no borrow is needed, and then correct the result based on the actual borrow required. This approach can potentially reduce the critical path and improve performance. 

The new architecture will utilize two main components: a speculative subtractor and a correction unit. The speculative subtractor will perform the subtraction operation without considering the borrow, while the correction unit will determine the actual borrow required and correct the result accordingly. 

Additionally, to improve the overflow detection, we can use a separate overflow detection unit that examines the sign bits of the operands and the result to determine if an overflow has occurred. This unit can operate in parallel with the speculative subtractor and correction unit, allowing for a more efficient and scalable design.

The main challenge in this approach lies in accurately determining the borrow required and correcting the result in a timely manner. This will require careful consideration of the timing and synchronization of the signals between the speculative subtractor, correction unit, and overflow detection unit.
