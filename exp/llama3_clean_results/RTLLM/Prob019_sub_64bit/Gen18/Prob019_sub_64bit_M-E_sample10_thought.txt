The task is to design a 64-bit subtractor with overflow checking. The new approach involves utilizing a combination of bitwise operations and arithmetic logic to achieve high performance, low power consumption, and area efficiency. 

The key idea is to leverage the properties of two's complement representation to simplify the subtraction operation and overflow detection. In two's complement, the most significant bit (MSB) represents the sign of the number. 

For subtraction, we can use the fact that `A - B` is equivalent to `A + (-B)`, where `-B` is the two's complement of `B`. We can calculate `-B` by inverting all bits of `B` and then adding 1. 

Overflow detection can be simplified by examining the carry-out from the most significant bit position after performing the addition. If the carry-out is 1 and the signs of `A` and `-B` are the same, or if the carry-out is 0 and the signs are different, then an overflow has occurred.

This approach allows us to perform the subtraction and overflow detection in a single step, reducing the complexity of the design and improving its efficiency.
