To develop a novel architectural idea for the 64-bit subtractor with overflow checking, we can leverage the concept of signed binary representation and the properties of two's complement arithmetic. Our new idea involves breaking down the subtraction operation into smaller, more manageable parts, and then recombining the results to obtain the final difference and detect potential overflows. This approach can be achieved by dividing the 64-bit input operands into four 16-bit segments, performing the subtraction on each segment, and then propagating the borrow and overflow information across the segments. The key insight here is that by processing the operands in segments, we can simplify the overflow detection logic and reduce the overall complexity of the design.
