To develop a novel architectural idea for a 64-bit subtractor with overflow checking, we can leverage the concept of a carry-save adder (CSA) and modify it to support subtraction. The CSA architecture allows for efficient addition by eliminating the need for carry propagation, which can be a significant bottleneck in large arithmetic operations. By extending this concept to subtraction, we can create a high-performance subtractor that also efficiently detects overflows. The key idea is to represent the subtraction operation as an addition of the two's complement of the subtrahend (B) and then handle the overflow condition based on the carry-out and the most significant bits of the operands and the result.
