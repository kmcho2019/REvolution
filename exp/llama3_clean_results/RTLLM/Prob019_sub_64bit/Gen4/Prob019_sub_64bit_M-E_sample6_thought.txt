To design a novel 64-bit subtractor with overflow checking, we can leverage a hierarchical approach, breaking down the subtraction operation into smaller, more manageable parts. This can be achieved by dividing the 64-bit operands into four 16-bit segments and performing the subtraction on these segments sequentially. 

Our new architectural idea involves:
1. **Segmenting the operands**: Divide each 64-bit operand into four 16-bit segments. This allows us to perform the subtraction operation in a hierarchical manner.
2. **Sequential subtraction**: Perform the subtraction on each segment sequentially, starting from the least significant segment. This approach enables us to handle the borrow from one segment to the next efficiently.
3. **Overflow detection**: Implement overflow detection at each segment level. If the result of a segment's subtraction operation would cause an overflow (i.e., the result is larger than what can be represented by 16 bits), we generate a borrow for the next segment.
4. **Final overflow checking**: After all segments have been processed, perform a final check for overflow by examining the most significant bit of the result and the signs of the original operands.

This approach allows for a more modular and scalable design, potentially offering advantages in terms of area and power efficiency, especially when dealing with large operands.

To further optimize this design, we can explore the use of dedicated arithmetic blocks for the 16-bit subtraction operations and implement clock gating to reduce power consumption when the subtractor is not in use.
