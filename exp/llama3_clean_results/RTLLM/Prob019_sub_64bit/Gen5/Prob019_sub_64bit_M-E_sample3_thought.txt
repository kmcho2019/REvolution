The novel architectural idea for the 64-bit subtractor with overflow checking involves utilizing two's complement representation for signed numbers and exploiting the properties of binary arithmetic to simplify the overflow detection logic. Instead of directly comparing the sign bits of the operands and the result, we can calculate the overflow by checking the carry-out of the most significant bit (MSB) during the subtraction operation. This approach reduces the complexity of the logic and potentially improves the design's performance and area efficiency. We will also consider using a hierarchical design approach, breaking down the 64-bit subtraction into smaller, more manageable parts, such as 32-bit or 16-bit subtractors, and then combining the results. This hierarchical approach can simplify the design, improve readability, and facilitate optimization.
