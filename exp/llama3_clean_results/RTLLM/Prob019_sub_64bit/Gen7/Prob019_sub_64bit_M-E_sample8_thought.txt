Our novel approach involves designing the 64-bit subtractor with overflow checking using a hierarchical architecture. The idea is to break down the 64-bit subtraction into smaller, more manageable parts, and then combine these parts to obtain the final result. We will divide the 64-bit operands into four 16-bit segments and perform subtraction on each segment. This approach allows for a more modular design and can potentially simplify the overflow detection logic.

To detect overflows, we will examine the carry-out from each 16-bit subtraction. If a carry-out is generated when subtracting a segment of B from the corresponding segment of A, and this carry-out propagates to the next segment, it could indicate an overflow. However, the actual overflow condition depends on the signs of the operands and the result. Therefore, we will also examine the sign bits of A, B, and the result to determine if an overflow has occurred.

By using this hierarchical approach, we can potentially simplify the design and reduce the complexity of the overflow detection logic. Additionally, this architecture could provide better optimization opportunities for synthesis tools, as they can optimize each 16-bit subtraction and the overflow detection logic separately.
