The novel architectural idea for the fixed-point subtractor involves utilizing a combination of arithmetic and logical operations to enhance precision and synthesizability. This approach introduces a three-stage pipeline to handle the subtraction process, ensuring accurate handling of sign bits and fractional parts. 

The first stage involves determining the sign of the result based on the signs of the input operands and their relative magnitudes. The second stage performs the actual subtraction or addition based on the signs, utilizing a combination of signed and unsigned arithmetic to maintain precision. The third stage adjusts the result to ensure correct handling of the fixed-point representation, including any necessary shifts or sign bit adjustments.

This pipeline approach not only improves the precision of the fixed-point operations but also enhances the synthesizability of the design by breaking down the complex subtraction operation into simpler, more manageable stages. The use of arithmetic and logical operations in each stage ensures that the design can be efficiently implemented on a wide range of FPGA and ASIC platforms.

The key components of this novel architecture include:

1. **Sign Determination Unit**: This unit determines the sign of the result based on the input operands' signs and magnitudes.
2. **Arithmetic Unit**: This unit performs the subtraction or addition based on the signs and relative magnitudes of the input operands.
3. **Precision Adjustment Unit**: This unit ensures that the result is correctly represented in the fixed-point format, including any necessary shifts or sign bit adjustments.

By leveraging a pipeline architecture and combining arithmetic and logical operations, this novel design achieves high precision and synthesizability while minimizing area and power consumption.
