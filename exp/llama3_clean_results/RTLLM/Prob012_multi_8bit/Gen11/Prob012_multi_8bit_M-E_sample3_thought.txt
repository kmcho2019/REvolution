To design a novel 8-bit multiplier module, we will utilize a hybrid approach combining the benefits of array multipliers and the Booth multiplier algorithm. The Booth multiplier is particularly efficient for two's complement multiplication and can reduce the number of partial products that need to be summed. 

The proposed architecture will implement a modified Booth multiplier algorithm tailored for unsigned 8-bit numbers. The idea involves splitting the 8-bit multiplier into two parts: the most significant 4 bits and the least significant 4 bits. We will then use two smaller Booth multipliers, each handling 4-bit multiplication, and their outputs will be combined to form the final 16-bit product.

This approach aims to reduce the complexity and the number of operations required for the multiplication, potentially leading to improvements in area utilization and power consumption compared to traditional methods. Additionally, by leveraging the inherent parallelism in the Booth algorithm and the divided multiplier approach, we can explore opportunities for pipelining or parallel processing to enhance performance.

The control logic will manage the multiplication process, ensuring that the partial products from each Booth multiplier are correctly aligned and added to produce the final result. This design should offer a balanced trade-off between area, power, and performance, making it suitable for a variety of applications requiring efficient multiplication.
