To create a novel and different solution, we'll focus on designing a modular and scalable ALU architecture. This approach will involve breaking down the ALU into smaller, independent modules, each responsible for a specific set of operations. The main benefits of this approach are:

1.  **Improved Maintainability**: With each module handling a specific set of operations, modifications and updates become more straightforward, reducing the risk of introducing bugs or affecting other parts of the design.
2.  **Enhanced Scalability**: Adding new operations or modifying existing ones becomes simpler, as it only requires updating the relevant module without affecting the rest of the design.
3.  **Better Reusability**: Each module can be reused across different designs, reducing development time and improving overall design efficiency.

The proposed ALU architecture will consist of the following modules:

*   **Arithmetic Module**: Responsible for performing arithmetic operations like addition, subtraction, and their unsigned counterparts.
*   **Logical Module**: Handles logical operations such as AND, OR, XOR, and NOR.
*   **Shift Module**: Performs shift operations, including left and right shifts, both logical and arithmetic.
*   **Comparison Module**: Handles comparison operations like SLT (set less than) and SLTU (set less than unsigned).
*   **Control Module**: Manages the overall operation of the ALU, routing input operands to the appropriate module based on the operation code and controlling the output.

This modular approach allows for a clean, organized, and efficient design, making it easier to implement, test, and maintain the ALU.
