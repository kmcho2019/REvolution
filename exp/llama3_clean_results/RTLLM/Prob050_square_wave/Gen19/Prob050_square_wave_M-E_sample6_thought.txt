To create a novel solution for the square wave generator with variable frequency, let's introduce a new architectural idea that involves a hybrid approach combining a linear feedback shift register (LFSR) with a frequency divider. This design exploits the properties of LFSRs to generate a pseudo-random sequence that can be used to modulate the square wave output, allowing for a more flexible and potentially area-efficient implementation.

The key components of this new architecture include:
1. **Linear Feedback Shift Register (LFSR)**: An LFSR is used to generate a pseudo-random sequence. The LFSR's length and polynomial can be chosen such that the sequence repeats after a specific number of cycles, allowing for control over the frequency of the square wave output.
2. **Frequency Divider**: Instead of directly using the frequency input to control the counter, we will implement a frequency divider that takes the frequency input and generates a control signal that influences the LFSR's output to toggle the square wave at the desired rate.
3. **Toggle Control Logic**: This logic interprets the LFSR's output and the frequency divider's control signal to decide when to toggle the square wave output.

This approach could offer several advantages, including reduced area usage due to the efficient generation of the sequence, and the potential for lower power consumption by minimizing the switching activity in the counter logic.
