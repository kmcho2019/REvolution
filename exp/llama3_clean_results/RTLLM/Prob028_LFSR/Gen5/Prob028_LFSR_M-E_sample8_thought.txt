To create a novel architectural solution for the Linear Feedback Shift Register (LFSR), we can explore the concept of using a Galois LFSR instead of the traditional Fibonacci LFSR. The Galois LFSR configuration allows for the taps (feedback points) to be distributed across the shift register, potentially offering better statistical properties and a longer period for the generated pseudo-random sequence.

The new idea involves implementing a 4-bit Galois LFSR with taps at specific bit positions, utilizing a polynomial that is known to produce a maximal-length sequence. The chosen polynomial will dictate where the taps are placed within the shift register.

The taps for a 4-bit Galois LFSR can be chosen based on a primitive polynomial that generates a sequence with the maximum possible period of 2^4 - 1 = 15. A common choice for such a small LFSR is the polynomial x^4 + x + 1, which suggests taps should be placed at the most significant bit (MSB) and the least significant bit (LSB), but considering the simplicity needed for a 4-bit implementation and to adhere to common practices for educational purposes, we might simplify the approach while still introducing the concept.

In this novel solution, we aim to demonstrate the Galois configuration's potential, acknowledging that for very small LFSRs like this 4-bit example, the period and randomness properties might not be as significant as in larger, more complex implementations. Nonetheless, this approach paves the way for designing LFSRs with specific tap configurations that can be optimized for larger, more complex applications.

For simplicity and to focus on the architectural difference, we will implement a basic Galois LFSR with a straightforward tap configuration that illustrates the principle, while noting that for optimal performance in terms of sequence length and randomness, further optimization of tap positions based on a chosen polynomial would be necessary.
