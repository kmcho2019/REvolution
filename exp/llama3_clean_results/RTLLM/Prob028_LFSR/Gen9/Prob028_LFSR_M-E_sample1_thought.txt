To create a novel architectural idea for the Linear Feedback Shift Register (LFSR), we can leverage a different approach that involves a combination of bit manipulation and modular arithmetic. This approach, known as the "Galois LFSR," utilizes a specific set of taps to generate the feedback, which is then used to shift the bits in the register. 

The key insight in this design is to select taps that maximize the period of the LFSR, ensuring that the generated sequence is as long as possible before it repeats. For a 4-bit LFSR, we can choose taps that correspond to a polynomial with a high degree of primitive-ness, ensuring a maximal period.

In the proposed architecture, we will implement a Galois LFSR with a set of predefined taps. The feedback will be calculated based on the bits at these tap positions, and the result will be used to shift the register. 

This approach not only provides a novel architectural idea but also offers advantages in terms of area efficiency and potentially improved timing characteristics, as the critical path only involves the calculation of the feedback based on the tapped bits.
