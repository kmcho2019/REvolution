The novel approach to designing the Linear Feedback Shift Register (LFSR) involves utilizing a more modular and scalable architecture. This new design incorporates a separate module for the feedback calculation, enhancing the reusability and maintainability of the code. The primary LFSR module will focus on the core shifting operation, while the feedback generation will be handled by a dedicated submodule. This strategy allows for easier modification of the feedback mechanism if needed, without altering the main LFSR logic. Additionally, this design will incorporate a more explicit reset mechanism to ensure the LFSR is properly initialized.

The architectural idea is to create a hierarchical design where the `LFSR` module is the top level, containing an instance of a `feedback_generator` module. The `feedback_generator` module will take the relevant bits from the LFSR's current state and produce the feedback value. This separation of concerns simplifies the design, making it easier to understand, test, and modify.

By structuring the design in this manner, the code becomes more modular, adhering to principles of good digital design such as separation of concerns and reusability. This approach also facilitates the integration of more complex feedback mechanisms if required, by simply modifying or replacing the `feedback_generator` module without affecting the rest of the LFSR logic.
