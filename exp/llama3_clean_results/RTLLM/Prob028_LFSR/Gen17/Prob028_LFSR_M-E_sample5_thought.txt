To create a novel architectural solution for the Linear Feedback Shift Register (LFSR), we will introduce a multi-stage LFSR design. This approach involves dividing the LFSR into multiple smaller stages, each with its own feedback mechanism. Each stage will operate on a subset of the bits of the overall LFSR state, allowing for a more complex and potentially more random sequence generation.

The key components of this new design are:
1. **Stage Division**: Divide the 4-bit LFSR into two stages, each handling 2 bits. This allows for a hierarchical approach to sequence generation.
2. **Inter-Stage Feedback**: Implement a mechanism where the output of one stage can influence the feedback of another stage, creating a more complex sequence.
3. **Intra-Stage Feedback**: Each stage will have its own feedback mechanism, similar to a traditional LFSR, but now applied to a subset of the bits.
4. **Synchronization**: Ensure that both stages operate in sync with the clock signal, maintaining the integrity of the generated sequence.

By introducing these features, the new LFSR design aims to produce a sequence that is more complex and less predictable than a traditional single-stage LFSR, while still maintaining the efficiency and simplicity that LFSRs are known for.

The intra-stage feedback for each 2-bit stage can be calculated by XORing the bits within each stage. The inter-stage feedback mechanism can be designed to combine the outputs of both stages in a way that influences the next state of each stage, potentially through another XOR operation.

This multi-stage approach with inter-stage feedback adds a layer of complexity to the sequence generation, making it more suitable for applications requiring pseudo-random numbers with specific properties.
