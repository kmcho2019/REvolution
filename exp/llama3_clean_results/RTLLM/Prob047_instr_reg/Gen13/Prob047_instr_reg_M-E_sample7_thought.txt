The existing instruction register module can be redesigned with a novel architectural approach that utilizes a single, wider register to store instructions from both sources and incorporates a multiplexing scheme to manage the data flow. This approach can potentially reduce area consumption and simplify the overall design. The new architecture involves a 16-bit register to store the concatenated instructions from both sources, allowing for more efficient use of resources. The `fetch` signal is used to control a multiplexer that selects the appropriate 8-bit segment of the 16-bit register for updating based on the input data. This method eliminates the need for separate registers for each source, reducing the overall area and potentially improving the design's power, performance, and area (PPA) metrics. The outputs `ins`, `ad1`, and `ad2` are derived directly from the stored instruction, ensuring minimal combinational logic and thus optimizing performance and power consumption.
