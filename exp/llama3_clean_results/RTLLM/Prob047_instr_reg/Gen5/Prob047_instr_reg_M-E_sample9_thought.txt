A novel approach to designing the instruction register module involves utilizing a single 16-bit register to store instructions from both sources. This register, named `instruction_buffer`, can be divided into two 8-bit fields, `ins_p1` and `ins_p2`, to store instructions from different sources. The `fetch` signal determines which field of the `instruction_buffer` is updated with the incoming `data`. On the rising edge of the clock, if the reset signal is low, the entire `instruction_buffer` is initialized to zero. The outputs `ins`, `ad1`, and `ad2` are derived from the `ins_p1` and `ins_p2` fields of the `instruction_buffer`. This design reduces the number of registers and simplifies the logic, potentially leading to area and power savings. Additionally, using a single register for both instructions can simplify the reset and clock synchronization logic.
