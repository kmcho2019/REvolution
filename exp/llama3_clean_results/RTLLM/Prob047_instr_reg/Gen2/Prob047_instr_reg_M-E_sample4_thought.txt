To implement the instruction register module, we can utilize a single 16-bit register to store instructions from different sources, rather than using two separate 8-bit registers. This approach can simplify the design and potentially reduce area and power consumption. We'll use the fetch signal to determine which half of the 16-bit register to update on the rising edge of the clock. If the reset signal is low, the entire register will be initialized to zero. The outputs will be derived from the stored instructions by bit-slicing and concatenation. The ins output will be the high 3 bits of the first half of the register, ad1 will be the low 5 bits of the first half of the register, and ad2 will be the second half of the register.
