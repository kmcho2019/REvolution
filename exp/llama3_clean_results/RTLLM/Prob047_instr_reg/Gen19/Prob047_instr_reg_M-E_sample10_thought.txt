The novel approach for the instruction register module involves utilizing a time-division multiplexing (TDM) technique to store and process instructions from different sources. This method enables the efficient use of a single register to store instructions while minimizing the complexity of the control logic. The design incorporates a TDM controller that manages the storage and retrieval of instructions based on the fetch signal. The TDM controller ensures that the instruction register is updated with the correct instruction at the right time, reducing the need for complex combinational logic and minimizing area consumption. 

Additionally, the output logic is designed to directly derive the ins, ad1, and ad2 signals from the instruction register using continuous assignments. This approach simplifies the design, improves readability, and allows for easy modification and optimization of the output logic if needed.

To further optimize the design, clock gating can be applied to reduce power consumption when the module is not in use. The TDM controller can also be designed to minimize switching activity, which can help reduce power consumption. Overall, this novel approach aims to provide a more efficient and scalable solution for the instruction register module.
