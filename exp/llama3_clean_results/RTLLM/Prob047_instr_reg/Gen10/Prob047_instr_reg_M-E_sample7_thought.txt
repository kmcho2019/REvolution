A novel approach to designing the instruction register module involves utilizing a Time-Division Multiplexing (TDM) scheme to store and process instructions from different sources. This approach takes advantage of the fact that the instruction register needs to capture instructions from various sources and separate them into distinct fields for further processing. By using TDM, the design can efficiently utilize a single register to store instructions from different sources, thereby reducing area consumption. The TDM scheme involves dividing the clock cycle into multiple time slots, each assigned to a specific source. The instruction register is updated during its assigned time slot, and the outputs are derived from the stored instruction. This approach also incorporates a reset mechanism to initialize the register when the reset signal is low. By leveraging TDM, the design aims to achieve better PPA metrics while maintaining the required functionality.
