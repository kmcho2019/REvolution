To address the instruction register module design, we introduce a novel architectural approach focusing on modularity, flexibility, and optimization. Our design, named "instr_reg_mod", incorporates two separate 8-bit registers, "ins_p1" and "ins_p2", to store instructions from different sources. This allows for parallel processing and reduces the dependency on a single register.

We introduce a "control_unit" module responsible for managing the flow of instructions between the registers and the output ports. This unit will decode the "fetch" signal and determine which register to update based on its value. For the output logic, we implement a "decoder" module that takes the contents of the registers and generates the "ins", "ad1", and "ad2" outputs.

This modular approach enables easier maintenance, modification, and optimization of the design. Each module can be independently tested, verified, and refined, reducing the complexity of the overall design. Furthermore, this architecture allows for potential scalability, as additional registers or processing units can be integrated as needed.

By separating the control logic from the data storage and output generation, we aim to improve the design's performance, power efficiency, and area utilization. The use of distinct modules for control, data storage, and output decoding facilitates a clearer understanding of the design's functionality and simplifies the optimization process.
