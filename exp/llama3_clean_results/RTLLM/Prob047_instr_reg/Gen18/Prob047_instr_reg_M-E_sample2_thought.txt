To devise a novel architectural solution for the instruction register module, we will introduce a dual-buffer approach with a built-in instruction decoder. This design aims to enhance the module's functionality by incorporating a decoding stage that can interpret the instruction and provide additional information to the outputs. The dual-buffer strategy involves two 8-bit registers: one for storing the instruction and another for temporarily holding the decoded information. This approach allows for a more flexible and scalable design, potentially enabling the handling of more complex instructions in the future.

The instruction decoder will be implemented using combinational logic, focusing on minimizing area usage and reducing power consumption. This will involve using optimized logic structures and potentially leveraging synthesis tools' capabilities to further reduce area and power.

The control logic for managing the buffers and decoder will be designed to optimize the flow of instructions, ensuring that the module can efficiently handle incoming instructions from various sources. This will include a simplified state machine or a set of conditional statements that determine when to capture new instructions, when to decode them, and how to output the relevant information.

Furthermore, to enhance the module's performance and reduce power consumption, we will incorporate clock gating techniques. This involves controlling the clock signal to parts of the circuit that are not currently in use, thereby reducing unnecessary switching activity and power consumption.

By integrating these novel architectural elements, the new instruction register module is expected to offer improved performance, reduced power consumption, and enhanced scalability compared to existing designs.
