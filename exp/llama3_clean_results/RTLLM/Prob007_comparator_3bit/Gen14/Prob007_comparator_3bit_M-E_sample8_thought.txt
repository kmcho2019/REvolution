The key idea is to compare the most significant bits (MSBs) of the input operands A and B first. If the MSBs are different, we can immediately determine the comparison result without needing to examine the less significant bits. If the MSBs are the same, we then compare the next most significant bits, and so on.

This approach can be implemented using a combination of conditional statements and bitwise operations. We will use a case statement to examine the MSBs of A and B and then use if-else statements to compare the remaining bits if necessary.

The hierarchical comparison approach can potentially reduce the switching activity and power consumption of the comparator by minimizing the number of bits that need to be compared.
