A novel approach to designing the 3-bit comparator module is to utilize a case-based comparison strategy. This strategy involves examining the most significant bits (MSBs) of both input operands first and then progressively checking the less significant bits as needed. By structuring the comparison in this hierarchical manner, we can potentially reduce the number of logic gates required, thus optimizing the design for area and power.

The key idea is to start by comparing the MSBs of A and B. If they are not equal, we can immediately determine the relationship between A and B without needing to examine the less significant bits. If the MSBs are equal, we then compare the next most significant bits, and so on. This process continues until we find a pair of bits that are not equal, at which point we can determine the relationship between A and B.

This approach can be efficiently implemented using a combination of logical operations and conditional statements. By carefully crafting the comparison logic, we can minimize the number of gates required, leading to improved area efficiency and potentially reduced power consumption.
