A novel approach to designing a 3-bit comparator module involves utilizing a state machine-like structure, but since the comparator is a combinational circuit, we can implement it using a case statement within a single always block. This method allows for explicit control over the output signals based on the comparison of the input operands A and B.

The architectural idea is to enumerate all possible combinations of A and B and then directly assign the corresponding output values based on these combinations. Given that both A and B are 3-bit numbers, there are 2^3 * 2^3 = 64 possible combinations. However, this approach can be optimized by considering the relative values of A and B rather than their absolute values, thus reducing the complexity of the case statement.

The logic will compare A and B bit by bit from the most significant bit (MSB) to the least significant bit (LSB), and as soon as a difference is found, it determines the relationship between A and B (greater, equal, or less). If all bits are the same, then A and B are equal.

This approach ensures that the comparator logic is straightforward and easy to understand, potentially leading to better synthesis results in terms of area and performance. Moreover, by explicitly handling all possible comparison outcomes, the code maintains readability and simplicity.
