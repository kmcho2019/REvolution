To create a novel architectural solution for the 3-bit comparator, we can utilize a cascaded comparison approach. This method involves comparing the most significant bits (MSBs) of the two input numbers first and then progressively comparing the less significant bits only when the previous bits are equal. This approach can potentially reduce the overall combinational logic required and may offer advantages in terms of power consumption and area, especially for larger comparators.

The key idea is to use a hierarchical comparison structure where each stage compares a bit from each input number. If the bits are equal, the next stage compares the next less significant bits. If the bits are not equal, the comparison result can be determined immediately, and the subsequent stages can be bypassed or their outputs directly determined without needing to evaluate them.

For a 3-bit comparator, this approach might not yield significant benefits over a straightforward implementation due to its simplicity and small size. However, it demonstrates a scalable method that could be beneficial for larger comparators.
