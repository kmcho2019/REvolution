A novel approach to implementing the frequency divider is to utilize a phase-locked loop (PLL) architecture, which can provide a divided clock output by adjusting the phase of the input clock. However, since the PLL requires complex analog components and is generally more suited for high-speed applications, an alternative digital approach is proposed. This approach involves using a single counter that increments on every clock edge, and two flip-flops that are triggered at specific points in the counter's cycle to generate the divided clock output. The key insight here is to use the counter's value to determine when to toggle the flip-flops, ensuring that the divided clock output is correctly synchronized with the input clock and accurately represents the desired frequency division by an odd number.
