The new architectural idea for the frequency divider is to use a single counter and a state machine to generate the divided clock output. The counter will count up to the divisor value (NUM_DIV) on each clock cycle, and the state machine will toggle the divided clock signal when the counter reaches half of NUM_DIV on the rising edge and when it reaches NUM_DIV on the falling edge. This approach simplifies the design and reduces the number of flip-flops required.

The state machine will have two states: IDLE and DIVIDED. In the IDLE state, the counter will count up to half of NUM_DIV, and when it reaches half of NUM_DIV, the state machine will transition to the DIVIDED state and toggle the divided clock signal. In the DIVIDED state, the counter will continue to count up to NUM_DIV, and when it reaches NUM_DIV, the state machine will transition back to the IDLE state and reset the counter.

This approach ensures that the divided clock signal is toggled at the correct frequency and accounts for both rising and falling edges of the clock. The use of a state machine simplifies the design and reduces the number of combinational gates required.
