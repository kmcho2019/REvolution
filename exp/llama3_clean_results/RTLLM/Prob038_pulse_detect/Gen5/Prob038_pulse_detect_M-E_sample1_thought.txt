To design a novel pulse detection module, we can adopt a different architectural approach by utilizing a combination of shift registers and comparators to identify the specific pulse pattern. The idea involves shifting the input data into a register to track the previous two cycles of data. By comparing the shifted data with the expected pulse pattern (0-1-0), we can accurately detect pulses and generate the output signal accordingly. This approach avoids the complexity of finite state machines and instead leverages simple yet efficient digital logic components.

The module, named `pulse_detect`, will utilize a 3-bit shift register to store the current and two previous cycles of the input signal `data_in`. A comparator will be used to check if the shifted data matches the pulse pattern. The output `data_out` will be set to 1 only when a pulse is detected, i.e., when the comparator indicates a match with the pulse pattern, and 0 otherwise.

This design offers advantages in terms of simplicity, ease of implementation, and potentially improved performance and area efficiency compared to traditional FSM-based designs.
