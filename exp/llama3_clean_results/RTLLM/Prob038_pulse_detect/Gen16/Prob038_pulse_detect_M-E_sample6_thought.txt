A novel approach to pulse detection involves using a counter-based mechanism. The idea is to count the number of consecutive cycles that the input signal `data_in` is high after being low, and then reset the counter when `data_in` goes low again. If the counter reaches a specific value (indicating a pulse of the desired length), the output `data_out` is set to 1 for one cycle. This method avoids the complexity of a traditional finite state machine (FSM) and directly addresses the requirement for detecting a specific sequence (0-1-0) by counting the duration of the high state.

To implement this, we can use a single register to keep track of the counter value. The counter is incremented when `data_in` is high and `prev_data_in` (the previous state of `data_in`) is low, indicating the start of a high pulse. The counter is reset when `data_in` is low and `prev_data_in` is high, indicating the end of a high pulse. When the counter value reaches 2 (since we are looking for a sequence of 0-1-0, and we count the high state once), `data_out` is set to 1 for one cycle, indicating the detection of a pulse.

This approach simplifies the logic required for pulse detection and reduces the complexity of the design by avoiding the explicit definition of multiple states and their transitions.
