To implement a pulse detection module, we can use a novel architectural approach based on a Counter-Based Pulse Detector (CBPD). This approach utilizes two counters to track the duration of high and low periods in the input signal. 

The CBPD architecture consists of two counters: a high-duration counter (HDC) and a low-duration counter (LDC). The HDC increments when the input signal is high and resets when the signal goes low. Conversely, the LDC increments when the input signal is low and resets when the signal goes high. 

When the input signal transitions from low to high, the LDC starts counting, and when the signal transitions from high to low, the HDC starts counting. If the HDC counts to a specific threshold (in this case, 1), it indicates the end of a high period, and if the LDC counts to a specific threshold (in this case, 1), it indicates the end of a low period. 

If the input signal goes low after a high period (HDC threshold met) and then goes high after a low period (LDC threshold met), the module outputs a pulse indication. This approach ensures that the module detects pulses with a minimum duration of one clock cycle.

The CBPD architecture provides a simple and efficient way to detect pulses in the input signal and can be easily implemented using digital logic.
