The novel architectural idea for the 8-bit adder involves utilizing a tree-based structure, where each node of the tree represents a full adder. The inputs to the adder are propagated through the tree in a way that minimizes the number of nodes required, thus reducing the area and potentially improving the performance. 

We will design a 2-3 tree adder, where each internal node takes two inputs and produces a sum and a carry, similar to a full adder. The leaves of the tree will be the input bits of the two operands. This design allows for a more efficient use of resources and potentially reduces the propagation delay.

To implement this, we can use a recursive approach, where each internal node is represented by a module that instantiates two child nodes, until we reach the leaf nodes which are the input bits. This recursive structure will allow us to easily expand or modify the design.

The benefits of this approach include reduced area due to the minimized number of nodes, improved performance due to the reduced propagation delay, and increased scalability as the design can be easily modified to accommodate larger operands.
