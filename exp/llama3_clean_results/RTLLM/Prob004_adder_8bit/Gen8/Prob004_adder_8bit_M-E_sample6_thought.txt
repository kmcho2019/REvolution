The proposed architectural idea for the 8-bit adder is based on a hybrid approach that integrates the benefits of carry-lookahead adders (CLA) and ripple-carry adders (RCA) to achieve an optimal balance between area, power, and performance. 
This novel design, named "Split-Level Carry-Propagation Adder" (SLCPA), divides the 8-bit addition into two halves, each processed by a 4-bit CLA. 
The outputs from these CLAs are then combined using a compact, tree-based carry-propagation stage to minimize the critical path delay.
To further reduce power consumption and area, the SLCPA design incorporates clock gating for reducing switching activity and utilizes optimized Verilog constructs to minimize the logic required for the adder operations.
Additionally, the design leverages the inherent parallelism of the two 4-bit CLAs to improve performance by allowing for concurrent processing of the lower and upper 4 bits of the operands.
