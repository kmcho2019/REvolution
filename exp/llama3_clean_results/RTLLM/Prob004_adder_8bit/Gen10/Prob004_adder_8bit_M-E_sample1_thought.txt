To design an 8-bit adder with improved performance, power efficiency, and area utilization, we can adopt a novel architectural approach based on a hierarchical, tree-like structure. This approach involves dividing the 8-bit addition into smaller segments, processing each segment in parallel, and then combining the results. The proposed design utilizes a combination of 2-bit and 4-bit adders to achieve this goal. The 8-bit input operands A and B are divided into four 2-bit segments each. Each pair of corresponding 2-bit segments from A and B is added using a 2-bit adder, producing a 3-bit sum and a carry-out. The carry-outs from the 2-bit adders are then processed in a tree-like structure using 2-bit adders to generate the final carry-out. Meanwhile, the 3-bit sums from the 2-bit adders are combined using 4-bit adders to produce the final 8-bit sum. This hierarchical structure reduces the critical path length and minimizes the number of logic levels, resulting in improved performance and power efficiency.
