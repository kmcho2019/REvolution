To simplify the existing 8-bit adder implementation, we can use a ripple-carry adder structure, which is a straightforward and area-efficient approach. This method involves chaining full adders to compute the sum and carry bits. Each full adder takes two input bits and a carry-in, producing a sum and a carry-out. By connecting these full adders in a chain, we can efficiently compute the sum of two 8-bit numbers.

The key components of this simplified design are:
1. Full adders: These will be used to compute the sum and carry for each bit position.
2. Ripple-carry chain: The carry-out of each full adder will be connected to the carry-in of the next full adder, allowing the carry to propagate through the chain.

This approach reduces the complexity of the design by eliminating the need for carry lookahead logic, which can simplify the overall implementation.
