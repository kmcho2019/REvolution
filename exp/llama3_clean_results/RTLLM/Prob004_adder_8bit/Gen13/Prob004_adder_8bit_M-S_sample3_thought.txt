To simplify the existing 8-bit adder design, we can focus on the essential logic required for the addition operation, eliminating unnecessary complexity. The basic principle of an 8-bit adder is to add two 8-bit numbers (a and b) along with a carry-in (cin) and produce an 8-bit sum and a carry-out (cout). We can break down the operation into simpler full adder components, each responsible for adding two bits and a carry-in, producing a sum bit and a carry-out.

A full adder can be represented by the following logic equations:
- Sum = a ^ b ^ cin
- Cout = (a & b) | (a & cin) | (b & cin)

By cascading these full adders for each bit position from the least significant bit (LSB) to the most significant bit (MSB), we can achieve an 8-bit adder. The carry-out from one full adder becomes the carry-in for the next full adder.

This approach maintains the functionality of the original design but simplifies the implementation by focusing on the core logic required for addition.
