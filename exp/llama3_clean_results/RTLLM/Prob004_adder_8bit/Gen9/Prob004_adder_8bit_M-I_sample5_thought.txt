The improvement strategy involves enhancing the existing 8-bit adder design to optimize its Power, Performance, and Area (PPA) metrics. Given the current design's functional correctness and acceptable PPA metrics, the focus will be on refining the architecture to improve these aspects.

For performance optimization, the use of carry-lookahead adders (CLAs) for the first level 4-bit adders is efficient. However, to further reduce the critical path delay, we can consider implementing a hybrid adder that combines the benefits of CLAs with those of ripple-carry adders (RCAs) or even more advanced architectures like carry-select adders.

To minimize power consumption, techniques such as clock gating can be applied to reduce switching activity. Additionally, power-aware design methodologies can be employed to minimize the capacitance being switched, thus reducing dynamic power consumption.

For area optimization, more area-efficient adder implementations can be explored, such as utilizing shared logic or adopting compact architectures. Logic restructuring to minimize the number of gates and their complexity can also lead to area savings.

Given these strategies, the improved design will aim to strike a balance between performance, power efficiency, and area utilization, making it more suitable for a wide range of applications.
