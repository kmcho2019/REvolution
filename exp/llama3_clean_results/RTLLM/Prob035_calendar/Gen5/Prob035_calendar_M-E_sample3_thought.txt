To create a novel architectural idea for the perpetual calendar module, we can leverage a finite state machine (FSM) approach. The FSM can manage the transitions between different states based on the current values of seconds, minutes, and hours. Each state can represent a specific condition, such as "normal" (where seconds are less than 59), "minute wrap" (where seconds are 59 and minutes are less than 59), or "hour wrap" (where seconds and minutes are 59 and hours are less than 23). By using an FSM, we can decouple the complex conditional logic into separate states, potentially simplifying the design and improving readability. This approach also allows for easier extension or modification of the calendar's functionality by adding or modifying states as needed. Furthermore, an FSM can be optimized for area and power consumption by using techniques such as state encoding and clock gating.
