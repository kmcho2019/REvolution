To implement a perpetual calendar module with improved performance and area efficiency, we can utilize a counter-based approach with a novel architecture. The idea is to use a single counter that increments every clock cycle and then use combinational logic to extract the hours, minutes, and seconds from this counter. This approach eliminates the need for multiple always blocks and sequential logic updates, potentially simplifying the design and improving PPA metrics.

The counter will have a sufficient number of bits to cover the maximum value of seconds in a day (86400 seconds). We can then use bit manipulation and arithmetic operations to derive the hours, minutes, and seconds from this counter. This method reduces the complexity of the design and may lead to better performance and area utilization.

The clock and reset signals will control the counter's operation, allowing for easy synchronization and initialization. By using a single counter and leveraging combinational logic for time extraction, we can potentially reduce power consumption and improve the overall efficiency of the design.
