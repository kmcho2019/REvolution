First, interpret the Karnaugh map with variables x[1]x[2] as row and x[3]x[4] as column. The map is:

       00  01  11  10  (x[3]x[4])
00 |  1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |  (x[1]x[2] = 00)
01 |  0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |  (01)
11 |  1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |  (11)
10 |  1 | 1 | 0 | 1 |  (10)

We can write the minterms where f=1:

- (00,00) => x[1]=0,x[2]=0,x[3]=0,x[4]=0
- (00,10) => x[1]=0,x[2]=0,x[3]=1,x[4]=0
- (11,00) => x[1]=1,x[2]=1,x[3]=0,x[4]=0
- (11,01) => x[1]=1,x[2]=1,x[3]=0,x[4]=1
- (11,11) => x[1]=1,x[2]=1,x[3]=1,x[4]=1
- (10,00) => x[1]=1,x[2]=0,x[3]=0,x[4]=0
- (10,01) => x[1]=1,x[2]=0,x[3]=0,x[4]=1
- (10,10) => x[1]=1,x[2]=0,x[3]=1,x[4]=0

From these, we can find groups to simplify:

Group 1: All with x[3]=0 and x[4]=0 and x[1]=1 (rows 10 and 11, col 00) => x[1] & ~x[3] & ~x[4]

Group 2: All with x[1]=1, x[2]=0 or 1, x[3]=0, x[4]=1 (positions (11,01) and (10,01)) => x[1] & ~x[3] & x[4]

Group 3: (11,11) => x[1] & x[2] & x[3] & x[4]

Group 4: (00,00) and (00,10) => x[1]=0,x[2]=0,x[4]=0, x[3]=0 or 1 => ~x[1] & ~x[2] & ~x[4]

Group 5: (10,10) => x[1] & ~x[2] & x[3] & ~x[4]

But (10,10) is already covered by group 1? No, group 1 is x[1] & ~x[3] & ~x[4], but here x[3]=1, so no.

So group 5 is separate.

Alternatively, we can write the sum of minterms directly or use the groups:

Final expression:

f = (~x[1] & ~x[2] & ~x[4]) // group 4

  | (x[1] & ~x[3] & ~x[4])  // group 1

  | (x[1] & ~x[3] & x[4])   // group 2

  | (x[1] & x[2] & x[3] & x[4]) // group 3

  | (x[1] & ~x[2] & x[3] & ~x[4]) // group 5
