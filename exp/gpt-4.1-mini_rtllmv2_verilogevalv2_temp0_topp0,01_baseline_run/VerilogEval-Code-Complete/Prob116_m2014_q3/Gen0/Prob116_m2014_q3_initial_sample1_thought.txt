Analyze the Karnaugh map to find a minimal sum-of-products expression for f, using the don't-care conditions to simplify the function. Then implement the simplified logic in Verilog.

K-map (x[3]x[4] rows, x[1]x[2] columns):

        00  01  11  10
00 |  d | 0 | d | d |
01 |  0 | d | 1 | 0 |
11 |  1 | 1 | d | d |
10 |  1 | 1 | 0 | d |

Let's assign the cells with coordinates (x[3]x[4], x[1]x[2]):

(00,00) = d
(00,01) = 0
(00,11) = d
(00,10) = d

(01,00) = 0
(01,01) = d
(01,11) = 1
(01,10) = 0

(11,00) = 1
(11,01) = 1
(11,11) = d
(11,10) = d

(10,00) = 1
(10,01) = 1
(10,11) = 0
(10,10) = d

We want to cover all 1's and can use d's as 1 or 0 to simplify.

1's at:
(01,11), (11,00), (11,01), (10,00), (10,01)

Try to find groups:

- Group1: (11,00), (11,01), (10,00), (10,01) = x[3]x[4] = 1x (11 or 10), x[1]x[2] = 00 or 01
  This corresponds to x[3] = 1 and x[2]' (since x[2] = 0 in 00 and 01)
  So group1: x[3] & ~x[2]

- Group2: (01,11) = x[3]x[4] = 01, x[1]x[2] = 11
  Single 1, but d's at (00,11) and (11,11) can be 1 to form a vertical group in column 11:
  (00,11) = d, (01,11) = 1, (11,11) = d, (10,11) = 0
  Can't form a group of 4 because (10,11) = 0
  But can form a group of 2 with (00,11) and (01,11) if (00,11) = 1 (d)
  So group2: x[4]' & x[1] & x[2] (since row 00 = x[3]'x[4]', row 01 = x[3]'x[4])
  Actually, rows 00 and 01 differ in x[4], so group2 is x[3]' & x[1] & x[2]

So final expression:

f = (x[3] & ~x[2]) + (~x[3] & x[1] & x[2])

Implement this in Verilog.
