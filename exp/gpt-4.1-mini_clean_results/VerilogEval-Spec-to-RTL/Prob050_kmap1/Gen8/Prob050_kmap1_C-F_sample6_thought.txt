To fuse the best ideas from both examples, we want a design that is:

- As minimal and efficient as the first example with a direct assign statement for the OR of inputs a, b, and c, giving minimal gate count, area, and delay.
- Also, clearly modular and easy to understand structurally like the second example, but without the overhead of a separate instantiated Or3 module.

Hence, I propose to use a parameterized, reusable 3-input OR module defined as a localparam-like macro or generate-style small function inside the TopModule. However, since Verilog does not support local functions inside modules simply for synthesis, the best middle ground is:

- Define a simple parameterized macro (via `define) or a local function (SystemVerilog) for the OR.
- Since the problem states Verilog, the cleanest is to use a simple continuous assign inside TopModule directly, but clearly commented and documented to highlight the OR function.

This yields minimal gate count and delay (like Example 1), but with the clarity and directness of functional RTL style from Example 2 without adding module hierarchy overhead.

This balances clarity, minimal PPA, and ease of understanding. The final code is a clean single module with an explicit comment linking the assign to the Karnaugh map OR simplification, avoiding unnecessary structural module instantiation or excess complexity.
