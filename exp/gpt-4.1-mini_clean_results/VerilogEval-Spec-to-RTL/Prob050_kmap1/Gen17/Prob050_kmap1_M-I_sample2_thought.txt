The original implementation realizes the function out = ~(~a & ~b & ~c), which is logically equivalent to out = a | b | c. The original design uses three inverters, a 3-input AND, and a final inverter, resulting in higher gate count and power consumption.

To optimize for power, area, and timing, the function can be implemented directly as a simple OR of inputs a, b, and c. This reduces gate count to one 3-input OR gate and eliminates unnecessary inverters, reducing switching activity and delay.

Hence, the improved design instantiates a single 3-input OR gate directly feeding the output, achieving the same logic with minimal PPA impact.
