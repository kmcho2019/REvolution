To implement the logic defined by the Karnaugh map, the output is 1 if any of the inputs a, b, or c is 1, and 0 only when all are zero. Instead of direct OR gates or simple identity comparators, I will use a tree of 2-input XOR and AND gates configured to emulate an OR function in an unconventional way.

Specifically, I will use the Boolean identity:
out = a + b + c
      = ((a XOR b) XOR c) OR (a AND b) OR ((a XOR b) AND c)

To avoid direct OR gates on all inputs, I will break the logic down:

1. Compute xor_ab = a XOR b
2. Compute and_ab = a AND b
3. Compute xor_ab_c = xor_ab XOR c
4. Compute and_xor_c = xor_ab AND c
5. Finally, output = xor_ab_c OR and_ab OR and_xor_c

This approach uses a combination of XOR and AND gates in a tree structure and merges with OR gates, giving a different gate-level structure that implements the OR of all three inputs in a more elaborate manner. This unconventional approach can help explore gate-level synthesis optimization and demonstrates understanding of Boolean algebra beyond direct OR.

- Xor2Gate: 2-input XOR gate
- And2Gate: 2-input AND gate
- Or3Gate: 3-input OR gate
