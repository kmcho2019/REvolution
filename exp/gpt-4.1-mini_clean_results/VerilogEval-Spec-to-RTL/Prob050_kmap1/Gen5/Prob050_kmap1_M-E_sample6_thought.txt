Analyzing the K-map, the output is 0 only when all inputs a, b, and c are zero; otherwise, it is 1. Another way to implement this logic is to use a NAND gate on all inputs and then invert the output, which is equivalent to an OR gate. However, to provide a distinct architectural approach, I will use a sum-of-products form derived from the K-map: the output is high when at least one of b or c is 1, or when a is 1 and b and c are 0. This gives the expression: out = b | c | (a & ~b & ~c). Implementing this expression directly will yield a new structural design different from a simple OR gate.
