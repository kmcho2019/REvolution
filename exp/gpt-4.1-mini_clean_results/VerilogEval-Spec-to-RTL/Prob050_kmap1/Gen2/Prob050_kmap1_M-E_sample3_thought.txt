Instead of directly implementing the minimal logic with an OR gate, I will implement the logic by decoding the zero case explicitly. The output is zero only when a=0, b=0, and c=0; otherwise output is 1.

Thus, I will implement the function as the negation of the AND of the inverted inputs:

out = ~(~a & ~b & ~c)

This effectively means out = a | b | c, but implemented through a small internal gate hierarchy:

1. Invert inputs a, b, c.
2. AND the inverted inputs.
3. Invert the AND output to generate final out.

This architectural approach uses only inverters and an AND gate internally, illustrating the logical derivation with basic gates instead of directly assigning an OR operation. This could be beneficial for a technology where certain gate types are more efficient, or for educational clarity on how the logic comes from the K-map minimal form.
