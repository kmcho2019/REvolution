Instead of instantiating eight separate single-bit D flip-flops, I will implement the eight-bit DFF as a single always block with a vector register, capturing all bits of input `d` simultaneously on the rising edge of `clk`. This approach minimizes module hierarchy and instantiates only one register vector. The output `q` will directly reflect the stored register value.

This method emphasizes simplicity and compactness in coding, clearly showing the register nature of the output without additional module overhead or generate constructs. This differs from the previous solution by using a single multi-bit register instead of multiple single-bit modules. It is more behavioral but still synthesizes efficiently to eight parallel flip-flops in hardware.
