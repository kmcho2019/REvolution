The current design efficiently shares the xor_xy computation between submodules, minimizing redundant XOR gates. However, the two B submodules each compute ~xor_xy separately, causing duplicated inverters and slight power/area inefficiency.

To further optimize power and area, we can compute the inverted xor_xy once in the top module and share it with both B submodules, reducing duplicated inverter gates.

Additionally, the outputs of the B submodules (both equal to ~xor_xy) are connected respectively in OR and AND with outputs of their paired A submodules. Since both B outputs are identical, this sharing reduces inverter usage and gate duplication.

This strategy keeps the modular structure intact (two A and two B submodules), shares common signals for less logic, and reduces switching by minimizing duplicated inverted signals.

No changes in functionality or timing paths occur since this is purely combinational sharing of signals.

Hence, the improvement strategy is:

- Precompute both xor_xy and its inverse (inv_xor_xy) at top level.
- Pass xor_xy to A submodules and inv_xor_xy to B submodules.
- B submodules become simple buffers (passing inv_xor_xy), eliminating the inverter inside B.
- This halves the inverter count for B modules.
- The rest of the logic and hierarchy remain unchanged.

This reduces power and area slightly while preserving design clarity and correctness.
