Instead of sharing intermediate signals like xor_xy and its inversion among all submodules, this new approach encapsulates all logic of A and B modules inside the top-level module as parameterized functional units with inputs x and y only, making each instance self-contained. Each A module computes z = (x ^ y) & x internally, and each B module computes z = ~(x ^ y) internally as well.

The top module instantiates two A modules and two B modules with inputs x and y. Then it connects the output of the first pair (A1, B1) to a 2-input OR gate, the output of the second pair (A2, B2) to a 2-input AND gate, and finally XORs these two results for the output.

This design respects the requirement of two A and two B instances, but does not share intermediate signals outside submodules. The A and B modules are self-contained and compute their function from primary inputs directly.

By doing so, the design is more modular and easier to maintain. It allows each module to be independently changed or replaced without affecting the others or top-level wiring. This may increase the total gate count due to repeated XOR and inversion calculations inside each submodule, but improves clarity and reusability.

The top module uses simple gates (OR, AND, XOR) to combine the submodule outputs as specified.

This new architecture trades off minimal sharing for higher modularity and separation of concerns.
