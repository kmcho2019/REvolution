Instead of instantiating separate A and B modules and then combining their outputs externally, we can design a single composite submodule that directly computes the final top-level output logic internally. This submodule will take inputs x and y and internally implement the two copies of A and B logic, followed by the OR, AND, and XOR operations, producing the final output z for that pair.

Then, the top module instantiates two of these composite submodules with the same inputs. Their outputs are finally combined using a simple gate (e.g., OR) to produce the top module output.

This architecture differs by moving the internal composition of A and B and the intermediate gates into a single submodule called PairLogic, which encapsulates all logic per pair. This reduces the number of module instances at the top level (only two composite pairs), improves encapsulation, and simplifies wiring.

This approach modularizes the repeated logic cleanly, allows easier local optimization within the PairLogic module, and keeps the top-level design clean. Also, by merging the multiple gate operations in the pair into one module, we can reduce interconnect overhead and improve timing due to localized logic.

Finally, the top-level output z is computed by OR-ing the two PairLogic module outputs, completing the function.

Summary of improvements:

- Combine the two PairLogic outputs by an OR gate to produce final z.
- This modularizes the design and reduces redundant external wiring and gate instantiations.
