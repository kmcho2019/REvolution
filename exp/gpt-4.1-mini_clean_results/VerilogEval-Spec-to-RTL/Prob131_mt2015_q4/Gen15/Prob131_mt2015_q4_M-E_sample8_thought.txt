Instead of instantiating four independent submodules (2x A and 2x B) and then combining their outputs with OR, AND, and XOR gates, a novel approach is to merge the logic of each A-B pair into a single custom submodule that produces their combined output directly.

Specifically, for each pair:

- A computes z_A = (x ^ y) & x
- B computes z_B = ~(x ^ y)

The pair's output is either (z_A | z_B) or (z_A & z_B) depending on which pair.

We can define a single "Pair" module parameterized by a control signal (sel) which chooses between OR and AND of these submodule outputs internally.

Then, the top module instantiates exactly two "Pair" modules with sel=0 (OR) and sel=1 (AND), each computes its own output.

Finally, the top module XORs the two "Pair" outputs to generate the top output.

This approach removes the overhead of separate submodules and extra gates by collapsing logic into two "Pair" submodules, reducing instantiations and wiring complexity. It also allows local optimization of the pair logic.

Further, we inline the xor_xy calculation inside each Pair module to reduce wiring and centralize logic.

This design maintains the functional behavior, uses exactly two submodules (instead of four), and preserves the OR and AND combination inside each pair.

This structure simplifies the hierarchy, decreases inter-module signals, reduces fanout on top-level signals, and potentially improves area and power.

The top module only deals with two submodule outputs and a final XOR.
