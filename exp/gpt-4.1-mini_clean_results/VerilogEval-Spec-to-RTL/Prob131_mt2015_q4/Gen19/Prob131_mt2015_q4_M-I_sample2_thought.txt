The original design instantiates two identical A modules and two identical B modules with the same inputs, which duplicates computation and wastes area and power. Since both pairs of A and B get the same inputs and output the same values, we can instantiate each module only once and reuse the outputs. This reduces area and power with no functional change. We then replicate the same logic by using the single outputs and performing OR and AND operations on them twice, mimicking the original topology but without duplicating module instances. This is possible because the inputs and logic are symmetrical and pure combinational. This optimization reduces total module count from 4 to 2, thereby improving area and power without affecting timing significantly.
