To optimize for power and area, the key observation is both AB modules compute the same intermediate signals (x ^ y) independently with identical inputs. Instead of duplicating the XOR and its complement in each AB instance, compute these signals once in the top module and feed them as inputs to simpler A and B modules that only implement their unique output logic. This eliminates redundant computations, reduces toggling, and decreases area by sharing common sub-expressions. The final combinational logic remains identical.
