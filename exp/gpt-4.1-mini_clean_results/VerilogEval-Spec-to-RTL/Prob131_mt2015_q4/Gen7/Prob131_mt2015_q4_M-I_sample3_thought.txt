The original design instantiates duplicate A and B modules with identical inputs, resulting in redundant logic and area/power inefficiency. Since both pairs use the same inputs, their outputs will be identical. Therefore, we can instantiate only one A and one B module each, reuse their outputs, and compute the OR and AND on the same pair of outputs. This reduces logic duplication by half, saving area and power without affecting functional correctness. This also simplifies wiring and improves readability.

Specifically:
- Instantiate a single A module and a single B module with inputs x and y.
- Use the single outputs a_out and b_out to compute both OR and AND results.
- Perform OR and AND on the same a_out and b_out.
- XOR these two results to produce the final output z.

This optimization keeps the original functional behavior but reduces area and switching activity significantly.
