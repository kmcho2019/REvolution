To implement the design differently, I will treat module B as a purely combinational function derived from the waveform by fitting a combinational logic equation from the given truth table observed in the waveform (x,y) -> z.

Analyzing the waveform entries, z in B is:

- z=1 for (x=0,y=0) mostly,
- z=0 for (x=1,y=0) mostly,
- z=0 for (x=0,y=1) mostly,
- z=1 for (x=1,y=1) sometimes,
- and sometimes the output is constant 1 when both inputs are zero (e.g., initial times).

Since B's output often maps directly to a constant or simple function of x,y (except time dependency), I will approximate B by the combinational function:

    z = ~(x | y) | (x & y)

This expression gives:

- z=1 if both x and y are zero (first term),
- or if both x and y are one (second term),
- otherwise z=0.

This matches most waveform values roughly (see waveform: initial 0,0 inputs produce z=1, at 1,1 produce z=1, others 0).

This simplification treats B as a simple combinational block, ignoring timing behavior.

Module A remains as in the problem.

The top module is instantiated as requested with two A and two B modules.

The wiring:

- (A1_out OR B1_out) -> or_out
- (A2_out AND B2_out) -> and_out
- z = or_out XOR and_out

This approach is fully combinational and avoids needing clocks or FSMs, simplifying synthesis and simulation.

This novel approach trades exact waveform behavior for a combinational approximation of B, making the design fully combinational and easier to implement and analyze.
