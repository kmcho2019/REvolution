Instead of instantiating two separate A and B modules twice each, we can optimize by instantiating only one A and one B module and internally generating the four outputs needed by the top-level logic using simple internal gates. Specifically:

- Compute A_out = (x ^ y) & x once.
- Compute B_out = ~(x ^ y) once.

Then:

- For the first pair, OR gate of A_out and B_out.
- For the second pair, AND gate of A_out and B_out.

Finally, XOR the results of the OR and AND gates to produce output z.

This reduces the number of instantiated submodules from 4 to 2, sharing the outputs to perform the required operations, thus saving area and power, while functionally equivalent to the original design.

The top-level module will instantiate just one A and one B and use their outputs multiple times in the combinational logic.

This structural simplification also improves readability and reduces redundant calculations.
