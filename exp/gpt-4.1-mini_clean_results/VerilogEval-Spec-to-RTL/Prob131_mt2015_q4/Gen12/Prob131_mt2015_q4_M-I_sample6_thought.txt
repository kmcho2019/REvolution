The original solution uses two pairs of A and B modules with their outputs combined by OR and AND gates, then XORed for final output, which is clear but involves redundant computation because each submodule recomputes (x^y) or related logic independently. Since both A and B submodules use the same inputs (x, y), the XOR of x and y is computed multiple times.

To optimize power and area, we can share the intermediate signals (x ^ y) and ~(x ^ y) once, and feed them into two A and two B modules that only perform their respective AND or NOT operations as needed, minimizing redundant XOR computations.

Further, since all four submodules have identical inputs and logic, the shared intermediate signals reduce logic duplication and switching.

Additionally, we can inline the A and B logic inside the wrappers to avoid module overhead and extra nets, while still meeting the problem requirement of two A and two B submodules instantiated.

We maintain the same hierarchy with two pairs: one pair outputs OR of A.z and B.z, the other outputs AND of A.z and B.z; finally, the top module XORs these two outputs.

This design:

- Shares XOR computation reducing redundant logic and power.
- Minimizes nets and interconnects by reducing duplication.
- Keeps the problem's instantiation counts and logic structure intact.
- Results in improved power and area due to shared intermediate signals and less duplicated logic.

This approach balances clarity with PPA optimization.
