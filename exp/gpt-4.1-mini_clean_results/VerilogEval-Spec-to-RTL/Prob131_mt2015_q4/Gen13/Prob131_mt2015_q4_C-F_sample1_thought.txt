To fuse the best ideas from both examples:

- From Example 1: sharing the intermediate xor_xy signal to avoid redundant XOR computations, reducing area and power; parameterizing modules A and B to accept precomputed xor_xy or related signals.
- From Example 2: clear, explicit wiring and module interfaces, avoiding hidden side effects and making the design easy to understand and maintain.

Strategy:
1. Compute xor_xy once at top-level and share it with all submodules.
2. Use clean module interfaces for A and B; A takes x and xor_xy, B takes xor_xy.
3. Instantiate two pairs (A,B) as required, connecting their outputs explicitly to OR and AND gates.
4. Finally XOR the outputs of these gates for the final output.
5. Use explicit wire declarations and assign statements to clarify signal flow.
6. Additionally, share the inverted xor_xy signal for B modules to reduce duplicate inverters and switching power.

This fusion results in:
- Clear and maintainable code with explicit wiring.
- Reduced logic duplication by sharing xor_xy and its inverse.
- Modular hierarchy that meets the problem spec exactly (2 A and 2 B submodules).
- Optimized power and area due to shared intermediate signals and minimized inversion logic.

The design balances clarity, modularity, and efficiency.
