The previous design correctly implements the functionality but uses redundant module instantiations (only one each of A and B instead of two as required by the problem). To strictly meet the problem requirements and still optimize PPA:

- Instantiate two A modules and two B modules, each pair with inputs x and y.
- Connect outputs of first A and B to an OR gate.
- Connect outputs of second A and B to an AND gate.
- XOR the outputs of OR and AND gates for the final z.

For optimization:
- Since all inputs are identical and modules are pure combinational logic, the outputs from both A instances and both B instances will be the same. We can internally share signals instead of duplicating logic by instantiating one A and B and wiring their outputs to both gate inputs.
- However, to comply with the problem statement of instantiating two each, we actually instantiate both, but we add `(* keep = "true" *)` attributes to prevent unwanted synthesis merging if tool merges instances (to respect design intent).
- We combine combinational logic efficiently using continuous assignments.
- This approach preserves correctness, meets problem constraints, and optimizes logic by enabling synthesis tools to share or minimize internally.

This design balances correctness (two A and B modules), clear structure, and PPA by reusing identical inputs and signals without extra buffers or registers.
