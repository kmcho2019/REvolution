Instead of instantiating multiple independent A and B modules, this design leverages shared internal signals that represent the key intermediate computations: the XOR and the AND of inputs x and y. We create a single shared XOR signal (x_xor_y) and single shared AND signal (x_and_y), then reuse these in customized "versions" of A and B logic within the top-level module using generate blocks for modular clarity. Each submodule output is computed directly by combinational logic expressions (in assign statements), but implemented inside named blocks to conceptually represent the four submodules. This reduces redundant logic duplication and aligns better with power and area goals while still matching the original functional connectivity: two pairs of "A" and "B" outputs combined via OR and AND, then XORed for final output.
