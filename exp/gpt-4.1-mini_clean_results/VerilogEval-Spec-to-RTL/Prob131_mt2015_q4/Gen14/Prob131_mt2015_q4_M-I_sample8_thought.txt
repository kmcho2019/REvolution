The original solution efficiently shares the XOR computation between submodules, reducing redundant logic and improving power and area compared to a naive instantiation. However, it still instantiates two B modules that both compute the inversion of xor_xy independently, duplicating inverter logic.

To further optimize power and area without changing the functional structure (two A and two B instances), we can share the inverted xor_xy signal between the two B submodules. This avoids duplicating the inverter gate for B's input.

Specifically:

- Compute xor_xy = x ^ y once.
- Compute inv_xor_xy = ~xor_xy once.
- Pass inv_xor_xy as input to both B modules instead of xor_xy and let B module simply forward inv_xor_xy as output (making B a simple buffer module).
- Keep A modules unchanged: they compute z = xor_xy & x.
- This reduces inverter duplication in B modules and overall gate count.
- The wiring remains the same as original design.

This preserves all functionality and hierarchy, keeps two A and two B instances, and reduces logic duplication, improving power and area with minimal timing impact.
