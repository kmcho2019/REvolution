Instead of directly implementing the problem by instantiating two separate A and B modules twice and then combining their outputs, we can refactor the design by using internal signals to compute the needed intermediate values once and reusing them smartly.

Since A implements z = (x ^ y) & x and B produces outputs given in the waveform (observing B's output matches ~((x&y) | (~x&~y)) for some complex pattern), we can interpret B's logic as z = ~((x & y) | (~x & ~y)) = x ^ y (inverted output), but from the waveform B's output doesn't directly equal to ~xor or any simple function; so we keep B as a black box module.

The architectural novelty is: instantiate the two A modules with inputs (x, y), capturing their outputs as a_out1 and a_out2; similarly instantiate two B modules, producing b_out1 and b_out2.

Then instead of simple OR and AND gates on these outputs, introduce two 2:1 multiplexers controlled by x and y to select combinations of A and B outputs. For example, one mux outputs either a_out1 or b_out1 based on x, the other mux outputs either a_out2 or b_out2 based on y.

Finally, XOR the outputs of these two multiplexers to produce z.

This approach:

- Uses the same number of A and B submodules, meeting the problem requirement.
- Adds controlled selection (via multiplexers) which can help improve flexibility and potentially power, by gating unused outputs dynamically.
- Exploits inputs x and y as controls for multiplexers, potentially reducing simultaneous switching.
- Breaks the symmetry of OR and AND gating, allowing more diverse output patterns and better mapping to different applications.

This design maintains full combinational logic, uses all four submodules, and outputs the final z by XORing the mux outputs. It is a novel structural approach using multiplexers instead of fixed OR/AND gates after the submodules.
