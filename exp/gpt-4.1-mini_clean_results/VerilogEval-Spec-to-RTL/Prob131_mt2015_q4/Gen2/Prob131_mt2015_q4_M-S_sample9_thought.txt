The top-level module instantiates duplicate pairs of A and B modules that compute the same outputs given identical inputs, so their outputs will be identical. Instead of instantiating duplicates, we can instantiate one A and one B module each and reuse their outputs. The OR and AND gates then combine the same outputs, which simplifies to or_out = a | b and and_out = a & b. The final XOR of or_out and and_out simplifies to a ^ b since (a | b) ^ (a & b) = a ^ b. Therefore, the entire top-level logic simplifies to z = a ^ b, where a is the output of A and b is the output of B. This reduces the complexity by removing redundant module instances and gates while preserving the same functionality.
