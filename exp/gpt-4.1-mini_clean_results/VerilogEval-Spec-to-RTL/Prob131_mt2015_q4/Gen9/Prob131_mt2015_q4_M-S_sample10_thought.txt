Since all four submodules receive the same inputs x and y, and modules A and B implement combinational logic based only on these inputs, their outputs are identical across instances. Therefore, we can instantiate a single A and a single B module, and reuse their outputs for both pairs. This avoids redundant instantiations and reduces wiring complexity while maintaining identical functionality.

We then connect the outputs of A and B to the OR and AND gates as before, and XOR their results to get the final output z.

This simplification reduces the number of module instantiations from four to two, keeping the functionality intact and reducing area and power without changing the logical behavior.
