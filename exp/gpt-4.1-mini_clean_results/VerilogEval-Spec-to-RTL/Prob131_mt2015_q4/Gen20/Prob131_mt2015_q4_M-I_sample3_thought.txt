The original design correctly implements the required functionality, but it redundantly instantiates two identical A modules and two identical B modules with the same inputs, increasing area and power unnecessarily. Since all four submodules use the same inputs and have deterministic outputs, we can share the outputs of single A and B instances instead of duplicating modules. Specifically, we instantiate only one A and one B module and then use their outputs for both logic branches (the OR and the AND). This eliminates redundant computation, reduces area and power, and simplifies wiring without changing the functional behavior. The rest of the logic (OR, AND, XOR gates) remains the same. This approach preserves correctness and optimizes PPA metrics by cutting the number of submodule instances in half.
