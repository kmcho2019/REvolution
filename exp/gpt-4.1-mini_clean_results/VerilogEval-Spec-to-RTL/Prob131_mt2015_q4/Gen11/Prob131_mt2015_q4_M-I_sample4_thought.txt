To improve PPA, reduce gate count and transitions by merging the identical inputs of the two A modules and two B modules before the top-level logic. Since both A and B modules get the same inputs, the two instances produce identical outputs a1=a2 and b1=b2. Therefore, the OR and AND gates at the top can be simplified by removing one of each module instance. We keep just one A and one B instance, then compute or_out = a | b, and and_out = a & b (which equals a & b since single instance). Finally, z = or_out ^ and_out. This reduces module instantiations from four to two, cutting area and dynamic power, and decreases logic depth slightly for better performance. The functionality remains logically equivalent due to input symmetry. This optimization leverages the fact that inputs are identical for both instances, making the double instantiations redundant.
