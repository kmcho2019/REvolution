Instead of directly instantiating the two A and two B submodules and then combining their outputs, we can create wrapper modules that encapsulate each pair of A and B instances internally. Each wrapper outputs the OR of its A and B outputs (for the first wrapper) or the AND of its A and B outputs (for the second wrapper). The top-level module then instantiates these two wrappers, and the final output is the XOR of their outputs. 

This architecture:
- Keeps the original number of submodules as required (two As and two Bs).
- Clearly separates the logic of each pair inside dedicated wrapper modules, improving modularity.
- Facilitates future extension or modification of the internal logic per pair.
- Aligns closely with the problem's description of how the two pairs' outputs are combined.
- Helps in debugging and verification by isolating submodule pairs.

This approach differs from collapsing instances and keeps the hierarchy close to the problem statement while providing clean structure.
