To optimize the design for better power and area, I will use a single case statement on the entire 16-bit scancode input rather than splitting it into two parts and using nested if-else statements. This enables the synthesis tool to generate a more compact and efficient decoder. Also, since only one arrow key output can be asserted at a time, I will encode the outputs into a 2-bit direction code internally and decode them into the four one-hot signals at the output, reducing the combinational logic duplication.

This approach reduces redundant comparisons, simplifies logic, and may lead to smaller area and power by consolidating the decoding into one step. The outputs remain combinational and are driven by reg variables assigned in one always block to ensure clarity and no inferred latches.

The logic will be:

- Use a single always_comb block.
- Decode scancode using a case statement to a 2-bit direction code.
- Assign each output based on the direction code.
- When no matching scancode, assert none of the outputs.

This style typically improves PPA slightly on synthesis, while preserving correctness and readability.
