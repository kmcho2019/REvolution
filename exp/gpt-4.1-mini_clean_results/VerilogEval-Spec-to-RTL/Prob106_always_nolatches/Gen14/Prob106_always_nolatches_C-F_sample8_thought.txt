I will fuse the clarity and straightforwardness of the if-else approach with the mutual exclusivity and synthesis friendliness of a case statement. To do this, I will first check the upper byte once to confirm the 0xE0 prefix, and then use a case statement on the lower byte to assign one-hot outputs.

To combine the best of both examples:
- Use a single always_comb block to ensure all outputs are assigned in one place, avoiding latches.
- Default outputs to zero at the start for safety and clarity.
- Use the if condition on the upper byte first, then a case statement for the lower byte to cleanly encode all four arrow keys and no others.
- Keep outputs as reg since they are assigned inside the always_comb.
- This approach is compact, readable, and synthesis-friendly, allowing synthesis tools to optimize the mutually exclusive outputs efficiently.
- Also, by using a case statement rather than an if-else chain inside the prefix check, synthesis tools often produce cleaner decoding logic and possibly smaller area.
- Overall, this solution is simple, clear, efficient for power, area, and timing, and easy to maintain or extend.

This synthesis of ideas yields a clean and efficient combinational decoder for arrow key scancodes from PS/2 input.
