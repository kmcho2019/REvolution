Instead of decoding the 16-bit scancode in one combinational block, I will separate the high byte and low byte into two separate signals first. Then I'll use a one-hot approach to decode each arrow key independently by comparing both bytes in parallel with continuous assignments. The outputs will be assigned as the logical AND of these two signals (high byte matches 0xE0 and low byte matches the specific code). This approach uses parallel equality comparisons without any procedural block, offering a very straightforward combinational structure and can map easily to simple combinational logic gates. It also makes it clearer that the high byte must be 0xE0 to qualify the key, enabling easy extension if needed.
