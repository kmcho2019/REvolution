To combine the best of both examples, I will use a single always_comb block with a case statement decoding the lower byte of the scancode when the prefix matches 8'hE0. This method is clean, easy to read, and allows the synthesis tool to optimize decoding logic efficiently. I use reg outputs inside the always_comb block to guarantee combinational logic without latches.

Compared to Example 1's if-else, a case statement reduces the number of comparisons and can lead to smaller, faster decoding logic. Compared to Example 2's continuous assignments, the case block avoids repeated prefix comparisons and groups all decoding in one place, reducing redundant logic toggling and potentially saving power.

This fusion delivers:
- Clarity and maintainability (case statement)
- Efficiency by checking prefix once
- Clean combinational logic (always_comb with blocking assignments)
- Mutual exclusivity of outputs ensured by case
- Zero latch risk, no unnecessary registers

Thus, this design achieves readability, area, power, and performance balance superior to both prior examples.
