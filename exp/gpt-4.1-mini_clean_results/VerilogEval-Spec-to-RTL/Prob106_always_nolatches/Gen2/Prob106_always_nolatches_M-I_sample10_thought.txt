To optimize power and area slightly, instead of parallel full 16-bit comparisons for each output, decode the scancode using a single combinational block and a case statement that sets a 4-bit one-hot encoded signal representing the arrow keys. This avoids duplicating comparison logic four times and may share partial decoding internally. Outputs are then driven from this one-hot signal. This approach can reduce the amount of comparator logic and switching activity because only one comparator is effectively needed with a case decode. It also preserves combinational logic and timing.
