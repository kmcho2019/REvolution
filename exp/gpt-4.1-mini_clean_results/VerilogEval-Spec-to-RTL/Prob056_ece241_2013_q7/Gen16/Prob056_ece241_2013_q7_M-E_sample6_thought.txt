I will implement the JK flip-flop using a more structural style by explicitly computing the next state as a separate wire based on the inputs and current output. Then, on the positive clock edge, I will update Q with that next state.

This approach separates combinational next-state logic from sequential storage. The next state logic will use straightforward boolean expressions derived directly from the JK truth table to minimize logic depth:

- For J=K=0, Q(next) = Qold (no change)
- For J=0, K=1, Q(next) = 0
- For J=1, K=0, Q(next) = 1
- For J=1, K=1, Q(next) = ~Qold

The boolean formula for the next state can be derived as:  
Q(next) = (J & ~Q) | (~K & Q)  

But to keep it very explicit and easy to verify, I will use a multiplexer style logic with if-else branches in a combinational always block or continuous assign for next_state.

This style makes the design clear, allows easy modifications, and gives synthesis tools flexibility to optimize the combinational logic separately from the register.

Finally, the sequential block will be minimal, just updating Q with next_state at posedge clk.

This modular style often helps with testbench generation, debugging, and formal verification.
