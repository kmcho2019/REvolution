The original implementation is already clean and minimal for a JK flip-flop, using a direct Boolean expression for nextQ followed by a clocked always block.

To optimize for better PPA (Timing, Power, Area):

1. **Timing:** The original `nextQ` logic expression involves three product terms combined in an OR. We can try to simplify or reorganize it for faster combinational delay. 

   The expression:
   nextQ = (j & ~k) | ((~j) & (~k) & Q) | (j & k & ~Q)

   We can factor terms:

   nextQ = (j & ~k) | ((~k) & (~j) & Q) | (j & k & ~Q)
          = (~k & ((j) | (~j & Q))) | (j & k & ~Q)
          = (~k & (j | (~j & Q))) | (j & k & ~Q)

   Since (j | (~j & Q)) = j + (~j * Q)
   This is equivalent to: j + Q when j=1, else Q when j=0, so same as (j | Q). So:

   nextQ = (~k & (j | Q)) | (j & k & ~Q)

   This reduces logic depth: only two main terms ORed.

2. **Power:** This factoring reduces the number of AND gates and might reduce glitching by simplifying logic.

3. **Area:** This version uses fewer gates.

4. Use continuous assignment for nextQ to retain combinational nature, and register Q updated on posedge clk.

Overall, this rewrite is logically equivalent but potentially slightly better in PPA due to reduced logic depth and simpler logic.

The module interface remains the same.
