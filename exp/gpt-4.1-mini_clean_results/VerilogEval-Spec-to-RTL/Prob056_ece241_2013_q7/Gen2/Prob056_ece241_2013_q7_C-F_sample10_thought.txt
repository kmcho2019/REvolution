The Boolean expression for nextQ derived from the truth table is:
- When J=0 and K=0, nextQ = Q (hold)
- When J=0 and K=1, nextQ = 0 (reset)
- When J=1 and K=0, nextQ = 1 (set)
- When J=1 and K=1, nextQ = ~Q (toggle)

This can be written as:
nextQ = (j & ~k) | (~k & ~j & Q) | (j & k & ~Q)
which simplifies to:
nextQ = (j & ~k) | (Q & ~j & ~k) | (j & k & ~Q)

But notice (~k & ~j) is the hold condition, so can just use Q when hold:
nextQ = (j & ~k) | ((~j) & k & 1'b0) | (j & k & ~Q) | ((~j) & (~k) & Q)

Since ((~j)&k & 0) = 0, we can remove that term:
nextQ = (j & ~k) | ((~j) & (~k) & Q) | (j & k & ~Q)

Alternatively:
- For set: j & ~k
- For reset: ~j & k (goes to 0)
- For toggle: j & k & ~Q
- For hold: ~j & ~k & Q

The reset condition forces nextQ to 0, so it's simpler to write:
nextQ = (j & ~k) | ((~j) & (~k) & Q) | (j & k & ~Q)

This expression covers all cases exactly.

This logic avoids nested ternary operators and avoids any explicit "do-nothing" in the clock block.

This fusion should enhance readability, synthesis, and performance.
