To optimize the FSM for better Power, Performance, and Area (PPA), I will simplify next-state and output logic using case statements, which generally synthesize more efficiently than complex boolean expressions. Also, the output is combinationally dependent only on state == D, so I'll use a direct assign statement for 'out' to reduce logic.

I will keep the 2-bit encoding (for area efficiency) and use a case statement inside a combinational always block for the next-state logic, which is straightforward for the synthesis tool and reduces combinational path complexity.

The synchronous reset and state register remain unchanged.

This approach reduces the depth and complexity of combinational logic, improves readability, and likely decreases switching activity and gate count, thus improving timing and power without adding flip-flops.
