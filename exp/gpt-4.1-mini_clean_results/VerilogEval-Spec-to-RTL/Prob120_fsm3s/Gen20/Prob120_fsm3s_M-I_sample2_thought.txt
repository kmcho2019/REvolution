To optimize the FSM for better PPA while maintaining clarity and correctness, I will switch from one-hot encoding to binary encoding for the four states. This reduces flip-flop count from 4 to 2, thus lowering area and power.

- Use 2-bit binary encoding for the states A=2'b00, B=2'b01, C=2'b10, D=2'b11.
- Implement the FSM with a synchronous reset and a single always block.
- Use a case statement for next state and output logic, which synthesis tools optimize efficiently.
- The output depends only on the current state (Moore FSM).
- Binary encoding reduces flip-flop count and switching activity, improving power and area.
- The combinational logic depth is minimal with a simple case structure, benefiting timing.
- This approach is the classical FSM design, easier to maintain and read.

Overall, this design will have fewer flip-flops (2 vs 4), potentially simpler combinational logic, and cleaner code while maintaining functionality and reset behavior.
