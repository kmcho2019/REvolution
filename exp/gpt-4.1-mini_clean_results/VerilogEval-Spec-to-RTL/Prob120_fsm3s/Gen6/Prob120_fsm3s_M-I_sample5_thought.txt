To improve the original design's PPA, we target lower power and better timing by switching to one-hot state encoding. One-hot encoding uses one flip-flop per state, which simplifies next state decoding and reduces combinational logic depth, often improving timing (negative WNS). Although it uses more flip-flops (area tradeoff), for four states this overhead is minimal and acceptable given the small FSM. Additionally, one-hot encoding naturally minimizes glitches and reduces switching activity in combinational logic, thus lowering dynamic power. The output logic remains a simple OR of the 'D' state flip-flop. We keep synchronous reset and non-blocking assignments for correctness and synthesis friendliness. This change aims to improve timing (reduce WNS) and power with minor or negligible area impact for this small FSM.
