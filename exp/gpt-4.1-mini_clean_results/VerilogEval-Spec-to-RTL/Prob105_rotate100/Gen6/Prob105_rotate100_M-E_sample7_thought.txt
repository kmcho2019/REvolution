Instead of performing rotation by simple bit concatenation of the whole 100-bit register, implement the 100-bit rotator as a circular shift register formed by a 100-element array of flip-flops connected in a ring topology. On each clock edge, depending on load and ena, each flip-flop takes input from either the corresponding data bit (load), or from the neighbor flip-flop either on the left or right for rotation. This structure naturally models rotation and avoids large concatenations, potentially improving routing and synthesis scalability. The output q is read directly from the register array.
