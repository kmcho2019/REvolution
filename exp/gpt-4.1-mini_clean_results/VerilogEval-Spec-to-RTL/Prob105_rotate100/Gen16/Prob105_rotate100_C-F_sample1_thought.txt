Then register `next_q` synchronously on the clock edge. This preserves clear isolation of combinational and sequential logic, improving readability and easing synthesis optimizations.

Additionally, implement a clock-enable signal internally based on load or a valid single-direction rotation enable (either ena==2'b01 or ena==2'b10) to reduce unnecessary toggling of the register on ena=00 or 11, improving power efficiency without adding a separate clock gating cell. This clock enable enables the register update only on actual changes, reducing dynamic power.

This approach combines:
- Explicit next state combinational logic for easier maintenance.
- Minimal ternary mux logic for rotation and load for faster synthesis.
- An inferred clock enable to reduce toggling and power.
- No case statements or verbose if-else, preserving compactness.
- Correct synchronous load and rotation behavior with the exact enable semantics.

This fusion yields a clean, readable, efficient, and low-power 100-bit rotator.
