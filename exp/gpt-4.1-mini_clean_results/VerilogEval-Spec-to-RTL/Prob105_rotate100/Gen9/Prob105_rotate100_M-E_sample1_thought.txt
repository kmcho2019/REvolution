Implement the 100-bit rotate register using a parallel multiplexing approach controlled by the ena signals to select between load, rotate-left, rotate-right, or hold states. Instead of using vector concatenations directly in the always block, precompute three candidate values: the load data, the left-rotated value, and the right-rotated value. Then select among them using a combinational mux before registering the result synchronously.

This modularizes the logic and makes it explicit that the rotation is just data selection between shifted versions. It also clarifies that rotation is just a bit permutation of q. The actual rotation uses concatenation as before but done outside the sequential block.

Finally, on the clock edge, assign q with the selected candidate value if load is high or ena requests rotation; otherwise hold q. This approach can also help synthesis tools optimize the multiplexers better by separating combinational rotation generation from register update.
