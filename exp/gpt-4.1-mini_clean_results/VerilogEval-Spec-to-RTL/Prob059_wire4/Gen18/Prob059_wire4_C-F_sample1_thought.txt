The best solution fuses the modular clarity from the Buffer module in Example 1 with the minimal area and power advantages of direct continuous assignments in Example 2.

Here is the fusion strategy:
- For uniquely driven outputs (w and z), use direct continuous assignments to minimize area and power, avoiding unnecessary buffers.
- For outputs driven by the same input signal (x and y both driven by b), define a simple dedicated Buffer module that drives both outputs simultaneously from a single input, consolidating the duplicated signal fanout into one module instance. This reduces wiring redundancy and improves modular clarity without increasing area or power significantly.
- This avoids instantiating multiple buffer modules for the same signal (which wastes area and power) and avoids the overhead of redundant buffers for uniquely driven signals.
- This approach is both synthesis friendly and clean, maintaining simplicity while slightly improving scalability if multiple duplicated outputs arise.

Hence, the design blends direct continuous assignments for unique connections with a shared buffer module for duplicated outputs, achieving an optimal balance in PPA and maintainability.
