The existing solution is already very efficient for a 4-bit priority encoder, but we can slightly optimize for area and power by removing explicit priority masking signals and directly encoding priority using a simple if-else ladder in a combinational always block. This avoids multiple AND gates and intermediate signals, thus potentially reducing gate count and switching activity. The combinational block with an always_comb (or always @*) block and if-else statements will synthesize into minimal priority logic with short critical path.

This approach maintains correctness, ensures clear priority from LSB to MSB, and directly outputs the first set bit index or zero if none are set. It's a classic and compact priority encoder style that is synthesis-friendly and may yield small improvements in area and power over the explicit AND-based logic while preserving optimal timing for 4 bits.

Hence, the improvement strategy is to use a combinational always block with priority if-else encoding instead of explicit priority signals with AND masking.
