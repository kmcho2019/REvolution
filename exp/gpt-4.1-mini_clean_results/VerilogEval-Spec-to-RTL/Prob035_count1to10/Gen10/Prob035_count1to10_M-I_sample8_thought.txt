To further optimize for power and timing while maintaining area, implement clock gating explicitly using a clock enable (ce) signal to halt counting during reset without toggling the register unnecessarily. This reduces dynamic power by preventing needless toggles in the counter flip-flops.

Also, slightly simplify the terminal count detection by recognizing that counting is linear from 1 to 10 and wrapping after 10, so use a straightforward comparator.

The synchronous reset remains active-high, setting counter to 1.

Using a clean non-blocking always block with the clock enable to keep toggling minimal and improve overall timing and power metrics.

This strategy avoids gating the clock itself (which can be unsafe in some flows) but uses a clock enable, a best practice for reducing switching activity in FPGA and ASIC environments.
