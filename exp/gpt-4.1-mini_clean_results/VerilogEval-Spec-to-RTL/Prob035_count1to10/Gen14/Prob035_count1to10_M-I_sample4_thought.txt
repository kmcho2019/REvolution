To improve the PPA (timing, power, and area) of the decade counter, I will make the following optimizations:

- Instead of checking the count value 10 (binary 1010) using a bitwise pattern match, I will use a simpler integer comparison `q == 4'd10`. Modern synthesis tools optimize such comparisons efficiently, often better than manual bitwise operations.

- Remove the reset check from the combinational next-state logic and instead handle synchronous reset directly in the sequential block. This separation reduces combinational logic complexity and can improve timing.

- Use a direct increment or wrap in the sequential always block to reduce the combinational path to a simple adder with comparison, which often maps efficiently to hardware.

- By embedding the reset and wrap logic into the clocked block, I can avoid the additional combinational register `next_q` and its associated muxing, reducing area and power.

- This also reduces toggling by avoiding unnecessary intermediate signals.

Overall, this approach leads to a minimal critical path (increment plus compare) and lowers dynamic power due to reduced combinational switching and simplified logic. It also slightly reduces area by eliminating the extra register and combinational mux.

This tradeoff slightly reduces code clarity compared to fully separated combinational next-state logic but gains in performance and power due to simpler logic.

Functionality remains the same: synchronous reset sets q to 1, the counter increments each clock cycle, and wraps from 10 to 1.
