Instead of using binary counting with a comparator, implement the decade counter as a ring counter with one-hot encoding. The 10 states are represented by 10 flip-flops, where exactly one bit is high at a time, indicating the count from 1 to 10. On each clock, the '1' bit shifts to the next position, and after the tenth state, it returns to the first. The synchronous reset sets the first bit high (representing count=1). Finally, convert the one-hot encoded position to a binary 4-bit output using a priority encoder or by mapping the active bit index to its numeric value. This approach avoids adders and comparators and can simplify combinational logic at the cost of more flip-flops. The binary output is produced combinationally from the one-hot state for easy interfacing.
