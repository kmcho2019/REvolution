The function outputs '1' for inputs 2, 7, and 15; '0' for certain inputs; and inputs 3, 8, 11, and 12 never occur (don't-cares). Given the don't-cares, the simplest minimal SOP that covers all ones is:

- For 7 (0 1 1 1) and 15 (1 1 1 1), the common part is b & c & d.
- For 2 (0 0 1 0), the term is ~a & ~b & c & ~d.

Thus, out_sop = (b & c & d) | (~a & ~b & c & ~d).

To simplify the POS, note that the function is zero for inputs 0,1,4,5,6,9,10,13,14 and don't care for 3,8,11,12. Using the zero-set, the minimal POS is simpler and can be derived by covering zeros:

out_pos = (a + b + ~c + ~d) & (a + ~b + ~c + d) & (~a + b + c + d)

This minimal POS covers all zeros and is consistent with the function.

This reduces the complexity while maintaining correctness and is consistent with the input bit ordering a (MSB) to d (LSB).
