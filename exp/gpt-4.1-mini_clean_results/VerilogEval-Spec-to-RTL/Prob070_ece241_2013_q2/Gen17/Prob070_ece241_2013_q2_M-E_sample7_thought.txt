Use a direct 4-bit input vector formed from inputs {a,b,c,d} where 'a' is MSB and 'd' is LSB, and implement the output generation by explicitly matching the required numbers with equality comparators. This avoids errors in Karnaugh map simplification and the ambiguity caused by don't-cares.

Specifically:
- Concatenate inputs to form a 4-bit number: num = {a,b,c,d}
- For out_sop, output logic-1 if num equals 2, 7, or 15.
- For out_pos, output logic-1 except for inputs that correspond to zeros (0,1,4,5,6,9,10,13,14). The inputs 3,8,11,12 never occur, so they can be treated as don't-cares.

Implementing out_pos as the NOR of zero numbers: out_pos=1 except when num is a zero number, which is equivalent to out_pos = ~(num == zero_set). This gives a correct minimal POS behavior since POS is essentially an AND of sums corresponding to zeros.

This method is straightforward, less error-prone, and guarantees correct functional behavior per problem specification without relying on complex minimization.

This solution is architecturally different from minimized Boolean expressions and uses direct matching logic instead.
