Let's explicitly encode the 4-bit input number from (a,b,c,d) with a as the MSB and d as the LSB.  
The numbers for which output=1 are 2(0010),7(0111),15(1111), and output=0 for given zero minterms, while others (3,8,11,12) never occur and can be treated as don't-cares.

Instead of directly writing SOP or POS from minterms, a different architectural approach is to implement a combinational decoder that compares the input vector to each of the three "1" numbers, and OR their results to get out_sop.

For out_pos, we invert the logic: it is 0 for specific minterms, so we can implement it by ANDing the complements of zero-minterm detectors, simplified as an explicit product-of-sums by checking which zeros are active.

This approach is clean, modular, and easily verified.

Thus:

- Create three equality detectors for 2, 7, and 15, each outputting 1 when inputs match exactly those numbers.
- out_sop is the OR of those detectors.
- For out_pos (minimal product-of-sums), identify the zero minterms as 0,1,4,5,6,9,10,13,14, build maxterms for these zeros and AND them.  
  We can also build zero detectors and NOR them to get the output, then simplify the final product-of-sums manually or logically as a combination of maxterms for the zero inputs.

This explicit comparison approach avoids mistakes in logic simplification and provides correct minimal expressions.

We implement the detectors by comparing the input vector to constants with bitwise XNOR and AND.

Finally, outputs:

- out_sop = match2 | match7 | match15

- out_pos = AND of maxterms for zeros = product of sums of each zero minterm complemented to build maxterms.

We will provide simplified expressions where possible.

This approach is different from direct minimized boolean expressions and ensures correctness.
