The original solution uses a single sequential always block that combines the 2:1 mux and the flip-flop, which is a valid and efficient way to implement the desired functionality. However, it declares a local variable inside the always block, which is illegal in Verilog and may cause unpredictable results. To fix this, we should remove the local variable and instead directly assign to the output register using a non-blocking assignment with a ternary operator. This approach keeps the design simple, synchronous, and synthesizable without any unnecessary intermediate signals, improving clarity and ensuring compliance with Verilog standards.
