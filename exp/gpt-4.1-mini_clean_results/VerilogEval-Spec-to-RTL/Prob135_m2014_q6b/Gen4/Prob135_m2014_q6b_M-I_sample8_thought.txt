The original logic can be further optimized by using Karnaugh map simplification for next_y1, aiming to reduce gate count and switching activity. Observing the states and transitions:

- States A to F are encoded as 3-bit y (000 to 101).
- Output Y1 is simply y[1].

The next-state logic for y[1] can be minimized by grouping the minterms corresponding to the next state bit y[1] = 1.

Examining all transitions and their next y[1], a simplified logic is:

next_y1 = (w & ~y2 & ~y1) | (~y2 & y0) | (y2 & ~y1 & (y0 | w))

This can be factorized and expressed in terms of fewer AND/OR gates:

next_y1 = (~y2 & ((~y1 & w) | y0)) | (y2 & ~y1 & (y0 | w))

This reduces one inversion from the original expression and groups terms better.

Implementing this reduces logic depth and gate count, thus improving area and power while maintaining the same output.

Also, the output Y1 = next_y1 as requested.

This expression avoids redundant decoding and shares sub-expressions, improving PPA (power, performance, area).
