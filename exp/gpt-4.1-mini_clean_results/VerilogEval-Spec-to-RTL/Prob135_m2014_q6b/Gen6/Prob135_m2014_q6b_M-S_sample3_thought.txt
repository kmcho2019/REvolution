We can simplify the decoding of states by noting that only states B, C, E, and F affect next_y1. Since we only need B, C, E, and F for the expression, we can use minimal conditions.

Notice:
- B = 001 => y = 3'b001 (y2=0, y1=0, y0=1)
- C = 010 => y2=0, y1=1, y0=0
- E = 100 => y2=1, y1=0, y0=0
- F = 101 => y2=1, y1=0, y0=1

We can reduce the decoding logic by observing patterns:

- B and C have y2=0 and y1 and y0 complementary for each state.
- E and F have y2=1 and y1=0.

Alternatively, we can directly express next_y1 using y and w without explicit B,C,E,F signals:

From original expression:
next_y1 = B | F | ((C | E) & w)

Express each term directly:
B = ~y[2] & ~y[1] & y[0]
F = y[2] & ~y[1] & y[0]
C = ~y[2] & y[1] & ~y[0]
E = y[2] & ~y[1] & ~y[0]

Therefore:
next_y1 = B | F | ((C | E) & w)
= (~y2 & ~y1 & y0) | (y2 & ~y1 & y0) | ( ((~y2 & y1 & ~y0) | (y2 & ~y1 & ~y0)) & w )

Note that (~y2 & ~y1 & y0) | (y2 & ~y1 & y0) = ~y1 & y0

Also, (C | E) = (~y2 & y1 & ~y0) | (y2 & ~y1 & ~y0)

To keep it simple, use these directly.

This eliminates intermediate wires B,C,E,F and reduces code size, while maintaining clarity and functionality.
