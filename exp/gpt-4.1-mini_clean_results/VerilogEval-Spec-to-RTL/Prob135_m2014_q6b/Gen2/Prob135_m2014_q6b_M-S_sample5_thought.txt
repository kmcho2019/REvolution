To simplify the next state logic for y[1], first note that the output Y1 is simply the current y[1], so we just assign Y1 = y[1].

For the next_y1 logic, we implement only the next state logic for y[1] based on the given FSM transitions and state encodings:

- States A(000), B(001), C(010), D(011), E(100), F(101)
- Using only the y bits and input w to find next y[1]

From the FSM, listing next states and their y[1] bits:

A(000):
- w=0 -> B(001), y1=0
- w=1 -> A(000), y1=0

B(001):
- w=0 -> C(010), y1=1
- w=1 -> D(011), y1=1

C(010):
- w=0 -> E(100), y1=0
- w=1 -> D(011), y1=1

D(011):
- w=0 -> F(101), y1=0
- w=1 -> A(000), y1=0

E(100):
- w=0 -> E(100), y1=0
- w=1 -> D(011), y1=1

F(101):
- w=0 -> C(010), y1=1
- w=1 -> D(011), y1=1

Deriving next_y1:

- next_y1=1 for B(001) w=0 or w=1
- next_y1=1 for C(010) w=1
- next_y1=1 for E(100) w=1
- next_y1=1 for F(101) w=0 or w=1
- next_y1=0 otherwise

Express next_y1 with minterms:

- B(001): y=001 => y2=0,y1=0,y0=1
- C(010): 010 => y2=0,y1=1,y0=0
- D(011): 011 => y2=0,y1=1,y0=1
- E(100): 100 => y2=1,y1=0,y0=0
- F(101): 101 => y2=1,y1=0,y0=1

From the table, next_y1=1 for:

- (B and w=0 or 1): B=001 => y2=0,y1=0,y0=1 (always 1)
- (C and w=1): y=010,w=1
- (E and w=1): y=100,w=1
- (F and w=0 or 1): y=101 (always 1)

So next_y1 can be written as:

next_y1 = (B) OR (C & w) OR (E & w) OR (F)

where:

B = ~y2 & ~y1 & y0

C = ~y2 & y1 & ~y0

E = y2 & ~y1 & ~y0

F = y2 & ~y1 & y0

Combine E and F:

E | F = y2 & ~y1

So final simplified next_y1:

next_y1 = B | (C & w) | (E | F) & w or no w?

Check for E and F:

From E and F, next_y1=1 if w=1 (E) or any w (F). Actually, for F next_y1=1 regardless of w.

Thus, next_y1 = B | (C & w) | F | (E & w)

Because F covers all w, rewrite as:

next_y1 = B | F | (C & w) | (E & w)

Simplify (E & w) + F = (y2 & ~y1 & ((~y0 & w) | y0)) = y2 & ~y1 & (w | y0)

Therefore:

next_y1 = (~y2 & ~y1 & y0) | (y2 & ~y1 & (w | y0)) | (~y2 & y1 & w)

This matches the original logic but is already minimal.

For output, assign Y1 = y[1].

Therefore, the module just computes next_y1 for FSM updating elsewhere, but output is current y[1].

This matches the problem requirement and is simple.
