To optimize power and area further while keeping performance intact, I will reduce the number of intermediate decoded signals and use direct expressions. Instead of decoding states B, C, E, and F separately and then combining, I will express the next_y1 logic directly in terms of y bits and input w. This reduces fanout and switching from intermediate nets.

From the state codes (A=000, B=001, C=010, D=011, E=100, F=101), the decoded states can be expressed with fewer gates:

- B = (~y2 & ~y1 & y0)
- C = (~y2 & y1 & ~y0)
- E = (y2 & ~y1 & ~y0)
- F = (y2 & ~y1 & y0)

I will combine these directly in next_y1:

next_y1 = B | F | w & (C | E)

Substituting:

next_y1 = (~y2 & ~y1 & y0) | (y2 & ~y1 & y0) | w & ((~y2 & y1 & ~y0) | (y2 & ~y1 & ~y0))

Further factoring:

B | F = (~y1) & y0  (since both have ~y1 & y0)

C | E = (~y2 & y1 & ~y0) | (y2 & ~y1 & ~y0) - two terms

We can keep C|E as is due to their difference in y2 and y1 bits.

This reduces intermediate nets, simplifies logic depth, and reduces toggling on intermediate nets.

Finally, assign Y1 = next_y1 as required.

This approach should slightly reduce area and power by removing intermediate wires and leveraging direct logic expressions while keeping logic depth minimal.
