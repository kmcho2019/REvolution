The original design implements next_y1 = B | F | w & (C | E) with explicit decodings for B, C, E, F. The states are encoded as:

A=000, B=001, C=010, D=011, E=100, F=101

We only need next_y1 based on current state y and input w.

To optimize further for power and area, I will:

1. Avoid explicit state decoding by expressing next_y1 directly using y bits and w with Boolean simplification.
2. Use Karnaugh Map or Boolean algebra to find minimal expressions for next_y1 from the state transition table.
3. This avoids multiple AND gates for decoding, reduces gates, and thus reduces switching and area.

From transitions:

next_y1 = 1 in these transitions (where next state has y[1] = 1):

- E=100 (state with y=100) on w=1 leads to E or D (which have y[1]=1 or 1 respectively)
- Other transitions (from C and E and F) to states with y[1]=1 occur when w=1 or from states B=001 (next y[1]=1 because next state is D=011, y[1]=1)

Summarizing next_y1 outputs for all states and inputs:

State | y2 y1 y0 | w=0 -> next y | next y1 | w=1 -> next y | next y1
-------------------------------------------------------------
A(000) | 0 0 0 | B(001) | 0 | A(000) | 0
B(001) | 0 0 1 | C(010) | 1 | D(011) | 1
C(010) | 0 1 0 | E(100) | 0 | D(011) | 1
D(011) | 0 1 1 | F(101) | 0 | A(000) | 0
E(100) | 1 0 0 | E(100) | 0 | D(011) | 1
F(101) | 1 0 1 | C(010) | 1 | D(011) | 1

Thus, next_y1 is:

- For w=0: next_y1=1 when from B or C or F states (i.e., y=001, 010, 101)
- For w=1: next_y1=1 when from B, C, E, F states (y=001,010,100,101)

This can be represented as:

next_y1 = (w & (B | C | E | F)) | (~w & (B | C | F))

Rewrite with y bits:

- B = ~y2 & ~y1 & y0
- C = ~y2 & y1 & ~y0
- E = y2 & ~y1 & ~y0
- F = y2 & ~y1 & y0

Group the terms:

B|C = ~y2 & ( (~y1 & y0) | (y1 & ~y0)) = ~y2 & (y1 ^ y0)

E|F = y2 & ~y1

Thus,

next_y1 = w & ( ~y2 & (y1 ^ y0) | y2 & ~y1 ) | (~w & ( ~y2 & (y1 ^ y0) | y2 & ~y1 & y0))

But B | C | F for ~w is ( ~y2 & (y1 ^ y0) ) | (y2 & ~y1 & y0)

Hence next_y1 = w & ( ~y2 & (y1 ^ y0) | y2 & ~y1 ) | ~w & ( ~y2 & (y1 ^ y0) | y2 & ~y1 & y0 )

To optimize, note that the ( ~y2 & (y1 ^ y0) ) term is common:

next_y1 = ( ~y2 & (y1 ^ y0) ) | ( w & y2 & ~y1 ) | ( ~w & y2 & ~y1 & y0 )

This reduces gates, no explicit decoding of states B,C,E,F needed. The expression is compact, avoids unnecessary inverters, reduces switching since the (y1 ^ y0) is a single XOR gate, and selects based on y2,y1,y0 and w.

Finally, output Y1 = next_y1.

This expression is minimal and more efficient than the original since it uses a single XOR and fewer ANDs/ORs, thus improving PPA.
