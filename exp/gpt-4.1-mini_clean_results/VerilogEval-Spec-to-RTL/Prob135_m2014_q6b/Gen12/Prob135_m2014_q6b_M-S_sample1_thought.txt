I will simplify the logic for y[1]_next by expressing it directly as a minimal Boolean expression without explicit state decoding signals. Observing the FSM and output assignments, y[1]_next is 1 for states B, F always; and for states C and E only when w=1. All other cases yield 0.

Given the state codes:
A=000, B=001, C=010, D=011, E=100, F=101

We can derive y[1]_next = (state is B or F) OR (state is C or E AND w).

- State B: y = 001 → y2=0,y1=0,y0=1
- State F: y = 101 → y2=1,y1=0,y0=1
- State C: y = 010 → y2=0,y1=1,y0=0
- State E: y = 100 → y2=1,y1=0,y0=0

We observe:
- B or F: y0=1 and y1=0 (both) → (~y1 & y0)
- C or E: y1=1 or y2=1 but y0=0? Actually E has y1=0, y2=1, y0=0 and C has y1=1,y2=0,y0=0

So, (C or E) can be represented as (y1 | y2) & ~y0

Therefore:
y[1]_next = (~y1 & y0) | (w & ( (y1 | y2) & ~y0 ))

This yields a minimal, clean expression directly from y bits and w without intermediate signals.

Finally, output Y1 = y[1]_next.

This approach reduces gate count, removes explicit state decoders, and keeps the output logic clear and minimal.
