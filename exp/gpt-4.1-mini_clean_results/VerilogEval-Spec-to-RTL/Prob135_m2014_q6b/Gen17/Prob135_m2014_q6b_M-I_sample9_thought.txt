The original implementation uses some intermediate wires for clarity and reusability, which is good for readability but might introduce slight area overhead. Given the simple logic and very low area and power already, the main improvement can be made by further simplifying the boolean expression to reduce the number of gates and transitions, and removing any redundant intermediate signals.

Re-examining the next_y1 function:
next_y1 = (~y1 & y0) | (w & ( (y1 | y2) & ~y0 ))

We can factor it as:
next_y1 = (~y1 & y0) | (w & (y1 | y2) & ~y0)

No trivial further simplification is evident, but to reduce gate count and transitions:

- Use direct expression for next_y1 without defining separate wires.
- Keep inversion only where needed, reuse inverted inputs if possible in the expressions directly.
- Assign Y1 as next_y1 directly without extra wires.
- This may reduce the total gate count and wiring complexity slightly, thus improving area and power marginally.

Since the original design is already minimal and the synthesis tool can optimize internally, this manual simplification mainly removes intermediate signals, which might slightly reduce area and switching power.

Thus, the improvement is: implement the same logic as a single expression without extra intermediate wires, reducing wire overhead and possible switching activity.
