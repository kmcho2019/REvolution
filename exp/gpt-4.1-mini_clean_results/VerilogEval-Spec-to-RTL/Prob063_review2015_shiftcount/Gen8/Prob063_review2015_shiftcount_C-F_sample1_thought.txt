Fuse the best aspects of both examples by implementing a clean separation of next-state logic and synchronous update with a single combinational always block that computes `next_q`. This block will prioritize `shift_ena` over `count_ena` (as allowed), ensuring clear control flow and minimal multiplexing. To strictly follow the problem statement of shifting in data MSB-first, we shift right, inserting `data` into the MSB. This aligns the semantics clearly and avoids confusion. Using a single `next_q` signal eliminates redundant intermediate registers, reducing area and power. The synchronous always block updates `q` on the clock’s rising edge. This approach keeps the design simple, clean, power-efficient, and easy to optimize by synthesis tools.
