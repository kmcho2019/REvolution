I will implement the shift register and the down counter as two separate 4-bit registers internally. On each clock edge, depending on the control signals, the top register updates either by shifting in the data at MSB if shift_ena is asserted, or by decrementing the value stored in the register if count_ena is asserted. To make the circuit more modular and clear, I will use a combinational logic block to compute the next value of the register based on the inputs and then update the register in a single always block. This approach avoids multiple always blocks, ensuring clarity.

For the shift operation, since data is shifted in MSB first, I will shift the register right and put 'data' at the MSB. For counting down, I will subtract 1 from the current register value. When both enables are zero, the register holds its value. When both are one (undefined by spec), I will prioritize shift_ena for the update. This explicit priority is acceptable since the specification allows it.

This design keeps the logic straightforward and uses clear combinational next-state logic before the clock edge, making it easy to understand and potentially easier to optimize for timing and area.
