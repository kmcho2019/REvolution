Refactor the design by separating the next-state logic into combinational logic and the register update into a synchronous always block. This approach improves code readability and modularity by clearly distinguishing combinational next-state computation from state updating. We compute `next_q` based on `shift_ena` and `count_ena` with conditional priority for shifting, then update the register `q` on the clock's positive edge. This structure is common for sequential designs and facilitates easier debugging, simulation, and formal verification while keeping the core behavior unchanged.
