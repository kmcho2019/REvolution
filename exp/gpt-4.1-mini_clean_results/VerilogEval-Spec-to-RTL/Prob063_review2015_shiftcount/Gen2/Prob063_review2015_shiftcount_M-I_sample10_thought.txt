The original solution lacks a synchronous reset, which is important for predictable initialization of the shift register and avoiding unknown simulation states. Although an initial block sets `q` to zero, this is not guaranteed to synthesize and may cause simulation mismatches.

To improve robustness and synthesis compatibility, add an active-high synchronous reset input (`reset`). On reset, `q` initializes to zero. This ensures a known start state for both simulation and hardware.

The logic of shifting in `data` at the MSB on `shift_ena` and decrementing on `count_ena` remains the same, with mutual exclusion of control signals as the problem allows any priority when both are high (we keep shift_ena prioritized).

This change removes initial blocks (which are sometimes ignored in synthesis) and replaces them with an explicit reset mechanism, making the design more robust and predictable.

No other optimizations are necessary since the design is straightforward, and the main issue was initialization.
