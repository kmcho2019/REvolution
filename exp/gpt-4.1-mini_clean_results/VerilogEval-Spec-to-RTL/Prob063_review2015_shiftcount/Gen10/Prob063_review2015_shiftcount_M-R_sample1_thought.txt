Refactor the design by separating the next state logic from the register update. Compute the next value of the 4-bit register in a combinational always block based on the control signals: if shift_ena is set, perform the left shift with data loaded into MSB; else if count_ena is set, decrement by 1; otherwise hold the value.

Then, update the register q on the rising edge of clk with the computed next value. This separation clarifies the combinational and sequential parts and makes the logic easier to analyze or optimize for synthesis.

Since shift_ena and count_ena are not asserted simultaneously, a simple if-else suffices. This also makes it straightforward to change priorities if needed without nesting shift and decrement operations directly in a clocked always block.

This structure is a common and clean style in RTL coding.
