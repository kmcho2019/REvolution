I will refactor the design by separating the combinational next-state logic from the sequential register update. Instead of embedding all logic in a single clocked always block, I'll first compute the next value of the 4-bit register in a combinational always block based on the control signals. Then, on the positive edge of clk, I'll update the register.

This clearly separates combinational logic from sequential logic, making the RTL easier to understand and verify.

Specifically:
- When shift_ena is asserted, shift `q` right by one bit towards LSB, inputting `data` at MSB.
- When count_ena is asserted, decrement `q` by 1.
- Otherwise, hold the current value.

Because both signals being high simultaneously are don't care, the priority will be given to `shift_ena` for clarity.

This approach keeps the same functionality but improves modularity and readability, making debugging easier if mismatches arise.

No reset signal is used, consistent with the problem statement.
