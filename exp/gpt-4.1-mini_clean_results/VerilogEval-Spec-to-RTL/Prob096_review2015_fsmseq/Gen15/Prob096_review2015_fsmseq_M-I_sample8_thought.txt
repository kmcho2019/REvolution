To improve the PPA (Performance, Power, Area) of the original solution, I will optimize the FSM state encoding from binary to one-hot. One-hot encoding simplifies next-state logic to mostly muxes or simple logic, reducing logic depth and improving maximum clock frequency (performance), typically at the cost of area.

However, since the original design area is quite small (33 LUTs) and the critical path is relatively short (0.19 ns), this trade-off is justified to achieve better timing (lower WNS).

One-hot encoding also reduces switching activity in combinational logic by localizing changes to only relevant bits, potentially reducing power.

Additionally, I'll keep the synchronous reset and synchronous output generation with a Moore FSM for stable output and glitch avoidance.

Finally, I'll use an explicit registered output to potentially help place-and-route tools optimize output buffers and reduce glitches.

In summary, one-hot FSM encoding with minimal combinational logic depth should improve performance and potentially power, with a minor area increase, fitting the balanced PPA goal.
