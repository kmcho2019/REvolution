Improvement strategy:
- Use a 4-bit one-hot encoding FSM instead of 3-bit binary encoding. One-hot FSMs typically have simpler combinational next-state logic with fewer transitions per flip-flop, reducing glitching and dynamic power.
- The one-hot FSM will have 5 states (S0 to S4), with each represented by one flip-flop.
- Synchronous reset remains active-high.
- After detection (state S4), the FSM stays latched in that state indefinitely, asserting start_shifting forever until reset.
- Output logic becomes a simple OR of the S4 flip-flop.
- This reduces the combinational logic depth for next state, potentially improving timing (WNS) and reducing power due to fewer glitching transitions in next state logic.
- Area overhead of one-hot FSM is minimal here (only 5 FFs vs 3 FFs) and justified by the power and timing benefits.

Overall, this approach trades a bit of area for improved power and timing, aligned with the provided metrics and design goals.
