Instead of one-hot encoding, use a minimal binary-encoded FSM with explicit state transition logic that checks the input bit to track partial matches of the sequence "1101". The FSM states correspond to the progress in matching the sequence. When the sequence is detected, a sticky output is asserted and the FSM stops transitioning further, remaining in the detected state until reset.

This approach uses fewer flip-flops (3 bits for states), reducing area and static power. The synchronous reset initializes the FSM, and all sequential logic is clocked on posedge clk. The output is registered to avoid glitches and reflect the sticky detection.

The FSM states are:

- S0: no match yet
- S1: matched "1"
- S2: matched "11"
- S3: matched "110"
- S4: matched "1101" (final sticky state)

After reaching S4, FSM stays there until reset.

This binary FSM with registered output balances minimal area with clear logic and avoids combinational output glitches.
