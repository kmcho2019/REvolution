To optimize the FSM for better timing while maintaining minimal area and power, I will switch from binary encoding to one-hot encoding. One-hot encoding reduces combinational logic depth in next-state logic, which typically improves timing (tns and wns) at the expense of slightly more flip-flops. Given the FSM has only 5 states, the area and power overhead will be minimal and likely outweighed by the timing benefits.

The FSM remains Moore style with synchronous active-high reset on clock's positive edge. The output start_shifting is asserted when the FSM enters the detected state (S4) and remains asserted (sticky) until reset.

Next state logic will be combinational with blocking assignments for clarity, with each state represented by a single flip-flop bit, making transitions simple AND/OR conditions.

This approach targets reducing logic depth, improving slack while keeping power and area close to the original.
