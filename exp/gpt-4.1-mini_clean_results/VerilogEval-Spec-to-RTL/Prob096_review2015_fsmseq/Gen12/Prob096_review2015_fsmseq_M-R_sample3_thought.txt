I will refactor the FSM by encoding the states in binary as before, but instead of using a separate always block for next-state logic, I will compute the next state using continuous assign statements combined with a combinational function. This approach reduces the number of always blocks and separates combinational next-state logic from sequential state updates more explicitly. The output will also be registered so that start_shifting asserts only on the clock cycle after detection, improving timing robustness. The reset remains synchronous active high, and the FSM latches in the final detected state until reset. This change keeps the FSM behavior identical but offers a clearer separation of combinational and sequential logic, potentially helping synthesis tools optimize logic cones.
