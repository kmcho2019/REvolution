To optimize the FSM for better power, performance, and area (PPA), I will apply a one-hot encoding scheme for the states. One-hot encoding simplifies next-state logic to a set of simple AND/OR gates, often reducing the combinational path delay, which can improve the worst negative slack (WNS) and critical path timing.

Also, one-hot encoding can simplify output logic as the output can be driven directly by the "detected" state flip-flop. Although it slightly increases the number of flip-flops (5 flip-flops for 5 states), the reduction in combinational logic depth can lower power consumption due to simpler gates and reduce clock-to-Q delay, improving timing.

I will keep the synchronous active-high reset and the output held asserted after detection indefinitely.

This approach trades minor area increase (1 extra flip-flop compared to 3-bit binary encoding) for better timing and potentially reduced dynamic power due to less complex logic. The synchronous reset ensures clean behavior.

The FSM will have states: S0, S1, S2, S3, S4 (each one bit). The next-state logic will use combinational assignments based on current state and input. The output `start_shifting` is asserted directly by the detected state flip-flop.

This should improve WNS and power slightly without a large area penalty.
