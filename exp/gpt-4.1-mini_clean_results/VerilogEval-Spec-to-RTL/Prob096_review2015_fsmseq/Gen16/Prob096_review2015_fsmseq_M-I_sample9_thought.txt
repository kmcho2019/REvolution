To improve the existing FSM design for detecting the sequence 1101 and asserting start_shifting, the focus is on reducing power and potentially improving timing while maintaining or reducing area.

The original design uses a 3-bit binary-encoded FSM with 5 states, which is minimal in area but the next-state logic might cause glitches due to combinational logic transitions.

Improvements:

1. Use one-hot encoding for the FSM states. One-hot encoding increases the number of flip-flops (5 FFs instead of 3), but reduces combinational logic complexity, leading to simpler and faster next-state decoding with fewer glitches, which lowers dynamic power and can improve timing.

2. Hold the detected state (S4) indefinitely to keep start_shifting asserted as before.

3. Keep synchronous active-high reset.

4. Output start_shifting directly from the S4 state flip-flop.

One-hot FSMs typically trade area (more FFs) for better timing and lower dynamic power due to simpler combinational logic and reduced glitching.

Given the very small area of the original design, the area increase is negligible in absolute terms but can reduce power and improve WNS/TNS.

This approach balances area overhead against improved power and performance. The synchronous reset and clean output logic remain unchanged.

The next-state logic will use simple AND/OR of current state and input, avoiding a case statement, which can be more efficient for synthesis and results in fewer glitches.

Overall, this change targets power and performance improvements with minimal impact on area.
