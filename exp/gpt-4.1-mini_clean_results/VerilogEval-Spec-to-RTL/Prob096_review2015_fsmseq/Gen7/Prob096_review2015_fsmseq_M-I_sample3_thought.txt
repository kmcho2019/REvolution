To optimize for power and area while maintaining performance, switch from one-hot to binary state encoding to reduce the number of flip-flops used. This reduces dynamic power and area since fewer FFs toggle. Implement the FSM with a binary-encoded state machine with four states representing the partial matches of "1101" plus one final detected state. Keep the synchronous reset active high and synchronous as required. The FSM remains Moore type with output driven directly from the state register. This reduces FF count from 5 to 3, lowering power and area with minimal impact on critical path delay. The next-state logic and output logic remain simple for good performance.
