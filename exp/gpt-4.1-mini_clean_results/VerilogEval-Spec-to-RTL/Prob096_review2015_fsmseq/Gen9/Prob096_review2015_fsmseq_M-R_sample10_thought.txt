Refactor the FSM by encoding the states in binary instead of one-hot to reduce flip-flop count and simplify the register width, and separate the state update and output logic using a registered flag `detected` that latches high upon sequence detection and stays asserted until reset. The next state logic is implemented combinationally with a case statement, and the `detected` output is driven by a flip-flop updated synchronously with the clock and reset. This approach reduces area by using fewer flip-flops and keeps output registered, improving timing by minimizing wide one-hot comparisons. Reset remains synchronous active high.
