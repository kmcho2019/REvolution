To improve the PPA metrics of the given FSM design, I will apply a one-hot state encoding which often improves timing (reducing clock-to-output delays and improving slack) at the expense of a slight area increase. However, given the FSM has only four states, the area increase is minimal and this often helps timing noticeably. 

Additionally, I will separate the output register update from the state register update logic to reduce combinational logic dependency between state transitions and output signal assertion. The output signal start_shifting will be latched once the sequence is detected and held high until synchronous reset, with minimal toggling thereafter, good for power.

Also, I will explicitly use non-blocking assignments consistently. The FSM logic will remain synchronous with a single clock domain and synchronous reset.

This restructuring improves timing by:
- Using one-hot encoding (which can improve clock-to-output path)
- Separating state update and output register update for cleaner timing paths
- Keeping logic simple and fully synchronous

This should improve the worst negative slack (WNS) and total negative slack (TNS) without significantly increasing area or power.
