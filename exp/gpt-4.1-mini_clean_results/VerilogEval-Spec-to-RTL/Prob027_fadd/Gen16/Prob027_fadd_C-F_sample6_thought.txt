Fuse the modular clarity and reuse from the half-adder based hierarchical approach with the area- and power-efficient direct combinational assignments. To do this, define a concise HalfAdder module for modular clarity and code reuse, but instantiate it just once and implement the remaining logic using direct continuous assignments within TopModule. This hybrid approach leverages modular design benefits while minimizing gate count and intermediate signals by inlining part of the logic. Specifically:

- Use one HalfAdder instance to add inputs a and b, producing an intermediate sum and carry.
- Compute the final sum by XORing the intermediate sum with cin directly (avoiding a second HalfAdder instantiation).
- Compute cout as the OR of the first HalfAdder carry and the AND of intermediate sum and cin (which is the carry-out from the second addition).

This reduces the number of instantiated modules and internal wires compared to two full half adders, reducing area and switching activity while maintaining clarity and correctness. It also keeps modularity for the initial half adder calculation, which can be reused or replaced easily, while flattening the carry logic for efficiency.

This fusion leads to a design with fewer gates and nets than the classic two-half-adder structure, but clearer and more reusable than a fully flattened direct assignment. It balances modularity, area, power, and performance effectively.
