Implement the 256-to-1 4-bit multiplexer using a flattened approach with a single continuous assignment. Instead of building a multi-stage tree of smaller muxes, use the 8-bit selection index to compute the starting bit position in the large input vector and directly select the 4-bit slice from the 1024-bit input vector. This approach relies on the Verilog indexed part-select operator and will synthesize efficiently as a single large multiplexor primitive in modern synthesis tools. It is simple, clean, and reduces intermediate wires and hierarchy, favoring ease of reasoning and minimal RTL overhead at the cost of a potentially longer critical path compared to hierarchical muxes. This suits designs where simplicity and minimal logic overhead are preferred over maximum frequency.

Advantages:
- Minimal RTL complexity and code size.
- No intermediate wires or complex generate loops.
- Direct slicing is intuitive and clean.
- Synthesis tools recognize and optimize large mux directly.
- Suitable for moderate speed requirements or when synthesis optimization is trusted.

Disadvantages:
- Potentially deeper combinational path for high frequency.
- May consume more power due to large mux logic.
- Less modular or reusable compared to hierarchical mux blocks.

This flat mux is a valid alternative to the hierarchical approach, trading off timing for simplicity and compactness.
