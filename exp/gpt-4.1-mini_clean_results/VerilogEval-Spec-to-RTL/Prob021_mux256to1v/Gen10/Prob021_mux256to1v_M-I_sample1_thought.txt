The original design uses a 2-level hierarchy with 16-to-1 multiplexers to build the 256-to-1 4-bit mux. While modular and clear, a 2-level mux tree with 16-to-1 muxes at each stage leads to a relatively large mux input fan-in per module and a 2-level critical path, which may impact timing and power.

To optimize for better power and performance (PPA), I propose to:

1. Use smaller 4-to-1 4-bit multiplexers as building blocks instead of 16-to-1 muxes. This reduces mux input fan-in, which can reduce delay and power in synthesis.
2. Build a 4-level mux tree because 4^4 = 256:
   - Level 1: 64 x 4-to-1 muxes selecting among 4 inputs each (4 * 64 = 256 inputs)
   - Level 2: 16 x 4-to-1 muxes selecting among outputs of Level 1
   - Level 3: 4 x 4-to-1 muxes selecting among outputs of Level 2
   - Level 4: 1 x 4-to-1 mux selecting among outputs of Level 3 to produce final output
3. Partition the 8-bit sel into four 2-bit slices corresponding to each level's mux selector:
   - sel[1:0] for level 1
   - sel[3:2] for level 2
   - sel[5:4] for level 3
   - sel[7:6] for level 4
4. The smaller mux size reduces combinational complexity, potentially improving delay and power.
5. Although this introduces more hierarchy levels (and thus potentially more mux stages in critical path), smaller input fan-in muxes are generally more efficient and easier for synthesis to optimize.
6. The module will remain fully combinational with no registers for simplicity.
7. Use a parameterized 4-to-1 4-bit mux submodule for reuse and clarity.

This hierarchical approach balances fan-in per mux and depth of the mux tree, likely reducing total gate count and power consumption while maintaining reasonable timing.
