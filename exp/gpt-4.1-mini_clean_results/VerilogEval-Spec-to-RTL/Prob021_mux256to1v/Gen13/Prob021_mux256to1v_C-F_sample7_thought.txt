The optimal design fuses the simplicity and power/area efficiency of direct indexed bit slicing (from Example 2's PPA feedback and Example 1's recommendations) with the clarity and manageable hierarchy of the first example’s approach. 

Specifically:
- Use a direct indexed continuous assignment (`out = in[sel*4 +: 4];`) to minimize area, power, and delay by eliminating intermediate mux layers and unpacked arrays.
- This flattens the multiplexer into a single level, avoiding multiple mux stages that increase combinational delay.
- Maintain clear coding style with no unnecessary generate loops or intermediate registers to improve synthesis results.
- Preserve full correctness and clarity by using a simple single continuous assignment, which synthesis tools map efficiently to mux logic.
  
This fusion keeps the performance and power benefits of direct indexing while being structurally straightforward, avoiding large intermediate structures. It is the best tradeoff for PPA and readability.
