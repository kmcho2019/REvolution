The original design uses a hierarchical balanced tree reduction with multiple explicit intermediate levels. To refactor while keeping the core approach, I will flatten the hierarchy by implementing the reductions in fewer explicit stages with larger groupings. Instead of many small intermediate signals and multiple reduction levels, I'll:

- Use reduction operators (&, |, ^) directly on grouped slices of the input, but this time group inputs in 10 groups of 10 bits each (instead of 20 groups of 5 bits).
- Then perform one more reduction stage with those 10 results to get the final output.
- This reduces the number of intermediate signals and levels, trading off some fan-in size for simpler code.
- I will keep all logic in continuous assignments without generate blocks (except one for concise partial group computation).
- This approach keeps a balanced tree feel but reduces code complexity and intermediate signals, enhancing readability and maintainability.

This refactoring still ensures:
- Combinational logic only
- Balanced reduction with a two-stage approach
- Use of reduction operators for clean partial results
- Less verbose, simpler structure with fewer wire arrays
