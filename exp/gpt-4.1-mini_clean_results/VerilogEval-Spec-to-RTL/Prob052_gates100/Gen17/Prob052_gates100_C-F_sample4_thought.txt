Combine the clarity and conciseness of Verilog's built-in reduction operators (from Example 1) with the balanced tree hierarchical approach (from Example 2) for improved timing and power, but with an optimized grouping factor to balance fan-in and logic depth.

We use a 4-level balanced tree with groups of 4 bits at the first level (instead of 5), creating 25 groups, and then reduce these groups pairwise in subsequent levels. This leads to:
- First level: 25 groups of 4 bits each (except last group with the remaining bits)
- Second level: reduce 12 pairs + 1 leftover
- Third level: reduce 6 pairs
- Fourth level: reduce 3 pairs
- Fifth level: final combination

This smaller group size (4 bits) optimizes gate fan-in (4 inputs max at base), giving good timing and area benefits, as fan-in of 4 is often a sweet spot in ASIC libraries.

We also leverage the built-in reduction operators (&, |, ^) for partial groups for cleaner code and synthesis optimization, and use generate loops for modularity.

This hierarchical reduction tree improves timing by limiting gate fan-in, reduces glitches and switching power through balanced trees, and maintains manageable area through modular grouping.

Finally, we unify the three reduction trees (AND, OR, XOR) in parallel but keep them separate for correctness and clarity.

This fusion offers a structured, scalable, and efficient design that improves over both examples in readability, PPA, and synthesizability.
