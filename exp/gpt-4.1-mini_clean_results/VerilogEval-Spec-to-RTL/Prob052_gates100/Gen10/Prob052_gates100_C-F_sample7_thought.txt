To fuse the strengths of both examples, we combine the clarity and control of explicit iterative accumulation with the efficiency of hierarchical grouping to optimize performance and potentially reduce power and area.

Specifically:
- Use a hierarchical reduction approach dividing 100 inputs into 10 groups of 10 bits each.
- Within each group, use built-in Verilog reduction operators for concise, efficient partial reductions.
- Combine these intermediate results using built-in reduction operators again to produce final outputs.
- Implement this in a purely combinational always block for clarity and to explicitly show grouping.
- This reduces the large fan-in problem by breaking down the 100-input logic into smaller chunks.
- Using reduction operators at the group level leverages synthesis optimization for balanced trees.
- This approach is a middle ground between manual loops and direct full-vector reductions, improving PPA by limiting gate fan-in depth and encouraging sharing.

This design explicitly partitions the input vector, making the structure clear and potentially improving synthesis quality compared to a fully flat reduction or a fully loop-accumulated approach.
