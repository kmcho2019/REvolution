Instead of using Verilog's reduction operators directly, this design manually implements the three 100-input gates using hierarchical binary-tree style logic combining pairs of bits iteratively. This structure enables synthesis tools to map the logic into balanced trees, potentially improving timing by reducing the logic depth compared to a flat reduction operator on 100 bits.

The approach uses generate-for loops and intermediate wires to build balanced AND, OR, and XOR trees with log2(100) levels, combining the inputs in pairs until one result remains per output. This explicit tree structure also makes it easy to modify or expand, and to analyze the circuit depth.

Outputs are assigned from the final wire level. The design is fully combinational, with no registers or sequential logic, and uses only wire nets.

This novel approach shows how a large multi-input gate can be architected as a balanced tree of smaller gates, giving designers more control over the logic structure.
