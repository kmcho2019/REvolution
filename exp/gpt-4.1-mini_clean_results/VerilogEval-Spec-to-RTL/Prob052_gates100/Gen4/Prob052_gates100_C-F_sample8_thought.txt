To fuse the best ideas from both solutions, we retain the hierarchical reduction approach from Example 2 for balanced tree benefits and improved timing, while leveraging Verilog's reduction operators from Example 1 for clean, concise partial reduction expressions.

We implement a binary-tree style hierarchical reduction with two levels:
- First, split the 100 inputs into 20 groups of 5 bits each, which balances fan-in at first level more finely than 10 groups of 10 bits.
- Then, reduce the 20 intermediate results in a balanced binary tree manner with pairs at the second level, and finally combine the last 2 results.

This approach reduces gate fan-in at each stage to 5 or 2 inputs, improving timing by avoiding very large fan-in gates.

We use generate loops for partial reduction of 5-bit groups with reduction operators (&, |, ^), then combine pairs of those with simple bitwise operations for the second level, and finalize the output with a few gate levels.

This fusion offers:
- The succinctness and clarity of reduction operators
- Balanced tree reduction to minimize delay and switching activity (reducing power)
- Smaller fan-in gates in all stages for better area and timing
- Minimal intermediate signals for readability and efficiency

Overall, this is a clear, modular, and hardware-efficient design suitable for synthesis with good PPA characteristics.
