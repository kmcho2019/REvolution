To fuse the best ideas from both examples, we combine clarity, structured hierarchical reduction, and the efficiency of built-in reduction operators.

From Example 1, we see a straightforward explicit loop-based reduction inside a combinational always block, showing clear stepwise aggregation, but it is less optimal for PPA.

From Example 2, hierarchical grouping breaks the 100 inputs into manageable blocks (10 groups of 10), allowing a modular reduction approach which can reduce fan-in and improve timing and power.

However, Example 2 uses registers and nested loops, which add area and complexity.

Our fusion strategy:

- Use hierarchical grouping as in Example 2 to partition the 100-bit input into 10 groups of 10 bits.
- For each group, use Verilog’s built-in reduction operators (&, |, ^) for fast, clean partial reductions instead of manual loops.
- Then, apply built-in reduction operators again on the 10 partial results to get the final output signals.
- This approach blends hierarchical reduction for PPA benefits and built-in operators for synthesis efficiency.
- Use continuous assignments for outputs instead of reg to emphasize combinational nature and allow straightforward synthesis.
- The design will be clean, structurally hierarchical, minimal code, and optimized for synthesis tools.

This fusion provides a balanced, optimized, and clear solution that is superior in PPA to either example alone.
