To improve the PPA (Power, Performance, Area) while keeping the design clean and synthesizable, the following strategy is applied:

1. **Reduce the number of intermediate wires**:
   - Instead of multiple hierarchical levels with many intermediate signals, use a balanced tree reduction approach with fewer stages.
   - Combine partial reductions with larger groups at once to minimize the total number of logic levels, reducing delay.

2. **Uniform grouping for all outputs simultaneously**:
   - Since all outputs are reduction operations on the same input vector, perform the grouping once and share those intermediate signals for AND, OR, and XOR reductions. This reduces redundant hardware.

3. **Balance between fan-in and levels**:
   - Use groups of 10 bits for the first level (10 groups), which reduces the number of intermediate signals drastically compared to groups of 5.
   - Then do a second level of reduction on the 10 intermediate results down to a final output.
   - The maximum fan-in is 10, reasonable for modern synthesis tools, while the reduction depth is minimized.

4. **Minimize logic levels**:
   - Two-level tree leads to at most two combinational levels per output, reducing critical path delay.
   - XOR reduction might still have longer delay, but this two-level balanced tree is a practical tradeoff.

5. **Use reduction operators consistently**:
   - Use built-in reduction operators (&, |, ^) for concise synthesis-friendly expressions.

Overall, this approach reduces wire count, logic levels, and fan-in, likely improving power and area by reducing redundant logic and signal transitions, and improving timing by fewer combinational stages.
