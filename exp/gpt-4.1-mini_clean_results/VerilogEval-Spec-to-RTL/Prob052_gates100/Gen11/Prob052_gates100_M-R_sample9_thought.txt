To refactor the design while maintaining the same functionality, I will replace the always block with continuous assign statements using Verilog's built-in reduction operators. This approach is more concise and typically synthesizes to more optimal hardware in terms of timing, area, and power because the synthesis tool can efficiently map these operators to balanced gate trees.

The outputs can be declared as wires (instead of reg), and the combinational logic is expressed using direct assignments with reduction operators: '&' for AND, '|' for OR, and '^' for XOR over the entire 100-bit input vector.

This structure:
- Eliminates the explicit for-loop and procedural block.
- Simplifies the code and potentially improves PPA.
- Retains clear readability and functionality.

It's a classic and recommended way to implement multi-input logic reductions in Verilog.
