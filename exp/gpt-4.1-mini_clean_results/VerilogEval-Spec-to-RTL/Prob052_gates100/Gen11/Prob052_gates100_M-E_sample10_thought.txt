To provide a novel architectural approach different from direct reduction operators or simple for-loop accumulation, I will implement a hierarchical tree reduction structure for the AND, OR, and XOR computations. This mimics how hardware reduction trees are often built to optimize speed and balance the fan-in.

Specifically, I will:
- Break the 100-bit input vector into smaller groups (e.g., groups of 10 bits).
- Compute partial AND, OR, XOR results for each group using built-in reduction operators.
- Then iteratively combine these partial results in a tree-like fashion until a single final output per function is obtained.

This hierarchical approach explicitly models a balanced multi-level reduction tree, which helps illustrate a hardware-friendly structure. Although synthesis tools often infer such trees from direct reductions, writing it out explicitly as a multi-stage module showcases a systematic methodology.

All logic will be combinational with no registers; outputs update immediately on input changes. This also reduces fan-in per gate level, which can improve timing and scalability for very wide inputs.

In summary:
- Stage 1: Compute partial results on small input chunks.
- Stage 2+: Combine partial results hierarchically until final outputs.
- Outputs: Final AND, OR, XOR signals after full reduction.
- Entire design in a single module for compactness.
