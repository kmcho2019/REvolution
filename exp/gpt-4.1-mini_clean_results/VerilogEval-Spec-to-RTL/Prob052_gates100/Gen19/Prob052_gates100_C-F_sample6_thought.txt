To fuse the best ideas from both examples, implement the TopModule using purely combinational continuous assignments with built-in Verilog reduction operators for AND, OR, and XOR. This method is succinct, clean, and allows synthesis tools to optimally map the large fan-in gates into efficient logic trees, improving power, area, and potentially performance.

However, to maintain clarity and explicitness favored in the examples, we can declare the outputs as wire and use continuous assignments rather than an always block with reg outputs. This approach reduces unnecessary procedural coding while leveraging the synthesis advantages of reduction operators.

Thus, the fusion strategy is:
- Use wire outputs with continuous assignment.
- Use built-in reduction operators (&, |, ^) for concise, optimized reduction.
- Avoid procedural loops and reg declarations to keep code simple and clean.
- Retain purely combinational behavior with no registers or clocks.

This fusion combines the clarity and synthesis friendliness of built-in operators with the purely combinational continuous assignment style for a superior, efficient RTL code.
