To implement the 100-input AND, OR, and XOR gates differently, I will create a hierarchical reduction using a balanced tree structure explicitly in Verilog. Specifically, I will break the 100 inputs into smaller groups (e.g., groups of 10), compute partial results within those groups, and then combine those partial results to produce the final outputs. This approach explicitly shows the intermediate reduction stages and the hierarchical nature of large fan-in gates.

This design:
- Uses an explicit two-level reduction tree instead of a flat reduction operator.
- Enhances synthesis visibility into tree-based logic, which may help tools map the logic better if needed.
- Maintains purely combinational logic with zero clock latency.
- Improves modularity and code structure by using generate loops and local wires.

This also provides a different architectural style from the flat reduction operator approach, potentially useful for manual timing or power optimization, while still functionally equivalent.

The logic:
- Divide 100 inputs into 10 groups of 10 bits.
- Compute 10 partial AND, OR, and XOR results from each group.
- Then combine these 10 partial results into the final output signals using similar logic.

This hierarchical tree approach is more explicit about the structure of the reduction and suitable for illustrating a novel architectural style.
