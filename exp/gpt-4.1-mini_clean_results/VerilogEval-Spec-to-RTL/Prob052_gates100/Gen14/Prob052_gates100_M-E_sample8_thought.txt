Implement the 100-input AND, OR, and XOR outputs using a pipelined tree of 10-input reduction blocks that each reduce 10 inputs down to one bit per operation using Verilog reduction operators. We first break down the 100 inputs into 10 groups of 10 bits each, performing reduction on these groups in parallel.

Next, for the resulting 10 intermediate bits per output, use a balanced 10-input tree reduction to combine them into the final output. The second-level reduction uses 5-input groups: split the 10 bits into 2 groups of 5 bits each, and reduce these using the same Verilog reduction operators.

Finally, combine the two second-level reduction results for each output into the final output with simple 2-input gates.

This approach provides:
- Simplicity and readability through hierarchical grouping by 10-bit chunks, easier to reason about.
- Balanced and small fan-in gates at each stage: max fan-in is 10 for first reduction, then 5, then 2.
- Reuse of Verilog reduction operators at leaf groups for compact and efficient synthesis.
- Reduced intermediate nets compared to previous wide fan-in trees by structuring the reduction in 3 stages.
- The small groups enable synthesizers to optimize gates and balance timing well.

Though pipelining is not implemented here (since outputs are combinational), the staged reduction idea mimics pipelining by limiting gate fan-in and could be adapted for pipeline easily.

Overall, the design is modular, easy to verify, and suited for high fan-in logic with good PPA.
