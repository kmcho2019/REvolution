The original solution is already concise and uses Verilog reduction operators which synthesize efficiently. However, for large input vectors, synthesizing a single 100-input AND/OR/XOR gate may lead to a deep logic tree and longer delay.

To optimize for better performance (shorter critical path) and possibly reduce power, we can implement the reductions hierarchically by splitting the 100-bit input into smaller groups (e.g., 10 groups of 10 bits each), reducing each group first, then combining the group results. This reduces gate fan-in and can create a balanced tree structure for faster logic evaluation.

This approach may increase area slightly due to intermediate signals, but can reduce delay and improve timing slack, and potentially reduce power by lowering fan-in complexity.

The code will define wires for partial reductions and then combine them.

We implement:
- Partial AND of 10-bit groups (10 groups)
- Final AND of the 10 partial AND results
Similarly for OR and XOR.

This hierarchical reduction approach strikes a good balance between complexity, performance, and power.
