Since the one-hot encoding means only one state bit is high at a time, the next state bits can be simplified by combining conditions directly without intermediate signals or factoring the input signal repeatedly. Each next_state bit is the OR of its originating states masked by input or its complement, but we can rewrite directly based on the transitions:

- next_state[0] (A) = (A & ~in) | (C & ~in) = ~in & (A | C)
- next_state[1] (B) = (A & in) | (B & in) | (D & in) = in & (A | B | D)
- next_state[2] (C) = (B & ~in) | (D & ~in) = ~in & (B | D)
- next_state[3] (D) = (C & in)

Since the logic is already minimal, the simplest is to directly assign these expressions without intermediate wires or more signals, keeping the code compact while preserving clarity.

Output is just D, the current state bit for D.

This reduces logic lines to minimal and avoids unnecessary wires.
