Since only one state bit is high at a time, we can simplify next_state expressions by leveraging this one-hot property and reducing repeated terms. For example, `next_state[1] = in & (A | B | D)`, which is simpler and equivalent. Also, express next_state[0] as `(A | C) & ~in` and next_state[2] as `(B | D) & ~in`. The output is simply `out = D`. This yields cleaner, minimal logic expressions with the same functionality.
