Instead of using multiple OR gates for intermediate signals, implement the next_state logic by exploiting the one-hot property: exactly one bit of 'state' is set at a time. This allows direct ANDing of input conditions with current state bits without combining states beforehand.

For each next_state bit, directly list the transitions:

- next_state[0] (A): from A or C when in=0 → (state[0] & ~in) | (state[2] & ~in)
- next_state[1] (B): from A or B or D when in=1 → (state[0] & in) | (state[1] & in) | (state[3] & in)
- next_state[2] (C): from B or D when in=0 → (state[1] & ~in) | (state[3] & ~in)
- next_state[3] (D): from C when in=1 → state[2] & in

This eliminates intermediate OR signals, reducing logic depth and possibly power.

The output remains the D state bit.

This direct approach is conceptually simpler and should map well to hardware with minimal gates.
