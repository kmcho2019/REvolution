To optimize the next state logic for better PPA while preserving clarity and correctness, factor common terms to minimize gate levels and reduce the number of logic operations. Since the state signals are mutually exclusive one-hot encodings, use AND terms gated by the current state and input, and then OR these terms. This reduces redundant logic evaluations and deep logic trees, improving timing and power.

Specifically:
- For next_state[0] (A), it transitions from A and C when in=0. So next_state[0] = ~in & (A | C).
- For next_state[1] (B), it is reached from A (if in=1), from B (regardless of in=1), and from D (if in=1). Rewrite as in & A + B + in & D.
- For next_state[2] (C), it is reached from B and D when in=0: next_state[2] = ~in & (B | D).
- For next_state[3] (D), only from C when in=1: next_state[3] = in & C.

This structure uses fewer large OR gates by explicitly separating terms, which helps synthesize smaller and shallower logic. The output remains just the D state bit.

This approach yields minimal gate levels and less switching for inactive states, thus improving timing, power, and area.
