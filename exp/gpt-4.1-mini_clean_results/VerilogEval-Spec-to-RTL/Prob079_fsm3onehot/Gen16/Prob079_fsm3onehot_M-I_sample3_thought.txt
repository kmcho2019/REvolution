To optimize for Power, Area, and timing, we can factor the next_state logic expressions to reduce the total number of AND gates and shared inputs. Since 'in' is common in many terms, factoring it out reduces redundancy and gate count. Also, since states are one-hot encoded and mutually exclusive, using ORs of fewer terms improves logic simplification.

Specifically:
- next_state[0] = A&~in + C&~in = (A + C) & ~in
- next_state[1] = (A & in) + (B & in) + (D & in) = (A + B + D) & in
- next_state[2] = (B & ~in) + (D & ~in) = (B + D) & ~in
- next_state[3] = C & in

This factoring reduces the number of AND gates and simplifies wiring, improving power and area slightly without affecting timing. The output logic remains the same.

This approach maintains clarity and directly corresponds to the state transition table, with minimal logic depth and gate count.
