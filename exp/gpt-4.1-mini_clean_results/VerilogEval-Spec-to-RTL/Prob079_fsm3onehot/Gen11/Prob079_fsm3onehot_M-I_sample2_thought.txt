The existing solution is already minimal and clean, but we can optimize for better power and area by factoring common terms to reduce the number of gates and fan-in, which also can improve performance.

In the transition logic:

- next_state[0]: (~in & (state[0] | state[2])) can be written as one AND gate with one OR gate input, reducing gates.
- next_state[1]: (in & (state[0] | state[1] | state[3])) similarly, factor 'in'.
- next_state[2]: (~in & (state[1] | state[3])) also factor.
- next_state[3]: (in & state[2]) is simple.

This factoring reduces redundant AND gates and the number of inputs to OR gates, lowering gate count and switching.

The output remains simply state[3].

This approach reduces logic fan-in and gate count, which typically lowers switching power and area, while preserving correct functionality and possibly improving timing.

Thus, the main improvement is factoring the terms to share the input 'in' or '~in' signals across ORed states, yielding a cleaner and more area/power-efficient solution.
