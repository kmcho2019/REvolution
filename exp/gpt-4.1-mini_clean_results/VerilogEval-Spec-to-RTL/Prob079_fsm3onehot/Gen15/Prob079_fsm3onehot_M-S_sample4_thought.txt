Since the states are one-hot encoded and mutually exclusive, we can simplify the next_state logic by factoring common terms and combining conditions. For example, next_state[1] is active when in=1 and any of A, B, or D is active, so it can be assigned as in & (A | B | D). Similarly, next_state[0] is active when in=0 and A or C is active. Next_state[2] is active when in=0 and B or D is active. Next_state[3] is active only when in=1 and C is active. The output is simply the D state bit. This reduces redundancy and gate count while preserving the logic.
