The original function z = (x ^ y) & x can be algebraically simplified to z = x & ~y, which reduces logic complexity. Implementing this simplification directly as a continuous assignment eliminates the need for separate XOR and AND gate modules, thereby reducing gate count and power consumption, and saving area.

Since the signals are single-bit, parameterization and modular submodules are unnecessary overhead. The simplified, flat implementation offers better power, area, and possibly performance due to fewer gates and transitions.

This approach directly targets optimal PPA by leveraging Boolean simplification and minimal logic in a clean, straightforward RTL description.
