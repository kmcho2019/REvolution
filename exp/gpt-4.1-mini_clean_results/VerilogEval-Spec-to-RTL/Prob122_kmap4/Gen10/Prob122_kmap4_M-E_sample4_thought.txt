Instead of directly implementing the parity function as XORs of pairs and then their result, we can exploit the symmetrical structure of the Karnaugh map by identifying and grouping minterms.

Observing the K-map, the output is 1 when exactly one or three of the inputs are 1, i.e., it behaves like a parity function. Another approach is to express the output as the sum of minterms where the number of 1's in input bits is odd.

A different novel approach is to use the combinational logic of sums-of-products (SOP) by explicitly enumerating minterms with output 1, then simplifying using XOR and AND gates.

Alternatively, we can exploit the pattern that out = (~a & ~b & c & d) | (~a & b & ~c & ~d) | (a & ~b & ~c & d) | (a & b & c & ~d) | ... but this becomes cumbersome.

Instead, we can implement the parity function by using a cascaded XOR operation for each input sequentially, which is still different structurally from the previous two-stage XOR.

The idea:

- Use a 4-input XOR gate implemented as nested XORs: (((a ^ b) ^ c) ^ d).
- In Verilog, implement this by a chain of assign statements or in one line.
- This is a minimal-depth implementation but shows a structural difference from the previous approach by explicitly chaining the XOR operations.

This approach avoids intermediate wires and uses a single assign statement chaining XORs, clearly indicating sequential XORing.

This provides a structurally novel solution with minimal hardware and clear implementation.
