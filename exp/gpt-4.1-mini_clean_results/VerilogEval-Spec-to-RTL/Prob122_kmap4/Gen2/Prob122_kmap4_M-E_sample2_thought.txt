The Karnaugh map pattern corresponds to a parity-like function with a twist: the output toggles for each combination where the sum of inputs a, b, c, and d modulo 2 equals 1. Since the pattern is symmetrical and repeats diagonally, it can be recognized that the output equals the XOR of inputs a and c XORed with the XOR of inputs b and d. This leads to a novel two-stage XOR approach: first XOR pairs (a with c, and b with d), then XOR those results to get the final output. This alternative architecture splits the function into smaller XOR gates in stages, which can help with modular design and possibly facilitate pipelining or staged logic optimizations.
