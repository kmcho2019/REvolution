Instead of directly implementing the XOR function as a single expression, we can decompose the output into two intermediate XOR terms corresponding to the rows (c,d) and columns (a,b). Specifically, compute the parity of (c,d) and the parity of (a,b) separately, and then XOR these two results. This matches the output pattern since the output is 1 when the parity of the combined four inputs is odd, which is equivalent to the XOR of parity(c,d) and parity(a,b).

This approach modularizes the logic and can be extended if the inputs were wider, enabling reuse of parity blocks. It also clarifies the design by separating row and column parity computations.
