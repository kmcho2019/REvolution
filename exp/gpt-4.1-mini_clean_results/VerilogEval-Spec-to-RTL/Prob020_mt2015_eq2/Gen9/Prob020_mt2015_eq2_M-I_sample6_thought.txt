The original solution using the '==' operator is already minimal and optimal for such a small input size, synthesizing into a handful of simple gates. However, to potentially reduce power and area even more in a gate-level sense, we can manually implement the equality comparison using bitwise XNORs followed by an AND. This can sometimes result in slightly smaller or more power-efficient gate-level implementations because it directly represents the minimal equality logic and might allow the synthesis tool to optimize transistor-level implementations better. It also avoids the high-level equality operator that could be interpreted in different ways by tools. Since inputs are just 2 bits, this approach is simple and possibly yields marginal improvements in gate count and power while maintaining zero delay and clear combinational logic.
