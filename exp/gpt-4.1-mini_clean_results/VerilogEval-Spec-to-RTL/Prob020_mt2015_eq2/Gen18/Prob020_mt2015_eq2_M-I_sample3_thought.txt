Given the simplicity of the equality check and the already minimal logic, the existing implementation using the `==` operator is optimal for both area and performance. The power consumption is already very low due to minimal switching and gates.

To further optimize power slightly, especially in a real hardware scenario, we can gate the output to zero when inputs are stable or use clock gating in a larger system. But as a standalone combinational module, no further significant improvements are feasible.

Therefore, I will keep the concise `assign z = (A == B);` statement which synthesizes into minimal logic, ensuring the best balance of readability, area, and performance.
