The original design is already optimal for such a simple 2-bit equality comparator implemented in combinational logic using the equality operator. The continuous assignment is clear and synthesizes to minimal hardware. Since the PPA metrics are excellent with zero timing slack and minimal area and power, no logical changes can improve performance or area.

However, a slight stylistic improvement is to remove the redundant ternary operator because the equality operator (==) already returns a 1-bit value suitable for direct assignment. Assigning `z = (A == B);` is simpler, reduces generated logic slightly by not introducing a redundant mux, and clarifies the design intent more directly.

This does not affect PPA significantly but is a more idiomatic and efficient way to express the comparison in Verilog, potentially yielding marginal area and power benefits by eliminating the conditional operator. This is the best optimization at this level without changing the function.
