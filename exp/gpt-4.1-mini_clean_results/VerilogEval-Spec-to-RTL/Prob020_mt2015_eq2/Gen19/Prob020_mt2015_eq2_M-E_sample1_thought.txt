An alternative approach is to compare the two 2-bit inputs A and B by encoding them into one-hot signals and then checking if they match via a simple combinational logic.

Specifically, decode A and B into one-hot 4-bit vectors (each bit representing one of the four possible 2-bit values). If the decoded one-hot vectors are identical (i.e., have the same active bit), then A equals B.

This method converts equality checking into a one-hot comparison, which can be implemented via bitwise AND and a reduction OR to check if exactly one matching bit is set.

This design showcases a different style of implementing equality without directly using equality or bitwise XNOR on the inputs, instead relying on decoder logic and comparison of one-hot codes.
