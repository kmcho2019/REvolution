To fuse the strengths of both examples, we aim for the simplicity and readability of using the equality operator (==) while retaining the clarity of explicit bitwise operations. The equality operator directly expresses the intended comparison and lets synthesis tools generate minimal and efficient logic. However, explicitly showing the bitwise XNOR and AND gates can sometimes aid synthesis in simpler, small-width cases.

Our fusion strategy is to use the equality operator for clarity and let synthesis handle optimization, but also ensure the output is a single bit by explicitly casting the result to 1-bit (although in Verilog the comparison yields 1-bit by default). This provides code readability and optimal synthesis mapping.

Overall, the fused approach favors the direct equality operator for succinctness and minimal logic with guaranteed functional correctness and optimal PPA characteristics for small bit-widths.
