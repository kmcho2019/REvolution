To implement a different architectural approach for the equality check, I will compare corresponding bits of A and B individually using XOR gates, then combine their outputs with an OR gate. Since XOR outputs 0 when inputs are equal, I will invert the XOR outputs and then AND them to produce 'z'. In other words, z = (~(A[1]^B[1])) & (~(A[0]^B[0])) = !( (A[1]^B[1]) | (A[0]^B[0]) ).

This design uses basic bitwise operations instead of the equality operator. It explicitly illustrates how bitwise equivalence is derived from XOR and AND gates, providing an educational and different hardware structure while still being synthesizable and efficient.
