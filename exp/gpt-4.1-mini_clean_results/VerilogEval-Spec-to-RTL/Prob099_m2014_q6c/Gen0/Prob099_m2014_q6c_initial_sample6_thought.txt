The state machine has 6 states A-F, one-hot encoded as y[5:0]. The transitions are given based on input 0 or 1 (input w here). We want to implement next-state logic for signals Y1 and Y3, corresponding to y[1] and y[3] (which correspond to states B and D). We are to derive the next state signals corresponding to y[1] and y[3] by inspecting the transitions.

Since one-hot encoding is used, each bit corresponds to a state:
y[0] = A, y[1] = B, y[2] = C, y[3] = D, y[4] = E, y[5] = F.

We analyze transitions from all states to see when next state will be B (y[1]) and D (y[3]):

Next state B (y[1]):
- From A (y[0]) with input=0 --> B
- From C (y[2]) with input=1 --> D (not B)
- From no other transitions to B except from A with w=0

So, next y[1] = (y[0] & ~w)

Next state D (y[3]):
- From B (y[1]) with w=1 --> D
- From C (y[2]) with w=1 --> D
- From E (y[4]) with w=1 --> D
- From F (y[5]) with w=1 --> D
- From D (y[3]) with w=1 --> A (not D)

So next y[3] = (y[1] & w) | (y[2] & w) | (y[4] & w) | (y[5] & w)
= w & (y[1] | y[2] | y[4] | y[5])

The module will have inputs y[5:0] and w, outputs Y1 and Y3, which are the next-state bits for y[1] and y[3].

Reset to state A means y = 6'b000001 on reset (not used in combinational logic here).

Implement next state logic for Y1 and Y3 as derived.
