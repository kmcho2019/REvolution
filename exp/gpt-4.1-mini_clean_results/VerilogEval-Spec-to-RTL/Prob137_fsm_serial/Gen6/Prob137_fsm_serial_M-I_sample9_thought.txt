To optimize the FSM for better power, performance, and area (PPA), we switch from one-hot to binary state encoding. This reduces flip-flop usage from 4 to 2 for the states, saving area. We keep the FSM logic straightforward to maintain good timing.

We add a clock enable signal to the shift register and bit counter updates, so they only toggle in the RECEIVE state, reducing unnecessary switching and power.

We move done generation to the sequential block and keep it asserted only for one cycle after a valid stop bit.

The error recovery logic remains the same: waiting for a valid stop bit before returning to IDLE.

Overall, this reduces area and power, while maintaining good timing and functionality.
