To optimize the FSM for better power, performance, and area (PPA), remove the unused shift register to reduce area and switching activity, as the problem only requires detecting correct byte reception and generating a done pulse, not storing data.

Also, fix the bit-shifting direction so the LSB-first convention is properly handled by shifting right and inserting the new bit at MSB (bit 7), which represents receiving bits from LSB to MSB order sequentially. This makes the design semantically clearer and aligns with typical UART reception semantics.

Keep the done pulse generation synchronous and one-cycle wide as in the original, but generate it explicitly on the transition from CHECK_STOP with a correct stop bit.

Use a single always block for sequential logic to reduce control complexity and switching overhead.

Maintain synchronous reset and binary-encoded 2-bit FSM states to keep area low.

In WAIT_STOP state, avoid unnecessary resetting of bit counter every cycle since bit_count is only meaningful in RECEIVE, further reducing toggle.

Overall, by removing the shift register and clarifying bit count and done logic, area is reduced and toggling minimized, while performance remains good due to simple FSM and minimal logic depth.
