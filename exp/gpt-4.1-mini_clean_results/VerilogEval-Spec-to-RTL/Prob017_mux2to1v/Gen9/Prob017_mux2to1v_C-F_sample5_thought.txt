To create a superior 100-bit 2-to-1 mux, we combine the modular clarity and hierarchical structure from Example 1 with the minimalistic, vectorized mux logic from Example 2.

Specifically:
- Use a single parameterized vector mux module with clean vectorized assignment for optimal synthesis of combinational mux logic.
- Partition the 100-bit vectors into a moderate number of chunks that balance instance overhead and synthesis optimization. Instead of 5 chunks of 20 bits or 10 chunks of 10 bits, use 4 chunks of 25 bits each.
  This reduces instance count further (only 4 instead of 5 or 10), which lowers wiring complexity and module instantiation overhead.
- Each chunk mux handles 25 bits with a simple vector mux assignment (sel?b:a).
- The top module concatenates the outputs of the chunk muxes for the final 100-bit output.

This fusion strategy:
- Minimizes module instance overhead (4 instead of 5 or 10 instances)
- Maintains good modularity and clarity for maintainability and future scaling
- Uses vectorized mux assignments inside submodules for minimal gate-level logic
- Enables synthesis tools to optimize chunk size appropriately
- Potentially reduces routing complexity and power due to fewer module boundaries compared to smaller chunks

In sum, this design balances performance, power, and area (PPA) effectively, fusing modular and flat vectorized approaches with balanced chunk sizing.
