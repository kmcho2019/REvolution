To combine the strengths of both example solutions, the new design will implement the 2-to-1 mux as a single vectorized conditional assignment without generate loops or hierarchical muxing. This merges the clarity and simplicity of a vector mux (Example 1's insight on simplifying hierarchy) with the modular clarity and synthesis friendliness of Example 2, but without unnecessary per-bit generate blocks.

This approach yields:

- **Performance:** A single vector mux expression (`assign out = sel ? b : a;`) enables synthesis tools to create the most optimized wide multiplexer structure, minimizing logic depth and improving timing compared to hierarchical or bitwise muxes.

- **Power:** Avoiding intermediate nets and per-bit muxes reduces signal switching overhead and fanout, lowering dynamic power.

- **Area:** Removing generate loops and hierarchical instantiations lets synthesis share mux logic efficiently, resulting in smaller area.

Furthermore, this style remains highly readable, clean, and straightforward for verification and maintenance.

In summary, this fusion retains correctness and modularity while maximizing PPA by leveraging a single vector-level mux expression without extra hierarchies or generate blocks.
