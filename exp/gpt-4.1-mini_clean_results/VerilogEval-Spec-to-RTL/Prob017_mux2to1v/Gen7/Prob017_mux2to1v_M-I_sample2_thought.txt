The existing design is already optimal for area and performance because it uses a single vectorized conditional assignment implementing a 100-bit 2:1 mux without hierarchy or duplication.

To improve power without compromising performance or area significantly, we can implement the output with a bitwise masking approach instead of the ternary operator. This can sometimes help synthesis tools map mux logic to AND-OR structures, potentially reducing glitching and switching power due to selective gating.

Specifically, implement:
  out = (~{100{sel}} & a) | ({100{sel}} & b);

This replaces the single conditional operator with bitwise ANDs and OR, explicitly controlling signal switching paths.

Although this change is subtle, it can reduce internal signal transitions and dynamic power, especially in large buses, while maintaining the same logic functionality and timing behavior.

This approach preserves the single-level combinational assignment, maintaining minimal area and simple timing paths.

No instantiation of individual bit muxes is done to keep area and routing complexity low.

Thus, the key improvement is replacing the ternary operator with explicit masking logic for better power efficiency.
