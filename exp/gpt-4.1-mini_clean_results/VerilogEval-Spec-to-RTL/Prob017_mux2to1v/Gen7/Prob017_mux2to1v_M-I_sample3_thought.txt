The original design is modular but instantiates multiple smaller muxes, which adds unnecessary routing and area overhead for a straightforward vector mux. Since the operation is a simple 2-to-1 selection on wide vectors, a single-level 100-bit-wide ternary operator is more efficient. This reduces instantiation overhead and enables synthesis tools to optimize mux implementation globally, improving area and power.

Hence, I will flatten the mux hierarchy and implement the 2-to-1 mux with one continuous assignment using the built-in Verilog ternary operator on the entire 100-bit vector. This approach minimizes area (no extra module instantiations), potentially reduces power by eliminating intermediate logic stages, and keeps the design fully combinational and clean.

This aligns with typical RTL design practices where wide vector muxes are coded directly, letting synthesis handle optimization at the vector level.

No change in functionality; just a cleaner, more optimal coding style.
