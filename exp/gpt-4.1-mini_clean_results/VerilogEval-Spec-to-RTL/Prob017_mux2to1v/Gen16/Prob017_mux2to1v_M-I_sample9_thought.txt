To improve PPA, focus on reducing power since timing and area are already optimal for a simple mux. The direct combinational mux is minimal in logic depth and area, so the main opportunity is to reduce unnecessary toggling caused by the output switching every time 'sel' changes. 

A common technique is to register the output with a clock to reduce glitching and power, but since no clock is provided, we cannot do that here.

Alternatively, since the mux chooses between two large vectors, gating the selection control signal itself to avoid glitches or glitches propagation can be beneficial if 'sel' changes asynchronously or glitches occur, but this is usually handled at higher-level logic.

Given these constraints and no clock or enable signals, the existing direct vector-wide ternary assignment is the best for minimal area, delay, and power.

Hence, the current design is already optimal; no changes improve PPA significantly.

One minor improvement is to mark the Mux2to1 module outputs and inputs explicitly as wire to match good practices, but this is a style change only.

Therefore, I will retain the same architecture, confirming the clean parameterized mux and top module for maintainability and scalability.

In conclusion: The original solution is already optimal for PPA given the problem requirements and available signals.
