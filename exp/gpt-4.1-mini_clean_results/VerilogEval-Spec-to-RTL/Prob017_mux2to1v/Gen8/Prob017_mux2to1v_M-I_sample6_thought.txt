The existing solution is already optimal for a wide 2-1 mux by using a single vectorized conditional assignment. However, to slightly improve power and possibly timing, we can:

1. Use a `wire` intermediate signal for sel to emphasize it's stable and used as control.
2. Avoid the ternary operator, replacing it with a bitwise operation-based mux to make the mux explicitly implemented as `out = (a & ~sel) | (b & sel)`. Some synthesis tools optimize this better and it can lead to more balanced transistor-level mux implementation.
3. Remove the unused BitMux2to1 module to reduce confusion and clarify the top module is a flat mux.

This bitwise approach can marginally improve power by avoiding conditional operator logic and letting synthesis tools infer a straightforward multiplexer made of AND/OR gates, potentially minimizing glitching. Since performance and area are already good, gains will be subtle but possibly measurable in low-power or timing-critical contexts.

This remains purely combinational and synthesizable, preserves the clear interface, and is compatible with all synthesis tools.
