The original design used 10 separate 10-bit mux submodules, which introduces extra module boundaries and possible routing overhead. Since the operation is a simple 2-to-1 mux on a 100-bit vector, flattening the design into a single continuous vector-level mux should reduce area and power by enabling synthesis tools to optimize the logic more effectively. Using a single assign with a vector-wide ternary operator reduces interconnect and routing complexity, minimizing switching nodes and possibly decreasing power. This approach keeps the code simple, clean, and purely combinational, providing better PPA (power, performance, area) than the hierarchical approach.
