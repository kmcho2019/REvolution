The original design uses a hierarchical approach instantiating ten 10-bit mux modules, which adds unnecessary module overhead and routing complexity. Since the entire operation is a simple 2-to-1 mux on 100-bit vectors controlled by a single bit sel, it can be more efficiently implemented as a flat assignment: 'out = sel ? b : a;'. This minimizes area by avoiding multiple module instantiations and reduces routing complexity, potentially improving power and performance slightly. The critical path is very short in both cases, so performance impact is negligible. Overall, a flat assign provides the simplest, smallest, and most power-efficient implementation.
