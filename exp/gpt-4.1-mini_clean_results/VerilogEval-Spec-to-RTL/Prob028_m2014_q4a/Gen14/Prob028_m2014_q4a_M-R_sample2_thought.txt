Instead of using an always block to infer the D latch, use a continuous assignment with a ternary operator to implement the latch behavior combinationally. By declaring `q` as a reg and assigning it conditionally to `d` when `ena` is high and retaining its previous value otherwise, it mimics latch behavior. However, since continuous assignments cannot store state, we create a level-sensitive latch with a transparent latch inferred by assigning `q` directly from `d` gated by `ena`. To maintain `q` when `ena` is low, we declare `q` as a `reg` and update it only when `ena` is high inside an always block sensitive to `d` and `ena`. This approach is cleaner, uses blocking assignments, and is more commonly recognized by synthesis tools without ambiguous `q <= q;` assignments.
