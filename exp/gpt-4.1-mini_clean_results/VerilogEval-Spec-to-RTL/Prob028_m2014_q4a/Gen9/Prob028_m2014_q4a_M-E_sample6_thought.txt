Instead of using a single always block sensitive to d and ena, implement the D latch by splitting the enable gating and data storage into two always blocks: one always block to latch the input data when enable is high (using a non-blocking assignment for synthesis friendliness), and a separate combinational assignment to hold the output stable otherwise. This separation can help synthesis tools better recognize the latch behavior. The output q is declared as reg to retain state. The always block sensitivity uses posedge of enable and negedge of enable to cover level-sensitive behavior by capturing d on enable's rising edge and holding it when enable is low. This approach differs from the usual latch coding style and may improve synthesis tool inference without requiring special latch primitives.
