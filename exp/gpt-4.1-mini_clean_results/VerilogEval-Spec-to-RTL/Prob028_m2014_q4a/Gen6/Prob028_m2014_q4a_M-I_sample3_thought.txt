To improve synthesis compatibility and ensure correct D latch inference, use a standard coding style recommended for latch inference: an always block triggered on changes to `ena` or `d`, with a nonblocking assignment to `q` when `ena` is high, and no assignment otherwise to allow latch behavior (retaining previous value). Using nonblocking assignment (`<=`) in level-sensitive latches is generally recommended for better synthesis and simulation consistency. Also, use `posedge` or `negedge` in a latch is incorrect; this is level-sensitive, so we keep sensitivity to `ena` and `d`. This style is more broadly accepted by synthesis tools and improves the chances of correct mapping to standard latch primitives or proper latch inference.
