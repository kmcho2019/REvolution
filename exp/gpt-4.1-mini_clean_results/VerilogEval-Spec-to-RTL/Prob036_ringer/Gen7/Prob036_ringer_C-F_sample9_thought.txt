To fuse the best ideas from both examples, we note:

- Both examples implement correct functionality using a combinational always block with reg outputs. This is clear and straightforward.
- Example 1 highlights using a single always block assigning both outputs simultaneously to reduce redundant logic and potential glitches.
- Example 2 emphasizes readability and extensibility through structured if-else blocks.
- The feedback on both examples suggests further optimization for area and power by using continuous assignments and wire outputs rather than reg outputs and always blocks.
- Continuous assignments with simple combinational expressions (ring & ~vibrate_mode for ringer, ring & vibrate_mode for motor) minimize logic complexity, enable synthesis tools to optimize better, and reduce inferred registers or latches.
- Using wires instead of reg outputs removes the need for always blocks, reducing area and power.
- Simple combinational expressions eliminate nested ifs, shortening critical path and reducing toggling, which benefits power and performance.

Therefore, the fusion strategy is:

- Implement the logic as continuous assign statements with wire outputs.
- Define outputs as wire types.
- Use simple Boolean expressions to express mutual exclusivity and conditional output assignments:
  - ringer = ring & ~vibrate_mode
  - motor  = ring & vibrate_mode

This approach combines the clarity and correctness of the previous designs with more optimal PPA characteristics: simpler logic, no inferred registers, no procedural blocks, and minimal switching.

It also maintains the mutual exclusivity constraint ensuring only one output is asserted at a time.

This solution is thus superior in performance, power, and area while maintaining functional clarity and correctness.
