The previous solution is clear and modular but includes intermediate signals like nand_ab and or_ab, which add logic levels and gates. To optimize power and area, we can revisit the Karnaugh map minimization and implement a minimal sum-of-products directly, eliminating shared intermediate signals.

Analyzing the K-map:

              ab
   cd   00  01  11  10
   00 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 |
   01 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
   11 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
   10 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 |

We observe these minimal groups:
- Group1 (4 cells): cd=00 with ab=00,01,10 (cells with output 1) and cd=01 with ab=00,10 (some overlap)
- Group2 (4 cells): cd=10 with ab=00,01 and cd=00 with ab=10 (some overlap)
- Group3 (4 cells): cd=11 with ab=01,11,10 (some overlap)

From this, we derive the minimal sum-of-products:

out = (~c & ~d & ~b)  // covers cd=00 & b=0 (covers 00, 01)
    | (~c & d & ~a)   // covers cd=01 & a=0
    | (c & ~d & ~a)   // covers cd=10 & a=0
    | (c & d & b)     // covers cd=11 & b=1

This matches the output cells exactly and uses only four product terms with simple literals, avoiding OR and NAND intermediates.

Implementing this expression directly reduces logic gates, levels, and switching activity, improving power and area without extra wires.
