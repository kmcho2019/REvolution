The Karnaugh map can be directly translated into sum-of-minterms form, but to simplify, I will first write down all minterms where the output is 1, then try to factor or combine them simply.

From the map, outputs of 1 are at these positions (cd, ab):

00 00: a=0,b=0,c=0,d=0 => ~a~b~c~d  
00 01: a=0,b=1,c=0,d=0 => ~a b ~c ~d  
00 10: a=1,b=0,c=0,d=0 => a ~b ~c ~d  
00 11: no (0)  
01 00: a=0,b=0,c=0,d=1 => ~a ~b ~c d  
01 10: a=1,b=0,c=0,d=1 => a ~b ~c d  
01 01 and 11: no  
10 00: a=0,b=0,c=1,d=0 => ~a ~b c ~d  
10 01: a=0,b=1,c=1,d=0 => ~a b c ~d  
10 10 and 11: no  
11 01: a=0,b=1,c=1,d=1 => ~a b c d  
11 10: a=1,b=0,c=1,d=1 => a ~b c d  
11 11: a=1,b=1,c=1,d=1 => a b c d  

Now, looking for simplified groups:

- All terms where c=0 and b=0: (c=0 & ~b) -> covers several terms.
- Also terms with c=1 and d=1 & a or b set.

An easier approach: Use boolean algebra to minimize:

Observation:

- When c=0 and d=0, output=1 except when a=1,b=1 (from K-map)
- So for c=0, d=0 output= ~a + ~b (since only 11 is 0)
- When c=0,d=1 output=1 except for a=1,b=1? Actually 01 row d=1 includes 4 minterms with some zeros.

Alternatively, group all terms with ~c:

(~c & ( ~a | ~b ))  // covers all when c=0 and either a=0 or b=0

Then for c=1:

- When c=1, output=1 when d=1 and (a or b) (see last group)
- When c=1, d=0, output=1 when a=0, b=any or b=1,a=0 in some cases

Given that, a simpler expression would be:

out = (~c & (~a | ~b)) | (c & d & (a | b)) | (c & ~d & ~a)

This expression has only three terms and maintains all minterms.

This matches some groups of the original, but is simpler.

Hence the implementation:

1) define na, nb, nc, nd

2) assign out = ( ~c & ( ~a | ~b ) ) | ( c & d & (a | b) ) | ( c & ~d & ~a )

This reduces to three product terms and one or more ORs, fewer wires, less complexity while maintaining the original functionality.
