The original design cleanly partitions the K-map by rows of {c,d}, implementing four partial functions on {a,b} and multiplexing them. This is clear and modular but incurs redundant negations and separate partial expressions.

To improve PPA:
- Eliminate redundant negations by sharing a single negation per input.
- Avoid multiple negations of the same signal; compute na, nb, nc, nd once and reuse.
- Combine the multiplexer logic into a single expression to minimize gate levels.
- Alternatively, derive a fully minimized sum-of-products (SOP) expression from the K-map for 'out' to reduce gate count and depth.

Group by common factors:

- When b=0 and c=0,d=0 or c=0,d=1 or c=1,d=1: many 1's appear. Carefully summing to simplify:

A minimal SOP from the K-map (already simplified) is:

out = (~a & ~b) & (~c | ~d)   // covers rows with ab=00 and cd=00 or 01 or 10 (with small adjustments)
      + (~a & b & c & d)      // m13: 0111
      + (a & ~b & c)          // m6(0 1 1 0), m14(1 1 1 0) coverage via a & ~b & c
      + (~b & ~c)             // ~b & ~c covers several minterms with b=0 and c=0

After systematic Karnaugh simplification, a concise expression is:

out = (~b & ~c) | (~a & ~b & ~d) | (~a & b & c & d) | (a & ~b & c)

Implementing this expression uses fewer negations and gates.

Summary of improvements:
- Use a single negation per input.
- Implement the minimized expression directly.
- Reduce logic depth by merging terms.

This approach will reduce area and power by fewer intermediate signals and simpler logic, and improve timing by lowering logic levels.
