By analyzing the Karnaugh map and the previous solution, we see the expression can be simplified by factoring and combining terms more compactly.

The original simplified expression was:

out = (~c & ~d & ~(a & b)) | (~c & d & ~b) | (c & ~d & ~a) | (c & d & (a | b))

We can notice:

- (~c & d & ~b) and (~c & ~d & ~(a & b)) both share ~c.
- Similarly, (c & ~d & ~a) and (c & d & (a | b)) share c.

Group terms by c:

out = ~c & ((~d & ~(a & b)) | (d & ~b)) | c & ((~d & ~a) | (d & (a | b)))

Now, note that (~d & ~(a & b)) | (d & ~b) can be simplified:

(~d & ~(a & b)) | (d & ~b) = (~d & (~a | ~b)) | (d & ~b) = ~b | (~d & ~a)

Hence,

out = ~c & (~b | (~d & ~a)) | c & ((~d & ~a) | (d & (a | b)))

This can be further factored:

out = ~c & ~b | (~c & ~d & ~a) | c & ~d & ~a | c & d & (a | b)

Note that (~c & ~d & ~a) | (c & ~d & ~a) = ~d & ~a

So,

out = (~c & ~b) | (~d & ~a) | (c & d & (a | b))

This is a minimal and more elegant expression with fewer terms and logic levels.

Implementing this reduces gate count and complexity while maintaining correct functionality.
