The fusion strategy combines the clarity, modularity, and factoring approach of both examples to achieve a design that is both minimal and clean, with enhanced sharing of inverted and OR signals to reduce gate count and switching activity.

Key improvements:
- Use explicit inversion signals (na, nb, nc, nd) to avoid duplicated inverters.
- Share common OR sub-expressions (a|b) and (~a|~b) for gate sharing.
- Implement the minimal sum-of-products terms exactly as derived from the Karnaugh map.
- Combine and factor expressions for improved readability while maintaining minimal logic depth.
- Use a single assign statement with clearly commented terms for easy verification and maintainability.

This approach ensures:
- Minimal area by avoiding repeated gates and sharing logic.
- Reduced power by sharing inverted signals and OR gates, minimizing transitions.
- Optimal performance due to low logic depth and few levels of gates.

Overall, the design is concise, fully combinational, and easily synthesizable with good PPA characteristics.
