The Karnaugh map can be simplified by identifying common factors and merging terms to reduce the number of product terms and gates. Noticing that some minterms share similar variable combinations, we can factor the expression to use fewer terms and less negation, eliminating redundant OR gates over inverted inputs. By grouping cells in the K-map, a more compact expression can be derived such as:

out = (~c & ~d & ~a) | (~c & d & ~b) | (c & ~d & ~a) | (c & d & (a | b)) | (~c & ~d & b & a) | (c & d & a)

Further factorization shows that the expression can be compactly written as:
out = (~c & ~d & (~a | b)) | (~c & d & ~b) | (c & ~d & ~a) | (c & d & (a | b))

This expression avoids redundant negations and reduces complexity, with minimal usage of negated inputs and fewer logic terms, improving area and power without affecting correctness.
