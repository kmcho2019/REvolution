The original solution is already efficient in terms of clarity, modularity, and partial factorization. To further optimize PPA (performance, power, area), we can attempt to reduce logic depth and gate count by expressing the logic as a minimal sum-of-products or using consensus terms more aggressively. 

By analyzing the Karnaugh map:

  cd\ab | 00 | 01 | 11 | 10
  ------+----+----+----+----
   00   |  1 |  1 |  0 |  1
   01   |  1 |  0 |  0 |  1
   11   |  0 |  1 |  1 |  1
   10   |  1 |  1 |  0 |  0

Grouping the ones optimally gives the minimized SOP:

out = (~c & ~d) | (~b & d & ~c) | (~a & ~d & c) | (c & d & (a | b))

Notice the first term (~c & ~d) covers all '00' rows (cd=00), simplifying group1 and group2 in previous code.

Hence, merging group1 and part of group2 to one large prime implicant reduces complexity.

Similarly, we keep the other terms as is.

This reduces gate count by having fewer product terms.

Implementation improvements:
- Combine group1 and part of group2 into one product term: ~c & ~d covers cd=00 entirely.
- Use only one shared OR gate for (a|b).
- Keep shared inverted inputs.
- Output is OR of four terms, but fewer total logic gates.

This reduces area and power, and the logic depth remains small.

This approach preserves readability and modularity, while simplifying logic.
