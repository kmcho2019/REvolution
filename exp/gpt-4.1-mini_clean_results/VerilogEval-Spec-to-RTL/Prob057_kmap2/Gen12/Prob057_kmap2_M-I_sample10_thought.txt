The original solution achieves good logic factoring and sharing of complemented inputs, leading to low area and power. To further optimize, I will analyze the Karnaugh map for a possibly simpler minimal SOP or POS form to reduce product terms or literals.

From the K-map:

              ab
       00  01  11  10
   cd   
   00 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 |
   01 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
   11 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
   10 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 |

Notable groups:

- Large 4-cell group for cd=00 and (ab=00,01,10): covers cells with output 1 => this is (~c & ~d & (~a | ~b))

- Another 4-cell group at cd=10 and ab=00,01: (~c & d & ~b)

- Group at cd=11 for ab=01,11,10: (c & d & (b | a))

- Group at cd=10 ab=00 and 01 and also cd=01 ab=00 and 10: possibly can find consensus or merge terms.

After re-examining, I notice we can define terms more simply:

- T1: (~c & ~d & (~a | ~b)) covers first group

- T2: (~c & d & ~b) covers second group

- T3: (c & ~d & ~a) covers cd=10 ab=00,01

- T4: (c & d & (a | b)) covers cd=11 and 10 in parts

These match previous terms except for T3 where the original used c & d & a_or_b, but here it's c & ~d & ~a for another group, improving coverage with fewer overlaps.

Implementing with these terms reduces redundancy. The number of terms remains 4, but literals per term are minimized, and input complements shared once.

This should slightly reduce power and area by minimizing the number of literals and total AND gate inputs.

Hence, the improved design:

- Define complemented inputs once

- Define common OR terms once

- Implement final output as OR of four minimized terms

This minor refinement keeps logic minimal and easy to maintain with slightly fewer gates and transitions.
