Instead of using a minimal SOP expression with shared inversions, I will implement the function by explicitly decoding the inputs into four 2-bit vectors and then use a 16-entry lookup table (ROM) approach implemented via a combinational case statement.

This approach treats (c,d) and (a,b) as separate two-bit numbers forming an address into a 4x4 K-map. The combined 4-bit address (cdab) is used to directly map to the output.

This is a different architectural approach from factoring the expression. It may be slightly larger in area but offers a clear structural interpretation of the K-map and easier modifications if the K-map changes. It also explicitly shows the K-map indexing.

This method is often used in FPGA LUT mapping and can be implemented efficiently.

No inverted signals or common sub-expressions are used; logic is driven by a combinational case lookup.
