To optimize the given logic for better power, performance, and area, I will factor the expression further by using common sub-expressions and reducing redundant OR gates. 

Key insight:
- The Karnaugh map can be expressed as: 
  out = (~c & ~d & (~a | ~b)) | (~c & d & ~b) | (c & ~d & ~a) | (c & d & (a | b))

We notice that (~a | ~b) and (a | b) are common terms already factored out.
Further factoring can be done by grouping the expression as:

out = (~c & ((~d & (~a | ~b)) | (d & ~b))) | (c & ((~d & ~a) | (d & (a | b))))

This lets us implement it with fewer gates by:
- Defining inverted inputs na, nb, nc, nd
- Defining na_or_nb and a_or_b as before
- Computing two intermediate terms for c=0 and c=1 conditions and OR them

This approach reduces gate fan-in, balances logic depth, and reuses sub-expressions efficiently, improving timing and lowering switching activity (thus power). The area may slightly improve due to fewer large OR gates.

The new expression mapping:
- when c=0 (nc=1): out_c0 = (~d & na_or_nb) | (d & nb)
- when c=1  (c=1): out_c1 = (~d & na) | (d & a_or_b)
- out = (nc & out_c0) | (c & out_c1)

This matches the Karnaugh map and is optimized for PPA.
