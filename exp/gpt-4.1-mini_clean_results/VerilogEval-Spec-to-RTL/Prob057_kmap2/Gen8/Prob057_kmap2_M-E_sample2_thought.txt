To implement the given K-map, an alternative is to express the function as a combination of two-level logic but with emphasis on selecting prime implicants directly from the map groups, leading to a different sum-of-products structure.

Examining the K-map:

              ab
       00  01  11  10
cd 00 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 |
cd 01 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
cd 11 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
cd 10 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 |

Let's group:

- Column 00 (ab=00) with rows 00,01,10 have output 1 (cells m0,4,8)
- Column 01 (ab=01) with rows 00,10,11 have output 1 (m1,9,11)
- Column 10 (ab=10) with rows 00,01 have output 1 (m2,6)
- Column 11 (ab=11) with rows 11,10 have output 1 (m14,15)

From these groups:

- Group 1: (a=0,b=0) covers m0,m4,m8 → output=1 for c=0 or 1 and d=0 or 1. So, a=0,b=0 is sufficient: ~a & ~b
- Group 2: (b=1,d=0) covers m1,m9,m11 → b=1 & d=0
- Group 3: (a=1,d=1) covers m6,m14,m15 → a=1 & d=1
- Group 4: (a=0,c=0,d=0) covers m0,m4 → a=0 & c=0 & d=0 (but covered already)
- Group 5: (c=1,b=1,d=1) covers m11,m15 → c=1 & b=1 & d=1

Simplifying, main prime implicants can be:

- ~a & ~b          (covers m0,m4,m8)
- b & ~d           (covers m1,m9,m11)
- a & d            (covers m6,m14,m15)

This covers most 1's except for m2 (00 10) and m10 (10 10) which are 0, so no problem.

Checking m2 (ab=10,cd=00) = 0; not covered by the above implicants.

Checking m10 (ab=10,cd=10) = 0; also not covered.

Hence, this covers all 1's perfectly.

Thus, the minimized function is:

out = (~a & ~b) | (b & ~d) | (a & d)

This form is minimal and different from the previous solution.
