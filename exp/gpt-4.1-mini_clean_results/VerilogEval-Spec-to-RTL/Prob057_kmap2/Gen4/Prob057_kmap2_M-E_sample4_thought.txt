A new approach is to implement the output as a pure sum-of-minterms expression directly derived from the K-map without intermediate grouping or muxing. We enumerate all input combinations where output=1 and write a sum of minterms accordingly.

From the K-map, the minterms with output=1 are:
(c d a b) = 
0000(0 0 0 0) => c=0,d=0,a=0,b=0
0001(0 0 0 1)
0010(0 0 1 0)
0100(0 1 0 0)
0110(0 1 1 0)
1000(1 0 0 0)
1001(1 0 0 1)
1011(1 0 1 1)
1101(1 1 0 1)
1110(1 1 1 0)
1111(1 1 1 1)

Let's write out the minterms in terms of literals:
Minterms (cdab):
- 0000: ~c & ~d & ~a & ~b
- 0001: ~c & ~d & ~a & b
- 0010: ~c & ~d & a & ~b
- 0100: ~c & d & ~a & ~b
- 0110: ~c & d & a & ~b
- 1000: c & ~d & ~a & ~b
- 1001: c & ~d & ~a & b
- 1011: c & ~d & a & b
- 1101: c & d & ~a & b
- 1110: c & d & a & ~b
- 1111: c & d & a & b

We implement these minterms as AND gates and OR them all together for output.

This explicit minterm enumeration, although larger gate count, is a direct structural implementation of the given truth table, with no partial functions or muxing.

This is structurally distinct from the previous solutions, which used partial functions and muxes, and different from a minimized boolean equation.

It gives a straightforward mapping of the truth table to hardware, useful for explicit testing or simple synthesis scenarios.
