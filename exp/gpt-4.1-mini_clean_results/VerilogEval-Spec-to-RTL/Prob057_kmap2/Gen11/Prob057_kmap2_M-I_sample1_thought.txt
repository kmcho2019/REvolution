The original implementation uses intermediate wires for each cd row and then multiplexes the result based on c and d, which introduces extra levels of logic and more gates than needed.

To optimize for better PPA (power, performance, area), I will:
1. Derive the minimized Boolean expression directly from the Karnaugh map.
2. Implement the expression as a single continuous assign statement without intermediate wires.
3. This reduces gate levels, number of signals, and switching activity, improving timing and reducing area and power.

From the Karnaugh map:

              ab
   cd   00  01  11  10
   00 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 |
   01 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
   11 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
   10 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 |

We have variables a,b,c,d and their possible values.

Step 1: Write minterms for output=1:

Positions with 1:

cd=00 (c=0,d=0):
  ab=00: ~a & ~b & ~c & ~d
  ab=01: ~a & b & ~c & ~d
  ab=10: a & ~b & ~c & ~d

cd=01 (c=0,d=1):
  ab=00: ~a & ~b & ~c & d
  ab=10: a & ~b & ~c & d

cd=11 (c=1,d=1):
  ab=01: ~a & b & c & d
  ab=11: a & b & c & d
  ab=10: a & ~b & c & d

cd=10 (c=1,d=0):
  ab=00: ~a & ~b & c & ~d
  ab=01: ~a & b & c & ~d

Step 2: Group minterms to simplify:

Group 1: (~a & ~b) terms with c and d varying: 
  (~a & ~b & ~c & ~d) + (~a & ~b & ~c & d) + (~a & ~b & c & ~d) = ~a & ~b & (~c & ~d + ~c & d + c & ~d)
  The term inside parentheses is (~c & ~d) + (~c & d) + (c & ~d) = ~c & (~d + d) + c & ~d = ~c + c & ~d
  This simplifies to (~c) + (c & ~d) = ~c + (c & ~d)

Group 2: (~a & b & c & d) + (~a & b & c & ~d) + (~a & b & ~c & ~d)
  Check which are ones: From above, only (~a & b & ~c & ~d), (~a & b & c & ~d), (~a & b & c & d) are present?
  Actually from Karnaugh map only:
  - (~a & b & ~c & ~d) (cd=00 ab=01) = yes
  - (~a & b & c & ~d) (cd=10 ab=01) = yes
  - (~a & b & c & d) (cd=11 ab=01) = yes

  So (~a & b & ((~c & ~d) + (c & ~d) + (c & d))) = (~a & b & ( (~c & ~d) + (c & ~d) + (c & d) ))

Group 3: (a & ~b & ~c & ~d) + (a & ~b & ~c & d) + (a & ~b & c & d) (cd=00 ab=10, cd=01 ab=10, cd=11 ab=10)
  So (a & ~b & ( (~c & ~d) + (~c & d) + (c & d) ))

One can see that for a=0,b=0 output=1 when ( ( ~c ) + ( c & ~d ) )

For a=0,b=1 output=1 when ( (~c & ~d) + (c & ~d) + (c & d) ) = (~d) + (c & d)

For a=1,b=0 output=1 when ( (~c & ~d) + (~c & d) + (c & d) ) = (~c) + (c & d)

Step 4: Express output as sum over ab terms:

out = (~a & ~b & (~c + (c & ~d))) | 
      (~a & b & (~d + c & d)) | 
      (a & ~b & (~c + c & d)) |
      (a & b & c & d)
