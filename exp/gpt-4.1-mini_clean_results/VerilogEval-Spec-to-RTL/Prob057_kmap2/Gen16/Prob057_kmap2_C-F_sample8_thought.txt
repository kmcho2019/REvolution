The first example offers a minimal and efficient flat combinational logic implementation using shared inverted inputs and common OR terms, which improves area and power by reducing redundant inversions and logic. The second example shows a modular approach with reusable, mask-driven product term checks, enhancing clarity and reusability but increasing area and power overhead.

To fuse the best of both:

- Retain the flat, single-assign combinational style from Example 1 for minimal area, power, and performance overhead.
- Introduce meaningful intermediate signals to improve readability and maintainability (like the common inverted inputs and OR terms).
- Add clear comments linking each product term to the respective Karnaugh map groups for clarity.
- Avoid the full generality and overhead of the mask-based modular ProductTerm to keep logic simple and synthesis-friendly.
- Use a consistent naming style and document the K-map groupings for ease of future modifications.

This fusion yields an implementation that is concise, efficient, and clear, striking a balance between performance, area, power, and code readability.
