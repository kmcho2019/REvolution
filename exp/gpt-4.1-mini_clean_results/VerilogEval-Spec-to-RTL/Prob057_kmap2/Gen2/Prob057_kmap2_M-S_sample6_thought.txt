The previous solution is already optimized from the Karnaugh map, but the expression can be simplified further by noticing some common terms.

Re-examining the terms:

out = (~c & ~d & (~a | ~b)) | (~c & d & ~b) | (c & ~d & ~a) | (c & d & (a | b))

We can try to factor by c:

out = (~c & ((~d & (~a | ~b)) | (d & ~b))) | (c & ((~d & ~a) | (d & (a | b))))

Note that (~d & (~a | ~b)) | (d & ~b) = ~b | (~d & ~a)

Since:

- (~d & ~a) is a subset of ~b | (~d & ~a) (because if ~b is true, the whole is true),

The expression (~d & (~a | ~b)) | (d & ~b) can be simplified to (~b | (~d & ~a)).

Similarly, (c & ((~d & ~a) | (d & (a | b)))) can be kept as is.

Alternatively, check if the entire function can be expressed in a simpler factored way.

Another approach: The K-map can also be interpreted as out = (~b & ~c) | (c & d & (a | b)) | (c & ~d & ~a) | (~c & ~d & ~a) | (~c & d & ~b)

But this is similar.

Trying consensus and combining terms:

- (~c & ~d & (~a | ~b)) can be written as (~c & ~d & (~a | ~b))

- (~c & d & ~b) stays

- (c & ~d & ~a)

- (c & d & (a | b))

Trying to reduce the number of parentheses:

Instead of explicitly computing (~a | ~b), use De Morgan to write as ~(a & b), so:

(~c & ~d & (~a | ~b)) = (~c & ~d & ~(a & b))

Since (~a | ~b) = ~(a & b).

This is a single AND-OR-INVERT form, potentially beneficial in logic synthesis.

Hence, the expression becomes:

out = (~c & ~d & ~(a & b)) | (~c & d & ~b) | (c & ~d & ~a) | (c & d & (a | b))

This is simpler to understand and implement.

Overall, using this expression is a simplified, clear and minimal solution with less complex OR terms.
