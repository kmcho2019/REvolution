To optimize the given logic for better performance, power, and area, I will minimize the boolean function derived from the Karnaugh map instead of using the sum of all minterms directly. Grouping adjacent ones in the Karnaugh map yields a simplified expression:

From the K-map, notable groups are:
- Group1: (~c & ~d) covers minterms 0,1,2 (top-left corner)
- Group2: (~c & a & ~b) covers minterms 2,6
- Group3: (~c & ~a) & (d or ~d) effectively covers minterms with ~c and ~a, specifically minterms 0,1,4,9 (some overlaps)
- Group4: (c & d & a) covers minterms 14,15
- Group5: (c & d & b) covers minterms 13,15
- Group6: (c & ~d & ~a) covers minterms 8,9
- Group7: (~c & d & a & ~b) covers 6
- Group8: (~c & d & ~a & ~b) covers 4
- Group9: (~c & ~d & ~a) covers 0,1

By carefully choosing prime implicants:

Simplified expression (example):

out = 
    (~c & ~d)         // covers 0,1,2
  | (c & ~d & ~a)     // covers 8,9
  | (c & d & a)       // covers 14,15
  | (c & d & b)       // covers 13,15
  | (~c & d & ~a & ~b) // covers 4
  | (~c & d & a & ~b)  // covers 6

This reduces the number of terms and gates compared to listing all minterms separately.

Implementing this expression will reduce gate count, improve power by reducing switching, and improve performance by reducing logic levels.
