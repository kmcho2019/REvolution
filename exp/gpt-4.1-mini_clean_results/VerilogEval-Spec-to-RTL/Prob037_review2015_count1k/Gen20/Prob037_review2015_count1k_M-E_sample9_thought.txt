Implement the 0-to-999 counter as a two-stage hierarchical counter: use a 4-bit sub-counter for the lower decimal digit (0-9) and a 6-bit sub-counter for the upper digits (0-99). The lower digit counts from 0 to 9 and when it overflows, it increments the upper digit counter. When the upper digit counter reaches 99 and the lower digit is 9, the full counter reaches 999 and resets.

This architecture uses decimal digit counting logic instead of pure binary counting, which naturally maps to the maximum count of 999. It also avoids a large 10-bit comparator by managing smaller counters with known max values (9 and 99). This can simplify carry and terminal detection logic and potentially reduce critical path delay since each sub-counter has fewer bits and simpler roll-over conditions.

The output q is constructed by concatenating the upper and lower counters converted to a binary value corresponding to the decimal count (upper * 10 + lower). Since the upper counter is 0-99 and the lower is 0-9, the combined count is in 0-999.

The reset is synchronous active high, initializing both counters to zero.

Summary:
- Use two counters: lower 4-bit (0-9), upper 7-bit (0-99).
- Increment lower counter each cycle.
- When lower hits 9, reset lower to 0 and increment upper.
- When upper hits 99 and lower is 9, reset both to 0.
- Output q = upper*10 + lower.
