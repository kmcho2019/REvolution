To improve power and performance without increasing area significantly, implement the modulo-1000 counter using three decimal digit counters (units, tens, hundreds) that count from 0 to 9, 0 to 9, and 0 to 9 respectively, with synchronous reset. This BCD-style counting reduces the complexity of the comparator logic as it is split into smaller 4-bit counters with simpler overflow detection rather than a wide 10-bit comparator to 999. The overflow logic cascades from units to tens to hundreds, and resets on the next cycle after reaching 9-9-9. This approach reduces glitches on the comparator and may reduce switching activity since carry chains are more localized. The overall 10-bit output is assembled by concatenating the three digits suitably converted to binary for output q, or can be directly represented as a 10-bit binary count by converting the digits internally. Here, for simplicity and area, output q represents the combined decimal count as a binary number reconstructed from the three digits (q = hundreds*100 + tens*10 + units). This approach optimizes comparator complexity, reduces switching activity, and improves timing by limiting the width of sequential comparators and simple BCD carry logic, with negligible area overhead.
