To optimize the counter for better performance, power, and area (PPA), I will implement an optimized counter that minimizes comparator complexity and potential critical path delay. 

Since 1000 is not a power-of-two, comparing against 999 involves a 10-bit comparator. Instead, we can redesign the counter logic to detect overflow by checking when the count reaches 999 before incrementing. However, the comparator can't be fully eliminated.

One optimization is to implement the counter using a binary-coded decimal (BCD)-style approach, dividing the count into a 3-digit decimal counter (units, tens, hundreds), each from 0 to 9, which makes the limit-check simpler and reduces adder complexity as they only count from 0-9, rolling over at 9.

The counting logic:
- units increment every cycle
- when units overflow (from 9 to 0), tens increment
- when tens overflow (9 to 0), hundreds increment
- when hundreds overflow from 9 to 0, reset all digits to 0

This approach replaces one 10-bit comparator and one 10-bit adder with three 4-bit BCD counters with simple rollover logic, potentially reducing critical path delay and area.
