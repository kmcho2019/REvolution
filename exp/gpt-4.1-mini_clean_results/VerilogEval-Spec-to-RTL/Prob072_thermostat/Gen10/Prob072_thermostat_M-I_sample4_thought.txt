The original design is already minimal, fully combinational, and well-structured for the given functionality. The power and area usage are very low, and timing is optimal since there is no sequential logic.

To optimize further for power and area, I can reduce the number of logic gates slightly by combining expressions and eliminating intermediate signals. For example, instead of computing heater and aircon separately and then OR'ing for the fan, I can directly compute the fan signal without storing heater and aircon as outputs separately, using continuous assignments for outputs, but this does not reduce complexity significantly.

Alternatively, since the fan depends only on whether the heater or aircon is on or fan_on is set, and heater and aircon depend on mode and temperature flags, one might reuse partial expressions to reduce gate count.

In this context, the current design is already so minimal that attempts to further combine the logic risks reducing readability without meaningful PPA gain.

However, I can slightly reduce the number of inversions by defining a wire for inverted mode once and reuse it for aircon assignment, saving an inverter gate. Also, I can use one line assign statements with ternary operators to produce the outputs directly.

This minor change could potentially reduce the inversion count, slightly optimizing power and area without loss of clarity.

Hence, the improvement is to create a wire for inverted mode and use it, simplifying the inverter count. The fan logic remains the same.

This yields minimal, but measurable gate count improvement.
