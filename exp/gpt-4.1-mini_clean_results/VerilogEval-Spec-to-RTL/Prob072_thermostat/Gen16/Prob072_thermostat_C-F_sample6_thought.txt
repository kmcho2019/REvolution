To fuse the best ideas from both examples, I will keep the clear separation of heating and cooling activation signals from Example 1, which improves modularity and makes the design self-documenting and easy to expand. At the same time, I will implement the output logic within a single always_comb (or always @(*) in Verilog-2001) block as shown in Example 2, which centralizes control and improves readability and maintainability, especially for future scalability.

Specifically:
- Use intermediate wires heating_active and cooling_active to indicate if heater or aircon should be on.
- Use an always_comb block to assign heater, aircon, and fan outputs based on these signals plus the fan_on input.
- This approach merges the clarity of hierarchical signals with the procedural style output logic, improving code clarity and making it easy to add features without scattering combinational assignments.
- I will define heater, aircon, fan as reg since they are assigned inside always_comb.
- The fan is turned on if heater or aircon is active or user requests fan_on.
- The design remains purely combinational and efficient.

This fusion yields a clean, modular, maintainable, and easily extensible design with straightforward control flow, balancing clarity and compactness.
