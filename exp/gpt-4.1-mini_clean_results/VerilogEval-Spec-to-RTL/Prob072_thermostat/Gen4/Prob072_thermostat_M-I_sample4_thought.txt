The original solution is already minimal and optimal for this simple combinational thermostat logic. The signals heater and aircon depend directly on mode and temperature inputs, and fan is a simple OR of heater, aircon, and fan_on, which is minimal logic.

To slightly improve power and area, we can combine the heater and aircon signals into a single intermediate wire controlling the fan, avoiding duplicate logic and potentially reducing gate count slightly. Also, since heater and aircon are simple AND gates with inverted or direct mode signals, we can directly assign outputs without intermediate wires to reduce wiring.

Another minor improvement is to explicitly declare outputs as wires and avoid unnecessary internal signals if possible, making code more concise.

However, these gains are very minor given the simple logic. The main advantage is in improving readability and slightly reducing wiring, which can marginally improve synthesis results.

Thus, the optimized version will:
- Assign heater and aircon outputs directly from inputs.
- Use a single wire for combined heating/cooling signal to drive the fan.
- Output fan as OR of combined signal and fan_on.

This results in slightly simpler wiring and potentially marginally smaller area and power.

No functional change will occur.
