The original design is already optimal and minimal in logic complexity. However, to further optimize for power, area, and possibly slightly reduce switching activity, I will implement the fan signal in a way that minimizes transitions. Specifically, since the fan is turned on whenever heater or aircon is on or fan_on is asserted, I can use a single OR gate as before, but additionally, I will use a registered output to avoid glitches and reduce switching caused by combinational fan control. This requires adding a clock input and registering outputs, trading off a bit of latency for cleaner signals and possibly better power.

But since no clock is specified, I will keep the purely combinational approach but rewrite the code using intermediate wires for clarity and to allow the synthesis tool to better optimize the logic. Using intermediate signals for heater_on and aircon_on also improves readability and potentially helps the synthesis tool map these signals to common sub-expressions, potentially saving area.

This small restructuring could aid tools in optimization, though the logic remains the same.

Hence, my strategy:
- Introduce intermediate signals for heater_on and aircon_on.
- Assign outputs based on these intermediate signals.
- Keep logic purely combinational.
- Remove unnecessary comparisons to '1'b1' as done previously.

This approach should maintain correctness and potentially improve optimization by synthesis tools.
