The fusion strategy is to leverage the clarity, minimalism, and optimal gate usage of Example 1 (direct use of XOR and AND gates for sum and cout) while incorporating the universality and educational value of NAND gate decomposition from Example 2 in a parameterized or optional way.

However, since direct XOR and AND gates are more synthesis-friendly and optimal in PPA metrics than NAND-based constructions, the superior design should primarily use direct built-in XOR and AND primitives for the main functional logic.

To retain educational value and universality, I will embed the NAND-based half adder logic inside the module as an alternative generate block controlled by a parameter (e.g., USE_NAND_IMPL). By default, the module uses direct gates for best PPA and simplicity; if set, it uses the NAND-only implementation, showing how to derive sum and cout from NAND gates.

This fusion:
- Provides optimal performance, power, and area by default using direct gates.
- Allows designers to easily switch to NAND-based implementation for educational or specific technology reasons.
- Enhances maintainability and clarity by clearly separating implementations with descriptive comments.
- Keeps the top-level interface consistent and minimal.

This approach combines the best of both examples, allowing practical optimal use or pedagogical demonstration without losing synthesis or PPA efficiency.
