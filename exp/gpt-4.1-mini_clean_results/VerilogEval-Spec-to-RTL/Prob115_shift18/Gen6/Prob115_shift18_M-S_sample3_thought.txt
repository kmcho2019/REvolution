The shift amounts are fixed (1 or 8 bits) and the direction is either left or arithmetic right. We can simplify the design by splitting the shifting operation into two steps: first shifting by 1 bit, then by 8 bits, and selecting the final result with a simple mux. Since only one shift amount is selected at a time, we can first perform both shifts for left and right directions separately and then select based on the amount. This reduces the combinational logic complexity compared to the original barrel shifter that combined all cases at once.

The logic can be structured as:
- Compute shift left by 1 and by 8.
- Compute arithmetic shift right by 1 and by 8.
- Use a 2-bit mux indexed by amount to select the right shift amount.
- Use amount[1] as direction selector (0 for left, 1 for right).
- On each clock, synchronously load if load=1, else if ena=1, shift based on amount.

This approach maintains the same functionality but simplifies the combinational logic and muxing, improving clarity and potentially reducing critical path complexity.
