To optimize the existing 64-bit arithmetic shift register for better PPA (Timing, Power, and Area), we can leverage a more uniform and scalable approach to shifting by implementing the shift amounts using a small staged barrel shifter structure rather than separate explicit shift-by-1 and shift-by-8 wires.

Improvement strategy:
- Replace fixed shift-by-1 and shift-by-8 shift wires with a two-level barrel shifter:
  - First stage shifts by 1 or 0 (based on amount[0])
  - Second stage shifts by 8 or 0 (based on amount[1])
- For left shift, use logical left shifts by these amounts.
- For right shift (arithmetic), use arithmetic right shifts by the same staged amounts, with sign-extension replicated accordingly.
- This two-stage shifting reduces duplication of logic and muxing compared to separate shift-by-1 and shift-by-8 muxes and allows for easier scalability.
- Since shifts are either by 1 or 8 bits, this approach elegantly combines the shifts.
- Minimize sign extension logic by performing sign extension only once before shifting.
- Power can be reduced because partial shifts enable less switching and smaller mux trees.
- The code remains synchronous and clean.

This approach also keeps the code compact, easier to maintain, and likely achieves lower area due to reusing the staged shifts. Timing should improve by reducing the number and complexity of muxes and concatenations, and power is saved by simpler control.

Note: Since only four shift operations (left1, left8, right1, right8) are valid, the staged barrel shifter approach aligns perfectly with amount[1] and amount[0] bits controlling the shift by 8 and 1 respectively.
