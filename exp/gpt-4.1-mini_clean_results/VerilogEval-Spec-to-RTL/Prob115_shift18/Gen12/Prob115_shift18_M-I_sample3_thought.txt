To improve the initial design's timing, power, and area metrics, we will optimize the shift operations and muxing structure. Since shifts are by fixed amounts (1 or 8 bits) and directions (left or arithmetic right), we can implement these shifts using simple concatenation and slicing instead of variable shifts, which synthesis tools might implement as expensive barrel shifters.

For example:
- Shift left by 1 bit: concatenate q[62:0] and 1'b0
- Shift left by 8 bits: concatenate q[55:0] and 8'b0
- Arithmetic right shift by 1 bit: concatenate msb and q[63:1]
- Arithmetic right shift by 8 bits: concatenate 8 copies of msb and q[63:8]

This removes large 64-bit variable shifters.

Additionally, we replace the nested conditional operator muxing with a single case statement inside a combinational always block to reduce mux levels and improve synthesis optimization.

By doing so, we reduce the critical path muxing, limit switching activity by controlling when shifts happen, and lower logic complexity and area.

These optimizations should improve timing slack (reduce negative slack), reduce power consumption, and slightly reduce area while maintaining correctness.
