I will implement the 64-bit arithmetic shift register using a two-stage shifting approach combining a small shift left or right by 1 and by 8 bits, but organized as a variable-shift approach using a priority encoder style muxing. Instead of straightforward combinational concatenation for each shift amount, I will implement two separate combinational steps:

- First, based on the direction bit (amount[1]), select either a left or right shift data path.
- Then, based on the amount[0], select between shifting by 1 or 8 bits.

This time, I'll use a single always_comb block with a case statement to clearly encode the shift operations as a combinational function, improving readability and synthesis friendliness.

To implement arithmetic right shifts, I will extend the sign bit (MSB) appropriately while shifting right.

This architectural approach simplifies the selection logic, avoids nested conditional operators, and uses explicit case statements. This approach reduces complexity of muxing circuits and allows easy extension later. The shift register update logic remains synchronous with load and enable signals.

By using a combinational function for the shifted value and clean synchronous update, the design should be clear, concise, and efficient, while functionally correct.
