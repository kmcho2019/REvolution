Instead of precomputing all shifts and then selecting the output via muxes, we can split the shift operation into two sequential stages within the same clock cycle using an internal pipeline register (combinational logic only, no extra clock cycles). This involves performing the shift by 1 bit first, then shifting the result again by 8 bits if needed, depending on the amount input. Because the shift amounts are only 1 or 8 bits, and direction is either left or arithmetic right, the 64-bit shift operation can be decomposed into smaller wiring assignments and sign extension, reducing combinational logic depth.

Specifically:
- On each clock cycle (if load is 0 and ena is 1), first shift q by 1 bit in the specified direction.
- Then, depending on the amount bits, optionally shift by another 8 bits.
- The amount bits determine the shift direction and total shift:
  - 00: left by 1 (shift once by 1, no second shift)
  - 01: left by 8 (skip first shift, just shift by 8)
  - 10: right arithmetic by 1 (shift once by 1, no second shift)
  - 11: right arithmetic by 8 (skip first shift, just shift by 8)
- This logic can be achieved by interpreting amount[0] as shift magnitude selector and amount[1] as direction.
- If amount[0] == 0, do 1-bit shift; if 1, do 8-bit shift directly.
- This approach limits the muxing and simplifies the logic, and by splitting the shifts into stages (1-bit shift then 8-bit shift conditionally), we reduce large wide muxing.

This structural approach reduces large combinational mux complexity, and breaks the problem down into two smaller shift operations, done combinationally in the same clock cycle before registering the result.

Additionally, shifts are implemented by simple concatenations and replication of MSB for arithmetic right shifts to minimize logic complexity.
