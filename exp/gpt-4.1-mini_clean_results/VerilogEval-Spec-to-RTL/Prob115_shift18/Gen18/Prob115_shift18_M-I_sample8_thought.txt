The original design uses a two-stage shift approach by shifting either 0 or 8 bits first, then shifting 0 or 1 bit next, chosen based on the amount input bits. This balances the mux complexity and reduces delay compared to a direct 4-mux structure of all four shifts in parallel.

To further optimize PPA:
- Simplify the two-stage shift implementation by clearly separating the shift stages controlled by amount bits to avoid redundant muxing.
- Implement the left shift and arithmetic right shift barrel stage shifts as smaller multiplexers based on the amount bits, avoiding unnecessary intermediate signals.
- For left shifts, shifting by 8 or 1 bit is simply zero fill with wiring, so avoid redundant signals.
- For arithmetic right shifts, replicate the MSB for sign extension, but minimize the number of times the MSB is replicated by computing it once and using a variable-width replicate.
- Combine the shifts in a two-step barrel shifter approach:
  * Step 1: shift by 8 bits if amount[0] is 1, else no shift
  * Step 2: shift by 1 bit if amount[1] is 1, else no shift
- Instead of separate signals for stage1 and stage2, compose the shifts using conditional assignments to avoid multiple intermediate wires and reduce mux logic.
- Remove unnecessary muxing for shifts by 0 (no shift) by assigning the intermediate result directly.
- Use non-blocking assignments only in the always block.
- Combine direction control to select between left shift and arithmetic right shift at the final stage only.
- Gate shifting logic with ena signal to avoid unnecessary toggling inside the combinational logic.

This improved approach reduces mux layers, simplifies logic, and keeps the arithmetic right shift sign extension efficient by reusing the sign bit. It should improve timing and reduce switching power slightly while maintaining the same functional behavior.
