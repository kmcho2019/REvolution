Instead of computing all shift variations in parallel and then muxing, implement a two-stage shift register with two separate shifting stages chained sequentially. The first stage performs a 1-bit arithmetic shift (left or right) when enabled. The second stage performs an 8-bit shift (left or right) conditionally, depending on the amount bits. 

By separating the shifting into two pipeline-like steps within the same clock cycle (using combinational logic), the logic for shifts by 1 and by 8 can be selected and combined by simple multiplexing after these stages. We can interpret the amount as a concatenation of two bits: amount[0] controls the first stage (shift by 1), and amount[1] controls the second stage (shift by 8). The direction (left or right) is determined by amount[1], but to simplify control, we use amount directly.

On load, the register loads data synchronously. On enable, the shift happens sequentially: first the 1-bit shift stage, then the 8-bit shift stage. This approach reduces the complexity of large multiplexers and distributes shifting logic, potentially improving timing and area.

Arithmetic right shifts replicate the MSB, while left shifts fill with zeros. The final output is registered.

This method also clarifies control signals: 
- If load=1: load data
- else if ena=1: shift through two stages, controlled by amount bits
- else: hold current value.

This two-step shifting approach is functionally equivalent but rearranged architecturally.
