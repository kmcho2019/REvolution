To optimize the existing design for better PPA without changing functionality, I will:

- Replace the combinational always block with continuous assignments using wires for stage1 and stage2, removing unnecessary reg usage and avoiding implied latches or more complex logic.
- Implement shift by 1 and 8 bits with mux-based logic instead of full shift operations or concatenations to reduce combinational depth.
- Compute the shift signals selectively based on 'amount' decoding, avoiding redundant logic.
- Use a single multiplexer stage per shift size (8-bit and 1-bit) to select shifted or original value, minimizing logic levels.
- Keep synchronous load and enable logic simple and as-is.

This approach reduces logic depth (critical path), combinational complexity, and area, potentially improving timing and power.

The core idea is that shifting by 8 bits can be done by choosing between the original q and q shifted by 8 bits (left or right), then shifting by 1 bit similarly. Each shift is conditionally applied with multiplexers, reducing the logic compared to multi-level conditional always blocks.

The arithmetic right shift uses sign extension for vacated bits, handled explicitly in the mux outputs.

The final shift input to the register is determined by the enable and load signals.

This should improve PPA metrics while maintaining correctness and simplicity.
