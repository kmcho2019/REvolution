Since the shifts are only by 1 or 8 bits and in fixed directions, and the right shift is arithmetic, the shift operations can be implemented by simple concatenations rather than using large shift operators. This reduces combinational complexity.

We can precompute four possible shifted results using simple wiring:
- Left shift by 1: drop MSB, append 0 at LSB
- Left shift by 8: drop top 8 bits, append 8 zeros
- Arithmetic right shift by 1: replicate MSB, then drop LSB
- Arithmetic right shift by 8: replicate MSB 8 times, then drop lower 8 bits

Then select the final shift output with a 2-level mux controlled by amount[1] and amount[0]. This avoids complex shift logic and large 64-bit shift operators.

On clock edge, if load is high, load data; else if ena is high, update q with the selected shift output; else hold q.

This simple, structural approach reduces mux and shift operator complexity and improves clarity.
