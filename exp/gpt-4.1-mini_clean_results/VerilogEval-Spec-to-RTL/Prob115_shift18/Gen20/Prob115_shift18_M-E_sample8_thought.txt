Implement the 64-bit arithmetic shift register using a two-stage shifting approach for both directions: first shift by 8 bits, then conditionally shift by 1 bit, or vice versa depending on the amount input. This avoids wide multiplexers selecting among multiple shift sizes at once, reducing combinational logic fanout and potential critical path delays.

Specifically:
- When amount indicates left shift:
  - If amount == 2'b00 (shift left by 1), shift by 1 bit only.
  - If amount == 2'b01 (shift left by 8), shift by 8 bits only.
- When amount indicates right shift:
  - If amount == 2'b10 (shift right by 1), shift by 1 bit with sign extension.
  - If amount == 2'b11 (shift right by 8), shift by 8 bits with sign extension.

To enable a uniform hardware structure, implement the shifts with two shift stages:
- Stage 1: shift by 8 bits or pass-through.
- Stage 2: shift by 1 bit or pass-through.

Use combinational muxes to select between these stages based on amount bits.

This modularizes the shift logic, reduces large multiplexers, and uses simple concatenations and conditionals for arithmetic right shifts.

Load and enable work synchronously in a single always block. This architecture is novel compared to the previous full-shift precomputing approach, balancing between structural simplicity and efficient critical path.

Overall, this two-step shift structure promotes better timing and potential area savings while maintaining the required functional behavior.
