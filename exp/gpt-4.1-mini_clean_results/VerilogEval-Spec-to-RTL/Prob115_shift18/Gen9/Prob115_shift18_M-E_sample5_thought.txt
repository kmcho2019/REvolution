Implement the 64-bit arithmetic shift register as a sequential register with a small combinational barrel shifter based on nested shift stages. To avoid large 64-bit wide multiplexers, break the shift into two sequential multiplexers controlled by the bits of 'amount':

- First stage: shift by 1 bit left/right or no shift (depending on amount[0])
- Second stage: shift by 8 bits left/right or no shift (depending on amount[1])

Combine shifts by cascading these two stages. This cascaded shift approach mimics a 2-level barrel shifter, but uses only small 2-to-1 muxes per bit, greatly reducing mux fanout and improving timing.

For arithmetic right shift, replicate MSB for any shifted-in bits.

Inside the always block on clk posedge:

- If load=1, load data to q synchronously.
- Else if ena=1, update q with the result of cascading shifts (amount[0] for 1-bit shift, amount[1] for 8-bit shift).
- Else keep q unchanged.

This modular approach balances logic complexity and timing by decomposing the shift into two smaller shifts combined sequentially, unlike a single large mux. It also allows simpler synthesis with fewer large multiplexers.

This design differs from a single big mux implementation by breaking the shift into two smaller steps and performing shifts in stages internally before registering.
