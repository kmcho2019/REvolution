To optimize the existing 64-bit arithmetic shift register for better performance, power, and area, we combine the two-stage shift into a single barrel shifter stage that directly performs the required shifts (left by 1 or 8, right arithmetic by 1 or 8) in one combinational operation. This reduces the combinational path delay and switching activity since the logic is evaluated once per shift.

Additionally, we gate the shift operation with ena so that when ena is low and load is low, the output remains unchanged without performing any unnecessary combinational operations, thus reducing power consumption.

We implement the arithmetic right shift by replicating the sign bit (MSB) accordingly using a left-padded concatenation. The left shifts are zero-extended on the right. For the right shifts, the sign bit is replicated to fill the vacated bits for arithmetic shift correctness.

The design retains a synchronous load and synchronous enable-based update, ensuring clean timing and no glitches on q.

This approach simplifies the combinational logic, reduces switching activity when shift is disabled, and shortens critical path delay, improving the overall PPA metrics.
