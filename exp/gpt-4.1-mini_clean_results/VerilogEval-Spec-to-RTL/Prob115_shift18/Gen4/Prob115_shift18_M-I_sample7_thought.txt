The original design correctly implements the shift logic but uses separate wires and muxes for left and right shifts by 1 or 8 bits, which duplicates logic and adds multiplexing overhead. 

Improvement strategy:
- Implement a unified barrel shifter structure that shifts by either 0, 1, 8, or 9 bits in a combinational way, eliminating separate muxes for shift_1 and shift_8.
- Decompose the shift into two shift stages controlled by amount bits, one for shifting by 8 bits, another for 1 bit, combining direction and sign extension for arithmetic right shifts.
- By splitting shift into two stages (shift by 8 then by 1), we can always perform a left or arithmetic right shift in two steps with minimal multiplexing and zero or sign extension only once.
- This approach reuses intermediate signals, reduces mux count, and balances logic depth, resulting in improved timing and reduced area.
- When load is active, load data synchronously.
- When enable is inactive, hold current q.

This is a classic barrel shifter approach optimized for only two shift amounts (1 and 8) and directions, with arithmetic right shift sign extension carefully applied during shifts.

This design reduces combinational logic complexity while maintaining exact functional behavior, thus improving PPA metrics.
