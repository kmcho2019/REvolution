Implement the 64-bit arithmetic shift register by splitting the shift operations into two stages controlled by 'amount': first shift by 8 bits (either left or right with sign extension), then shift by 1 bit (again left or right with sign extension). This approach modularizes the shift by fixed amounts into two smaller steps, reducing large multiplexers and long combinational paths of a full barrel shifter. On load, the register directly takes the input data. On shift enable, the data is shifted by the combined amount indicated by 'amount', which is decoded to a direction and shift distances of 8 and/or 1 bits. This two-stage staged shift reduces logic depth and can potentially improve timing. Arithmetic right shifts replicate the MSB to maintain the sign. The output register updates synchronously on the clock, with load having priority over shift. This differs structurally from a monolithic barrel shifter by explicitly breaking down shifting into two cascaded smaller shifts for better performance and area optimization.
