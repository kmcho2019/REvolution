Implement the 64-bit arithmetic shift register using a two-stage shift approach. First, perform a coarse shift by 8 bits if required, then perform a fine shift by 1 bit. This breaks down shifts of 1 or 8 bits into composable stages, reducing the complexity of multiplexers and combinational logic.

The design handles left and right shifts separately with explicit multiplexers, but unlike the previous approach, it applies shifts in two steps: 
- Step 1: Shift by 8 bits or no shift depending on amount[0].
- Step 2: Shift by 1 bit or no shift depending on amount[1], also considering shift direction.

For right shifts, apply arithmetic sign extension by propagating the MSB. For left shifts, zero fill.

On each clock edge:
- If load is high, load data synchronously.
- Else if enable is high, update with the combined shift result.

This staged shifting approach simplifies logic, enabling easier timing closure by limiting the maximum mux fanout and the wide concatenations. It also reduces power by only enabling multiplexers relevant for the specified shift amount.

This architecture is different from the one-step combined shift and distinct from computing all shifts in parallel, instead using sequential mux stages that compose the shift amount and direction.
