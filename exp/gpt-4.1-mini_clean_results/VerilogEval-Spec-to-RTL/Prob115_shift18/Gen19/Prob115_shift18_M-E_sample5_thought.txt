I will implement the 64-bit arithmetic shift register using a sequential multiplexing approach that shifts the register step-by-step by 1 bit or by 8 bits in a hierarchical manner based on the amount input. Instead of precomputing all four shift versions in parallel, the design will first determine the shift direction and magnitude, then perform the shift within the always block using a case statement. This reduces combinational logic duplication and switching activity outside the clock edge, potentially saving power.

For arithmetic right shifts, the sign bit extension will be done by replicating the MSB accordingly. On load, the register synchronously loads the input data. When ena=0, the register holds its value, avoiding unnecessary toggling.

This approach centralizes shift logic inside the sequential block, reducing combinational overhead and mux complexity outside the flip-flops. It also allows easy modification if more shift amounts or directions are needed.

The main benefits of this design:
- No parallel computation of all shifted outputs; shifting happens in the clock domain only when enabled.
- Use of a case statement for shift selection improves clarity and logic optimization by synthesis.
- Explicit arithmetic right shift with sign bit extension inside the always block.
- Lower switching activity in combinational paths since shifts are not precomputed.
