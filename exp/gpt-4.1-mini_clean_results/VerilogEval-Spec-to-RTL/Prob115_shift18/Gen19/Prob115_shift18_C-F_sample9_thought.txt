Fuse the best ideas by minimizing the number of parallel shift computations and multiplexers while maintaining clarity and efficiency. Use the example 1 approach for clarity in shift operations by explicitly computing four shift variants (shift left 1, shift left 8, shift right 1, shift right 8) to allow simple combinational selection. However, to reduce redundant shift operations and area/power overhead, only compute one set of shifts at a time by gating the shift operations with the enable signal. This avoids unnecessary shifting when ena=0 and prevents output toggling, thus saving power.

Further, combine direction and amount selection in a single multiplexer stage to reduce logic levels, improving timing. Use concatenations for shifts instead of full vector shifts for clear synthesis inference and sign-extension for arithmetic right shifts.

The synchronous load takes priority. When load is active, the register directly loads data. When load is low and ena is active, perform the shift based on amount. Otherwise, hold q.

This fusion achieves:
- Low switching when not enabled (power efficiency).
- Simple, explicit shift implementations for clarity and synthesis friendliness.
- Minimal multiplexing stages for performance.
- Area savings by gating shift computations.

Overall, this solution balances functional clarity and PPA improvements by combining enable gating of shift calculations with explicit shift wiring and a simple, single-level multiplexing structure.
