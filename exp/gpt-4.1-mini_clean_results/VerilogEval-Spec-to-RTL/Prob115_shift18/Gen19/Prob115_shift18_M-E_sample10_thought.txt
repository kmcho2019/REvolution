I will implement the shift register by partitioning the 64-bit register into eight bytes (8 bits each), and use a simple barrel shifter concept for shifting by 8 bits. For shifts by 1 bit, I will perform bitwise shifts within bytes and handle cross-byte bit transfers explicitly.

This approach splits the problem into two shift types:

- For 8-bit shifts, byte-wise rotation and zero/sign extension are simpler and efficient.

- For 1-bit shifts, explicit bit shifting and handling carry bits between bytes will be done.

The advantages:

- This byte-wise decomposition reduces large-wide multiplexers since only byte-level rotations are needed for 8-bit shifts.

- The 1-bit shift logic uses minimal combinational logic for bit movement between bytes.

- Only compute the selected shift operation dynamically, avoiding parallel computation of all shift variants.

- The shift direction and amount selection are done via a two-level mux (1-bit or 8-bit shift, then left or right) as before.

- The arithmetic right shift sign-extends by replicating MSB in the high bits.

This modular approach reduces large combinational fanout, improves clarity on the shift operations, and potentially lowers switching and area since no full 64-bit wide muxes or parallel all-shift computations are done.

Synchronous load and enable signals are implemented in a single always block with non-blocking assignments for clean sequential logic.

Overall, this approach balances clarity, modularity, and efficiency without requiring large barrel shifters or fully parallel shift calculations.
