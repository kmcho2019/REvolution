To create a superior solution, I will fuse the clarity and modularity of having an Inverter submodule with the minimal overhead of continuous assignment. Unlike Example 2, I will not use a generate block since it adds unnecessary structural overhead for a single inverter. Instead, I will instantiate the Inverter module directly in the TopModule to keep hierarchy clean and enable reuse and scalability if needed later, without extra complexity.

Inside the Inverter module, I will use a continuous assignment to implement the NOT gate efficiently, minimizing area and power. This avoids the procedural overhead seen in Example 1. The output will be a wire (not reg) since continuous assignment drives wires, further reducing complexity.

This fusion strategy keeps the design:
- Modular and reusable by defining a dedicated Inverter submodule.
- Optimized for power and area by using continuous assignment without an always block.
- Simple and clean by directly instantiating the submodule without a generate block.
- Ready for future extensibility without incurring current overhead.

The overall design balances readability, modularity, and PPA, achieving optimal synthesis quality while maintaining explicit hierarchy and separation of concerns.
