Implement the AND function using only NOR gates, leveraging De Morgan's law: AND can be realized by double negation of inputs followed by a NOR gate. Specifically, q = a & b = ~(~a | ~b). This design uses three NOR gates: two inverters (NOR with tied inputs) to create NOT gates for a and b, followed by a NOR gate taking those inverted signals as inputs to produce q.

This approach uses only NOR primitives, which are also universal gates, and demonstrates an alternative structural realization different from NAND/NOT or direct AND implementations. It emphasizes how universal gates can construct any logic function while illustrating modular, gate-level design clarity.
