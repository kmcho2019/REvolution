I will fuse the best aspects of both examples by implementing the logic directly inside TopModule using a continuous assign statement with the built-in AND operator.

This approach removes the modular hierarchy overhead, reducing net count and routing complexity, which benefits power and area. Using the built-in AND operator directly also ensures synthesis tools map it optimally to a single AND primitive gate, resulting in minimal delay and power.

This design remains straightforward, fully combinational, and easy to understand while providing the lowest possible power, performance, and area (PPA) footprint for this simple logic. The small loss in modularity is justified by the simplicity of the function, making it optimal for this specific case.

Advantages of this fusion:
- Minimal gate count (single AND gate).
- Lowest possible area and power due to no hierarchy.
- Simple, clean RTL easy to read and maintain.
- Optimal timing with no added delay from module instantiation.

This solution represents an elegant and efficient synthesis of the two examples' strengths.
