Implement a 12-hour clock using a single binary second counter that counts total seconds elapsed in the 12-hour cycle (0 to 43199 = 12 * 3600 - 1). Increment this counter on each ena pulse. From this total seconds value, derive hours, minutes, and seconds in combinational logic using integer division and modulus operations implemented via arithmetic shifts and subtractive comparisons since division is not synthesizable directly.

Keep pm indicator as the MSB of hours count based on whether total seconds is in the second half of the 12-hour cycle (12 hours / 2 = 6 hours), toggling pm when crossing noon or midnight.

On reset, total seconds is set to 0 representing 12:00:00 AM.

To generate hh (BCD), map hour (1-12) from total seconds hours (0-11) with hour 0 meaning 12, then convert to BCD by simple logic since range is fixed and small.

Minutes and seconds are derived similarly in combinational logic from total seconds modulo operations, converted to BCD by separate functions.

This method reduces sequential logic to a single counter, simplifies overflow and rollover logic, and uses combinational arithmetic to extract time digits and am/pm indicator. It also avoids nested counters and multiple always blocks, centralizes state, and simplifies enables to only increment one 16-bit counter.

This approach potentially improves timing and power by minimizing clocked registers and reduces design complexity and area by having one main register plus combinational arithmetic to derive all outputs.
