Fuse the advantages of both examples by implementing seconds and minutes as simple binary counters internally for minimal logic and easy increment, similar to Example 1, but convert them to BCD output using combinational subtraction logic to avoid multipliers. For hours, keep a single binary counter (1–12) with clear rollover logic and pm toggle on 11->12 transition, as both examples do. To optimize timing and modularity like Example 2, separate the seconds and minutes rollover carry signals for cascading increments. 

However, unlike Example 2’s BCD digit counters for seconds and minutes which add complexity, the binary approach minimizes combinational logic within the counters, reducing critical path length and area. The BCD conversion outputs remain combinational and simple, using subtraction-based tens and units extraction from binary counters without multiplication.

To improve timing, outputs are registered (one-cycle latency) to break long combinational paths from counters through BCD conversion to outputs. This merges Example 1’s binary counters and conversion logic with Example 2’s modular carry signals and separate always blocks, plus added output register stages for timing closure and power savings via minimal combinational switching on outputs.

Reset remains synchronous and dominant, counters only increment on ena pulse, pm toggles correctly at hour transition. The outputs hh, mm, ss are 8-bit BCD, pm is 1-bit indicating PM (1) or AM (0).

This fusion design balances clarity, modularity, timing, area, and power, producing a high-quality 12-hour clock suitable for synthesis and further optimizations.
