The previous solution tried to express the output and state update logic as q = state ? ~(a|b) : a ^ b, which doesn't match the waveform exactly.

Re-examining the waveform carefully focusing on state and q at each posedge clk:

- State is the flip-flop output, initialized unknown.
- q is combinational output depending on a, b, and state.

From the waveform:

At posedge clk:
- State updates to q of the previous cycle.

Check the q output behavior relative to state, a, b:

For state=0:
- q = a & b
  - At 45ns (state=0,a=0,b=1), q=1? No
- q = a | b
  - At 45ns: 0|1=1, q=1 good
  - At 65ns: 1|1=1, q=0 no
- q = a & ~b
  - 45ns: 0&~1=0, q=1 no
- q = ~a & b
  - 45ns: ~0&1=1, q=1 good
  - 65ns: ~1&1=0, q=0 good
  - 55ns: a=1,b=0, ~1&0=0, q=1 no
Try q = a & (~b) | (~a & b) = a ^ b (XOR)
  - 45ns: 0 ^ 1=1 q=1 good
  - 65ns: 1 ^ 1=0 q=0 good
  - 55ns: 1 ^ 0=1 q=1 good

For state=1:
Try q = ~(a | b)
  - At 75ns (a=0,b=0) q=1 (matches)
  - At 95ns (a=1,b=1) q=1 (should be 0 for NOR) no
Try q = ~(a & b)
  - 75ns: ~(0 & 0)=1 q=1 good
  - 95ns: ~(1 & 1)=0 q=1 no
Try q = ~(a ^ b)
  - 75ns: ~(0^0)=1 q=1 good
  - 95ns: ~(1^1)=1 q=1 good
  - 115ns: a=1,b=0, ~(1^0)=0 q=0 good
- This fits the waveform better.

Thus:

- q = state ? ~(a ^ b) : (a ^ b);

Since state updates to q at each clock rising edge, and q is combinational output and next state, this matches the observed waveform.

This corrects the previous solution's logic by replacing the state=1 case from ~(a|b) to ~(a ^ b).

Hence the implementation is:

- reg state_reg; // flip-flop storing state

- assign q_comb = state_reg ? ~(a ^ b) : (a ^ b);

- output q = q_comb;

- output state = state_reg;

- always @(posedge clk) state_reg <= q_comb;

This fits the waveform exactly.

Optimization: To reduce logic, use XOR and NOT gate only.

This is the minimal and efficient implementation matching the waveform.
