The original code incorrectly computed 'next_state' inside a clocked always block together with 'state', causing incorrect sequential behavior and timing mismatches for the output 'q'. To fix this, we separate the combinational logic for next_state from the sequential update of 'state'. 

The correct and standard FSM design pattern is:

- Compute next_state purely combinationally from current state and inputs.
- On the clock's positive edge, update the state flip-flop with next_state.
- The output 'q' should be the value of next_state (next output), as per the waveform, while 'state' is the registered current state.

From the waveform analysis:
- When state=0, next_state = (~a & b)
- When state=1, next_state = ~(a & ~b)  (which is ! (a & ~b))

Implementing next_state as combinational logic allows 'q' to reflect the immediate next_state based on current inputs and state, and state is updated synchronously.

This approach fixes timing issues, ensures correct functionality, and matches the waveform. It also improves timing, power, and area by removing an unnecessary flip-flop (previously assigned to next_state) and uses simple combinational logic for next_state calculation.
