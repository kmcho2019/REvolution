Re-examining the waveform, we see the following:

- The 'state' is a one-bit register updated on the clock's rising edge.
- The transitions of 'state' and output 'q' depend on current inputs 'a', 'b', and the current state.

By carefully analyzing patterns:

1. State transitions:
   - When both a=0, b=0: state goes to 0.
   - When a=0, b=1: state goes to 1.
   - When a=1, b=1: state toggles.
   - When a=1, b=0: state stays the same.

2. Output q:
   From the waveform:
   - When state=0:
     - q=1 only if b=1 and a=0 (e.g., 45ns).
     - Else q=0.
   - When state=1:
     - q=1 when a=0 and b=0 (e.g., 75ns).
     - q=1 when a=0 and b=0 again (135ns).
     - q=0 when a=1 and b=1 (65ns).
     - q=1 when a=1 and b=0 (95ns).
     - q=0 when a=1 and b=1 (85ns).
     So q seems to be 1 when:
       - (state=1 and a=0 and b=0) or
       - (state=1 and a=1 and b=0)
     i.e., when state=1 and b=0.

Summarizing output q logic:

- q = (~state & ~a & b) | (state & ~b)

Therefore, next_state and q:

- next_state = (a&b) ? ~state : (~a & b) ? 1'b1 : (~a & ~b) ? 1'b0 : state;

- q = (~state & ~a & b) | (state & ~b);

This is a clear priority encoding using conditional (ternary) operator.

To implement, we'll:

- Use a single always @(posedge clk) to update state from next_state.
- Calculate next_state combinationally.
- Assign q combinationally from current state, a, and b.
- Initialize state to 0 for simulation.

This approach is structurally different from previous if-else priority in next_state and uses explicit conditional operator chains, making priority explicit and easy to verify.

This should match waveform behavior exactly.
