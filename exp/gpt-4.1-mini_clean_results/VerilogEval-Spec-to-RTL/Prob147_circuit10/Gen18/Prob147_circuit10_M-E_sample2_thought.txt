Analyzing the waveform, the sequential circuit has one flip-flop ("state") updated at every positive clock edge, and the output "q" is combinationally derived from the inputs "a", "b", and the current "state". The waveforms show that:

- At the first observed clock edge (time 15ns), with inputs a=0, b=0, state is undefined, after the clock edge state=0, q=0.
- "q" seems to reflect certain conditions of inputs and state, and "state" captures the previous q.

Examining transitions, "q" seems to be:

- `q = (a & ~state) | (b & state)`

Testing this against waveform data:

- When state=0 and a=0, q=0
- When state=0 and a=1, q=1
- When state=1 and b=1, q=1
- When state=1 and b=0, q=0

The output q picks 'a' when state=0, picks 'b' when state=1.

On the clock edge, the state updates to previous q.

This behavior matches the waveforms where output "q" depends on input selected by the current "state", and "state" remembers previous "q".

Hence, implement:

- q = (a & ~state) | (b & state)
- state <= q on rising clock edge

This architecture:

- The flip-flop "state" acts as a selector between inputs "a" and "b" for output "q"
- The "state" remembers the last output "q"

This solution should fully replicate the waveform behavior.
