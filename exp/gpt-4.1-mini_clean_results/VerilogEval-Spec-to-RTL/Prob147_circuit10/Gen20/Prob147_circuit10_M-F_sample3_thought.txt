The previous design incorrectly assigned the output `q` to the combinational next_state logic, causing `q` to change immediately with inputs rather than synchronously with the clock. The waveform indicates both `state` and `q` update only on the rising clock edge, implying `q` is a registered output depending on the current state and inputs at the clock edge.

From the waveform, the logic is:

- The flip-flop (`state`) stores one bit of memory.
- On each rising clock edge, `state` updates based on inputs `a` and `b` and current `state`.
- The output `q` also updates synchronously on the clock edge and depends on the new state and inputs.
- Reading carefully, `q` looks like the output of some combinational logic combining `a`, `b`, and the *new* state, but since we have only one flip-flop, `q` must be determined as a combinational function of current inputs and the *next* state.
- Therefore, `q` is registered and updated at the clock edge, along with `state`.

We can implement this by:

- Computing `next_state` combinationally from `state`, `a`, and `b`.
- On clock edge, update `state` <= `next_state`.
- Also on clock edge, compute `q` as a combinational function of inputs `a`, `b`, and the new `state` (i.e., the value just assigned).
- Because `q` depends on the newly updated state, `q` should be assigned in the sequential block after updating `state`.

By analyzing the waveform transitions:

- When `a=0` and `b=1`, next_state = 1
- When `a=1` and `b=1`, next_state = 0
- When `a=0` and `b=0`, next_state = state (hold)
- When `a=1` and `b=0`, next_state = state (hold)
