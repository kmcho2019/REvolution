From the waveforms, the circuit has a 1-bit state register (flip-flop) with output 'state'. The output q is combinational and depends on inputs a, b, and the current state.

Observing the 'state' values at clock rising edges (when clk=1), it seems the state is updated as follows:
- At 15ns (clk=1), a=0,b=0, previous state unknown but becomes 0.
- At 45ns (clk=1), a=0,b=1, state=0 → next state=1
- At 65ns (clk=1), a=1,b=1, state=0 → next state=0
- At 75ns (clk=1), a=0,b=0, state=1 → next state=1
- At 85ns (clk=1), a=1,b=1, state=0 → next state=0
- At 95ns (clk=1), a=1,b=1, state=1 → next state=1
- At 115ns (clk=1), a=1,b=0, state=1 → next state=0
- At 125ns (clk=1), a=0,b=1, state=1 → next state=0
- At 135ns (clk=1), a=0,b=0, state=1 → next state=1

Looking at these, the next state function appears to be:

next_state = (state & ~a & ~b) | (~state & ~a & b)

Which can be simplified to:

next_state = (~a & ((state & ~b) | (~state & b)))

For output q, it is same as next_state, i.e., q = next_state.

Thus, the module updates state with the next_state on posedge clk, and q outputs the next_state combinationally.

Initial state is unknown, so no reset is provided.
