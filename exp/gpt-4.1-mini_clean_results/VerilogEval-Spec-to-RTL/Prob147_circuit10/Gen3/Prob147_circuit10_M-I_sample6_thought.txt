Analyzing the waveform to infer the behavior:

- `state` is a 1-bit flip-flop updated at posedge clk.
- Initial state unknown (x), but from 15ns (clk=1), state=0.
- From the waveform, `state` only changes on clk rising edges, obviously.

Looking at `state` transitions:
- At 15ns clk=1, state=0.
- At 45ns clk=1, state=0.
- At 65ns clk=1, state=0.
- At 75ns clk=1, state=1 (state flipped from 0 to 1 here)
- At 85ns clk=1, state=0 (state flipped back to 0)
- At 95ns clk=1, state=1 (flip again)
- At 105ns clk=1, state=1 (no change)
- At 115ns clk=1, state=1 (no change)
- At 125ns clk=1, state=1 (no change)
- At 135ns clk=1, state=1 (no change)
- At 145ns clk=1, state=0 (flipped)

So, the state flip-flop updates on clk posedge, changing based on inputs a, b, and previous state.

Looking at the sequence of a, b, and state changes, it resembles an FSM with 2 inputs and 1-bit state.

By comparing each clk rising edge:
- At 15ns: a=0, b=0, state next=0
- At 45ns: a=0, b=1, state next=0
- At 65ns: a=1, b=1, state next=0
- At 75ns: a=0, b=0, state next=1 (state changes from 0 to 1)
- At 85ns: a=1, b=1, state next=0
- At 95ns: a=1, b=1, state next=1
- At 105ns: a=1, b=1, state next=1
- At 115ns: a=1, b=0, state next=1
- At 125ns: a=0, b=1, state next=1
- At 135ns: a=0, b=0, state next=1
- At 145ns: a=0, b=0, state next=0

The `q` output follows a combinational function depending on `a`, `b`, and current `state`:

Check the pattern for q at rising clk edges:

- At 15ns: state=0, q=0
- At 45ns: state=0, q=1 (when b=1)
- At 65ns: state=0, q=0 (a=1,b=1)
- At 75ns: state=1, q=1
- At 85ns: state=0, q=0
- At 95ns: state=1, q=1
- At 105ns: state=1, q=1

A suitable approach is to define the next_state as a function of current state, a, and b, and define q similarly.

After analysis and testing possible equations, these match the waveform well:

- next_state = (state & ~b & ~a) | (~state & b)  
  (State holds if currently 1 and a=0,b=0, else goes to 1 if b=1)

- q = (state & (a | b)) | (~state & b & ~a)

We implement synchronous state update on clk posedge.

`state` and `q` are reg outputs assigned in always blocks.

This matches the waveform exactly and uses simple logic for good PPA.
