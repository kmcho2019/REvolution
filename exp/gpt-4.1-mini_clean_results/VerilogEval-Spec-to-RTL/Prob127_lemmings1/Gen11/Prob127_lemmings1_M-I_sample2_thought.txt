To optimize the original design for better PPA, replace the multi-branch if-else next state logic with a single compact boolean expression for next_state. This reduces combinational logic depth and improves timing (lower TNS/WNS) by minimizing gate levels. Keep the asynchronous positive-edge reset for state initialization as required.

Specifically, the next state equation can be simplified by noting that bump_left alone or bump_left & bump_right together cause walking right, except when both bumps occur, which toggles state. This can be expressed as:

next_state = (bump_left & ~bump_right) | (bump_left & bump_right & ~state) | (~bump_left & bump_right & 1'b0) // simplified as just the two terms

But since bump_right alone causes walk_left (state=0), the full compact expression is:

next_state = (bump_left & ~bump_right) | (bump_left & bump_right & ~state)

However, this misses the bump_right-only case, so we incorporate it explicitly:

- When bumped on right only: next_state=0 (walk_left)
- When bumped on left only: next_state=1 (walk_right)
- When both bumps: next_state = ~state (toggle)
- Otherwise: next_state = state (hold)

Expressed as a boolean expression:

next_state = (bump_left & ~bump_right) | (bump_left & bump_right & ~state)

If bump_right only, next_state=0, so that part can be incorporated as

next_state = (bump_left & ~bump_right) | (bump_left & bump_right & ~state) | ( ~bump_left & bump_right & 1'b0)

which simplifies to just the first two terms because bump_right-only corresponds to 0.

To properly implement all cases in one expression:

next_state = (bump_left & ~bump_right) | (bump_left & bump_right & ~state);

But this misses bump_right-only => walk_left=0.

So, better is:

next_state = (bump_left & ~bump_right) | (bump_left & bump_right & ~state) | (~bump_left & bump_right & 1'b0);

Which is effectively:

next_state = (bump_left & ~bump_right) | (bump_left & bump_right & ~state);

And if bump_right only, next_state=0 (walk_left).

Hence the logic for bump_right-only is next_state=0, which matches walk_left state.

So the final concise expression is:

next_state = (bump_left & ~bump_right) | (bump_left & bump_right & ~state);

When bump_left=0 and bump_right=1, next_state=0, correctly.

When bump_left=0 and bump_right=0, next_state=state (need to handle hold).

Therefore, to hold state when no bump, next_state should equal state when (bump_left==0 and bump_right==0). Since in expression above next_state=0 when no bumps, which breaks hold.

Hence, we add an or condition for no bumps:

next_state = ((bump_left & ~bump_right) | (bump_left & bump_right & ~state)) | (~bump_left & ~bump_right & state);

This equation handles all cases, replacing the if-else with a single assignment.

This reduces logic levels and uses a direct boolean formula.

The rest of the design stays the same, with outputs directly from state.

This simple modification can improve timing and reduce power by minimizing logic levels.
