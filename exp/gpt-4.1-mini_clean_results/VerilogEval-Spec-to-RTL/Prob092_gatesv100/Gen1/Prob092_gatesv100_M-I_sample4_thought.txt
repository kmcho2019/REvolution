The original design is already very efficient, using simple bitwise operations with vector slices and no loops. To optimize power slightly further, we can reduce switching activity by avoiding redundant computations, specifically by reusing intermediate results or limiting bitwise operations.

One improvement is to precompute neighbor vectors to minimize repeated indexing. For example, define a "left_neighbor" and "right_neighbor" vector once and reuse it for out_both, out_any, and out_different calculations. This can reduce duplicated wire usage and may help synthesis tools optimize better.

Also, explicitly using wire types for intermediate neighbors may help synthesis with clearer intent. For out_both and out_any, we keep edge bits zero as required.

Overall, this improves readability and potentially leads to better PPA by making neighbor relationships explicit and shared, reducing total gate count and switching.
