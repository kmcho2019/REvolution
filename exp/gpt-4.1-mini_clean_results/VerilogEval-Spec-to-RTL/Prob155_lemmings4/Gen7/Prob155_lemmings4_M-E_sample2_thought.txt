I will implement a Moore FSM with three main states: WALK, FALL, DIG, plus a SPLAT terminal state. The FSM stores current direction separately. Instead of a monolithic next-state combinational block, I will separate control signals with explicit priority logic for clarity: FALL has the highest priority, then DIG, then direction switches based on bumps.

I will encode direction as 1-bit, and the state as a 3-bit one-hot encoded vector to simplify combinational logic and speed up decoding at a small area cost. The fall timer is a 6-bit saturating counter to clearly accommodate >20 cycles.

The key idea is to store walking direction in a dedicated register only updated during walk state transitions, and to separate the fall timer increment logic gated by the falling state. I will use synchronous reset for all registers, with asynchronous positive edge reset.

Outputs are derived solely from current state (Moore FSM). When SPLAT state is entered, all outputs go low indefinitely until reset.

This modular and one-hot approach facilitates clear prioritization and straightforward combinational logic. The separation of direction updates and fall timer updates enhances maintainability and potentially improves timing and power by reducing logic depth.
