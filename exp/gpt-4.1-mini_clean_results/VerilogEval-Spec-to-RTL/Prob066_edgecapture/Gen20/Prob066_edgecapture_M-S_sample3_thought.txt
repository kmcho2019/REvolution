To simplify the design, keep just one 32-bit register `prev_in` to hold the previous cycle input. On each clock edge, synchronously reset `prev_in` and `out` to zero when `reset` is high. Otherwise, update `out` by OR'ing its current value with the bits that transitioned from 1 to 0 (`prev_in & ~in`). Then update `prev_in` with the current `in`.

This straightforward approach uses minimal state and logic, no intermediate wires, and clean non-blocking assignments. It maintains functionality by latching any detected falling edges until reset. The synchronous reset and registers keep timing simple and predictable.

This is the simplest form that achieves the capture of falling edges across 32 bits with minimal complexity.
