Refactor the design by splitting the combinational detection and registered storage more explicitly. Instead of assigning `out` inside the clocked always block, let `captured` be the registered latch of falling edges, and drive `out` combinationally as the current value of `captured`. This eliminates the one-cycle latency in updating the output register and aligns output changes immediately with the stored captured edges.

Use a separate `prev_in` register to hold the previous input sampled on the clock edge, detect falling edges combinationally as `prev_in & ~in`, and update the `captured` register accordingly each clock. This keeps the detected edges latched and the output continuously driven from the latched register.

Synchronous reset clears `prev_in` and `captured`. 

This structure makes the design timing clearer: 
- `prev_in` stores previous input to detect edges on the next cycle
- `falling_edge` is a combinational signal detecting the 1->0 transition
- `captured` registers the edges latched over time
- `out` is continuously driven by `captured`, so output updates immediately after `captured` updates, without additional register stages.

This approach should meet the spec cleanly and match testbench timing expectations.
