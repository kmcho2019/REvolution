The previous implementation captures the 1-to-0 transitions correctly by comparing `prev_in` with the current input `in`. However, the key issue is the timing of input sampling relative to the reset release and how the testbench applies stimuli.

To fix this:

1. Initialize `prev_in` to all ones on reset, as before, to avoid false detections right after reset.
2. However, update `prev_in` at the end of the clock cycle, **after** capturing transitions, ensuring that the detection happens between stable samples.
3. Most importantly, clear the output on reset and ensure that no spurious transitions are detected during reset and in the first cycle after reset.
4. The output bits should latch when the transition is detected and hold until reset.
5. To further ensure correct synchronization with the testbench, treat reset as synchronous and update all registers only on the clock edge.
6. Avoid combinational feedback loops by updating `prev_in` after computing the output.
7. The logic to detect the falling edges is `(prev_in & ~in)`.
8. The output accumulates detected transitions using OR.

This approach aligns the sampling, transition detection, and reset behavior cleanly and avoids mismatch caused by asynchronous inputs or improper initialization.

This guarantees that the detection of falling edges occurs correctly at the clock where inputs have stabilized post-reset, and the output holds until reset clears it.

No extra complexity or resources are introduced.
