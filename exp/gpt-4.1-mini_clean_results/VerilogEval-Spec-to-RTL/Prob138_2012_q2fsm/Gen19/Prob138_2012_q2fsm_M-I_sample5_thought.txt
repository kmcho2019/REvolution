To optimize the FSM for better power and area while maintaining good timing, I will switch from one-hot encoding (6 bits) to binary encoding using 3 bits for 6 states. This reduces the number of flip-flops from 6 to 3, significantly saving area and power.

I will implement the next-state logic and output logic in a combinational always @(*) block using a case statement on the current state and input w. This tends to synthesize to compact and efficient logic.

The state flip-flops will be updated synchronously on the positive clock edge with a synchronous active-high reset, as requested.

The output z is combinational and will be assigned 1 only in states E or F.

This approach balances area and power reduction with clear and maintainable code, while preserving good performance due to compact logic from case statements and fewer flip-flops.
