To optimize the FSM for better performance and potentially lower power, I will use one-hot encoding instead of binary encoding. One-hot encoding uses one flip-flop per state and usually leads to faster state decoding since each state corresponds to a single bit being set, minimizing the combinational logic complexity and critical path delays. Although it may slightly increase area (flip-flop count), for a small FSM with six states this is minimal and often worthwhile for timing and power benefits. I will maintain the separate always blocks for state registers and next-state combinational logic, and will use combinational always block to assign output z directly from the one-hot encoded states E or F bits. The synchronous active-high reset will clear all state bits except state A (which will be set on reset).
