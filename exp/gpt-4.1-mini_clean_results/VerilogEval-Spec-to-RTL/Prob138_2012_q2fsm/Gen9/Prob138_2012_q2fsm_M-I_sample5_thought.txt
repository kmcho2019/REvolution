To optimize for power and area without sacrificing timing, I will switch the FSM state encoding from one-hot to binary. This reduces the number of flip-flops from six to three, lowering area and dynamic power due to fewer toggling FFs. The next-state logic becomes slightly more complex but remains manageable for a small FSM. The output logic z will be a simple combinational expression based on the binary state code for states E and F. The synchronous reset and clock edge triggering remain unchanged. This tradeoff should reduce area and power metrics significantly, possibly improving or maintaining timing.
