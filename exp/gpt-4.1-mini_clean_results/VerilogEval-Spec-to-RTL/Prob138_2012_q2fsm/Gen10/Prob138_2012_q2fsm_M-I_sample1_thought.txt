The original solution uses a one-hot encoding for states, which simplifies the next-state logic but uses more flip-flops and potentially increases area and power. To optimize PPA (performance, power, area), I'll switch to binary state encoding (3 bits for 6 states). This reduces the number of flip-flops from 6 to 3, lowering area and power.

With binary encoding, next-state logic is slightly more complex but manageable due to the small number of states. I will keep separate always blocks for state register and next-state combinational logic, and implement output logic with a continuous assignment for simplicity and minimal logic.

This approach should reduce flip-flop count and overall area, decrease switching power, and potentially improve timing by reducing register load. It trades off some logic complexity in next-state logic for gains in power and area, suitable here given the small FSM size.
