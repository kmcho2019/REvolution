To improve the given FSM for better power, performance, and area (PPA), I will switch from binary encoding to one-hot encoding since it can reduce next-state logic complexity and potentially improve timing (positive WNS). The original had a slightly negative WNS (-0.22ns). Though one-hot uses more flip-flops, it often results in simpler combinational logic and better timing, which can outweigh the area cost in many cases.

I'll keep the synchronous reset and separate always blocks for state registers and next-state logic. The output `z` will remain combinational, asserted only in states E and F.

The tradeoff is a small increase in flip-flops (6 vs 3), but simpler and faster next-state decoding should improve the clock period slack (positive WNS), possibly also reducing total power slightly due to reduced glitching in combinational logic.

Hence, the improved design uses one-hot encoding, with straightforward next state logic implemented via combinational if-else or case statements for clarity. Reset is synchronous active-high.

This approach targets improving timing slack and possibly reducing power due to simpler combinational logic, at the cost of moderate area increase.
