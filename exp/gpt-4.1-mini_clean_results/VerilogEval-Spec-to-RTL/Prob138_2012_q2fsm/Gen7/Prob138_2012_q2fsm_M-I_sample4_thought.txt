To improve the PPA metrics, specifically to reduce area and power while potentially improving timing, we will switch from one-hot encoding (6 bits) to a more compact binary encoding (3 bits) for the six states. This reduces the number of flip-flops and the width of next-state logic.

We'll assign 3-bit binary codes to states A-F. Next-state logic and output logic will be implemented using combinational always blocks with case statements.

We will keep the synchronous active-high reset, separate the sequential and combinational logic in two always blocks as required, and use blocking assignments in combinational logic.

The output z is high only in states E and F, so the output logic will be based on state codes.

This approach reduces flip-flop count from 6 to 3, reduces switching activity, and simplifies the combinational logic, resulting in better area and power without sacrificing functionality.
