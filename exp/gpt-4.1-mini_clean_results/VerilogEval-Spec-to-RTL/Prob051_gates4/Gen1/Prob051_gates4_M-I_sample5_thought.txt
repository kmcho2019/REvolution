The original design is already optimal for a simple 4-input AND, OR, and XOR computation using reduction operators, which synthesize efficiently. However, a marginal PPA improvement can be made by sharing intermediate signals where possible to potentially reduce gate count or synthesis effort.

Specifically, since XOR is associative and commutative, we can compute it stepwise with fewer levels; but the built-in reduction operator is usually optimal.

For AND and OR, we can create intermediate signals by first ANDing/ORing pairs of bits and then ANDing/ORing those results, which might lead to a smaller logic depth depending on the synthesis tool.

This approach makes the logic structure explicit and may help some synthesis tools optimize better, potentially reducing delay and area slightly.

In summary, the improvement is to implement the AND and OR outputs hierarchically instead of using single reduction operators while keeping XOR as a reduction operator due to its balanced property.

This does not degrade performance or power but may improve area and timing marginally in some toolflows.
