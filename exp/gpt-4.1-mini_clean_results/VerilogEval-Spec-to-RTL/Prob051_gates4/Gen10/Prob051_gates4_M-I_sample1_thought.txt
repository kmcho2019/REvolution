The original solution uses Verilog reduction operators which are already the most compact and efficient method for implementing multi-input AND, OR, and XOR gates. These operators typically synthesize into minimal gate-level hardware with very low delay and power. To optimize further for area and power, the design can be kept as-is since any additional logic or buffering would likely increase area and power.

One minor improvement is to reorder the outputs in the concatenation to align with a more natural bit ordering (e.g., {out_xor, out_or, out_and}) if the synthesis tool optimizes differently based on output bit order, but this is purely stylistic and unlikely to impact PPA significantly.

Since the original is minimal and elegant, I will maintain the structure but add an explicit wire declaration to reflect internal signals. This can aid some synthesis tools in optimization. Also, using explicit intermediate wires for the reductions could marginally improve readability and possibly synthesis optimization. However, this is trade-off against code brevity.

Overall, the design is already near optimal, so I will implement the explicit intermediate signals version, keeping the outputs assigned to these wires. This preserves clarity, may slightly help synthesis, but the PPA gains will be minimal or negligible.
