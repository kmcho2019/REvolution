The original solution uses built-in reduction operators which are already optimal for implementing a 4-input AND, OR, and XOR. However, slight optimization can be done by grouping inputs and reducing fan-in per logic step, which might help synthesis tools to optimize timing and power by creating balanced trees instead of a flat reduction. Specifically, compute intermediate signals for pairs of inputs and then combine these, effectively structuring the logic as a balanced tree. This potentially reduces logic depth and improves critical path delay and power slightly.

Also, breaking down XOR as a sequence of 2-input XORs preserves the associative property and may produce better optimized gate structures depending on the synthesis tool and target library.

Therefore, the improvement strategy is to:
- Create intermediate signals representing partial AND, OR, and XOR computations on input pairs.
- Combine these partial results to get final outputs, effectively implementing balanced logic trees.
- This approach may improve performance (lower critical path), and potentially reduce power by minimizing switching activity per gate.

The area increase should be negligible or none because the same logic is implemented with smaller fan-in gates and more balanced structure.
