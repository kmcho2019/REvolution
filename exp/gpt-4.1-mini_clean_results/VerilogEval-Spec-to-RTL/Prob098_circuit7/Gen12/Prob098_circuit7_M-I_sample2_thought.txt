The original design simply registers the inverted input 'a' on the rising edge of clk. To optimize for PPA without changing functionality, we can merge the inverter inside the flip-flop by inverting the stored signal at the output instead of at the input. This allows removing the explicit inverter before the DFF input, potentially reducing combinational delay and power by eliminating one gate in the critical path. Also, using a single always block to implement the flip-flop with output inversion internally saves area and power over instantiating separate modules. This refactoring keeps the behavior identical but reduces gate count and combinational delay, improving overall PPA.
