Observing the waveform, the output q behaves as the inverse of the input a delayed by one clock cycle, i.e., q at the current clock edge is the negation of a sampled at the previous clock edge. To implement this, a simple D flip-flop can store the current value of a, and then the output q is assigned as the inverted stored value. This is done by registering a in a flip-flop and outputting the negated registered value. This approach cleanly separates storage and inversion, improving clarity and potentially reducing combinational delay compared to inverting inside the flip-flop.
