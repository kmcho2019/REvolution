Improvement Strategy:
The original design inverts the input 'a' inside the DFF module via a parameterized flag. This adds a small combinational inverter before the flip-flop input, which is unnecessary overhead for this simple problem.

Since the output q is the inversion of the input 'a' captured on the rising edge of clk (q <= ~a), we can simplify the design by:
- Removing the parameterized inversion inside the DFF module.
- Using a simple standard DFF that captures its input directly.
- Inverting 'a' outside the DFF, passing ~a as the DFF input.
This reduces logic gates, switching inside the flip-flop, and area.

This is a more typical and minimal style:
- The DFF is a clean module with no inversion logic.
- The TopModule explicitly inverts input 'a' combinationally before feeding it to the flip-flop.
- This small optimization reduces power and area marginally without impacting timing.

The functionality remains unchanged: on every rising edge of clk, q becomes the inverted value of 'a'.

This approach improves PPA metrics by removing parameterization overhead and inversion logic from inside the DFF, making the RTL simpler and more efficient for synthesis.
