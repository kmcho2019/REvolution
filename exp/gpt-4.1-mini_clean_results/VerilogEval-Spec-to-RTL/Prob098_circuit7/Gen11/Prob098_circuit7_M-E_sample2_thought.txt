This waveform shows that at each rising edge of clk, the output q becomes the negation of the previous q when input a is high, and retains its value when a is low. In other words, when a=1 at a clock edge, q toggles; when a=0, q holds.

I will implement this behavior by creating a toggle flip-flop controlled by input a: q flips if a=1, otherwise q stays the same. This can be done by writing q <= q ^ a on the positive edge of clk.

This is a clean and minimal sequential design without extra combinational inversion or modules. It directly realizes the toggle logic indicated by the waveform.

Advantages:
- No separate inversion or extra wires
- Direct and simple non-blocking assignment
- Matches waveform perfectly
- Minimal resource use and straightforward RTL
