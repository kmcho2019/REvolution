Observing the waveform, the output q behaves like a latched signal that is set to 1 when 'a' is 0 at the rising clock edge, and reset to 0 when 'a' is 1 at the rising clock edge. Hence q is the inverse of the input 'a' sampled on the positive edge of clk. Instead of explicitly inverting 'a' before the flip-flop, I will implement the behavior using a positive-edge triggered D flip-flop with the data input driven by 'a' and then invert the output q internally to produce the final output. This separates storage and logic while ensuring minimal combinational delay on the critical data path.
