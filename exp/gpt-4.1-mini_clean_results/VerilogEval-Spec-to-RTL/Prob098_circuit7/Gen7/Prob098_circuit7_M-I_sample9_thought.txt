The original design implements q as the inverted version of input a registered on the positive edge of clk. This requires a flip-flop and combinational inversion inside it, which is minimal but can be optimized further.

Improvement strategy:
- Since q = ~a sampled at posedge clk, we can reinterpret this as q tracking the complement of a with one clock cycle delay.
- To optimize power and area, avoid combinational inversion inside the sequential block; instead, store the raw input 'a' in q_reg and invert the output q.
- This approach reduces the toggling inside the flip-flop (storing raw 'a' instead of inverted), potentially lowering dynamic power inside the sequential element.
- Output q is combinationally inverted from the register; this is a simple inverter buffer.
- This small combinational inversion on the output is usually less costly than having it inside the flip-flop.
- The design remains simple, clean, and synchronous with a single flip-flop.
- Non-blocking assignments are preserved for good coding practice.
- Overall, this optimization may reduce power and area slightly by removing inversion from the sequential logic and placing it at the output buffer.

I will implement a single always block flip-flop storing 'a' and output q as the inverted stored value.
