The original design implements q as the registered inversion of input a, which matches the waveform exactly. However, the continuous assignment for q creates a combinational path from the flip-flop output to q. To optimize power and slightly reduce the combinational delay, we can register q directly inside the sequential block rather than using a combinational assign. This avoids any combinational logic at the output, making q a pure registered signal. The flip-flop output q will then be the inverted previous cycle of a. This also slightly improves timing by removing the inverter from the output path and saves a small amount of dynamic power since q toggles only on clock edges, not combinationally. The area remains essentially the same, and this approach also yields a cleaner timing path.
