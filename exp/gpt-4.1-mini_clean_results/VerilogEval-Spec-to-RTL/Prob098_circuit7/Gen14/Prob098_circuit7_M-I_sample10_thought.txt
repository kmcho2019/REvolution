The original design cleanly implements q as the registered inversion of input a on the clock's rising edge. It uses an explicit inverter feeding a D flip-flop, which is modular and clear. 

To optimize for PPA:

- Performance: Combining the inversion and the flip-flop into a single always block removes the separate combinational inverter, potentially reducing the critical path and delay by making the inversion part of the flip-flop input logic rather than a separate gate before the flip-flop.
- Power: Removing the separate inverter gate reduces switching power and capacitance on the inverter output net.
- Area: The inverter logic can be absorbed into the flip-flop's input mux or logic, saving gate count.

This change simplifies the design by directly assigning q <= ~a at clock edge, eliminating the inverter wire and the separate combinational logic. It does not change the functionality but may improve timing and reduce area and power.

Thus, the improved design merges combinational inversion into the sequential block, keeping a single module without an explicit separate inverter wire or a dedicated DFF module to slightly reduce hierarchy overhead.

This leads to a minimal, synthesis-friendly, and PPA-optimized solution.
