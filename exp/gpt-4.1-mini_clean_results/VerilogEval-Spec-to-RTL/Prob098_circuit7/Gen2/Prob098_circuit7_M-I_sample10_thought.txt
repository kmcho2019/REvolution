The existing design correctly models q as the inverse of a sampled at each positive clock edge, matching the waveform perfectly. The design is already minimal and efficient: a single flip-flop whose D input is ~a.

To optimize power and area further, we can avoid unnecessary toggling of q when the sampled value doesn't change. That is, only update q if its next value differs from the current one. This can be done by adding a simple conditional check before assigning q, reducing switching activity (and thus power) and potentially reducing switching glitches.

This conditional assignment adds a small comparator (q vs ~a), but the trade-off is reduced toggling in q.

The performance impact is negligible since the comparison is simple, and the combinational path remains short.

Overall, this small change may reduce power consumption without adding significant complexity or delay.
