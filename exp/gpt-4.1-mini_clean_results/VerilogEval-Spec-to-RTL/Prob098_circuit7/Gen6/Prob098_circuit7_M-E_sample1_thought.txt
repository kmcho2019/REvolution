The waveform shows that q is updated only on the positive edge of clk and depends on both the current input 'a' and the previous value of q. Observing the transitions:

- When a=0 on clk rising edge, q stays at 1 once it becomes 1.
- When a=1 on clk rising edge, q toggles from 1 to 0.

From 15ns (clk=1, a=0), q becomes 1 and stays 1 as long as a=0 at clk edges. When a becomes 1 at 45ns rising clk, q becomes 0 and remains 0 for subsequent clk edges with a=1.

This behavior matches a latch where q is set to 1 when a=0 and cleared when a=1 on clk edges. In fact, q is the result of q_next = q & (~a).

So the circuit stores the logical AND of previous q and inverted a on each rising edge.

Implementing q <= q & ~a; on each positive clock edge will reproduce this behavior.

This approach uses a single register with combinational logic based on its current state and input a, representing a kind of state-holding AND latch that clears q when a=1.

This is a novel approach compared to the prior solution which simply stored inverted a. Here, q's next state depends on previous q and input a, implementing a kind of "hold or reset" behavior.
