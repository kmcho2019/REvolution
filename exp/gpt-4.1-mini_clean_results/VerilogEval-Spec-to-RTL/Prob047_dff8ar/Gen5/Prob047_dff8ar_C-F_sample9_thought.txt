To fuse the strengths of both examples, I will implement the eight-bit D flip-flops as a single vectorized always block with active-high asynchronous reset. This approach consolidates the flip-flops into one register, improving area efficiency and potentially reducing power by sharing reset logic. Using a vector register keeps the design concise and clear, while still fulfilling the asynchronous reset requirement. The code will use a single always block triggered on posedge clk or posedge areset, applying non-blocking assignments, which is both synthesizable and clear for sequential logic. This fusion leverages the modularity and clarity from example 2 and the area/power advantage of vectorized registers hinted at in example 1.
