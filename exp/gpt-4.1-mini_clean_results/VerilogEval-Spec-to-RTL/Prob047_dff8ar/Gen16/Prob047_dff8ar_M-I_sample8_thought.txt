To improve PPA metrics focusing on power and performance, I propose changing the asynchronous reset to a synchronous active-high reset. Synchronous reset simplifies timing analysis, reduces the risk of metastability related to asynchronous resets, and can allow better clock gating and power optimization by enabling more efficient clock gating techniques. This typically improves performance and reduces unnecessary toggling in the circuit.

Additionally, I will keep the parameterized vector DFF structure for clarity and scalability, maintaining clean modular design. The synchronous reset will be handled inside a single always block triggered only on the posedge of clk, avoiding asynchronous reset sensitivity and thus enabling synthesis tools to generate more optimized clock gating and reset logic.

This change slightly delays reset effect by one clock cycle but often leads to better overall design closure and lower dynamic power consumption, especially on modern ASIC/FPGA flows where asynchronous resets increase toggle rates and complexity.

The output is still zero after reset, and DFF behavior meets the specification with positive edge clock trigger and active high reset, now synchronous.

In summary, this improves:
- **Performance**: less critical timing uncertainty on reset paths
- **Power**: reduced glitch and switching activity on reset lines
- **Area**: potentially better optimized reset implementation with less gating

This is a standard trade-off from async reset to sync reset improving overall PPA.
