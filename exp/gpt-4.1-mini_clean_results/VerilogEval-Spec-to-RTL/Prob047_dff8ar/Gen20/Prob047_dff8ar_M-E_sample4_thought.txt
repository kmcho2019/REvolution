Instead of using a single vectorized always block to instantiate the eight D flip-flops, I will create eight separate single-bit asynchronous-reset D flip-flop modules and instantiate them individually inside the TopModule. This approach explicitly models each bit's flip-flop as a separate module instance, enhancing modularity and allowing bitwise extension or modification in the future (e.g., different reset behavior or additional logic per bit). 

Each single-bit DFF has asynchronous active-high reset, triggered on the positive edge of clk or reset. The TopModule connects these eight instances in a generate loop for clean and scalable instantiation. This design explicitly mirrors physical flip-flops in the RTL, which can help with fine-grained control in some synthesis flows or debugging.

Advantages:
- Highly modular: easy to modify/reset/control individual bits
- Clear asynchronous reset handling per flip-flop
- Easily extendable for bit-specific customizations
- Explicit clock/reset sensitivity for each bit

This differs significantly from vectorized register approach by breaking the design into multiple small, uniform units.
