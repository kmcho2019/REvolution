Implement the 8-bit asynchronous reset D flip-flops by individually instantiating eight separate 1-bit DFF modules with asynchronous active-high reset. Each DFF handles one bit of the input vector. The TopModule wires these eight flip-flops together to create the 8-bit register.

This approach offers fine-grained control over each bit's flip-flop, improves modularity by breaking down the design into smaller building blocks, and mimics how hardware often instantiates bit-slices of registers explicitly. This can help with debugging and synthesis tool optimization. The asynchronous reset is handled per flip-flop via sensitivity to posedge clk or posedge areset. All assignments use non-blocking style.

This solution is simple, clear, and easy to scale to arbitrary widths by replicating the 1-bit DFF module. It also emphasizes clear structural design rather than vectorized behavioral style.
