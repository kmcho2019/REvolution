To provide a different architectural approach, I will implement the TopModule with a single always block describing an 8-bit register with asynchronous reset. This approach uses vectorized registers and leverages the built-in ability of most synthesis tools to infer a multi-bit asynchronous-reset flip-flop array from the code.

This approach differs from the original by avoiding explicit instantiation of individual flip-flops, resulting in a compact and clean RTL description that is still synthesizable and meets the specification.

The asynchronous reset is handled by including it in the sensitivity list alongside the positive edge of the clock. When areset is high, the entire 8-bit output register q resets to zero immediately (asynchronously). Otherwise, at every positive clock edge, q takes the value of d.

This vectorized always block simplifies code, likely reduces area and power due to less overhead from multiple instances, and is straightforward to understand. It also fully satisfies the problem requirements for asynchronous reset and positive-edge clock triggering.
