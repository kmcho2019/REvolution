To improve the original implementation's performance and reduce the critical path, I will separate the next-state logic into a combinational block that computes the next value of the LFSR, then register it on the clock edge. This avoids multiple dependent non-blocking assignments within the same always block.

I will compute the feedback once as XOR of the tapped bits and the output bit q[0]. Then perform a right shift of the register with this feedback injected at the MSB. This reduces combinational depth and avoids multiple XOR gates on sequential logic.

The synchronous reset remains active-high and sets the LFSR to 1.

This approach keeps the design behavior identical but optimizes for synthesis by minimizing logic levels and making the feedback explicit and combinational.
