To refactor the design, I will separate the combinational logic that computes the next state of the LFSR from the sequential state update. This involves creating a wire `next_q` that holds the next state of the 5-bit LFSR based on the current state `q`. The feedback is calculated according to the Galois LFSR taps at bit positions 5 and 3 (q[4] and q[2]). Then in an always block triggered on the positive edge of the clock, the register `q` is updated with `next_q`, or reset synchronously to 1. This approach improves code clarity by isolating combinational logic and sequential logic, aiding readability and potential synthesis optimizations.
