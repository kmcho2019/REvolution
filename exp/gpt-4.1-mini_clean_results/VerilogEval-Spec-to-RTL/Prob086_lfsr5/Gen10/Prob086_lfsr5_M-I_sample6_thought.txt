To improve performance and timing while maintaining power and area efficiency, I will implement a combinational next-state logic for the 5-bit Galois LFSR. This approach eliminates read-after-write hazards by calculating all next bits based on the current state before registering them on the clock edge. This reduces combinational logic depth and helps timing closure.

I will:
- Introduce a wire [4:0] next_q to hold the computed next state.
- Assign next_q bits combinationally, applying the Galois LFSR taps (bits 5 and 3) XORed with the LSB.
- On the clock edge, if reset is asserted, synchronously reset to 1; else update q with next_q.
- This preserves the synchronous reset behavior and maximal-length LFSR characteristics.

This approach balances performance, power, and area:
- Performance: Reduced critical path through combinational logic before register, improving frequency.
- Power: Minimal change; combinational XORs are small and only one set of flip-flops.
- Area: Slightly more combinational logic but still very small for 5-bit LFSR.

Overall, this method cleanly separates combinational and sequential logic, facilitating better synthesis and timing results.
