Refactor the 5-bit maximal-length Galois LFSR by implementing the shift register as a combinational next-state logic followed by a synchronous register update. Instead of directly coding the shifting and XOR in the always block with individual bit assignments, compute the next state in a combinational always block and then register it synchronously. This separates combinational next-state logic and sequential state update, improving readability and making the structure clearer. The reset remains synchronous and active-high, setting the state to 5'b00001. Feedback is computed inside the combinational block from the LSB of the current state. This approach also makes it straightforward to expand or modify the LFSR if needed.
