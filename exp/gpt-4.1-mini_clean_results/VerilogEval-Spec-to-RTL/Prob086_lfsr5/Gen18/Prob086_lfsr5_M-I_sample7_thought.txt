The existing design is functionally correct and minimal in complexity for a 5-bit Galois LFSR with taps at bits 5 and 3. To optimize for better PPA (performance, power, area), we can reduce XOR gate fanout and logic depth by adjusting the update order. Instead of directly using q[0] multiple times in the same cycle, we can precompute the feedback signal once and then use it to update only the tapped bits. This avoids multiple XORs involving q[0] in parallel, potentially reducing critical path delay and switching activity.

Specifically, precompute the feedback bit as q[0] XOR q[2] (since taps are at bits 5 and 3 in 1-based indexing, corresponding to q[4] and q[2] zero-based). Then update the shift register bits with the feedback, only applying XOR at tapped positions, and shifting other bits normally. This approach is a common optimization for Galois LFSRs.

This will also reduce XOR gate count from two per cycle to one per cycle and simplify the logic. The synchronous reset remains the same. This should marginally improve timing and power while maintaining minimal area.
