To optimize the original design for better PPA, I will compute the entire next state as a combinational logic vector before registering it on the clock edge. This reduces the number of individual bit assignments and potentially eases timing and power optimizations by synthesis tools. 

The feedback bit is the current LSB q[0]. For taps at bit positions 5 (q[4]) and 3 (q[2]), only q[2] receives XOR with feedback. The other bits shift right by one position directly. 

By defining next_q as combinational logic derived from q and feedback, and assigning q <= next_q on the clock edge, we simplify the sequential logic and promote cleaner synthesis. The reset behavior remains synchronous and active-high, setting q to 5'b00001.

This approach improves timing by reducing sequential dependencies, lowers dynamic power through a single vector update, and potentially reduces area by allowing logic sharing for the feedback calculation.
