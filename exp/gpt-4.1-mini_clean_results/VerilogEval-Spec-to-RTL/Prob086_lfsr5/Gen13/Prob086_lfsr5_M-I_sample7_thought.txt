To optimize the design for better performance, power, and area, we will compute the next state of the 5-bit Galois LFSR as a single combinational vector before registering it on the clock edge. This approach shortens the critical path by isolating combinational logic from sequential logic, enabling the synthesis tool to optimize more effectively.

We will implement the taps at bit positions 5 and 3 (corresponding to q[4] and q[2], since q[0] is LSB), where the feedback is q[0]. The next state bits are computed according to the Galois configuration:

- q_next[4] = q[0] ^ q[4]; // tap at bit 5
- q_next[3] = q[3];
- q_next[2] = q[0] ^ q[2]; // tap at bit 3
- q_next[1] = q[1];
- q_next[0] = q[1];

Finally, we use a single non-blocking assignment to update the register q at each positive clock edge with synchronous reset.

This reduces switching activity, simplifies the code, and helps the synthesis tool to improve timing and area utilization.
