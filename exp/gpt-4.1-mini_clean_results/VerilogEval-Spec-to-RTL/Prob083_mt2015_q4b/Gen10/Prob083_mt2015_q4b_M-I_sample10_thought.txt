The original solution implements z as the XNOR of inputs x and y, which correctly matches the waveform: z=1 when x and y are equal, otherwise 0. This is already minimal combinational logic and optimal in area and power. Given the extremely low power and minimal area reported, no further gate-level optimization is possible.

However, a slight improvement can be made by explicitly using the Verilog logical equality operator (==) instead of the bitwise XNOR (~^). This may improve code readability and clarify intent without any impact on PPA.

Thus, I will replace the assign with: assign z = (x == y);

This is functionally identical, synthesizes to the same hardware, and can be preferred for clarity. No structural changes to logic or extra gate levels are added.

Hence, the improved solution is functionally and PPA equivalent, but slightly more idiomatic and readable.
