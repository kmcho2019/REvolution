The original design uses a combinational always block with a case statement to output z=1 when x and y are equal, otherwise 0. While correct and clear, this approach compiles into a small multiplexer logic for the case statement, which is slightly more complex than a direct logic gate.

To optimize for better performance, power, and area, the logic can be implemented using a simple continuous assignment with a single XNOR gate: z = ~(x ^ y). This is the minimal, canonical way to detect equality between two bits, synthesizing into a single XNOR gate with minimal area and power.

This approach reduces gate count and eliminates the always block, reducing synthesis and simulation complexity. It also clearly expresses the logic intent, making the code concise and efficient.

Summary of improvement strategy:
- Replace the always @(*) case statement with a continuous assignment.
- Use a direct XNOR operator for minimal logic.
- Keep module interface unchanged.
