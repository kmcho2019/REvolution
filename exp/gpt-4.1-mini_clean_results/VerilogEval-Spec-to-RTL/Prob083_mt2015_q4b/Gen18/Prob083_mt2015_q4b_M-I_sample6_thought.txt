The original implementation uses a 2-to-1 MUX with an explicit inverter to produce an XNOR-like function. To optimize power and area, I will remove the intermediate wire and express the output directly as a Boolean expression using only basic operators, specifically using the XNOR operator (`~^`), which synthesizes efficiently in most tools. This reduces unnecessary gates and wiring, potentially lowering switching activity and area.

Since the waveform corresponds exactly to the XNOR of inputs x and y, `z = ~(x ^ y)` is the simplest and cleanest expression that matches the functionality.

This approach eliminates the explicit inverter and mux operator, allowing the synthesis tool to generate the minimal logic, improving power and area metrics without changing functionality.
