Fuse the strengths of both examples by implementing z as the negation of the XOR of x and y, i.e., z = ~(x ^ y). This is functionally equivalent to the equality check but uses a dedicated XOR gate with a simple inverter, which can lead to slightly more efficient hardware in many synthesis tools than the equality operator or separate AND/OR gates. The continuous assignment keeps the design purely combinational with minimal area and power. This approach combines clarity, minimal area, and optimal power by leveraging basic gates efficiently.
