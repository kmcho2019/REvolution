The original implementation correctly models the XNOR behavior between inputs x and y using an always block and if-else statements. However, this approach is more verbose and may infer unnecessary logic. To optimize for power and area, the logic can be simplified into a continuous assignment using the built-in XNOR operator (~^(bitwise XNOR) or equivalently ~(x ^ y)). This leads to a smaller gate count, reduces switching activity by eliminating the always block and conditional logic, and improves readability and synthesis quality. The performance impact is neutral since the combinational delay of a single gate is minimal. Therefore, I'll replace the always block with a single continuous assignment statement for z.
