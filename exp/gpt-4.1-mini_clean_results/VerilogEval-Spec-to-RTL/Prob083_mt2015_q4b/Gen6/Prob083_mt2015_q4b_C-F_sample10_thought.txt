From a hardware perspective, the XNOR operator directly maps to an XNOR gate, which is the natural and minimal logic implementation. The equality operator in Verilog is often synthesized to the same hardware but is semantically more generic (it can be used with multi-bit vectors). For single-bit inputs, the XNOR operator is more explicit about the intended hardware, improving code clarity without sacrificing simplicity.

The fusion strategy is to explicitly use the XNOR operator to express equivalence clearly and keep the code concise and readable, while also commenting the intent to clarify that z is the equivalence of x and y. This gives the clarity of intent and minimal hardware, effectively combining the conceptual clarity of example 1 with the hardware specificity of example 2.
