The original design implements z as the XNOR of inputs x and y, which is logically minimal and directly matches the desired waveform. From a gate-level perspective, this requires an XOR gate plus an inverter. However, XNOR gates are usually available as a single gate in standard cell libraries, often offering better power and area efficiency compared to an explicit XOR plus inverter implementation.

Therefore, the improvement is to use the built-in Verilog XNOR operator (^) in a direct assignment: z = x ~^ y; This explicitly uses the XNOR operator instead of negating an XOR, which synthesis tools can map directly to an XNOR gate, potentially improving area and power.

Additionally, since the module only needs one line of combinational logic, keeping it succinct and clear benefits optimization. This change does not affect functionality but could yield better PPA.

Hence, the optimization is to use the XNOR operator (~^) instead of negating XOR (~(x ^ y)).

No other structural or behavioral changes are necessary.
