To provide a completely different architectural approach, I will implement the logic using a small two-input priority encoder style with explicit gates. Instead of using a direct combinational case or XNOR, I will express the logic in terms of basic AND, OR, and NOT gates instantiated as primitives.

The output `z` is '1' if both inputs are equal (00 or 11). This is equivalent to `(x AND y) OR (~x AND ~y)`.

I will implement two AND gates to detect the equal pairs and then an OR gate to combine them.

This structural gate-level design contrasts with behavioral always blocks or continuous assignments, giving a clear hardware perspective and a novel architecture.

Summary:
- Implement equal-input detection via two AND gates: one for both high, one for both low.
- Combine the two AND gate outputs with an OR gate.
- Output z is the result.
- Use gate primitives (and, or, not) to emphasize explicit hardware.
