The original design used a continuous assignment with the XNOR operator directly on inputs x and y. To refactor while preserving the same functionality, I will implement the logic inside a combinational always block using a procedural assignment. This approach explicitly demonstrates the output z depends combinationally on inputs x and y, which might be more suitable in some coding styles or for pedagogical clarity.

The always block will use if-else conditions to replicate the XNOR behavior: z is 1 when x equals y, else 0. This maintains the minimal logic and exact behavior shown in the waveform but provides a structural variation by expressing the logic procedurally rather than with a continuous assign.

This refactor introduces no sequential logic or clock domain, so timing and area characteristics remain the same. It’s a stylistic alternative that may be preferred in contexts emphasizing procedural blocks over continuous assignments.
