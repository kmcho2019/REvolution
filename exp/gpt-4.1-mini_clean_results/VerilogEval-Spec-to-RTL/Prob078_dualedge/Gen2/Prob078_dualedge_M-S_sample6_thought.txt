A simpler approach is to use a single always block triggered on the positive edge of clk and a delayed version of clk (clk_d) that follows clk by one half clock cycle. By clocking a second flip-flop on the posedge of clk_d, which is the inverted clk delayed by half a cycle, we effectively capture 'd' on both clock edges. The output q then selects between the two stored values based on clk, similar to before but without separate modules or negedge triggered flip-flops. This removes the need for explicit negedge flip-flops and uses just two positive-edge triggered flip-flops with a generated delayed clock.
