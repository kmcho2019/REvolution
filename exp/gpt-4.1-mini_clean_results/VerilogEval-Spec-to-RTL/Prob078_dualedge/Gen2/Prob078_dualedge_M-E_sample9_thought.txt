Instead of using two flip-flops clocked by clk and inverted clk, we can use a single positive-edge triggered flip-flop clocked by clk, combined with an edge detection circuit that detects the negative edge of clk to latch the data into another register.

Specifically:
- Use a register that samples the input d at the positive edge of clk.
- Use another register clocked at the positive edge of clk to hold the previous clk state.
- Detect the negative edge of clk by comparing current clk and previous clk.
- When a negative edge is detected (current clk=0 and previous clk=1), sample d into a register.
- Finally, use a 2-to-1 multiplexer controlled by clk to select between the posedge-sampled data and the negedge-sampled data.

This uses only positive-edge triggered flip-flops and combinational logic, and no inverted clock is needed. The output updates on both edges, emulating a dual-edge triggered flip-flop.

This approach:
- avoids the use of an inverted clock as a clock input,
- uses a simple edge detection logic,
- maintains a clean synchronous design suitable for FPGAs.
