Use a single D flip-flop clocked on the rising edge of clk, combined with a toggle flip-flop clocked on the falling edge of clk to detect edges. By storing the input data at both edges through these two flip-flops and then selecting the appropriate output with a simple combinational logic based on clk level, we can mimic dual-edge triggered behavior without using a latch or complex mux. This reduces complexity by avoiding the transparent latch and separate mux, and only requires basic flip-flops and a combinational output assignment.
