To fuse the best ideas of both examples for a superior dual-edge triggered flip-flop design, we will:

- Use two flip-flops triggered on positive and negative edges of clk, respectively, to capture data on both edges.
- Replace the transparent latch (from Example 2) with a glitch-free clock-level multiplexer output implemented as a registered mux stage synchronized to clk.
- To avoid glitches and reduce dynamic power, the mux output selection will be sampled on the positive edge of clk from the latch/mux stage that chooses q_pos or q_neg based on clk level, essentially double-registering the output for glitch reduction and stable timing.
- This approach avoids combinational mux glitches and the hold time issues of transparent latches.
- The extra output register improves performance by isolating mux delay from the primary data path and reduces switching glitches compared to a continuous mux or transparent latch alone.
- The output register can be a lightweight flip-flop; this adds minimal area but improves timing closure and power efficiency.
- Overall, this hybrid combines the glitch-free mux from Example 1 and the glitch/power reduction advantages of the latch in Example 2, enhanced by adding a registered stage for stable output.

This design balances timing, power, and area better: dual edge data capture (two flip-flops), glitch-free output muxing, and registered stable output, ideal for FPGA implementation.
