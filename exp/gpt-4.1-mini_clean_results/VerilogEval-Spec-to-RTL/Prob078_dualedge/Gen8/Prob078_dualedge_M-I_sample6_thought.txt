To improve the original solution for PPA, I will maintain the dual flip-flop approach but merge both flip-flops into a single always block clocked on posedge and negedge using non-blocking assignments in synthesis-friendly style, avoiding any combinational always block or mux with clk in continuous assignment. However, since the original sensitivity list with both edges is illegal, the alternative is to use two always blocks for posedge and negedge as before.

To optimize power and area, I will remove the separate EdgeFF and EdgeFFNeg modules and combine them into one module with two registers updated on posedge and negedge, reducing hierarchy and potential overhead. Also, I will implement the output mux as a registered output that changes state on clock edges, reducing glitches from mux switching on clk level.

This means:

- Two internal registers: q_pos updated on posedge clk, q_neg updated on negedge clk.
- A small always block triggered on both edges that updates q by selecting q_pos or q_neg depending on clk state. Since always @ (posedge clk or negedge clk) is illegal, I will implement two always blocks: one posedge for q_pos, one negedge for q_neg, and a combinational continuous assign for q driven by q_pos and q_neg but synchronized with clk to avoid glitches by adding a small register or using gated clk signal.

Given the constraint, the best practical approach is to keep the two flip-flops and use a clocked register for output q, clocked on posedge clk only, that latches the selected value between q_pos and q_neg, effectively double-registering q and reducing glitches and dynamic power on q.

This reduces glitches and switching activity on output and improves timing robustness. The cost is an extra register stage on output q, which usually is acceptable.

Thus, the final structure:

- Two registers updated on posedge and negedge respectively.
- Output q updated on posedge clk selecting the correct value (q_pos if clk=1 else q_neg).
- This synchronizes output changes to posedge clk, reducing glitching mux output and controlling switching power.

This is a classic technique to implement dual-edge triggered FF behavior in single-edge FPGAs while minimizing glitches.
