To create a superior dual-edge triggered flip-flop implementation, I will fuse the best ideas from both examples:

- Keep modularity by having separate positive-edge and negative-edge triggered flip-flop modules, each clearly defined and synthesizable.
- Use continuous assignment for output muxing (as in both examples) to improve synthesis clarity and reduce combinational always blocks.
- To minimize glitches and improve timing, instead of directly muxing on the asynchronous clock signal, use a small internal registered version of the clock signal delayed by one half-cycle to drive the mux select. However, since the design must remain simple and synthesizable without extra complexity, and since this delay line may not be trivial or supported, I will implement a stable clock level detection using an internal combinational signal that tracks the current clock phase.
- To improve glitch reduction, synchronize the mux select signal from the clock by sampling the clock in a single edge-triggered FF (generally the same clock). But since the input is clock and the output is muxed by clock, this can introduce delay and complexity. Therefore, I will rely on the simple and well-known approach: mux output with the raw clock signal, which is standard practice for this technique and accepted in the previous examples.
- Use non-blocking assignments in sequential blocks for proper synthesis.
- Add comments for clarity and maintain best coding practices.

This fused strategy keeps design clarity, legal Verilog, standard FPGA friendliness, minimal area overhead, and the fundamental approach known for dual-edge FF emulation.
