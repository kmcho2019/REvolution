The existing design correctly mimics a dual-edge triggered flip-flop by using two flip-flops clocked on opposite edges and multiplexing their outputs based on the clk level. However, the combinational mux controlled by clk introduces a potential timing bottleneck on q due to switching clk in the data path, which may impact max frequency and power efficiency.

To optimize performance and power without increasing latency, I will:

1. Avoid using clk as a combinational select signal to reduce glitching and timing sensitivity. Instead, use a synchronized internal toggle signal that changes state at every clock edge to select between q_pos and q_neg.

2. Generate a toggle signal (toggle_clk) that flips at every clk edge (pos and neg) using the two flip-flops outputs, creating a stable select signal.

3. Use the toggle signal to select between the q_pos and q_neg flip-flop outputs in a registered output stage, synchronizing q to clk with a single clocked flip-flop, removing any combinational mux on q.

This removes the combinational mux on clk, lowers glitching on q, and improves timing and power. The area overhead is a single flip-flop for the toggle signal and no combinational mux.

This approach keeps the functional behavior identical and makes the q output timing easier to meet at higher frequencies.
