To optimize the dual-edge triggered flip-flop implementation, we reduce power and area by replacing the output-level mux with a transparent latch. This latch, enabled by the clock level, selects data from either the positive-edge or negative-edge triggered flip-flop. Unlike a continuous mux that toggles output based on the clock level (causing potential glitches and switching power), the latch holds stable data during clock phases, minimizing output glitches and dynamic power.

This approach retains the functional equivalence but with potentially less glitching and lower switching power at the output stage, improving power efficiency without increasing area.

We merge the posedge and negedge FFs into one generic EdgeFF module instantiated twice for clarity. The latch is enabled by clk (high) and disabled when clk is low, capturing q_pos during clk=1, and passing q_neg during clk=0 (since latch is transparent only when clk=1).

Overall, the design maintains clarity and modularity while reducing output mux toggling, helping power and potentially improving signal integrity.
