To implement a dual-edge triggered flip-flop without using illegal sensitivity lists or duplicating storage elements clocked on opposite edges, I will create a design that samples the input on the rising edge and then updates the output on the falling edge using an intermediate register. Specifically:

- Use a positive-edge triggered flip-flop (master) to capture the input d on the rising edge of clk.
- Use a negative-edge triggered flip-flop (slave) to capture the master's output q_master on the falling edge of clk.
- The output q comes from the slave flip-flop.
- This master-slave arrangement effectively updates the output on both edges of clk by first sampling d on the rising edge, then transferring it to the output on the falling edge.
- This approach avoids illegal always blocks, uses only standard flip-flops on single edges, and does not mux signals at runtime, simplifying timing and avoiding glitches.
- It resembles a classical master-slave flip-flop but explicitly uses both edges of clk via two single-edge triggered FFs.
