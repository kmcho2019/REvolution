To optimize power and area while maintaining correct dual-edge triggered behavior, we can replace the transparent latch with a clocked MUX approach using registered clock phase signals. Specifically:

- Use a positive edge-triggered flip-flop and a negative edge-triggered flip-flop as before.
- Instead of a transparent latch sensitive to clock level, generate a 1-bit clock phase indicator signal that is registered to minimize glitches.
- Use a clocked multiplexer (controlled by the clock phase signal) on the output, implemented with a simple conditional assignment in a synchronous process.
- This reduces glitching and switching power caused by latch transparency and combinational mux toggling.
- It also simplifies timing analysis and may improve hold time reliability by removing level-sensitive latch timing.
- This approach slightly reduces area by replacing a latch with a small register and a clocked MUX.
- Overall, it retains functional correctness but improves power, area, and timing characteristics.

Note: We add a small register to capture clk phase to avoid glitches on the mux select signal, which would otherwise cause switching at output. The clk phase register is clocked on posedge clk; for the clk phase signal on negedge, we exploit the internal flip-flop clocking. This solution is a balance between complexity and efficiency.
