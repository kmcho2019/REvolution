To improve the previous dual-edge triggered flip-flop design, the main objective is to reduce the mux delay and redundant toggling to improve timing and power while maintaining the functional equivalence. Instead of using two independent flip-flops triggered on opposite clock edges and then muxing their outputs, we can implement a single flip-flop that captures data on the rising edge and a simple latch transparent on the falling edge. This reduces sequential elements and mux complexity because the output can be derived from a flip-flop and a latch controlled by clk, ensuring data is captured on both edges without introducing a mux between two registers.

The improved design uses a rising edge triggered flip-flop to capture 'd' on the rising edge and a falling edge level-sensitive latch (transparent when clk=0) to capture data on the falling edge. The output 'q' is then driven by either the flip-flop's output or the latch output depending on clk level. This eliminates the muxing of two register outputs based on clk and reduces sequential logic by one flip-flop, improving timing and power.
