To implement a dual-edge triggered flip-flop without illegal sensitivity lists, I will fuse the best ideas of the two examples while addressing their limitations:

- Retain two single-edge triggered flip-flops: one positive-edge triggered, one negative-edge triggered. This is the fundamental approach for dual-edge behavior.
- Instead of using a transparent latch or a continuous clock-level mux (both can cause glitches or latch transparency issues), I will register the clock level signal in a dedicated flip-flop synchronized to the clock’s rising edge. This creates a stable, glitch-free mux select signal delayed by one clock cycle.
- Use this registered clock-level signal to multiplex between the two flip-flop outputs on the output stage with a purely combinational mux, reducing glitching and hold violations.
- This approach combines the glitch-free stable select of Example 1 with the glitch reduction strategy of Example 2, but avoids level-sensitive latch transparency or direct muxing on raw clock.
- All registers use non-blocking assignments for proper sequential behavior.
- This technique is synthesizable on FPGAs, uses only allowed sensitivity lists, reduces glitches at mux output, and minimizes hold time violations on mux select.
- Output port `q` is a reg updated in a clocked always block on posedge clk to safely register the mux output, reducing timing hazards further.
- Overall, this design yields good timing, minimal glitches, and clean functional dual-edge triggering with reasonable power and area.

This fusion strategy preserves dual-edge triggering by capturing data on both clock edges in separate flip-flops, uses a registered clock-level signal for glitch-free output selection, and registers the final output to stabilize timing and reduce glitches.
