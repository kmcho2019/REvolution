Using a single always block sensitive to the rising edge, and generating a derived signal that toggles on every clock edge, is not straightforward. Instead, since dual-edge triggering requires capturing data at both edges, the minimum is to have two registers. However, we can simplify by eliminating the combinational mux and use a clocked output register updated at every edge: we can generate an internal clock signal with both edges (e.g., a doubled frequency clock) using a clock domain crossing trick is complex. Since the problem restricts using both edges in sensitivity list and we want to avoid combinational muxes, the simplest acceptable design is to have two registers clocked at posedge and negedge, and assign q to one of them using a clock-synchronized flip-flop: actually, that's more complex.
