The original design uses two always blocks to register the input 'd' on the rising and falling edges of 'clk' and a combinational always block to multiplex the outputs based on clk level. To refactor while keeping the core concept, we can implement the two flip-flops as separate modules instantiated inside the top module, each triggered on opposite edges, to separate concerns more cleanly. We then use a continuous assign statement (instead of an always block) for the output mux. This structure improves modularity and replaces the output mux always block with a simple assign statement, potentially improving synthesis clarity and maintaining the same function.
