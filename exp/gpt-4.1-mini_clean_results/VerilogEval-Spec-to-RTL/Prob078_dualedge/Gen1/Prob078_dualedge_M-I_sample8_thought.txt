The original solution correctly implements dual-edge triggered behavior using two flip-flops clocked on opposite edges and a combinational mux. However, this mux in the output introduces a combinational path on every clock edge, which could limit the maximum frequency and cause timing issues.

To optimize for performance (reduce critical path delay) and power (avoid redundant toggling at the output), I will register the mux output with a third flip-flop clocked on the rising edge. This creates a synchronous output `q` with stable timing and reduces glitching.

Also, to reduce toggling on the negedge flop (q_neg), I will gate its data capture by detecting changes in `d` or by enabling it only when clk is low. Since such clock gating is tricky in Verilog and may not be synthesizable or necessary, the main gain is from registering the output.

This approach trades a 1-cycle latency for improved timing and power. This is generally acceptable in FPGA designs and improves overall PPA.

Summary of changes:
- Add a third flip-flop `q` clocked at posedge clk.
- Drive the input of this flop from the mux selecting between `q_pos` and `q_neg`.
- Remove combinational assignment of `q`.
