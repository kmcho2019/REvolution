To optimize the dual-edge triggered flip-flop for better PPA, I will focus on reducing glitching and critical path delay on the output mux, as well as lowering dynamic power by minimizing unnecessary toggling.

Improvements:

- Replace the continuous assign mux with a register-based latch to synchronize the output selection. This mitigates glitches and reduces the critical path delay caused by the mux toggling directly on clk level.
- Use a two-stage approach: sample data on posedge and negedge into two flip-flops, then use a simple register clocked on posedge to latch the output selection. This pipelining balances timing by removing combinational mux on clk level.
- Eliminate generating inverted clock signals; use single clock domain.
- Maintain modular EdgeFF for clarity and reusability.
- This approach introduces a cycle latency (output delayed by half clock), but improves timing and power by reducing glitches and glitch-induced switching.
- This is a classic approach to implement dual-edge sampling in single-edge FF FPGAs with better timing and power trade-offs.

The trade-off is minor latency for improved timing and power efficiency, which is beneficial for high-frequency designs.

This approach will lower dynamic power by reducing toggling from mux glitches, lower mux critical path delay, and possibly improve max frequency and area due to simpler routing and logic.
