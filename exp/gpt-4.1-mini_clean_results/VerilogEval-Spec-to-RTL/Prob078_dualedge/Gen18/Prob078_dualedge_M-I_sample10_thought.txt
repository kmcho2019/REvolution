To improve the initial design for better performance, power, and area (PPA) while preserving functional correctness and synthesizability, I adopt these enhancements:

- Replace the transparent latch with a clocked mux approach to avoid level-sensitive latches, which often cause hold-time violations and increase power due to extended transparency windows.
- Use a flip-flop to register the mux output, clocked by the rising edge of clk, thereby doubling the clock frequency internally using clk and its negation.
- Generate a negated clock inside the module via inverting the clk signal (allowed as a wire assignment), not using it as a clock domain; instead, we sample data at both edges using two flip-flops.
- Use a mux to select between the outputs of the two flip-flops based on the clock (clk) signal: when clk=0, output the negedge-sampled value; when clk=1, output the posedge-sampled value. This mux is synchronous (registered) rather than transparent latch to reduce glitches and hold violations.
- This approach converts the dual-edge sampling problem into a 2-flop, 1-mux, and 1 FF stage, still simple but better balanced for timing and power, as the output register filters glitches and switching.
- Using clock inversion as a wire does not violate FPGA guidelines since no always block is triggered on negedge clk directly.
- Non-blocking assignments everywhere to guide synthesis and simulation consistency.
- This modification reduces latch-related timing issues, limits glitching at output, and slightly improves power by removing level-sensitive transparency.

This solution maintains the dual-edge triggering behavior, improves timing closure by eliminating level-sensitive latch and asynchronous mux, and likely reduces switching power and area by removing the latch and adding a registering stage, a good PPA tradeoff.
