To optimize the existing dual-edge flip-flop implementation for better Power, Performance, and Area (PPA), I will apply the following improvements:

1. Replace the transparent latch mux at the output with a simple clocked mux implemented using a flip-flop enabled by the clock level. This reduces the complexity of latch-based timing, improves timing closure by removing transparency, and removes glitches on the output.

2. Eliminate the explicit transparent latch and instead use a 2-to-1 mux driven by the clock level and clocked by the positive edge. This removes latch timing complexities and allows synthesis tools to better optimize flip-flop usage and reduce glitches.

3. Keep two flip-flops capturing input data on posedge and negedge respectively as required.

4. Use a positive edge clocked register to select and hold the output from either posedge or negedge FF output depending on the clock level at the last clock edge. This avoids glitches at output transitions and reduces power compared to latch transparency.

5. This approach reduces glitching and transparent latch overhead, helping timing and power. It may slightly increase latency by 1 clock cycle but significantly enhances PPA and FPGA friendliness.

This method balances the advantages of dual-edge triggering with practical FPGA implementation constraints and synthesis friendliness.
