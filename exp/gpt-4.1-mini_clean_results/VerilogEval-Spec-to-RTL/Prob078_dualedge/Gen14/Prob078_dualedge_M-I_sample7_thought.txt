To improve the original solution, I will focus on reducing the latch transparency duration to lower switching power and minimize glitches without sacrificing correctness. Instead of a transparent latch controlled by the clock level (which is transparent throughout the clock level), I'll implement an edge-triggered multiplexing scheme that updates the output only on clock edges, effectively mimicking dual-edge behavior with less glitching and switching.

Concretely:

- Retain two flip-flops capturing data on posedge and negedge.
- Replace the latch with a clock-edge triggered mux register updated on both edges.
- Use a local signal toggled on every clock edge to select which flip-flop output to latch.
- This output register updates only on clock edges, reducing glitch exposure and dynamic power.
- This approach strictly uses only posedge or negedge always blocks (no illegal dual-edge sensitivity).
- Non-blocking assignments are used consistently.
- This also reduces hold-time complexity caused by a level-sensitive latch.

This approach trades the latch for a dual-edge updated register controlled by a toggle, preserving dual-edge behavior but with cleaner output transitions and less dynamic power.

Area is improved by removing the latch and its required feedback path, and the output register is minimal overhead. Performance is improved by removing latch transparency and potential hold violations around clock transitions.

This design is synthesizable on typical FPGAs and preserves the original functionality with better PPA trade-offs.
