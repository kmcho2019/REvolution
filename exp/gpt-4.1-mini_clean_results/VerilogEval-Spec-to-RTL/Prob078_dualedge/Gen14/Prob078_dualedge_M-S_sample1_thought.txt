To simplify the dual-edge triggered flip-flop implementation while maintaining functionality, we can avoid using two separate flip-flops and a latch/mux. Instead, we sample the input on the positive edge and update the output on the negative edge using a single always block triggered by the clock edges in sequence.

Since the standard Verilog does not allow combining posedge and negedge in sensitivity lists directly, a common technique is to double the clock frequency by toggling an internal clock signal at each clk edge, and then use a posedge triggered flip-flop on this doubled clock to sample d on both edges of clk.

However, this may increase complexity.

A simpler approach is to store the sampled data from both edges into a register by using two separate flip-flops and combine their outputs with a simple mux controlled by clk level (instead of a latch), which is easier to synthesize and simpler logically.

Therefore, the simplification is:

- Instantiate two flip-flops triggered on posedge and negedge of clk, respectively, both sampling d.
- Output q is assigned combinationally by a simple mux selecting q_pos or q_neg depending on clk level.
- Use assign statement instead of always block for output mux.
- Avoid the transparent latch for output to reduce complexity.
- Keep code short and readable.

This approach eliminates the always @(*) block with if-else and replaces it with a simple continuous assignment, simplifying code without changing behavior.
