Implement a toggle flip-flop design that captures the input d at the rising edge, and then uses a high-frequency divided clock generated internally to latch data on the falling edge by toggling a secondary register at the inverted clock phase. Specifically, use a single positive-edge triggered flip-flop to capture d on the rising edge, then create an inverted clock domain by generating clk_n = ~clk. Use a second positive-edge triggered flip-flop clocked by clk_n to latch the data from the first flip-flop, effectively capturing data on the falling edge of clk. Finally, combine these two registered values with a latch transparent when clk is low or high, avoiding illegal sensitivity lists and ensuring synthesizability. This approach emulates dual-edge triggered behavior by splitting data capture into two clock domains synchronized to opposite clock edges.
