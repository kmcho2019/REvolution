To improve the PPA metrics of the dual-edge triggered flip-flop implementation, I will focus on reducing glitching and mux delay in the output stage, which can limit performance and increase power.

Key improvements:
- Replace the continuous assign mux with a registered output stage that samples the mux output on a single clock edge, reducing glitching on the output and improving timing.
- Use a small 1-bit synchronizer flip-flop clocked on the positive edge to register the mux output, thereby smoothing output transitions and easing downstream timing constraints.
- This approach keeps the modular dual-edge sampling but ensures output changes only at a single clock edge, helping reduce dynamic power and timing closure challenges.
- Remove clock inversions to remain FPGA-friendly.
- Keep the EdgeFF module and two instantiated flip-flops for clarity and synthesis compatibility.

Overall, this solution trades a single extra flip-flop for better glitch suppression, improved timing, and reduced power due to less mux switching noise, with minimal area overhead.

This is a practical compromise that improves performance and power while maintaining correctness and synthesis friendliness.
