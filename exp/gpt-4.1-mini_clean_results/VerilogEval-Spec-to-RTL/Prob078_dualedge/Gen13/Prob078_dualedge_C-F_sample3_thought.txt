The goal is to create a superior dual-edge triggered flip-flop by combining the best aspects of both examples:

- Use two flip-flops triggered on opposite edges (posedge and negedge) to capture input data on both clock edges.
- Avoid illegal sensitivity lists by not using dual-edge always blocks.
- Use a clock-level controlled transparent latch as the output selector instead of a purely combinational mux to reduce glitches, switching activity, and power consumption.
- The latch is implemented with level-sensitive behavior controlled by the clock: when clk=1, latch transparently passes q_pos; when clk=0, it transparently passes q_neg.
- Implement the latch explicitly in an always block using a sensitivity list including clk and q_pos/q_neg for synthesis friendliness.
- Use non-blocking assignments consistently.
- Maintain modularity with separate edge-triggered flip-flop modules.
- Add clear comments to explain the operation for readability and maintainability.

This fusion preserves the functional correctness, reduces glitches and power on the output path, and avoids illegal Verilog constructs. The use of a latch-based output mux leverages stable clock-level gating to improve power and timing without complexity or exotic primitives.
