To create a superior dual-edge triggered flip-flop design, we fuse the strengths of both examples:

- Both use two flip-flops triggered on opposite edges, ensuring functional correctness.
- Both examples use a clock-level controlled output selection between the two flip-flops' outputs.
- Example 1 employs a transparent latch as a functional mux replacement, reducing glitches, switching activity, and power compared to a purely combinational mux.
- Example 2 notes the drawbacks of combinational muxing on clock signal and suggests a transparent latch for better timing and power.

Our fusion strategy:
- Use two flip-flops: one positive edge-triggered, one negative edge-triggered.
- Replace the combinational mux on output with an explicit clock-level controlled transparent latch, allowing the output to hold stable values and reducing glitching.
- Implement the latch with a level-sensitive clock gating style: latch is transparent when clk=1 to sample q_pos, and transparent when clk=0 to sample q_neg.
- This latch-based muxing reduces power and glitching on output compared to a purely combinational mux.
- To avoid combinational logic using the clock directly (which can cause glitches), use an explicit latch with clock-level enable.
- Keep code synthesis-friendly and straightforward.

This approach leverages the reliability of flip-flops, the glitch-free advantages of latches, and the intuitive dual-edge behavior, improving PPA without increasing complexity or requiring clock frequency changes.
