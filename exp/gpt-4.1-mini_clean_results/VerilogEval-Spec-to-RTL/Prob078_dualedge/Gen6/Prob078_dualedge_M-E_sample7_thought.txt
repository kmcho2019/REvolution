To implement a dual-edge triggered flip-flop without using a negative edge-triggered FF or illegal sensitivity lists, I will use a single positive edge-triggered flip-flop clocked by a doubled-frequency clock generated internally from the input clock signal. This is done by XORing the clock and a delayed version of itself to produce pulses on both edges. Then, an internal enable signal derived from this doubled frequency clock controls when to sample the input 'd' on the main clock's edges by internally toggling a sampling clock signal. A synchronizer flip-flop captures the sampled data on the original clock edges, effectively emulating dual-edge triggering with one flip-flop and no negative edge triggered FF. This approach avoids illegal sensitivity lists and extra negative-edge FFs, uses a clock doubling technique inside the design, and results in functional dual-edge triggering behavior.
