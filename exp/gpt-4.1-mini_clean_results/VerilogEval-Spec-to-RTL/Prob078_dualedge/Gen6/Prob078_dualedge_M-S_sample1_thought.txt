To simplify the design, I will eliminate the separate PosEdgeFF and NegEdgeFF modules and directly instantiate two flip-flops inside the TopModule: one triggered on posedge clk and the other on negedge clk. Both will store the input 'd' on their respective clock edges. Then, a continuous assign statement will multiplex their outputs based on the clock level (clk ? q_posedge : q_negedge), effectively creating a dual-edge triggered flip-flop. This reduces module count and code lines while maintaining the original functionality and synthesis legality.
