To refactor the design while keeping the same functional behavior, I will replace the parameterized EdgeFF module with two distinct modules: one positive-edge-triggered D flip-flop and one negative-edge-triggered D flip-flop. Both modules will use always blocks with appropriate sensitivity lists for clarity.

Instead of a continuous assignment mux controlled by the clock level, I will implement a combinational always block to multiplex the two sampled outputs. This approach still avoids latches by covering all cases and maintains synthesizability.

This refactor improves readability by clearly separating positive and negative edge FF modules and moves the output mux logic into an always @* block, which some might find easier to debug and understand. The main functional structure remains the same, ensuring legal FPGA synthesis without dual-edge FF primitives.

No inverted clocks are generated; the mux is implemented with a case on clk level, and non-blocking assignments are used inside clocked blocks, blocking assignment in combinational blocks.

This offers a clean, modular design variation with the same dual-edge triggered behavior.
