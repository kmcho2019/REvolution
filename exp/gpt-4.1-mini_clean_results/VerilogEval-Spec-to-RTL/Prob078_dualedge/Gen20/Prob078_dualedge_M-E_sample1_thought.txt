Implement the dual-edge triggered flip-flop by sampling the input 'd' on the positive edge of the clock and then using a transparent latch clocked by the inverted clock to capture this sampled value on the falling phase. The output 'q' is driven by this latch. This way, the data is first captured at the rising edge into a flip-flop, then latched through a level-sensitive latch enabled when clk is low, effectively capturing changes on both clock edges. This avoids any illegal sensitivity lists and uses only FPGA-friendly elements (flip-flop plus latch) while providing dual-edge behavior. It also reduces the area compared to having two flip-flops and a mux or latch, and avoids combinational mux glitches by relying on the latch's transparency.
