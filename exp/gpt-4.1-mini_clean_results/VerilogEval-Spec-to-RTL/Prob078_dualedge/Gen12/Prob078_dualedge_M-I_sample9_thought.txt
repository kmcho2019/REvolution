To optimize the dual-edge triggered flip-flop implementation for better PPA, I will focus on reducing the toggling activity and area while preserving functionality.

Improvements:
1. Instead of instantiating two flip-flops capturing the same data on opposite edges and muxing their outputs, use a single flip-flop clocked at the rising edge and a transparent latch controlled by the clock level (clock or inverted clock).
2. The rising-edge flip-flop captures the data at the positive edge, while the transparent latch follows the data during the negative half-cycle. The output is driven by the latch during clk=0 and by the flip-flop during clk=1.
3. This approach reduces the number of flip-flops from two to one flip-flop plus one latch, which is typically lighter in area and power than two flip-flops.
4. The latch enable is derived directly from the clock (or its inversion), ensuring transparent behavior during the negative half-cycle.
5. This also reduces clock toggling activity on one flip-flop, cutting power approximately in half.
6. The output mux is replaced by a simple combinational logic: since the latch output is used during clk=0 and flip-flop output during clk=1, a multiplexer based on clk selects between them.
7. Non-blocking assignments remain to ensure proper synthesis of sequential elements.
8. Add comments for clarity and maintain coding best practices.
9. This approach is a classic dual-edge FF emulation technique, FPGA-friendly, and synthesizable.

Overall, this optimized design improves power and area by reducing the number of flip-flops, and may improve timing by simplifying the muxing logic.
