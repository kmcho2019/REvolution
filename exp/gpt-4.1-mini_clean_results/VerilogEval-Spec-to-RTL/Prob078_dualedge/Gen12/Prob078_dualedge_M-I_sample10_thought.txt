To optimize the dual-edge triggered flip-flop implementation, I will reduce glitches and improve timing by registering the output `q` on the rising edge of the clock. Instead of directly multiplexing the two flip-flop outputs combinationally on clock level, the registered output `q` will update at every clock edge (since one of the two FF outputs updates at every edge), thus producing stable output and avoiding combinational glitches.

Concretely:
- Use the two edge-triggered flip-flops as before to sample input `d` on both edges.
- Use a positive-edge clocked register to latch the multiplexed output of these two FFs based on the current clock level.
- This ensures output `q` changes only synchronously on the rising edge, reducing glitching and improving timing.
- This adds an extra register stage, slightly increasing latency by half a clock cycle, but enhances overall signal integrity and timing closure.
- Clock domain remains simple, using only the rising edge clock for the final output register, making it friendly for FPGA implementation.
This strategy improves power and timing by eliminating combinational mux glitches at the output while maintaining dual-edge functional behavior.
