To create a superior dual-edge triggered flip-flop design, I will fuse the best ideas from both examples by:

- Keeping the modular, parameterized EdgeFF instantiated twice for clarity, reusability, and clean synthesis.
- Avoiding illegal sensitivity lists and explicit inverted clock generation for FPGA-friendly clocking.
- Using a transparent latch controlled by clk level for glitch and dynamic power reduction, but with a safer and more synthesis-friendly style.
- Implementing the latch with blocking assignments (typical for latches) inside an always block sensitive only to clk and the sampled data outputs, minimizing unintended simulation/synthesis behavior.
- Employing continuous assignment for the latch output is avoided to reduce mux glitches; instead, a latch (level-sensitive storage) is used to hold the output stable during the respective clock level.
- Adding a simple asynchronous reset (optional here) can be added in future if needed, but is omitted to keep minimal.
- Using consistent, nonblocking assignments only in flip-flops, and blocking assignments in the latch.
- Maintaining clear naming and hierarchy, facilitating timing and power analysis.
- This approach yields minimal glitches on output q, synthesis-friendly implementation, and FPGA-compatible clocking, while balancing area and power overhead.

The overall design is:
- Two EdgeFF modules sample input d on posedge and negedge clk respectively.
- A clock-level controlled transparent latch chooses which sampled data to present as q.
- The latch reduces glitches compared to pure combinational mux and is more robust in FPGA synthesis.

This fusion delivers a clean, modular, glitch-minimized, and FPGA-legal dual-edge flip-flop emulation optimized for performance, power, and area.
