To improve the existing dual-edge triggered flip-flop design for better performance and reduced glitch risk on the output mux, I will:

- Replace the continuous mux driven directly by the clk level with a clocked mux implemented by a small register that samples the appropriate edge-triggered FF outputs on the clock edges. This reduces glitches caused by direct clock-level muxing.
- Use a toggled clock enable technique to latch the output on both edges without relying on clock level muxing.
- Keep the two single-edge FFs as is to maintain FPGA friendliness.
- Add a simple flip-flop to synchronize and select the correct output on each clock edge, reducing combinational mux delay on the output.
- Avoid generating inverted clocks explicitly or complex logic, maintaining synthesis friendliness.
- This approach reduces the mux critical path and switching glitches, slightly improving performance and power.
- Area overhead is minimal as only one additional flip-flop is added on the output path.

Overall, this small pipeline stage smooths output selection, enhancing timing and power while preserving the functional behavior of the dual-edge triggered flip-flop.
