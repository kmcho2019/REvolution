To improve PPA (power, performance, area) of the dual-edge triggered flip-flop design, I'll replace the transparent latch at the output with a positive level-sensitive latch that only samples the output of the correct flip-flop when the clock is at the corresponding level. Specifically:

- Use two flip-flops triggered on posedge and negedge respectively to capture data.
- Replace the combinational mux / latch implementation with an explicit positive-level transparent latch controlled by clk.
- When clk is high, the latch is transparent and passes q_pos; when clk is low, the latch closes and holds the output stable.
- The latch output updates only on clock rising and holds stable otherwise, reducing switching activity and glitches.
- The latch is implemented as an always @(clk or ...) block, which is synthesizable and avoids purely combinational mux glitches.
- This also improves timing by isolating the output from combinational mux delay and reduces power by lowering output toggling.
- The design remains fully functional as dual-edge triggered flip-flop.

This approach reduces glitches and switching power at output, uses a proper latch that maps well to FPGA primitives, and should improve timing closure and area slightly compared to the initial design with continuous assignment mux.
