To design a superior dual-edge triggered flip-flop module, I fuse the best ideas from both examples:

- Use a single parameterized EdgeFF module instantiated twice, once for posedge and once for negedge triggers, maintaining clean, reusable, and modular code.
- Avoid direct inverted clock generation, which may not be FPGA-friendly, and also avoid illegal sensitivity lists.
- Replace a purely combinational mux with an explicit transparent latch controlled by clk level to minimize glitches and switching power.
- Implement the latch with non-blocking assignments in an always block sensitive to clk and the sampled data signals to better guide synthesis and timing tools.
- The latch is transparent when clk=1, passing q_posedge; and transparent when clk=0, passing q_negedge, reducing glitching and dynamic switching compared to combinational mux.
- Keep the design hierarchy clear for synthesis and timing optimization.
- This approach balances functional correctness, synthesis legality, glitch minimization, and modularity, while remaining minimal in area and power overhead.

This fusion strategy ensures a synthesizable, glitch-reduced, dual-edge triggered flip-flop emulation that is FPGA-friendly and practical for high-quality RTL design.
