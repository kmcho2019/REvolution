To combine the strengths of both examples, I will keep the modularity and clarity of the first example’s separated positive and negative edge flip-flops encapsulated in their own modules, but replace the combinational mux always block with a continuous assign statement for clarity and synthesis friendliness (as in example 1). To avoid explicit inverted clock usage in the flip-flop (which can cause routing/skew issues as in example 2), I will use a dedicated negative-edge triggered flip-flop module with its clock directly triggered on negedge clk. This approach is fully legal and clear in Verilog synthesis (sensitivity lists with posedge or negedge are allowed individually, just not mixed in one always block), and commonly supported by FPGA tools. The top-level module instantiates the two flip-flops, each capturing data on opposite edges, and a simple continuous assign selects the output depending on clock level (clk ? q_pos : q_neg). This fused design thus provides modularity, clarity, no inverted clock signals, legal and common synthesis constructs, and minimal mux overhead, combining the best ideas from both examples.
