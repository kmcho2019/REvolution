To fix the supplemental flow valve (dfr) logic, we must ensure it asserts whenever the current water level is strictly higher than the previous level before the last sensor change, and remains asserted as long as the water level stays above that previous level.

The original design only asserted dfr for one clock cycle when the level rose, clearing it immediately afterward, which does not reflect the spec's intent to keep supplemental flow valve open while the water level is higher than before.

To address this, track prev_level as before, updated only on a level change, then assert dfr whenever curr_level > prev_level.

If the level decreases or remains equal, dfr should de-assert.

This ensures dfr is stable and synchronized to the clock, reflecting a supplemental flow valve open whenever water level is above the previous level (i.e., rising water level condition).

Nominal flow outputs remain combinationally driven by curr_level as before.

On reset, initialize curr_level and prev_level to BELOW (0), and assert all flow outputs including dfr to simulate a long low water level condition.

This fixes the timing and state retention issues with dfr, yielding the expected persistent supplemental flow valve behavior and removing the large mismatch on dfr output.
