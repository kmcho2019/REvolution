Implement a state machine that explicitly tracks the water level as one of four discrete states representing below s[0], between s[0] and s[1], between s[1] and s[2], and above s[2]. On each clock, decode the sensor input to the new level state and compare it with the previous state.

Maintain a register that stores the previous water level *at the moment the sensor state changes* (i.e., when the water level state transitions). This allows detecting whether the level rose or fell.

Outputs are driven based on the current water level state as per the provided mapping:

- Above s[2]: no valves open (fr0=fr1=fr2=0)
- Between s[2] and s[1]: fr0=1 only
- Between s[1] and s[0]: fr0=fr1=1
- Below s[0]: fr0=fr1=fr2=1

The supplemental flow valve (dfr) is enabled if the water level rose compared to the previous stored level at the last change.

Synchronous reset sets the state to below s[0], opens all flow valves (max flow), and initializes previous level tracking accordingly.

This approach ensures precise level change detection, clean separation of states, and consistent output logic directly derived from state, avoiding glitches or ambiguous transitions.
