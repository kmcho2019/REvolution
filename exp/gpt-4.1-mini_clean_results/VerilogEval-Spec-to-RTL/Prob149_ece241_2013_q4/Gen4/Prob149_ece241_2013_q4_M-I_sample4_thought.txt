The main problem in the previous design is that `prev_level` was updated immediately to the current level whenever the sensors changed, which loses the "previous level before the last change" information needed to properly detect if the level increased.

To fix this, I will register the current water level each clock and store the *old* previous level one cycle later, effectively implementing a 2-cycle shift register of levels: 

- `curr_level` is decoded from sensors combinationally.
- `level_d1` holds `curr_level` from the previous clock.
- `prev_level` holds `level_d1` from two clocks ago.

Then, when comparing `curr_level` to `prev_level`, we are actually comparing the current level to the stable previous level before the last sensor change, preserving the semantics required.

On reset, the registers initialize to the lowest level (below s0) with all flows open, matching the reset condition.

All output flows (`fr2`, `fr1`, `fr0`) are assigned combinationally from `curr_level` using the lookup table.

The supplemental flow `dfr` is asserted when the current water level is strictly higher than the `prev_level` registered two clocks ago.

This approach cleanly separates combinational decoding from sequential state tracking and ensures synchronous reset behavior that directly reflects output states.

This small pipeline of levels avoids the problem of updating `prev_level` at the same time as `curr_level`, keeping previous stable state for correct comparison.
