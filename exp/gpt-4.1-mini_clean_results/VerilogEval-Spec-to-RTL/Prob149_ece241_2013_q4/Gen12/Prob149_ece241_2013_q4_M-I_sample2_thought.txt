The key issue with the original implementation is the logic used to set dfr during a water level change. It incorrectly compares new_level with stable_level after stable_level has been updated in the same clock cycle, causing dfr to malfunction. The fix is to update prev_level and stable_level registers in sequence but calculate dfr by comparing the newly decoded water level (new_level) with the previous stable_level *before* updating stable_level.

Specifically, at the clock edge when a sensor change is detected:
- dfr = 1 if new_level > stable_level (old stable_level, before update)
- dfr = 0 otherwise

Then update prev_level <= stable_level and stable_level <= new_level.

This guarantees the dfr decision is made based on the previous stable water level, as required by the spec.

In addition, we can simplify the combinational nominal flow outputs directly from stable_level without additional logic.

We keep the synchronous reset setting stable_level and prev_level to BELOW_S0 and dfr = 1, representing a long low-level stable state with all outputs asserted.

This approach ensures dfr asserts only when water level rises, holds steady otherwise, and avoids glitches. It also respects the previous water level before the last sensor change as required.

This correction fixes the large number of dfr mismatches and meets the spec perfectly.
