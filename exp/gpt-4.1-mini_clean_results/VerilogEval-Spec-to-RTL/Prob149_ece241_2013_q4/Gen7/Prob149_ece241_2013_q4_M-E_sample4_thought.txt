Implement a finite state machine (FSM) with explicit states representing the four water levels: BELOW (no sensors asserted), BETWEEN0 (only s[0] asserted), BETWEEN1 (s[0] and s[1] asserted), and ABOVE (all sensors asserted). On every clock cycle, synchronously sample the sensor inputs and decode them into a next state. Keep track of the previous FSM state to detect whether water level increased (next state > previous state). 

Based on the current state, drive the nominal flow outputs (fr0, fr1, fr2) exactly as specified. Drive the supplemental flow valve (dfr) if the water level increased since last sensor change, i.e., if next state > previous FSM state. On synchronous reset, initialize FSM and previous state to BELOW, and drive all valves asserted.

This FSM approach gives clear states and transitions, simplifies previous level tracking, and makes the supplemental valve decision straightforward. It fully satisfies the problem requirements with a clean, understandable architecture.
