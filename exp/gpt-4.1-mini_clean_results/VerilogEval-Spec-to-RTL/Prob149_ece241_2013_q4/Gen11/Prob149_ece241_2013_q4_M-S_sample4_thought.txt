I will simplify the design by treating the sensor inputs as a 3-bit vector representing the water level at the three sensors (from bottom s[0] to top s[2]). The nominal flow outputs (fr0, fr1, fr2) depend solely on which sensors are asserted following the problem table:

- Above s[2] (all sensors asserted): no valves open.
- Between s[2] and s[1] (s[0] and s[1] asserted, s[2] deasserted): fr0 on only.
- Between s[1] and s[0] (only s[0] asserted): fr0 and fr1 on.
- Below s[0] (no sensors asserted): fr0, fr1, fr2 on.

Other sensor patterns are rare or transient; I will treat them as below lowest sensor (all valves on).

For the supplemental flow valve (dfr), I will register the previous sensor input and set dfr=1 when the current water level is higher than the previous. To avoid complex ranking, I will define a simple numeric rank of water level as the number of asserted sensors (since sensors are stacked vertically and at fixed intervals, more sensors asserted means higher water).

This approach simplifies the rise detection and ensures correct nominal flow valve outputs. On synchronous reset, all valves including dfr are asserted, simulating the "below lowest" steady state.

This straightforward method reduces complexity while maintaining functionality.
