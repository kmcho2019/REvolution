Instead of tracking just the previous sensor state and comparing discrete levels, we can model the water level as a 2-bit code directly representing four water zones from low (0) to high (3), updating the "previous level" only when the sensor inputs change.

We implement a small state machine with two registers: prev_level and curr_level.

At every clock, if the sensors have changed, we update prev_level to the old curr_level, then update curr_level based on current sensors.

The supplemental valve (dfr) is asserted if the water level has increased compared to prev_level (i.e., the water level is now higher than before the last sensor change). This way, we always have a stable reference to the level before the last sensor change.

Nominal flows (fr0, fr1, fr2) are determined only by the current sensors, per the defined table.

On reset, prev_level and curr_level both reset to 0 (lowest), and all flow valves are asserted.

This approach cleanly separates current and previous water levels with updates only on sensor changes, avoiding transient glitches and capturing the correct "previous level before last sensor change" needed for dfr control.
