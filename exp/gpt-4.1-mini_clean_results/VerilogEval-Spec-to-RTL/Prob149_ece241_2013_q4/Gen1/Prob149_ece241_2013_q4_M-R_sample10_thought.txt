To address the issues with the original design, especially the unreliable dfr output, we can refactor the design by making the water level decoding fully combinational and the output signals driven by combinational logic depending on the current and previous water levels stored in registers. We track previous_level in a register updated only when the sensor reading changes, rather than every clock, so dfr reflects a rise relative to the previous stable level.

Key improvements:
- Use a combinational function to decode the water level.
- Store previous_level only when the sensor reading changes, avoiding glitches from intermediate or unstable readings.
- Drive fr signals and dfr entirely combinationally from current_level and previous_level registers.
- On reset, initialize previous_level and current_level to the lowest level and set outputs accordingly.
- Implement synchronous reset as requested.
- Use nonblocking assignments properly for registers.
- Assign outputs as combinational signals driven by registers, avoiding latches and glitches.

This approach simplifies the logic, explicitly compares current and previous stable levels to control dfr, and ensures outputs update consistently on clock edges.
