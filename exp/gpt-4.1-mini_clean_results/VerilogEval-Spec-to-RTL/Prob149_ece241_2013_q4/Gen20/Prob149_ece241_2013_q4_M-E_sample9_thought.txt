Instead of using a finite state machine with encoded states, directly decode the sensor inputs into four explicit water level conditions: BELOW (no sensors asserted), LOW (only s[0]), MID (s[0] and s[1]), and HIGH (all sensors). Store the previous water level as an integer from 0 (BELOW) to 3 (HIGH) to facilitate easy comparison. On each clock cycle, update the previous level before updating the current level based on the sensors. The supplemental flow valve `dfr` is asserted synchronously when the current water level is strictly higher than the previous level, indicating a rising water level. Nominal flow valves (`fr2`, `fr1`, `fr0`) are assigned combinationally from the current water level as per the table. This approach avoids complex state encodings and directly reflects water level changes with clear numeric values, simplifying the rising level detection logic and making the design robust and easy to verify. The synchronous reset initializes to BELOW level with all valves open, matching the requirement.
