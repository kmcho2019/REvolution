Use a one-hot encoded state machine representing the four discrete water levels: LOW (below s[0]), MEDIUM_LOW (between s[1] and s[0]), MEDIUM_HIGH (between s[2] and s[1]), and HIGH (above s[2]). On each clock edge, decode the sensor inputs into a state and compare it with the previous state. If the state has increased (water level rose), assert dfr for one cycle; otherwise, dfr is low. The nominal flow outputs are combinational logic derived directly from the current state. On synchronous reset, the FSM initializes to LOW state with all flow outputs (fr0, fr1, fr2, dfr) asserted high, matching the problem's requirement of starting as if water was low for a long time. This approach leverages explicit one-hot states for clarity and easier transition comparisons, cleanly separating state transitions from flow control outputs.
