To fix the design, we must exactly encode water level states based on the specific sensor pattern rather than just counting asserted sensors, since the problem defines distinct states by precise combinations of sensors:

- Above s[2]: s == 3'b111
- Between s[2] and s[1]: s == 3'b011 (s[0] and s[1] asserted only)
- Between s[1] and s[0]: s == 3'b001 (only s[0] asserted)
- Below s[0]: s == 3'b000 (no sensors asserted)

All other sensor combinations are invalid per the spec and can be treated conservatively as one of these levels or default to the safest.

We will encode water level states as a 2-bit enumerated type (e.g. 0=Below s0, 1=Between s1 and s0, 2=Between s2 and s1, 3=Above s2).

Store the previous water level state synchronously in a register at each clock.

The supplemental valve dfr should be asserted whenever the water level transitions upward (current level > previous level).

Use synchronous reset to initialize previous level to Below s0 (level 0) and all flow outputs (fr0, fr1, fr2, dfr) to 1, meeting the “low water level for a long time” condition.

Outputs fr0, fr1, fr2 should be combinationally assigned according to the exact current water level state per the table:

- 3 (Above s2): fr0=0, fr1=0, fr2=0
- 2 (Between s2 and s1): fr0=1, fr1=0, fr2=0
- 1 (Between s1 and s0): fr0=1, fr1=1, fr2=0
- 0 (Below s0): fr0=1, fr1=1, fr2=1

This precise state approach and correct comparison fixes the logic and reset behavior, matching simulation expectations.
