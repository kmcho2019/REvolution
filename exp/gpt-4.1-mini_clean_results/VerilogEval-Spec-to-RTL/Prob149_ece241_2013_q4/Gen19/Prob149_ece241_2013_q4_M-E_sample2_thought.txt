Use a finite state machine (FSM) to represent the water level states explicitly, with states corresponding to the four water levels: BELOW (no sensors), LOW (only s[0]), MID (s[0] and s[1]), and HIGH (all sensors). On each clock, transition to the state matching the current sensor pattern decoded. Maintain a register holding the previous state to detect level rises (if the new state is above the previous state, then assert dfr). Outputs fr2, fr1, fr0 are combinationally derived from the current state per the required nominal flow settings. On synchronous reset, initialize the FSM state to BELOW (lowest water level) with all flow outputs asserted. This FSM approach clearly models the water levels as distinct states, making level rise detection precise, and isolating combinational output logic, ensuring clarity and correctness.
