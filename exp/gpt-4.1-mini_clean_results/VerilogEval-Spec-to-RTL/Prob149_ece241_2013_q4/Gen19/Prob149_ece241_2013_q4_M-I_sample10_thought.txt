To correctly implement the dfr signal as per specification, we must track the water level two sensor changes ago, i.e., keep a two-level history: current_level, prev_level, and prev_prev_level. The dfr output should be asserted when the current_level is greater than prev_prev_level, indicating a rising water level compared to the level before the last sensor change. This solves the fundamental misunderstanding in the original solution.

We decode the sensor input into discrete water levels cleanly and update prev_prev_level and prev_level only on changes in current_level. The dfr signal is synchronously assigned and asserted for exactly one clock cycle on level rises.

The nominal flow outputs (fr0, fr1, fr2) remain combinational based on current_level as before.

This approach correctly models the requirement: dfr is high if current_level > prev_prev_level (previous to last level), updated only when level changes occur. On reset, state is set to "low water level" (0) with all flow outputs asserted.

This fixes the major bug causing incorrect dfr timing and value by explicitly tracking two previous levels and comparing with current level.
