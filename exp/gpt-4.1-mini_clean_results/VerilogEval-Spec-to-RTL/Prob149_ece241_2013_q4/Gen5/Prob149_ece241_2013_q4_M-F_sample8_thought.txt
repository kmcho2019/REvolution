The root cause of the incorrect 'dfr' behavior is that 'current_state' and 'prev_state' are synchronous registers updated on clock edges, but 'dfr' is computed combinationally using these registers and the input sensors asynchronously. This causes timing mismatches and glitches.

To fix this, we should compute both 'current_state' and 'prev_state' synchronously, and base all outputs, including 'dfr', on registered states inside a clocked always block. This eliminates combinational dependency on asynchronous inputs for outputs.

Specifically:
- Implement a synchronous decode: on each clock edge sample the input sensors and decode water level to 'next_state'.
- Update 'current_state' with 'next_state', and also keep 'prev_state' as the previous 'current_state'.
- Compute 'dfr' synchronously by comparing 'current_state' and 'prev_state' inside the clocked block.
- Drive outputs (fr0, fr1, fr2, dfr) from registers updated in the clocked block.
- On reset, initialize states and outputs accordingly.

This approach guarantees glitch-free, cycle-aligned outputs and resolves the 'dfr' timing problem, while preserving the nominal flow valve logic.

Additionally, by registering sensor decoding, we avoid any combinational use of asynchronous inputs in output signals, which is good design practice in synchronous designs.
