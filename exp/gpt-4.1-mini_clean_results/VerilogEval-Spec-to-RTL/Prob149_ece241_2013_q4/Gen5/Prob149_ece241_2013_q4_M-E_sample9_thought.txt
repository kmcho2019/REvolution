Instead of maintaining explicit discrete states, implement a purely combinational decoding of the sensor inputs to determine the current water level range and flow rates. Additionally, store the previous sensor state synchronously in a register on the rising clock edge.

To detect a rising water level, compare the integer-encoded current sensor level to the previous sensor level stored. Since the sensors are vertically arranged, encode the water level as the count of asserted sensors (0 to 3). When the number of asserted sensors increases, it means the water level has risen.

Use a synchronous reset to initialize the previous sensor level to zero (no sensors asserted) and assert all flow valves (maximum flow).

The outputs fr0, fr1, fr2 are combinationally assigned based on the decoded sensor level following the table:

- 3 sensors asserted: fr0=fr1=fr2=0
- 2 sensors asserted: fr0=1, fr1=fr2=0
- 1 sensor asserted: fr0=fr1=1, fr2=0
- 0 sensors asserted: fr0=fr1=fr2=1

The supplemental flow valve (dfr) is a one-cycle pulse asserted whenever the current water level is higher than the previous water level.

This approach avoids explicit FSM states, uses a numeric water level indicator derived directly from sensor inputs, and manages history cleanly by storing previous sensor level. It provides clear, simple logic and matches all requirements.
