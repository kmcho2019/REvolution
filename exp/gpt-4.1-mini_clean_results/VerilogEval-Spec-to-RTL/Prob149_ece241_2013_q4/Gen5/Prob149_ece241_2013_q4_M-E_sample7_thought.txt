Instead of encoding water levels into abstract states and comparing them, directly track the previous sensor pattern synchronously and detect rises by comparing the current and previous sensor values bitwise.

The water level order from low to high corresponds to the number of asserted sensors starting from the bottom:

- BELOW: no sensors asserted (s=000)
- Between s[1] and s[0]: s=001 (only s[0])
- Between s[2] and s[1]: s=011 (s[1] and s[0])
- ABOVE: s=111 (all sensors)

Since the problem states these exact sensor patterns define levels, we can classify them explicitly and treat any other pattern as closest valid by selecting the highest matching level below or equal to that pattern.

We will:

1. Sample the sensor inputs synchronously on the clock.
2. Hold the previous sensor pattern.
3. Define a function to map any sensor pattern to the nearest valid level: assign a numeric level based on sensor bits.
4. Detect a rise when the new water level is strictly greater than the previous water level.
5. Control the nominal valves (fr0, fr1, fr2) directly from the current water level as per the problem statement.
6. On reset, initialize previous sensor pattern and level to BELOW (all valves open).
7. This approach avoids asynchronous combinational decoding and removes ambiguous states.
8. Outputs are purely combinational functions of the synchronous register state, ensuring glitch-free outputs.

This direct sensor-pattern-based level tracking and rise detection is simpler, more robust, and naturally synchronous, avoiding the pitfalls of previous level state machines.
