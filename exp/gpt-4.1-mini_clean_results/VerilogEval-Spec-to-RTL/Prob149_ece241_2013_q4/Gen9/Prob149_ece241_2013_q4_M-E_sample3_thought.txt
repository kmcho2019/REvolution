A new architectural approach is to simplify the water level tracking by encoding the water level directly from the sensor bits into a 2-bit numeric value representing the level from 0 (below s[0]) to 3 (above s[2]). This encoding enforces a rule that sensors must be contiguous from the bottom up; invalid patterns will be normalized to the highest valid contiguous sensor level.

We track two registers:

1. `current_level` — the water level decoded from sensors each clock cycle.

2. `last_level_on_change` — the water level recorded at the last sensor state change.

On each clock, we compare `current_level` to the previous clock's `current_level`. If the level changed, we update `last_level_on_change` to the previous `current_level` (before the change).

The output nominal flow valves `fr0`, `fr1`, `fr2` are driven purely from `current_level` according to the table:

- LEVEL3 (3): No flow valves open.

- LEVEL2 (2): Only fr0 open.

- LEVEL1 (1): fr0 and fr1 open.

- LEVEL0 (0): fr0, fr1, fr2 all open.

The supplemental flow valve `dfr` is asserted only if the current water level is strictly greater than `last_level_on_change`, i.e., the water level has risen since the last sensor change.

On synchronous reset, all outputs go to maximum flow (all valves open) and the internal state initializes to the lowest level (0).

This design separates sensor decoding, level-change detection, and valve output generation clearly and uses simple numeric comparisons to meet the specification, ensuring clean and glitch-free outputs.
