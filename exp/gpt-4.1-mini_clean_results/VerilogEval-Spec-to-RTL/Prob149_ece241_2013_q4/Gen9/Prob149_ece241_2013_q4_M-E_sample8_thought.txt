A new approach is to explicitly detect sensor-level changes and remember the previous stable water level only when a change occurs. We will store two water level variables: 
- current_level representing the currently decoded sensor level, 
- last_stable_level representing the level before the most recent sensor-level change.

At every clock edge, if the decoded water level differs from current_level, it means the water level changed. Then we update last_stable_level with the old current_level, and update current_level to the new level.

The supplemental flow valve (dfr) is asserted only when the current water level is higher than the last stable water level. This means dfr activates only upon an upward transition in stable water levels, precisely matching the problem description "level previous to the last sensor change".

On reset, we initialize the state to BELOW (no sensors asserted), and open all valves (fr0, fr1, fr2, dfr asserted).

This method explicitly tracks transitions and ensures dfr reflects increases in water level relative to the previous stable level, preventing glitches caused by transient sensor readings or minor fluctuations.

The nominal valve outputs are directly assigned based on current_level as in the specification.

This design cleanly separates level detection, transition detection, and valve control, improving clarity and correctness.
