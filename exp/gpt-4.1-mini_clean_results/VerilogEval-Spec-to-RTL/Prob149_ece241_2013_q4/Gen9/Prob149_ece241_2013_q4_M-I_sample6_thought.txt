To fix the issue with the supplemental flow valve (dfr) output, we must ensure that the previous and current water levels used for comparison are properly registered on separate clock cycles. The original code updates both `current_state` and `prev_state` at the same clock edge before evaluating dfr, causing the comparison to be incorrect or unstable.

Improvement strategy:

1. Separate the state updates over two clock cycles:
   - Store the decoded sensor level into an intermediate register (`next_state`).
   - On each clock edge:
     - Update `prev_state` with the old `current_state`.
     - Update `current_state` with `next_state`.
   
   This ensures `prev_state` holds the water level before the most recent sensor input change, while `current_state` reflects the latest water level.

2. Compute dfr after these two registers have been updated, comparing `current_state` and `prev_state`. This guarantees stable and accurate detection of water level rising edges.

3. Keep the nominal flow valve outputs driven only by `current_state` for clarity and timing.

4. Simplify reset by resetting all registers and outputs together.

5. Explicitly define all possible sensor input patterns to avoid unintended decoding.

This approach fixes the subtle timing glitch that caused dfr mismatches and keeps the design clean and synthesis-friendly.
