This design implements the even parity bit calculation by successively XORing the bits in a pairwise manner through a two-level combinational logic tree without recursion or parameterized modules. The 8-bit input is divided into four pairs, each pair is XORed first, then those four results are XORed in pairs again, and finally the two resulting bits are XORed to produce the parity output.

This approach explicitly uses simple wire assignments and a straightforward structural style, which provides clear visibility of the XOR hierarchy. It does not rely on Verilog's reduction operator or recursive modules, thus explicitly showing the hardware structure while minimizing module instantiations. The design balances between clarity, ease of verification, and moderate hardware efficiency.

Advantages:
- Clear pairwise XOR tree structure, easy to understand and verify.
- No recursion or complex generate blocks, simple to synthesize.
- Shows explicit logical grouping of XOR operations.
- Easily adaptable or extendable to other input widths with minor modifications.

This method highlights a classic hardware XOR tree structure to compute parity while maintaining concise and readable code.
