The original design uses a hierarchical XOR tree composed of multiple HalfXor module instantiations to compute even parity, which is clear but introduces overhead in area and power due to extra wiring and module boundaries. To optimize for PPA (power, performance, area), we can replace the entire hierarchical XOR tree with a single vector reduction XOR operator, which is a built-in primitive in Verilog. This directly computes the XOR of all bits in a vector in one expression, resulting in a flatter logic structure. This reduces gate count, wire complexity, and switching activity, improving power and area without impacting timing significantly. The code becomes more concise, synthesizable, and optimal for typical synthesis tools. The functionality (even parity) remains exactly the same.
