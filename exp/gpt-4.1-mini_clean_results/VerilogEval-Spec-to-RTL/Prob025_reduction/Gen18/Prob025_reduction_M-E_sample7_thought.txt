To implement even parity for an 8-bit input, I will use a parallel prefix XOR structure inspired by the Kogge-Stone adder's prefix computation style. This approach efficiently computes the XOR of all bits in a logarithmic number of stages by propagating partial XOR results in a tree-like manner.

Specifically:
- Stage 0: The input bits themselves.
- Each subsequent stage XORs pairs of partial results separated by increasing powers of two.
- After log2(8)=3 stages, the last position will hold the XOR of all input bits.

This method:
- Avoids linear XOR chains or flat reduction operators.
- Shows a hardware-friendly, scalable parallel prefix structure.
- Is modular and clear, using generate loops for stage computations.
- Can be extended to wider input widths easily.

The final parity is the result at the last bit of the last stage. This prefix XOR tree is a novel architectural idea for parity calculation, emphasizing parallelism and structured computation rather than simple cascades or flat reductions.
