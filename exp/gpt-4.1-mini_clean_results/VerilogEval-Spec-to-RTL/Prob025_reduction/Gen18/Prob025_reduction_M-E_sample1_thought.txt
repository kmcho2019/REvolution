I will implement the parity calculation using a parallel prefix XOR tree structure (similar to a Kogge-Stone adder style tree), which is a widely known fast prefix computation architecture. This approach involves computing prefix XORs in a log2 depth tree, but here, since we only need the total XOR, we'll propagate XOR pairs through multiple stages, each combining neighboring partial results to eventually produce the final parity bit.

This method is novel compared to the previous simple linear or balanced binary XOR tree and illustrates a well-structured prefix XOR calculation in hardware, which can benefit timing by minimizing fanout and reducing long wire delays.

Specifically, for 8 inputs:
- Stage 0: Inputs as partial XOR results
- Stage 1: XOR pairs at distance 1
- Stage 2: XOR pairs at distance 2
- Stage 3: XOR pairs at distance 4

At each stage, the XOR results are combined to form the next stage's partial results, culminating in the parity bit at the highest index.

This hierarchical prefix computation is clean and easy to scale, and reveals the parity as the MSB of the final prefix result vector.

No auxiliary modules will be used; instead, the design will rely on internal wires and assign statements to build the prefix tree.

This approach provides:
- Clear timing advantages by minimizing XOR gate fanout
- Modular, scalable architecture without explicit module instantiations
- Structural clarity showing how prefix computation works for parity
