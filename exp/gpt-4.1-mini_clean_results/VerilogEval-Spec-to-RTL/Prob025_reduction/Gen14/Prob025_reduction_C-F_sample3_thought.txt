To combine the best ideas from both examples, I will create a concise, scalable, and easily readable design that also provides clear modularity without excessive module overhead or manual XOR instantiations. The fusion strategy is:

- Use the built-in Verilog reduction XOR operator for its synthesis efficiency, minimal area, and power benefits.
- Encapsulate this operator inside a small, reusable, parameterized module (XorTree) that can handle any input width cleanly, supporting modularity and scalability like Example 1.
- Avoid recursive module instantiations or many HalfXor instantiations to reduce overhead, keeping the design simple.
- The TopModule simply instantiates this reusable parameterized XOR module with the 8-bit input and produces the even parity bit.
- This approach balances clarity, modularity, scalability, and the best hardware efficiency, providing a production-friendly and educational design.

This fusion yields a clean, scalable, low-power, low-area, and easy-to-understand parity computation module.
