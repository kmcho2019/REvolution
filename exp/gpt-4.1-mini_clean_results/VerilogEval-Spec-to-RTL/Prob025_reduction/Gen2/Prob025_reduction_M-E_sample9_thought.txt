Instead of directly using the XOR reduction operator, we can compute the even parity by breaking down the 8 input bits into smaller groups, compute partial parity bits in a hierarchical manner, and then combine these partial results. Specifically, we can:

1. Divide the 8 input bits into four pairs.
2. XOR each pair to get four partial parity bits.
3. XOR the resulting four bits in pairs to get two partial parity bits.
4. Finally, XOR these two bits to get the final parity.

This approach explicitly demonstrates the hierarchical XOR tree structure, which may be helpful in understanding the logic flow or for implementation on hardware with constraints that prefer balanced trees. It also facilitates pipelining or selective optimization if extended. This is different structurally from a direct XOR reduction.
