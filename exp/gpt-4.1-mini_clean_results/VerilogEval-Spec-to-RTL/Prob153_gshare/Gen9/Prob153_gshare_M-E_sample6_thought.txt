Implement a gshare branch predictor with a clear separation of speculative and committed states using a pipeline-style staging of outputs to precisely capture the predictor state at prediction time. The idea is:

- Maintain a committed global history register (GHR) updated only on confirmed outcomes (training without misprediction).
- Maintain a speculative GHR used to make predictions and updated speculatively after each prediction.
- When a prediction request arrives, produce prediction outputs combinationally from the current speculative GHR and PHT state.
- The outputs `predict_taken` and `predict_history` are registered to hold stable values and reflect exactly the state of the GHR and PHT when the prediction was made.
- On the rising clock edge, first update the PHT with the training information (if any).
- If there is a misprediction indicated by training, rollback speculative GHR to the provided committed GHR snapshot (train_history).
- If there is a training update without misprediction, update the committed GHR by shifting in the actual branch result; also, update speculative GHR to match committed GHR, discarding speculative history.
- If there is no training or misprediction recovery, update speculative GHR by shifting in the predicted outcome just made.
- Output registers only update when `predict_valid` is asserted, otherwise outputs hold steady to avoid glitching.
- Ensure the prediction outputs reflect the PHT and GHR *before* any update occurs at the clock edge.
- To guarantee the outputs correspond to the prediction state before updates, we latch them *at the clock edge* using signals computed *before* the update logic.
- Handle priority cleanly: training misprediction recovery > training update > prediction update.
- Carefully handle the case when training and prediction happen simultaneously.
- This method avoids double registering or complex stall logic, ensuring outputs correctly reflect the prediction cycle state.

This approach keeps state machines simple, leverages synchronous registers for output stability, and guarantees outputs match the predictor state at prediction time exactly.
