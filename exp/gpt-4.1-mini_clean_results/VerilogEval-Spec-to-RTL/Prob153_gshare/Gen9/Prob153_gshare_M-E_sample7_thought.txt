To clearly separate prediction from training and eliminate timing ambiguity on the global history register (GHR), maintain two versions of GHR:

1. A **committed GHR** that reflects the global history state after retired (or trained) branches are resolved. This is only updated on train_valid cycles.

2. A **speculative GHR** used for prediction, which is updated every cycle with the predicted bit to reflect the speculative path history.

When prediction occurs, the output `predict_history` is the speculative GHR *before* updating with the current prediction, matching the pipeline fetch stage snapshot.

When training occurs and a misprediction is detected, the committed GHR is restored (recovered) to `train_history`, and the speculative GHR is also updated accordingly to reflect the recovery, overriding any speculative history.

Prediction and training can happen in the same cycle:

- If both happen, training takes priority: speculative and committed GHR are updated from training recovery if mispredicted, else only committed GHR is updated.

- The PHT is updated only on the training PC and history index at the clock edge, reflecting the true outcome.

By maintaining dual GHRs and carefully updating them, prediction outputs are stable and correspond exactly to the history used in indexing, and training updates correctly restore state on mispredictions.

This approach cleanly matches the specification's requirement that prediction output uses the history state *before* update and that training updates and recovery override speculation.
