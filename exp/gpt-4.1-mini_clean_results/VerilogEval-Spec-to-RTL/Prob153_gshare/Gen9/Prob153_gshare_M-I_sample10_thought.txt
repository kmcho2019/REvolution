The main issue in the original solution is the timing and ordering of the output registration and the speculative GHR update, especially when prediction and training (possibly misprediction recovery) happen in the same cycle. To correct this:

- Separate the combinational prediction logic from the registered outputs that must reflect the GHR and PHT state used to *make* the prediction before any updates in that cycle.

- On the rising clock edge:
  - First, update the PHT if training is valid.
  - Then update the committed and speculative GHRs:
    - If training misprediction occurs, speculative and committed GHRs recover to the training history (highest priority).
    - Else if training valid and not mispredicted, update committed and speculative GHR with the *actual* outcome.
    - Else if prediction valid and no training misprediction, update speculative GHR by shifting in the predicted bit.
  - Finally, register the prediction outputs (`predict_taken` and `predict_history`) *before* updating speculative GHR, so outputs exactly reflect the PHT and GHR state used during prediction, consistent with the timing diagram.

- When both prediction and training misprediction occur in the same cycle, training recovery dominates, and outputs reflect the pre-update state.

- When predict_valid is low, outputs hold their previous values.

This fix guarantees correct timing and avoids output mismatches due to race conditions between training updates and speculative updates.

For PPA optimizations:

- Implement the 128-entry PHT as a RAM-like array (no change here because it's simple and inferred).

- Minimize toggling by only updating outputs and GHR when necessary.

- The saturating counter update function is retained for clarity and synthesizability.

- Use non-blocking assignments consistently.

Overall, this results in a correct and efficient gshare predictor matching the specification.
