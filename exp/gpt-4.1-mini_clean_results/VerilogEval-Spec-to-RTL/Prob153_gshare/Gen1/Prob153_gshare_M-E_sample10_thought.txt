To implement the gshare predictor while ensuring clean separation of update and prediction, and to avoid read-after-write hazards, use a clocked pipeline stage register to hold the predicted taken bit and predicted GHR for use on the next clock edge. This allows the predictor to output stable combinational predictions based on the current PHT and GHR values, and update the GHR only one cycle after prediction, reflecting the predicted taken bit from the prior cycle. 

Key architectural points:

- The PHT is indexed by XOR of PC and GHR, just like before.

- For prediction output (predict_taken, predict_history), use the current GHR and PHT contents to form outputs combinationally.

- On a prediction request (predict_valid), latch the predicted taken bit and the GHR into registers (pipeline stage).

- On the next clock edge, update the GHR with the latched predicted taken bit.

- Training updates to PHT and GHR occur in the same clock edge as prediction's GHR update, but training takes priority in updating GHR and PHT.

- If train_valid and train_mispredicted are asserted, update PHT entry indexed by train_pc ^ train_history, and overwrite GHR with train_history (recover history).

- If train_valid (and not mispredicted), update PHT entry at train index.

- If both prediction and training happen in the same cycle, training's GHR update takes precedence (because the predicted instruction will be squashed).

- Prediction outputs are always based on PHT and GHR state before any update (i.e., combinational read from synchronous registers).

- On asynchronous reset, clear GHR and initialize PHT to weak not taken (2'b01).

This approach cleanly pipelines the GHR update following prediction, preventing combinational loops or hazards, and ensures that GHR updates from training overwrite prediction updates in the same clock cycle as required.

The predict_history output is simply the GHR value at the time of prediction request (before update).

This design separates prediction output (combinational read) and GHR update (registered), avoiding the mismatches due to simultaneous updates.
