To provide a novel approach, I will implement the gshare predictor using a synchronous read RAM-style PHT and introduce a small two-stage pipeline internally to isolate prediction and training timing, ensuring clear separation of prediction outputs and updates.

Key new ideas:

1. Use a synchronous RAM-like 128x2-bit register array for the PHT:
   - Prediction reads PHT entry synchronously at clock edge using the indexed address (PC ^ GHR) latched in a small predictor pipeline register.
   - This avoids combinational reads of PHT, preventing glitches.

2. Separate prediction pipeline stage:
   - On `predict_valid`, latch `predict_pc` and current GHR into pipeline registers.
   - On next clock, use latched pc ^ history to read PHT and produce prediction outputs.
   - This produces one cycle latency from prediction request to valid output but ensures stable and glitch-free output.

3. Training occurs on the same clock with synchronous write port to PHT, updating counter for the indexed PC and history from training inputs.

4. GHR updates:
   - GHR update happens after training update.
   - If a misprediction is trained, GHR is recovered to `train_history`.
   - Otherwise, GHR updates with the actual branch outcome from training.
   - Prediction update to GHR happens only if no training mispredict occurs (lower priority).

5. Outputs:
   - `predict_taken` and `predict_history` output the result from the prediction pipeline stage after the synchronous read of PHT.
   - This stabilizes outputs and decouples them from combinational logic and from training writes.

This structure cleanly separates prediction and training logic, with pipelined prediction to avoid output glitches and timing confusion. It respects priority on GHR update and matches the branch predictor semantics.

Though introducing a cycle of latency on prediction output, the specification does not forbid this. This trade-off yields clarity, correctness, and easier timing behavior.
