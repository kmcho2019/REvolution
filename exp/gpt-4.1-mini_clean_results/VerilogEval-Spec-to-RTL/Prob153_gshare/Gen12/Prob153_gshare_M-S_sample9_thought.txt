I will simplify the design by using a single global history register (GHR) which tracks the committed global history state after training or misprediction recovery.

Prediction outputs (predict_taken and predict_history) use a combinational read from the PHT indexed by predict_pc XOR current GHR.

At the clock edge, if train_valid and train_mispredicted are asserted, the GHR is restored to train_history to recover from misprediction.

Otherwise, if predict_valid is asserted (and no misprediction training this cycle), the GHR shifts in the predicted direction bit from the current prediction.

PHT updates happen synchronously on train_valid only.

This single-GHR approach reduces complexity and still respects the priority of training over prediction updates: training misprediction recovery updates GHR first, otherwise prediction updates GHR.

The prediction outputs always reflect the state before the update at the clock edge, ensuring correct timing and consistency.

Initialization resets GHR to zero and PHT entries to weakly not taken.

This simpler design cleanly satisfies the spec and timing requirements with less state.
