Refactor the design to separate combinational prediction logic from sequential state updates more explicitly and eliminate the register holding the predict history by instead using a pipeline register for prediction outputs. Use a two-stage pipeline approach: stage 1 samples inputs and reads PHT; stage 2 updates GHR and PHT synchronously.

Key points:

1. Prediction outputs (`predict_taken` and `predict_history`) are registered outputs from stage 1 to stabilize timing and align with clock edges.
2. Keep a prediction pipeline register that holds the history used and predicted outcome for that prediction cycle.
3. On the clock edge, update the GHR based on priority:
   - If training with misprediction: recover GHR from training input.
   - Else if prediction was valid in previous cycle: update GHR with predicted bit from that cycle.
4. PHT updates occur on clock edge if training is valid, updating the saturating counter indexed by training pc and history.
5. Use combinational functions to generate next saturating counter state.
6. Separate the reading of PHT entries combinationally for prediction stage.
7. Remove direct asynchronous reading of PHT indexed by the speculative GHR for GHR update; instead rely on the pipeline registers holding prediction info.
8. This two-stage approach aligns prediction outputs with the global history used, respects priority of training over prediction for GHR update, and cleanly models timing, avoiding the subtle mismatches in original code.

This approach keeps the same Gshare prediction algorithm but clarifies timing, aligns outputs with input requests, and cleanly handles simultaneous training and prediction without ambiguity.
