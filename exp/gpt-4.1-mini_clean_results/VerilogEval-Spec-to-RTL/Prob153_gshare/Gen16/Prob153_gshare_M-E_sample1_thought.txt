I will implement the gshare predictor with a slightly different structure to improve clarity and correctness:

1. Separate two history registers:
   - `ghr_spec`: Speculative global history register updated on prediction (holds predicted history).
   - `ghr_committed`: Committed global history register updated on training with actual branch outcomes.

2. Prediction uses `ghr_spec` to XOR with PC for indexing the PHT and producing prediction results.

3. When a prediction is made (`predict_valid`), output the `ghr_spec` as the predict_history, and update `ghr_spec` by shifting in the predicted bit at the next clock edge.

4. When training occurs (`train_valid`), update the PHT entry indexed by `train_pc ^ train_history` with the actual outcome.

5. If training indicates a misprediction (`train_mispredicted`), recover the speculative history to the committed history register from the `train_history`. This discards incorrect speculative updates.

6. The committed GHR is always updated on correct training outcomes, reflecting the true global history state. The speculative GHR is used for fast predictions and updated speculatively.

7. Priority on updates: training recovery of speculative history > training PHT update and committed history update > prediction speculative history update.

This approach cleanly separates speculative and committed histories, avoids overwriting history in the same cycle, and makes the timing and priority explicit, preventing timing races and mismatches between prediction and training.

The asynchronous reset initializes all registers and PHT entries.

This architecture aligns with modern speculative branch predictor designs, increasing robustness while remaining simple and synthesizable.
