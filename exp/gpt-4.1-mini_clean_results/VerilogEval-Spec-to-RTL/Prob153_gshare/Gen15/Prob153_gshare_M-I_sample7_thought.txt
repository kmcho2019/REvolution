To improve correctness and alignment with the specification, the key idea is to strictly separate combinational prediction outputs from sequential state updates, ensuring:

- Prediction outputs (`predict_taken` and `predict_history`) always reflect the current GHR and PHT state at the time of the prediction request, before any updates.
- The PHT and GHR updates happen only on the clock edge, and their updates do not affect the combinational prediction outputs of the same cycle.
- When training indicates a misprediction, we recover the GHR to the correct saved history immediately on the clock edge (training takes precedence over prediction).
- When both training and prediction requests happen simultaneously, training update (and especially misprediction recovery) has priority in updating GHR.
- Hold outputs stable when no prediction is valid by registering last valid outputs, to avoid output glitches.

For improved power and area efficiency and better synthesis compatibility:

- Use `initial` block for PHT initialization (commonly supported in FPGA and ASIC flows) rather than reset loops, to reduce reset latency.
- Minimize combinational logic in critical path by avoiding repeated XOR computations by storing indices as local signals.
- Use separate wires for prediction and training indices for clarity.
- Keep saturating counter update logic simple and use direct arithmetic with saturation checks.
- Register outputs only when prediction is valid to hold stable outputs otherwise.

These refinements ensure outputs are correct and timing matches expectations, which should fix the mismatches in prediction and history output observed in the original design. The improved clarity also aids synthesis and potential PPA optimizations.
