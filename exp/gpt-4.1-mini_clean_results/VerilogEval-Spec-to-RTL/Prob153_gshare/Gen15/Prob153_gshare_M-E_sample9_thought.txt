I will implement the gshare branch predictor using a classic two-stage approach with separate history registers for speculation and commit, but with an explicit saved speculative history snapshot for each prediction. This design will:

- Maintain a global committed history register (committed_ghr) that only updates upon training of non-mispredicted branches, representing the committed program state.

- Maintain a speculative history register (speculative_ghr) used during prediction, updated on every prediction by shifting in the predicted bit. This speculative history always represents the speculative state following all previous predictions.

- When a prediction is made (predict_valid), the module outputs the current speculative history (before shifting) as predict_history and reads the PHT using the XOR of the PC and this history. The predicted direction is output as predict_taken.

- Immediately after prediction, speculative_ghr is updated by shifting in predicted direction.

- When training is received (train_valid), the module updates the PHT entry indexed by the XOR of train_pc and train_history (train_history is the history at prediction time for this branch, supplied by the processor).

- If the training indicates a misprediction (train_mispredicted), the speculative_ghr is recovered to train_history to flush speculative predictions following the mispredicted branch.

- If the training indicates correct prediction, the committed_ghr is updated by shifting in the actual outcome (train_taken), representing the committed program state.

- To keep speculative_ghr in sync, if training is correct, speculative_ghr remains unchanged (already updated by predictions); if training is mispredicted, speculative_ghr recovers to train_history.

- The PHT is a 128-entry array of 2-bit saturating counters, initialized asynchronously to weakly taken (WT).

- This design cleanly separates the committed history (commit point in program) from the speculative history (pipeline speculative), and uses the supplied train_history for accurate indexing and recovery, avoiding ambiguity.

- Training and prediction that happen in the same cycle are carefully ordered: training updates PHT and may recover speculative_ghr on mispredict before prediction updates speculative_ghr, giving training priority as required.

This approach avoids subtle timing issues with overlapping GHR updates and cleanly tracks the program's progress, enabling correct prediction and recovery behavior per specification.
