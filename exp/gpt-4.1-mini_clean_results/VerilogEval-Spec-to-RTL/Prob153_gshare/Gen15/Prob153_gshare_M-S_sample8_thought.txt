Use a single always block triggered on the clock and asynchronous reset. Store the PHT entries in a 128-entry reg array, initialized to weakly not taken.

Maintain a single committed global history register (ghr) representing the branch outcomes after training recovery. On prediction, compute the index as predict_pc XOR current ghr, read the saturating counter for prediction, and immediately latch the current ghr and prediction output to registers for stable outputs next cycle.

At the clock edge, first update the PHT if training is valid, using train_pc XOR train_history, saturating towards train_taken. If training indicates a misprediction, restore ghr to train_history (the committed history after that branch). Otherwise, update ghr by shifting in the actual train_taken.

If training is not valid or no misprediction, and prediction is valid, update ghr by shifting in the predicted bit.

This ensures that if training with misprediction and prediction happen together, training's ghr update takes precedence because assignments in the always block are handled sequentially with non-blocking semantics; by prioritizing training first in the code, the prediction update won't override a training misprediction recovery.

For outputs, the latched prediction_taken and predict_history registers output the prediction results corresponding exactly to the prediction query in the previous cycle (one cycle latency), consistent with the problem statement.

This simple approach respects all specification constraints and eliminates conflicting multiple ghr updates on the same clock edge by ordering them and using if-else logic.

Initialization on reset clears ghr and sets all PHT entries weakly not taken (01).
