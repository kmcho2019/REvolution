Implement the gshare predictor using a single-port synchronous RAM modeled with a 128x2-bit register array updated only on clock edges. Separate combinational read and synchronous write phases to maintain consistent outputs without timing conflicts.

Key differences in this approach:
- Prediction outputs (`predict_taken` and `predict_history`) are registered outputs updated synchronously at the clock edge after combinational read. This ensures output signals are stable and aligned with the clock, simplifying timing relationships.
- Maintain a single global history register updated at clock edge according to strict priority:
  1) On a training misprediction, the GHR is restored to `train_history`.
  2) Else if training valid, GHR is updated by shifting in `train_taken`.
  3) Else if prediction valid, GHR is updated by shifting in the predicted bit.
  4) Otherwise, GHR holds its value.
- Prediction reads are performed combinationally from the PHT indexed by `predict_pc ^ ghr`.
- Training updates happen synchronously at the clock edge based on `train_pc ^ train_history`.
- To avoid conflicts when training and predicting the same entry simultaneously, prediction uses the PHT state from the previous cycle (since training writes happen on clock edge), which aligns with the spec requirement.
- Asynchronous reset clears the GHR and initializes the PHT entries to weakly not taken (2'b01).
- The outputs are always updated on clock edges; thus, `predict_valid` is used as an enable signal for updating outputs, and otherwise outputs hold the last valid prediction.
- This approach ensures timing clarity, avoids combinational output instability, and uses straightforward synchronous read/write logic typical in hardware.

This new approach sacrifices purely combinational output for registered, stable outputs synchronized with clk but aligns perfectly with typical synchronous design practice and simplifies handling of the branch history updates and prioritization.
