The main issue causing mismatches is the timing of updating `predict_history_r` relative to the speculative GHR update. Because all non-blocking assignments in the same always block update simultaneously after RHS are evaluated, `predict_history_r <= spec_ghr;` at the same clock edge as `spec_ghr <= {spec_ghr[5:0], predict_counter[1]};` will actually capture the old `spec_ghr` value correctly. However, any combinational logic or reads using `predict_history` may assume it changes instantly, causing discrepancies in tests.

To clarify and guarantee correct behavior, separate the capture of `predict_history_r` from the speculative GHR update timing by staging it in a separate always block triggered by the rising clock edge and `predict_valid`, *before* speculative GHR update happens in another always block. Alternatively, delay speculative GHR update until the next clock cycle so that `predict_history` output clearly reflects the history used for the prediction at the prediction request time.

Also, convert `predict_taken` output to register updated on the clock edge when `predict_valid` is asserted to avoid glitches and ensure stable prediction output.

Implementation strategy:

- Separate combinational read of PHT for prediction and register `predict_taken_r` and `predict_history_r` updated synchronously on `predict_valid` clock edge.

- Speculative GHR updates at clock edge after `predict_taken_r` and `predict_history_r` capture the current history.

- If `train_valid` and `train_mispredicted` happen simultaneously, speculative GHR is restored to `train_history` (recovery), and `predict_taken_r` and `predict_history_r` updates are suppressed in that cycle.

- Otherwise, if only prediction valid, speculative GHR is updated with predicted direction bit.

- PHT updates happen on training valid at clock edge.

This staging guarantees that the outputs `predict_taken` and `predict_history` reflect the prediction results *based on* the speculative GHR *before* update, matching the problem timing requirements.

This clearer separation also resolves ambiguity and timing hazards that likely caused the previous mismatches.

All asynchronous reset logic remains the same.

This approach strictly follows the problem specification, avoids race conditions, and ensures stable outputs suitable for downstream pipeline stages.
