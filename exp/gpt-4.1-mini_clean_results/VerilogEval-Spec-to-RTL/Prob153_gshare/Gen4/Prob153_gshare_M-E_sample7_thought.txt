Implement a dual-history, dual-port approach to cleanly separate prediction and training paths with independent history registers and pipeline the global history. Specifically:

- Maintain two copies of global history registers:
  - ghr_predict: used exclusively for prediction requests and updated on each prediction.
  - ghr_train: used exclusively for training and updated on each training, including recovery on misprediction.

- This separation avoids conflicts and prioritization logic for GHR updates within a single register.

- Use the current ghr_predict to compute prediction index and output prediction and history combinationally (predict_taken and predict_history).

- On each prediction (predict_valid), update ghr_predict with predicted bit.

- On each training (train_valid), update PHT entry indexed by (train_pc ^ train_history) to increment or decrement counter.

- If train_mispredicted, update ghr_train to train_history to recover, and also synchronize ghr_predict <= train_history to avoid prediction using wrong history.

- If prediction and training happen in the same cycle, training-related ghr_train and ghr_predict update takes precedence (by checking train_mispredicted).

- PHT is updated sequentially on clock edge, using training inputs only.

- Prediction outputs are combinationally generated from current PHT and ghr_predict.

This approach eliminates ambiguity of simultaneous updates, clarifies the temporal relations of history updates, and satisfies the specification's priority of training misprediction recovery. The outputs are stable because they are purely combinational based on registered ghr_predict and stable PHT state, and ghr_predict updates occur only after prediction.

This design simplifies reasoning about updates, neatly separates prediction and training history states, and ensures correct timing and output stability.
