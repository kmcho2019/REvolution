To ensure clear separation of prediction and training updates and avoid ambiguity or race conditions on the global history register, I propose to use a two-stage approach for global history management:

1. Maintain three 7-bit registers:
   - `ghr_committed`: The global history known at the start of the cycle, used to produce predictions (stable during the entire cycle).
   - `ghr_updated`: The global history after speculative prediction update, updated at clock edge if prediction occurs (but only if no misprediction recovery).
   - `ghr_recovered`: The global history recovered from training if a mispredicted branch is trained.

2. On each cycle's clock edge:
   - If training with misprediction occurs, commit the recovered history (`ghr_recovered`) to `ghr_committed`.
   - Else if prediction occurs (and no misprediction training), update the `ghr_committed` by shifting in the predicted direction.
   - Else keep the same `ghr_committed`.

3. During combinational prediction logic within the cycle, always use the `ghr_committed` to produce predictions and output `predict_history`, ensuring outputs reflect the stable global history used for indexing PHT.

4. Update the PHT entries on the positive clock edge only when training is valid, so prediction always reads the old PHT state during the cycle.

5. This explicit state separation cleanly handles the priority of training recovery over prediction updates, avoids off-by-one errors in global history speculation, and guarantees prediction outputs reflect the exact history used.

6. Initialize PHT entries to weakly not taken (01).

This approach effectively serializes the global history updates and resolves timing issues. It also makes the logic easier to understand and verify.

Note: Since only one training and one prediction operation per cycle are possible, this design handles priorities straightforwardly.
