The original solution is fundamentally correct and cleanly separates the committed global history (`ghr_reg`) from the speculative history (`ghr_spec`), ensuring prediction outputs use the stable committed history as required.

However, the main subtlety is ensuring that the speculative history update on prediction uses the *committed* history at prediction time, not the previously updated speculative history. Using `ghr_spec` in the update expression causes an off-by-one cycle delay in the global history shift chain.

To fix this, update `ghr_spec` on prediction as:
- `ghr_spec <= {ghr_reg[5:0], predicted_bit};`

instead of

- `ghr_spec <= {ghr_spec[5:0], predicted_bit};`

This change means the speculative global history update is always derived from the last *committed* global history (stable during the cycle and used for prediction), ensuring correct one-cycle shift semantics with no lag or off-by-one error.

Other logic remains unchanged:
- Training updates PHT at clock edge with saturating counters.
- Training misprediction recovery of global history has priority over prediction update.
- On reset, PHT initialized to weakly not taken (01).
- Prediction output uses committed history and PHT state before training update.

This fix aligns the `predict_history` output with the exact global history state used to index the PHT for prediction, preserving timing correctness and matching the spec precisely.

This approach requires minimal code change but resolves timing and state mismatches without increasing area or complexity.
