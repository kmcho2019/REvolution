The main issue is that the prediction output must reflect the GHR state used for indexing *at the moment of prediction request*, which is the GHR *before* being updated by the predicted outcome. Since the GHR updates on the clock edge after prediction, if multiple predictions happen in successive cycles, combinationally outputting ghr can cause timing mismatches.

To fix this:

- Create a *prediction history register* (a pipeline register) that captures the current GHR synchronously on the clock edge when predict_valid is asserted. This register holds the GHR state used to make the prediction and is output directly at the next cycle, matching the pipeline timing and keeping outputs stable.

- Similarly, hold the prediction index and prediction bit in registers so outputs are stable and consistent with pipeline timing.

- The combinational prediction logic reads from the current GHR and PHT to produce the prediction bits *for the next cycle*. Then on the clock edge, if predict_valid is high (and training mispredict is not taking priority), the GHR is updated speculatively.

- For training, the PHT updates on the clock edge based on the provided train_pc and train_history, and if train_mispredicted is true, the GHR is recovered to train_history (taking priority over speculative update).

- Prediction update to GHR only happens if no training misprediction in the same cycle.

- This scheme decouples prediction outputs (which are registered) from the combinational GHR state, ensuring stable and accurate predict_taken and predict_history outputs that match the problem’s timing requirements.

- Maintain asynchronous reset clearing PHT and GHR registers.

This approach strictly sequences and pipelines the predictor outputs to avoid combinational mismatches and conforms to the spec’s priority and timing requirements.
