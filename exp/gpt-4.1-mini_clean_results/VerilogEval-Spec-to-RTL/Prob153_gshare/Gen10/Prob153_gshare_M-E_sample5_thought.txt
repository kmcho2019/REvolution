To provide a clean and straightforward gshare predictor implementation that clearly separates prediction and training updates with correct global history tracking, I propose the following novel approach:

- Maintain two global history registers:
  - `ghr`: the committed global history used for indexing and outputting `predict_history`.
  - `ghr_next`: a combinational signal representing the speculative next global history after the prediction (or recovery on misprediction).

- Prediction logic uses `ghr` (stable during the cycle) to XOR with `predict_pc` and index the PHT, producing `predict_taken` and `predict_history`.

- At clock edge:
  - Update PHT only if `train_valid` is asserted, adjusting the counter indexed by `train_pc ^ train_history`.
  - Update `ghr` register according to priority:
    - If `train_valid & train_mispredicted`, recover history to `train_history`.
    - Else if `predict_valid`, shift in the predicted direction bit.
    - Else keep `ghr` unchanged.

- Since `ghr_next` is combinational, prediction outputs use stable `ghr`.

- Use nonblocking assignments and synchronous logic only on the clock edge; async reset sets `ghr` and PHT entries.

- This approach cleanly separates stable prediction inputs (`ghr`) and speculative next history (`ghr_next`), avoiding the subtle timing confusion of updating a speculative history register in the same always block as `ghr`.

- Initialize PHT to weakly not taken (01) on reset.

This architecture also cleanly prioritizes training misprediction recovery over speculative prediction history update, as required, with clear and understandable code.
