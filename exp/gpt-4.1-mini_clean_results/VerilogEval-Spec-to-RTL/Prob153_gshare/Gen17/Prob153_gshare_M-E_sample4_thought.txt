To ensure clean separation and precise control over the history state and PHT updates, implement a dual-history approach with a speculative history register and a committed history register.

1. Maintain two global history registers:
   - `committed_ghr`: reflects the last known good global history after all resolved branches.
   - `speculative_ghr`: reflects the speculative global history updated on predictions, before branches retire.

2. Prediction:
   - When `predict_valid` is high, index PHT with `predict_pc ^ speculative_ghr` to get prediction.
   - Output the `speculative_ghr` as `predict_history`, since it's the history used for prediction.
   - Speculatively update `speculative_ghr` by shifting in the predicted taken bit.

3. Training:
   - When `train_valid` is asserted, update the PHT indexed by `train_pc ^ train_history`.
   - If the branch was mispredicted, recover the `speculative_ghr` and `committed_ghr` back to `train_history` to discard incorrect speculation.
   - Otherwise, update `committed_ghr` to the latest correct global history (use the train_history shifted in with actual branch outcome).

4. Priority:
   - If both prediction and training (with misprediction) happen simultaneously, recovery of `speculative_ghr` and `committed_ghr` from training takes precedence.
   - The prediction uses the current `speculative_ghr` before update, so prediction sees consistent PHT state.

5. Reset:
   - Initialize both history registers and all PHT entries to weakly not taken (01).

This method models speculative prediction history updates separately from committed history, allowing recovery on mispredictions and consistent output timing without off-by-one cycle delays.
