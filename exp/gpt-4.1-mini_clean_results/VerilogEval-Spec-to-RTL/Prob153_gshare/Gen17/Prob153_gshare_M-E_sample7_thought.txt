I will implement the gshare predictor with a single global history register and a synchronous update scheme that ensures clear timing and prioritization:

1. Maintain a single 7-bit global history register `ghr` reflecting the speculative global history (used for prediction and updated speculatively).

2. The Pattern History Table (PHT) is a 128-entry array of 2-bit saturating counters.

3. When a prediction request (`predict_valid`) occurs:
   - Use `ghr ^ predict_pc` to index the PHT and output prediction.
   - Output the current `ghr` as `predict_history`.
   - Schedule updating `ghr` on the next clock edge by shifting in the predicted direction bit.

4. When a training request (`train_valid`) occurs:
   - Update the corresponding PHT entry (`train_pc ^ train_history`) based on actual outcome (`train_taken`).
   - If mispredicted (`train_mispredicted`), recover `ghr` to the supplied `train_history` at the next clock edge, thus rolling back speculative updates beyond the mispredicting branch.
   - Otherwise, on non-misprediction training, do not modify `ghr` since it already reflects speculative history, and updates to `ghr` come only from prediction.

5. If both prediction and training (with misprediction) occur simultaneously, training recovery of `ghr` takes priority over the prediction update.

6. This approach uses a single global history register `ghr` that may be updated speculatively on prediction, but is recovered on mispredicted training to ensure correctness.

7. The PHT updates occur on clock edge during training.

8. Asynchronous reset initializes `ghr` and PHT entries.

This architecture simplifies the history tracking, avoids maintaining two separate history registers, and aligns with the problem spec about priority and update order. Output `predict_history` is the `ghr` before updating for that prediction, matching the time of prediction request.

Because only one `ghr` exists, updates and recoveries on training are clean and straightforward.

This design is synthesizable, avoids races, and matches timing requirements.
