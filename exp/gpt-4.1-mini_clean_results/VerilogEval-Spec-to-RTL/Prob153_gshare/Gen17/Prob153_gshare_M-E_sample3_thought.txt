Implement a gshare predictor with a dual-port approach and explicit pipeline staging to cleanly separate prediction and training logic and avoid read-write conflicts on the PHT. 

Key idea:
- Maintain two separate global history registers: one "fetch_ghr" for prediction requests (the committed history at fetch time), and one "train_ghr" for training updates and recovery.
- On a prediction request, use fetch_ghr and predict_pc to form the index into the PHT (XOR), read the saturating counter, and produce prediction outputs immediately (combinationally). Latch the prediction outputs and the fetch_ghr value for output.
- In the next cycle, update fetch_ghr by shifting in the predicted direction.
- On training requests, use the provided train_history and train_pc to update the saturating counter (in the PHT). If misprediction occurred, recover train_ghr to train_history; else update train_ghr by shifting in train_taken.
- Maintain fetch_ghr and train_ghr separately but synchronize fetch_ghr with train_ghr on training flush (misprediction).
- This separation allows prediction and training to operate in parallel, avoids update conflicts, and respects the timing requirements.
- The predict_history output is latched fetch_ghr (the global history at the time of prediction).
- This approach cleanly respects priority: training updates happen to train_ghr and PHT; fetch_ghr recovers from train_ghr when misprediction flush occurs.
- Asynchronous reset initializes all states.

This new architecture avoids complex conditional update chains within one ghr_reg, avoiding subtle race conditions and improves clarity.
