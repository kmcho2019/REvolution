The main issue in the original code was the use of two separate history registers (ghr_pred and ghr_train) that could drift out of sync, causing prediction and training indexing inconsistencies and output mismatches.

To fix this, I will simplify to a single global history register (ghr) that is updated as follows:

- On reset, ghr is cleared to zero.
- On each prediction (predict_valid), the prediction is made using ghr and predict_pc.
- The ghr is updated (shift in predicted bit) at the next clock edge *only if* there is no simultaneous training of a mispredicted branch (train_valid && train_mispredicted).
- On training (train_valid), the PHT entry at (train_pc XOR train_history) is updated synchronously.
- If the training indicates a misprediction (train_mispredicted), then ghr is immediately recovered (at the clock edge) to train_history (the state right after the mispredicted branch completed execution), overriding any prediction update.
- If training is valid but no misprediction, ghr is not changed by training; ghr changes only on prediction.
- When training and prediction occur in the same cycle, training misprediction recovery takes priority for ghr update.
- Prediction reads the PHT asynchronously before the clock edge, and outputs the prediction and current ghr as predict_history, so outputs reflect the pre-update state.

This single-ghr approach matches the problem specification more closely, avoids state divergence, and resolves the root cause of errors.

The saturating counter update and PHT handling remain as before.

This design ensures correct and consistent global history updates and outputs in all cases.
