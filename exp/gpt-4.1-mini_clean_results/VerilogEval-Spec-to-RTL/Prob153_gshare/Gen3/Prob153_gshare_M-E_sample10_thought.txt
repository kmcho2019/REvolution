Implement a gshare predictor with a 7-bit global history register (GHR) and a 128-entry Pattern History Table (PHT) of 2-bit saturating counters. To eliminate timing conflicts and ensure the prediction output matches the PHT state before any training update, we split the PHT into two arrays:

- A **PHT_read** array for combinational read during prediction, updated synchronously.
- A **PHT_write** array updated only on training at clock edge.

At each clock:

- Prediction reads PHT_read indexed by predict_pc XOR GHR, outputs predicted taken and current GHR.
- Training updates PHT_write indexed by train_pc XOR train_history on clock edge.
- After update, PHT_read is updated with the values from PHT_write to reflect the new training state for next cycle.

For GHR updates:

- Maintain two versions: the *current GHR* (for prediction indexing) and a *recovered GHR* used only when train_mispredicted.
- On each clock edge, update GHR based on priority:

    1. If training misprediction (train_valid & train_mispredicted), GHR is restored to train_history (recover state).
    2. Else if predict_valid, shift in predicted taken into GHR.
    3. Else keep current GHR.

This ensures:

- Prediction always outputs immediate results using current GHR and stable PHT_read.
- Training updates happen on clock edge, updating PHT_write which copies into PHT_read afterwards.
- Misprediction recovery restores GHR precisely to the known good history.
- Training takes priority on GHR updates when misprediction occurs.
- Prediction and training can happen simultaneously without clashing because the read array is stable during prediction and updated only after clock edge.

This architecture cleanly separates prediction and training paths and their timing, resolving races and timing ambiguities.
