To fix the functional mismatches and ensure the GHR and PHT updates meet the spec precisely, I will:

1. Separate the combinational and sequential logic clearly by:
   - Using a *stable* version of GHR (`ghr`) for prediction outputs and indexing.
   - Computing prediction outputs combinationally from `ghr` (no changes here).
2. Update the PHT and GHR only at the clock edge.
3. For the GHR update at the clock edge, enforce clear priority:
   - If `train_valid && train_mispredicted` is true, restore `ghr` to `train_history` (flush recovery).
   - Else if `predict_valid` is true, update `ghr` by shifting in the *predicted* branch direction.
   - Else keep `ghr` unchanged.
4. To handle the case when training and prediction happen on the *same* PHT entry, prediction uses PHT state *before* update (combinationally), training updates PHT at clock edge, preserving the spec timing.
5. To avoid race conditions:
   - Read PHT state combinationally for both prediction and training indexes from stable storage.
   - Update PHT at clock edge only.
6. For asynchronous reset, initialize PHT entries to weakly not taken (2'b01) and GHR to zero.
7. Output `predict_history` is the GHR state *used* for the current prediction, i.e., before GHR update.
8. Output `predict_taken` is zero when `predict_valid` is low, to avoid spurious outputs.

This improved approach enforces the spec timing precisely, making predictions combinational and updates synchronous and prioritized correctly. It resolves previous subtle misprediction recovery and update conflicts.
