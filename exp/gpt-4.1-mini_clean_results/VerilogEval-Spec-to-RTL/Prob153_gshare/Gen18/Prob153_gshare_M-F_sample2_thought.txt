The key problem is the ordering and consistency of GHR updates and outputs when prediction and training (especially misprediction recovery) occur in the same cycle. The current design updates GHR at the clock edge giving priority to training recovery, but outputs `predict_history` directly from the current GHR register value, which might cause mismatch when misprediction recovery and prediction happen simultaneously.

To fix this, separate the stable "predict GHR" used during prediction (combinational) from the actual "working GHR" updated on the clock edge. Specifically:

1. Introduce a combinational wire `predict_ghr` holding the GHR used for prediction outputs and PHT indexing. This reflects the stable GHR state before any sequential update on this cycle.

2. Use `predict_ghr` for computing prediction outputs (`predict_taken` and `predict_history`) and the prediction PHT index. This ensures prediction outputs reflect the GHR state *before* speculative updates or training recovery.

3. On clock edge:
   - Update the PHT using the training inputs synchronously.
   - Update the working GHR:
     - If training valid and mispredicted, recover GHR to `train_history`.
     - Else if prediction valid, update GHR by shifting in predicted taken bit.
     - Else keep GHR unchanged.

This clear separation ensures the outputs correspond to the exact GHR used for prediction, matching spec timing.

Additional notes:
- The PHT read is combinational from the current stable array, so prediction uses the PHT state *before* any training update in that cycle, as required.
- The GHR recovery on mispredict has highest priority to ensure flush correctness.
- The `predict_taken` output is gated by `predict_valid`, outputting 0 otherwise, matching previous behavior.
- Reset initializes PHT and GHR reliably.

This approach aligns precisely with the specification and removes timing ambiguity, eliminating the prediction vs. training update conflict and correcting the mismatches.
