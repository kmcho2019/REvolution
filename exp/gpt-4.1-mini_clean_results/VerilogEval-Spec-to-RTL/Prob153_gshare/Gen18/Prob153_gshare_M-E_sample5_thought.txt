I will implement the gshare predictor with a single global history register and a pattern history table (PHT) of 128 2-bit saturating counters. To handle simultaneous prediction and training in the same cycle while respecting priorities, I will do the following:

1. Use one global history register `ghr` representing the speculative history state.

2. On reset, initialize PHT entries to weakly not taken (2'b01) and clear `ghr`.

3. For prediction:
   - Compute the index by XOR of `predict_pc` and the current `ghr`.
   - Read the PHT asynchronously.
   - Output `predict_taken` as the MSB of the saturating counter.
   - Output `predict_history` as the *current* `ghr` (the speculative history before update).
   - At the clock edge, if prediction is valid and no training (or training is invalid), update the `ghr` by shifting in the predicted bit.

4. For training:
   - Compute index by XOR of `train_pc` and `train_history`.
   - Update the PHT at that index with saturating counter updated by `train_taken`.
   - If `train_mispredicted` is high:
     - Recover `ghr` to `train_history` immediately on clock edge (recover global history after misprediction).
   - Else (correct prediction):
     - Update `ghr` by shifting in `train_taken`.

5. If both training and prediction valid in the same clock edge:
   - Training update (including possible recovery) takes precedence.
   - Prediction does not update `ghr`.
   - Prediction output uses `ghr` before update, so no conflict.
   
6. The PHT reads are asynchronous; prediction reads the PHT before update, so it sees previous state even if training updates the same entry.

This approach avoids maintaining separate committed and speculative histories, simplifying state and making updates atomic and deterministic. The output `predict_history` always reflects the global history that was used for prediction and which the PHT index is computed from.

This design cleanly respects timing and priority requirements and minimizes mismatch risk.
