I will refactor the design by separating combinational and sequential logic more explicitly, improving clarity and timing control as follows:

1. **Combinational Prediction Path**: Use combinational logic to generate `predict_taken` and `predict_history` directly from `ghr_spec` and `predict_pc` without registering the output history on clock edge. This ensures `predict_history` always outputs the speculative history used for the prediction instantaneously.

2. **Sequential History Registers**: Keep `ghr_committed` and `ghr_spec` as registers updated only on clock edges, but control updates explicitly based on training and prediction valid signals, with training taking precedence.

3. **Update Rules**:
   - On reset: initialize `ghr_committed` and `ghr_spec` to zero, and PHT entries to weakly not taken (01).
   - On training (valid):
     - Update PHT entry indexed by `train_pc ^ train_history`.
     - If misprediction: recover both `ghr_committed` and `ghr_spec` to `train_history` immediately.
     - Else: update `ghr_committed` by shifting in `train_taken` at LSB.
   - Else if prediction (valid and no training):
     - Update `ghr_spec` by shifting in predicted bit.

4. **Priority**: Training update has priority over prediction update for `ghr_spec`.

5. **PHT accesses**: Read entries combinationally for both prediction and training indices, before clock edge updates, ensuring the prediction sees the PHT state before training updates.

6. **Outputs**:
   - `predict_taken` driven combinationally from PHT and `predict_valid`.
   - `predict_history` driven combinationally from `ghr_spec` so it reflects the history used for prediction immediately.

This explicit separation removes the ambiguity about when `predict_history` is updated and aligns output with prediction timing, fulfilling problem constraints more clearly and correctly.
