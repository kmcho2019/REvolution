I will implement the gshare predictor with a single 7-bit global history register (ghr) and a 128-entry 2-bit saturating counter table (PHT). The PHT is asynchronously read for prediction.

On prediction (predict_valid=1), the predictor outputs the prediction based on PHT[predict_pc ^ ghr] and outputs the current ghr as predict_history (the history used for prediction). At the next clock edge, if no training or training invalid, update ghr by shifting in the predicted bit.

On training (train_valid=1), update the PHT entry indexed by train_pc ^ train_history with the actual taken outcome. If the branch was mispredicted, recover ghr to train_history (the state immediately after the mispredicting branch); otherwise, update ghr by shifting in train_taken.

If both training and prediction occur in the same cycle, training updates take precedence: the ghr update will be from training only and prediction does not update ghr.

PHT updates are synchronous at the clock edge; prediction reads asynchronous PHT values so predictions see PHT state before training updates.

This approach minimizes state complexity, cleanly separates update responsibilities, and respects priority and timing requirements, providing correct prediction outputs and history recovery.
