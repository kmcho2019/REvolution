I will implement the gshare branch predictor without pipelining the prediction path. Instead, I will make the following design choices for clear timing and correctness:

- Maintain a 7-bit global history register (GHR) updated synchronously on clock edges.

- Prediction output (`predict_taken` and `predict_history`) is combinationally generated from the current GHR and PHT content (using `predict_pc ^ ghr` as index) and valid only when `predict_valid` is high.

- The GHR update happens at the clock edge immediately following the prediction: if `predict_valid` was high in the *previous* cycle, GHR shifts in the predicted bit from that cycle.

- Training input updates happen at the clock edge when `train_valid` is asserted.

- On a training misprediction (`train_mispredicted`=1), the GHR is restored synchronously to the `train_history` provided, overriding the prediction-based update. This implements recovery on mispredict flush.

- If training and prediction occur in the same cycle, training takes precedence on GHR update and PHT update.

- Since prediction reads the current GHR and PHT state before updates occur at clock edge, the prediction logic correctly sees pre-update state.

- Initialize PHT to weakly taken (2'b10) on asynchronous reset.

This design avoids pipelining prediction and GHR update, making the timing straightforward and ensuring no race conditions between train and predict updating the GHR: training flush has higher priority at clock edge, otherwise prediction updates. The outputs always reflect current state before update.

This approach is simpler and directly adheres to the timing described: prediction output is combinational from current state, and GHR updates happen synchronously with priority for training mispredict recovery.

Overall, this guarantees correct predictor behavior with clear update precedence and clean interface timing.
