The key issue is timing and ordering of updates to the global history register (`fetch_ghr`) and how prediction outputs `predict_history` are generated. The spec requires that `predict_history` outputs the GHR state used *to make the prediction*, i.e., before the history register shifts in the predicted outcome.

In the original code, `predict_history_reg` is latched directly from `fetch_ghr` at the clock edge when `predict_valid` is asserted, but `fetch_ghr` is updated in the same clock edge *after* output latching, which can cause timing mismatches in simulation or synthesis.

To fix this, we separate the GHR used for prediction output and the GHR updated with new prediction results clearly. The best approach is to register the prediction history *before* the shift-in of the new predicted bit, i.e., store a copy of `fetch_ghr` just before updating it on the positive clock edge. This ensures that `predict_history` reflects the correct history used for indexing.

Additionally, when there is a misprediction training flush (`train_valid && train_mispredicted`), the flush recovery must take precedence: the global history must immediately recover to `train_history` instead of updating with predicted bits.

The code will:

1. Introduce a `predict_history_reg` latched *before* the history update each cycle to hold the history used for the prediction output.

2. On clock edge:
   - If flush due to misprediction, update `fetch_ghr` directly to `train_history`.
   - Else if prediction valid, shift in predicted taken bit to `fetch_ghr`.
   - Else hold `fetch_ghr`.

3. Update `train_ghr` only based on training outcomes; it is separate and used for training the PHT and recovery state.

4. The PHT update logic stays the same: update counters on `train_valid`.

5. Outputs `predict_taken` and `predict_history` come from registered values.

This approach aligns timing exactly to spec, avoids race conditions, and ensures prediction outputs show history state *prior* to the prediction update.

Additionally, all state updates and outputs happen synchronously on clock's positive edge, and asynchronous reset initializes registers properly.

This will fix the ~45% mismatch caused by timing and stale history values.
