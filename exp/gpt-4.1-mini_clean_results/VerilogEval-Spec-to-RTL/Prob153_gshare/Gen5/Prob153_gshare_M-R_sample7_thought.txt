Refactor the gshare predictor to model the PHT as a synchronous RAM with registered read data, ensuring prediction sees the PHT content *before* training updates at the same cycle. Implement a read port for prediction that registers the PHT data on the clock edge prior to output, and a write port for training that updates PHT synchronously.

Maintain a separate "predicted bit" register to hold the prediction for the current cycle, updating the global history (GHR) only on clock edge using a clear priority:
1. On reset, initialize PHT to weakly taken and GHR to zero.
2. On clock edge:
   - If training and misprediction: update PHT at training index, restore GHR to train_history.
   - Else if training only: update PHT at training index, update GHR by shifting in the predicted bit from the *previous* prediction cycle (if valid).
   - Else if prediction only: update GHR by shifting in the predicted bit from the *previous* prediction cycle.
   - Else hold GHR.

To avoid combinational PHT read on pht array, implement a synchronous PHT read using a register:
- Register the prediction index at clock edge.
- On next cycle, output the PHT entry indexed by that registered index.
- Thus, predict_taken and predict_history reflect the state *from previous cycle's* inputs.

Similarly, output predict_history (GHR) at the *prediction request* cycle, meaning hold a register of GHR at the prediction request.

This pipelined approach respects the spec timing: prediction requests get outputs corresponding to PHT and GHR state before training updates in that cycle, and GHR updates occur on clock edge after prediction/training signals.

The design clearly separates combinational and sequential logic and ensures stable and consistent timing of all signals.

This structure also prioritizes training over prediction for GHR updates when both valid, and handles simultaneous training and prediction for the same PHT entry by reading prediction before training write.

Overall, this solves the mismatch and timing issues caused by combinational reads from synchronous memory and ambiguous update order.
