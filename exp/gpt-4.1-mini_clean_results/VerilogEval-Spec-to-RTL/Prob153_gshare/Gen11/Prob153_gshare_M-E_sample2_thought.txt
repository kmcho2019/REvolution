I propose a new approach using a single global history register (ghr) updated only on the clock edge after prediction or training update. To correctly output the prediction results corresponding to the stable global history and PHT state at the start of the cycle, I will:

- Register the prediction outputs (`predict_taken` and `predict_history`) in flip-flops clocked by `clk` to provide cycle-aligned outputs consistent with stable `ghr` and PHT states at the cycle start.

- Perform prediction combinationally in the cycle using the current `ghr` and the input `predict_pc`.

- On the positive clock edge:
  - If `train_valid` and `train_mispredicted` are asserted, recover `ghr` to `train_history` and update the PHT entry indexed by `train_pc ^ train_history` with the actual branch outcome.
  - Else if `train_valid` without misprediction, update the PHT entry indexed by `train_pc ^ train_history`.
  - Else if `predict_valid` and no training misprediction in the same cycle, update `ghr` by shifting in the prediction bit from the PHT entry indexed by `predict_pc ^ ghr`.

This approach cleanly separates the combinational prediction logic from the registered outputs, avoiding output glitches and ensuring that the outputs correspond to the stable history and PHT states before any updates occur on that clock edge.

The PHT is initialized asynchronously on reset to weakly not taken.

This ensures correct prioritization and history recovery on mispredictions, and stable, cycle-aligned outputs for both `predict_taken` and `predict_history`.
