The current implementation is already a minimal and clear combinational LUT with direct mapping from the 3-bit input to the 16-bit output values. To optimize for power and area further without sacrificing performance, I will:

- Replace the LUT array of individual wires with a packed constant ROM implemented as a single 128-bit vector, indexed by 'a'. This approach can help synthesis tools infer a compact ROM or optimized logic.
- Use a continuous assignment with a case statement to guide synthesis for minimal logic and possibly use LUT primitives.
- Avoid multi-dimensional wire arrays, which some tools may handle less efficiently.
- The case statement also makes it explicit to the synthesis tool, enabling possible logic sharing.
- This reduces area slightly by avoiding multiple intermediate wires and can reduce power by reducing the output driver complexity.

This approach keeps the combinational nature intact, preserves direct combinational output, and leverages synthesis tool optimizations to reduce area and power, with zero impact on timing.
