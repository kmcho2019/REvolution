The original design already implements the minimal combinational logic to compute the population count for 3 bits in a very compact and fast manner. The outputs are the parity (odd number of ones) and a majority function indicating if at least two inputs are set. This logic is already minimal and balanced for power, performance, and area.

Given the tiny problem size (3 bits), there is no meaningful way to further reduce gate count or critical path delay without making the logic more complicated or less clear.

However, a minor tweak to slightly optimize power could be achieved by reordering the majority calculation to maximize shared terms or using a 3-input majority gate if available. Yet in pure Verilog, the original logic is best.

Thus, the improvement strategy is to keep the same logic but use intermediate wires to explicitly share partial terms which might enable synthesis tools to optimize gate sharing better and potentially reduce power by decreasing redundant gate duplication.

This refinement has no functional change but may give marginal improvements in power or area by hinting gate sharing.

Hence:
- Extract pairwise AND terms as intermediate signals.
- Reuse these signals for majority calculation.
- Keep parity unchanged for minimal gate delay.

This approach improves clarity and potentially helps synthesis optimization for PPA, although gains are expected to be marginal due to the small scale.
