The original implementation uses direct addition of the three input bits to compute the population count, which is already minimal and efficient. Since the input width is fixed at 3 bits, another way to possibly reduce area and power slightly is to implement the population count with explicit combinational logic instead of addition.

Specifically, the count of ones (0 to 3) can be encoded as a 2-bit output, where:

- out[0] = parity of inputs (odd number of ones)
- out[1] = majority signal (true if two or more inputs are 1)

Using explicit XOR and majority logic may reduce the total switching activity compared to an adder since the majority function can be implemented with just AND-OR gates and parity with XORs.

This approach may yield slightly lower power due to simpler and smaller logic and potentially better delay if implemented in logic gates rather than a multi-bit adder.

Therefore, I propose to implement the population count as:

- out[0] = in[0] ^ in[1] ^ in[2];
- out[1] = (in[0] & in[1]) | (in[1] & in[2]) | (in[2] & in[0]);

This is a known minimal logic implementation for 3-bit population count.

Overall, this may optimize power and area marginally while maintaining zero timing slack.

Note: For 3-bit inputs, this logic is minimal and commonly used in optimized population count circuits.
