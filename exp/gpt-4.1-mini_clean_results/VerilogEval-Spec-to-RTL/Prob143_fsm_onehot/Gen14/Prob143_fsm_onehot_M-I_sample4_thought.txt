To optimize the existing design for better power, performance, and area (PPA), I will focus on reducing the number of gate-level operations by exploiting the structure of the FSM transitions.

Key improvements:
1. Merge common conditions in next_state assignments to reduce repetitive logic, e.g., next_state[0] is frequently the target for input=0 transitions from many states, so express it as a single OR of state bits ANDed with ~in.
2. Use bitwise operations to compute next_state vectors in fewer expressions rather than individual bits where possible.
3. Retain one-hot outputs logic as it is minimal and already optimal.

By combining transitions with identical input conditions, we reduce the number of AND and OR gates, simplifying the logic and potentially reducing switching, power, and delay.

This approach keeps purely combinational logic, preserving clarity and correctness, while improving logic synthesis results by giving the synthesizer more concise expressions to optimize.

Summary of approach:
- Use combined masks of states for input=0 and input=1 transitions that lead to each next state.
- Compute next_state as OR of these masked conditions.
- Use direct bitwise operations instead of many separate assigns.

This should yield more compact and faster logic with less area and switching.
