To improve power and area efficiency, we can share common subexpressions among next_state bits to reduce redundant logic, and group the assignments logically. Since many next_state bits depend on similar conditions (e.g., transitions on input=0 mostly return to S0, transitions on input=1 follow a chain), we can precompute intermediate signals representing these conditions. This reduces logic duplication, lowering gate count and dynamic power. Additionally, expressing the next_state logic via intermediate signals can help synthesis tools optimize the circuit better. The output logic remains simple and can be kept as direct OR gates on the specified states.

This approach retains the fully combinational behavior, respects multiple active states in the input vector, and keeps the one-hot encoding consistent for next_state.
