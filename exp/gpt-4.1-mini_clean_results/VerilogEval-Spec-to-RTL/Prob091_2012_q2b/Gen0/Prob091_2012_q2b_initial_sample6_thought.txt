The finite state machine is one-hot encoded across 6 bits in `y[5:0]`.
The transitions specify the next state for inputs w=0 or w=1.
The output signals Y1 and Y3 correspond to the input signals of the flip-flops for states B and D respectively (since y[1] = B and y[3] = D).

To implement Y1 (the input to the B flip-flop):
- B is next state of A when w=1 (A= y[0], so Y1 += y[0] & w)
- B is next state of C when w=0 (C= y[2], so Y1 += y[2] & ~w)

So, Y1 = (y[0] & w) | (y[2] & ~w)

To implement Y3 (the input to the D flip-flop):
- D is next state of B when w=0 (y[1] & ~w)
- D is next state of C when w=0 (y[2] & ~w)
- D is next state of D when w=0 (y[3] & ~w)
- D is next state of E when w=0 (y[4] & ~w)
- D is next state of F when w=0 (y[5] & ~w)

So, Y3 = (~w) & (y[1] | y[2] | y[3] | y[4] | y[5])

We implement these two equations combinationally.
