The state machine uses one-hot encoding with states A to F represented by individual bits in y[5:0]. The output Y1 and Y3 correspond to the inputs of flip-flops for states B (y[1]) and D (y[3]) respectively.

By inspecting the transition table:
- Y1 (next y[1]) is high when:
  * From A (y[0]) and input w=1 (go to B)
  * From C (y[2]) and w=1 (go to E, not B, so no)
  * From other states, no transition goes to B except from A on w=1.
Thus, Y1 = y[0] & w

- Y3 (next y[3]) is high when:
  * From B (y[1]) and w=0 (go to D)
  * From C (y[2]) and w=0 (go to D)
  * From E (y[4]) and w=0 (go to D)
  * From F (y[5]) and w=0 (go to D)
  * From D (y[3]) and w=0 (go to A), so not D
So Y3 = (y[1] | y[2] | y[4] | y[5]) & ~w

No other next states lead to y[3] on the next cycle.

Therefore:

Y1 = y[0] & w
Y3 = (y[1] | y[2] | y[4] | y[5]) & ~w
