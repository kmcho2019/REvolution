The original design is already minimal and functionally correct. To optimize for better Power, Performance, and Area (PPA):

- Further reduce gate count and switching activity by factoring expressions.

- For Y1, currently Y1 = y[0] & w is minimal.

- For Y3, original is Y3 = ~w & ~(y[0] | y[3]) = (~w) & ~y[0] & ~y[3].

- Since y is one-hot, only one bit is '1' at a time. Given that, y[3] being 1 means state D, y[0] being 1 means state A.

- Y3 is high when w=0 and state is neither A nor D.

- Because y is one-hot, ~(y[0] | y[3]) is the same as (y[1]|y[2]|y[4]|y[5]).

- So, Y3 = ~w & (y[1] | y[2] | y[4] | y[5]).

This form avoids a NOR gate and replaces it with OR gates, which can be more efficient since only one state bit is set and OR with more inputs might reduce glitches.

Hence, implementing:

Y1 = y[0] & w;

Y3 = ~w & (y[1] | y[2] | y[4] | y[5]);

This optimization leverages one-hot property to simplify logic and potentially reduce switching activity and area.

The design remains modular, and logic is clean and easily scalable.
