Instead of directly implementing the next state input logic with simple gates, I will create a decoder module that identifies the current state based on the one-hot encoding of y, making the design easier to extend and understand. The decoder will output six signals representing states A-F. Then, I will write combinational logic for Y1 and Y3 using these decoded states and the input w.

This approach modularizes the state decoding from the next state logic, improving readability and maintainability, especially for larger FSMs. It also clearly separates the one-hot encoding interpretation from the combinational output logic.

The output logic will follow the FSM transitions:

- Y1 (input to y[1], state B flip-flop) is high when from state A (y[0]) with w=1.
- Y3 (input to y[3], state D flip-flop) is high when from states B or C with w=0, or from D with w=0, or from E with w=0, or from F with w=0 (per FSM).

Specifically:

- Y3 = (~w) & (B | C | D | E | F)
- Y1 = w & A

This matches the FSM transition conditions and explicitly uses decoded states.
