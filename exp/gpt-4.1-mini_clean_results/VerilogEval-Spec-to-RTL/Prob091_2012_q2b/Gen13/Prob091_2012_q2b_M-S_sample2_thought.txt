The FSM outputs Y1 and Y3 are simply next state inputs for states B (y[1]) and D (y[3]). According to the transitions:

- Y1 (for B) = y[0] & w (from A to B when w=1)
- Y3 (for D) = ~w & (y[1] | y[2] | y[4] | y[5]) (from B,C,E,F to D when w=0)

Given one-hot encoding, we can express y[1] | y[2] | y[4] | y[5] as (y[1] | y[2] | y[4] | y[5]) directly without using De Morgan transformations or optimization. This makes the expression simple and clear.

The simplification comes from removing module hierarchy and declaring the outputs directly in TopModule with simple continuous assignments. This reduces code complexity without changing functionality.
