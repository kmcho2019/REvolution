To optimize the design for better power and area, I will focus on simplifying the logic for Y3 by re-expressing it to minimize inverters and AND gates, reducing switching activity and gate count.

Starting from the FSM transitions and one-hot encoding:

Y3 corresponds to the next input for y[3] (state D).

From the transitions:

- A(0) --0--> A(0)  (no direct to D)
- B(0) --0--> D(3)
- C(0) --0--> D(3)
- D(0) --0--> A(0)
- E(1) --0--> D(3)
- F(1) --0--> D(3)

So, D is entered when (from B,C,E,F states) and w=0

Expressed in terms of states:

Y3 = (B & ~w) | (C & ~w) | (E & w & 0 ?) Actually E's transition to D on input 0, and E requires w=1 to stay E, so when w=0, from E go to D.

Actually, careful:

- B(0): on 0 -> D
- C(0): on 0 -> D
- D(0): on 0 -> A, no D input here.
- E(1): on 0 -> D
- F(1): on 0 -> D

So, Y3 = (~w) & (y[1] | y[2] | y[4] | y[5]) where states B=1, C=2, E=4, F=5

This is:

Y3 = (~w) & (y[1] | y[2] | y[4] | y[5])

This expression uses only one inverted input (~w) and a four-input OR gate on state bits, which is simpler and potentially less toggling than the previous Y3 = ~w & ~y[0] & ~y[3].

I will implement this minimal and clear expression for Y3.

For Y1:

Y1 = y[0] & w (state A, input 1 leads to B), which is already minimal.

This approach removes multiple negations of y bits and replaces them with a simple OR of positive signals, reducing inverter count and logic depth for Y3.

This will reduce switching and area slightly.

The code will remain modular, with localparams for readability.

No complex gating needed since inputs y and w are one-hot state and control input.

Overall, this optimization should improve power and area marginally by reducing inverter use and simplifying logic for Y3, while maintaining correctness.
