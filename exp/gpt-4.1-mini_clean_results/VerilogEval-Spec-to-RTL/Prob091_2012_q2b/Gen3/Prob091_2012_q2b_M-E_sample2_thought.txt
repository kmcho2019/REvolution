Instead of directly assigning Y1 and Y3 from the current state and input, we can express Y1 and Y3 as sum-of-products formulas derived from the state transitions. By inspecting transitions leading to states B (y[1]) and D (y[3]), we write out the exact minterms:

For Y1 (next y[1]):
- Y1 = (y[0] & w) (A to B on w=1)
There are no other transitions into B, so Y1 = y[0] & w.

For Y3 (next y[3]):
- B(0) --0--> D: y[1] & ~w
- C(0) --0--> D: y[2] & ~w
- E(1) --0--> D: y[4] & ~w
- F(1) --0--> D: y[5] & ~w
Thus Y3 = (y[1] & ~w) | (y[2] & ~w) | (y[4] & ~w) | (y[5] & ~w)

We can factor out ~w for Y3 = ~w & (y[1] | y[2] | y[4] | y[5]).
