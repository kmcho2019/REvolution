The original Y3 expression involves OR-ing y[1], y[2], y[4], and y[5]. However, y[2] (state C) is not a one-hot state reachable for Y3 input when w=0 from the FSM table: C(0) --0--> D. It's already included.

To simplify, we check if y[2] can be merged with others without adding complexity. The OR of these terms is minimal already.

However, we can note that Y3 activates when any of y[1], y[2], y[4], or y[5] is high and w=0. Since w is inverted and ANDed, we can write:

Y3 = (~w) & (y[1] | y[2] | y[4] | y[5])

The Y1 is just y[0] & w and cannot be simplified further.

Therefore, the original solution is already minimal. The best simplification is to rewrite using continuous assignment without parentheses (since & has higher precedence than |), keeping the logic clear and concise.

So, the simplification is stylistic:

Y1 = y[0] & w;

Y3 = ~w & (y[1] | y[2] | y[4] | y[5]);

This reduces parentheses and improves clarity without changing logic.
