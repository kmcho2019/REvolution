An alternate approach is to recognize that the one-hot encoding of states means only one bit in y is high at a time. The output Y1 is the next-state input for y[1] (state B) and Y3 is the next-state input for y[3] (state D).

Looking at transitions ending in these states:

- For Y1 (state B): Only the transition from A (y[0]) on input w=1 goes to B.
  So, Y1 = y[0] & w

- For Y3 (state D): Multiple transitions go to D on input w=0:
  From B (y[1]), C (y[2]), E (y[4]), and F (y[5]) on w=0.
  So, Y3 = (y[1] | y[2] | y[4] | y[5]) & ~w
