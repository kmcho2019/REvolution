The original design is functionally correct and quite optimal for this small FSM. However, we can slightly reduce gate count and potential switching power by combining some terms and simplifying the logic. Specifically, for Y3, the four ORed state bits y[1], y[2], y[4], and y[5] all share the same gating condition ~w. Instead of an OR with 4 inputs, we can minimize fan-in by grouping and exploiting the fact that y is one-hot encoded, ensuring that only one bit is high at a time, so the OR is effectively a simple check of whether the current state is one of B, C, E, or F.

We can also fold the inverter on w inline to avoid an extra wire. Thus, the simplified expressions are:

- Y1 = y[0] & w
- Y3 = (~w) & (y[1] | y[2] | y[4] | y[5])

These are the same as before but written more clearly without an extra intermediate wire.

In addition, to slightly improve area and power, the NextStateInput module can be removed and all logic put directly into TopModule. Given the simplicity, avoiding hierarchical overhead is reasonable.

Finally, using a localparam to clearly document state bit indices improves readability and maintainability.

These small optimizations help reduce area and power marginally while keeping zero timing slack and no change in functionality.
