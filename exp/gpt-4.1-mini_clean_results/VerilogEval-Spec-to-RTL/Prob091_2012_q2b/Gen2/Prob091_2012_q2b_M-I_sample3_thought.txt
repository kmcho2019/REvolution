The original implementation is already minimal and efficient, with simple AND/OR logic and direct one-hot input combinations, resulting in low power and area.

To improve power and area slightly, I will reduce the number of inputs on the OR for Y3 by factoring common terms, avoiding the explicit OR of four terms.

Note that y[4] and y[5] are separate states going to D with ~w, and y[1] and y[2] are also going to D with ~w.

We can rewrite:

Y3 = (~w) & (y[1] | y[2] | y[4] | y[5])  
   = (~w) & ((y[1] | y[2]) | (y[4] | y[5]))  

This grouping doesn't reduce gates but might help synthesis tools better optimize by enabling gate sharing.

Alternatively, since y is one-hot, only one bit is high at a time; thus the OR can be simplified by checking if any of these bits are set.

Therefore, a more efficient way is to use a 6-bit mask to pick y[1], y[2], y[4], y[5] and OR them.

I will implement a reduction OR over a masked y to keep logic compact and potentially enable synthesis tool optimization.

This might reduce area and switching by letting synthesis tools optimize bitwise reduction better.

Y1 remains the same as a simple AND.

This is a small micro-optimization for cleaner code and potential slight PPA gains.
