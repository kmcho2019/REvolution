The existing solution is already minimal and correct, using simple combinational logic derived directly from the FSM and one-hot encoding. The main outputs are:

- Y1 = y[0] & w (next input for y[1])
- Y3 = ~w & ~(y[0] | y[3]) (next input for y[3])

This uses minimal gates and low fan-in, which is good for power and area.

For further optimization:

1. Use De Morgan's theorem on Y3:
   Y3 = ~w & ~(y[0] | y[3]) = ~w & (~y[0] & ~y[3])
   Which is equivalent to:
   Y3 = ~w & ~y[0] & ~y[3]

   Writing Y3 as an AND of three signals may slightly reduce gate complexity or gate fan-in by enabling direct NAND-NAND style synthesis.

2. Inline the inversion of w to avoid an intermediate wire, reducing signal toggling and slight area reduction.

3. Combine the logic expressions into single assign statements without intermediate wires to minimize net count.

4. Retain modularity by keeping NextStateInput module.

These small changes may reduce switching and area slightly without affecting performance or correctness.
