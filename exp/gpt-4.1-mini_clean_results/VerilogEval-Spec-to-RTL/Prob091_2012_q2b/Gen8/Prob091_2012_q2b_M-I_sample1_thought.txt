The original solution uses a modular style with a separate combinational NextStateInput module and explicitly computes Y1 and Y3 signals for next-state flip-flops of y[1] and y[3].

Improvement Strategy:
1. Flatten the design to a single module since the FSM is small, avoiding module overhead and potential synthesis complexity.
2. Introduce a common wire for the OR of states relevant to Y3, reducing repeated OR operations and potentially improving area and power.
3. Simplify logic expressions by leveraging the one-hot encoding explicitly:
   - Y1 = y[0] & w (single AND)
   - Y3 = ~w & (y[1] | y[2] | y[4] | y[5]) (common OR gate combined with w)
4. Use wire assignment for intermediate signals to help synthesis tool optimize further.
5. This maintains zero timing slacks, minimal gate depth, and can slightly reduce area and power by sharing OR terms.

Overall, the changes aim for minimal structural modifications, improving power and area slightly without affecting performance or readability.
