Implement a simple FSM with four states: SEARCH, LOAD_DELAY, COUNT, WAIT_ACK. 

In SEARCH, shift in bits MSB first to detect pattern 1101. Shift new bits into MSB: pattern_shift <= {pattern_shift[2:0], data} is LSB-first, so instead shift right and insert at MSB: pattern_shift <= {data, pattern_shift[3:1]}.

Once pattern is detected, move to LOAD_DELAY where 4 bits are shifted in MSB first (same method). After all 4 bits are loaded, latch delay_value.

In COUNT, use two counters: cycle_counter counts 0..999, and tick_counter counts down from delay_value+1 down to zero. Each 1000 cycles decrement tick_counter by one. The output count shows current tick_counter-1 (stable for 1000 cycles). When tick_counter reaches zero and cycle_counter completes its last 1000 cycles, transition to WAIT_ACK.

In WAIT_ACK, assert done output until ack input goes high, then reset to SEARCH.

Use synchronous reset. Update counters and state only on clock edges with non-blocking assignments. Freeze shift registers outside of SEARCH or LOAD_DELAY.

Simplify by initializing counters explicitly when entering COUNT state to avoid tricky edge detection. Keep outputs directly derived from state and counters.

This approach guarantees pattern detection, delay loading, and precise counting behavior with clear, readable logic.
