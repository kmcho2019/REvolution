To improve and fix the original design's functional issues and meet the exact timing requirement without using costly division by 1000, I will:

- Replace the total cycle counter and division by 1000 with two counters:
  1. A 10-bit cycle counter (`cycle_count_1000`) that counts clock cycles from 0 to 999.
  2. A 4-bit remaining delay counter (`remaining_delay`) which starts at `delay` and decrements by 1 every 1000 cycles.
- The counting duration becomes exactly `(delay + 1) * 1000` cycles since `remaining_delay` counts down from `delay` to 0, and for each count, 1000 cycles are spent.
- `count` output is directly assigned the `remaining_delay` value, which changes every 1000 cycles.
- The FSM is cleaned to:
  - Detect pattern `1101` synchronously and transition immediately after shifting in the new bit.
  - During `LOAD_DELAY`, shift in delay bits MSB first over 4 cycles, then move to COUNT.
  - In COUNT, hold input data and pattern_shift stable.
  - When `remaining_delay` reaches zero and `cycle_count_1000` reaches 999 (end of last 1000 cycle period), move to DONE.
- Outputs `counting` and `done` asserted in their respective states.
- In DONE state, wait for `ack` to restart pattern search.
- Remove unnecessary self-assignments and reset signals only on reset or state transitions, avoiding glitches.
- All logic synchronous, and FSM next state logic consolidated and simple.
- This approach is hardware-friendly, avoids division, and guarantees exact timing cycle counts.

This design ensures exact `(delay+1)*1000` cycles timing, correct and stable outputs, and clean state transitions.
