Use a simple FSM with five states: IDLE, PATTERN_MATCH, LOAD_DELAY, COUNT, and WAIT_ACK.

- In IDLE, reset all registers and start pattern detection with a 4-bit shift register that shifts left to keep newest bit at LSB; after every bit, check if the last 4 bits match pattern 1101 (i.e. bits [3:0] == 4'b1101).
- When pattern detected, move to PATTERN_MATCH briefly to confirm and then to LOAD_DELAY.
- In LOAD_DELAY, shift in 4 delay bits MSB first by shifting delay register left and inserting the new bit at LSB: delay <= {delay[2:0], data}.
- After loading 4 bits, transition to COUNT.
- In COUNT, use two counters: a 12-bit master counter counting total cycles and a 4-bit segment counter showing remaining segments.
- The total count cycles = (delay + 1)*1000; master counter counts down every clock.
- The segment counter shows which 1000-cycle segment remains by counting from delay down to 0; decrement segment counter every 1000 cycles.
- While counting, output `count` = current segment count, `counting`=1, `done`=0.
- When counting finishes (master counter=0), assert done=1, counting=0, and wait for ack.
- Upon ack, return to IDLE to restart pattern detection.
- All synchronous logic on posedge clk with synchronous active-high reset.
- Use simple parameters for states (no enums) and avoid don't-care x-values, set `count` to 0 when not counting.

This approach simplifies bit shifts, uses a single master counter, and tracks segments by dividing master counter. It clearly separates pattern detection, delay loading, counting, and waiting.
