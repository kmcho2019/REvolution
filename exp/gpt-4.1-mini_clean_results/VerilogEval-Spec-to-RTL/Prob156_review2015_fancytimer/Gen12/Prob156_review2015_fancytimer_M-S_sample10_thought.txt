I will simplify the design by implementing a clean FSM with the following states:

- IDLE: shift in serial bits to detect the pattern 1101.
- LOAD_DELAY: shift in 4 delay bits MSB first.
- COUNTING: count cycles and output remaining count.
- DONE: assert done, wait for ack.

Key simplifications and clarifications:

- Use a 4-bit shift register always shifting in data each clock, both for pattern detection in IDLE and delay bits in LOAD_DELAY.
- In IDLE, shift serial data until pattern 1101 detected.
- In LOAD_DELAY, shift next 4 bits MSB first into delay_reg.
- Once delay loaded, start counting for (delay+1)*1000 cycles.
- Maintain a 10-bit cycle counter 0..999 counting clock cycles per segment.
- Maintain a 4-bit segment counter initialized to delay, counting down to 0.
- The count output reflects current segment counter (remaining delay segments).
- counting is high in COUNTING state.
- done is high in DONE state.
- When counting completes (segment==0 && cycle_counter==999), move to DONE.
- After ack in DONE, return to IDLE to detect next pattern.
- All outputs and state updates are synchronous and updated once per clock.
- Reset synchronous active-high resets all registers.

This approach reduces complexity by merging pattern detection and delay loading into simple shift-register operations, and a straightforward counting down implementation. It guarantees correct timing, clear output behavior, and fewer registers and states.
