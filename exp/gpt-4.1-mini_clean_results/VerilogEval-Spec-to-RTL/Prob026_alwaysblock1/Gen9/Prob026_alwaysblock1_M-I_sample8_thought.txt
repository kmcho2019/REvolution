The original design is functionally correct and minimal in logic. However, since both outputs implement the same AND function separately, it results in duplicated logic, increasing area and dynamic power consumption. To optimize for area and power, the AND operation can be computed once internally and its result assigned to both outputs: one via assign, the other via the always block. This approach avoids duplicating the AND gate, reducing area and switching power, while keeping both outputs driven from a single computation. This still meets the requirement of using both an assign statement and an always combinational block.
