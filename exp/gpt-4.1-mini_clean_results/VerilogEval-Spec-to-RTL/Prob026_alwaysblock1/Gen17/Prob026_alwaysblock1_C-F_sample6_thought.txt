To fuse the best ideas from both examples, implement a single intermediate wire to compute the AND of inputs 'a' and 'b'. Drive `out_assign` directly from this wire with an assign statement, and assign `out_alwaysblock` from the same wire inside a combinational always block. This approach centralizes the combinational logic once, enabling the synthesis tool to share the AND gate between the two outputs, reducing area and power. It keeps the clarity of separating continuous and procedural assignments and ensures consistent outputs. This fusion avoids duplicating the AND expression in two places (which might lead to two separate gates), but also avoids relying on two separate AND gates inferred from separate expressions. It also provides clean, maintainable code with minimal net overhead.
