To optimize the original Mealy FSM design for better PPA (performance, power, area), we will:

1. Adopt one-hot state encoding for the 3 states to reduce combinational logic depth in next-state and output logic, improving timing and potentially reducing glitch power.

2. Combine next-state and output logic into a single combinational always block to share conditions and minimize redundant logic.

3. Keep the asynchronous negative edge reset and positive edge clock sequential logic as per the requirement.

4. Use non-blocking assignments in sequential block and blocking assignments in combinational block as best practice.

5. Maintain output `z` as a combinational signal asserted only when sequence "101" is detected (transition condition in TEN state with input x=1).

This approach trades a small increase in flip-flop count (3 FFs) for simpler combinational logic and potentially better timing and power due to reduced logic depth and glitching. The simple one-hot FSM with explicit states and combined logic enables the synthesis tool to optimize more aggressively.
