Implement the 3-state Mealy FSM using one-hot encoding for states instead of binary encoding. This means each state is represented by a separate flip-flop bit. This approach may increase area slightly but simplifies next state logic, often improving timing by reducing logic levels. The output logic will still be combinational and assert z when the "101" sequence is detected. Maintain asynchronous negative-edge reset on all state flip-flops for immediate reset response. To handle overlapping sequences, transitions will be carefully designed so after detecting the sequence, the FSM returns to the appropriate state. This one-hot FSM style offers a different architectural perspective from binary encoded states.
