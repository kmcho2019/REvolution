To optimize the FSM for better performance and potentially lower power while maintaining the same three states and Mealy behavior, we can:

- Use one-hot state encoding instead of binary encoding. One-hot encoding typically allows faster state decoding, reducing critical path delay especially on FPGAs, though at a slight area increase.
- Separate the output z signal to a dedicated combinational logic expression derived directly from state and input, which can help reduce glitches and improve timing.
- Use non-blocking assignments for sequential logic and blocking assignments in the combinational always block.
- Keep the asynchronous negative-edge reset as specified.
- Make the combinational logic straightforward and minimal to improve timing.

This approach likely slightly increases area but improves performance (lower TNS and WNS) due to simpler combinational logic and one-hot states. Power may remain similar or slightly reduced due to reduced glitching on the output.

We keep the same 3 states:

- S0: no match
- S1: matched '1'
- S2: matched '10'

The state transitions remain the same but encoded in one-hot:

- S0 = 3'b001
- S1 = 3'b010
- S2 = 3'b100

Output z is asserted when in S2 and x == 1.

This design balances the trade-offs well and keeps clarity.
