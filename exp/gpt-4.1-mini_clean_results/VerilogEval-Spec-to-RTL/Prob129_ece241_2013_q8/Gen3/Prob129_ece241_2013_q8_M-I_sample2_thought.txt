To optimize the FSM for better performance, power, and area (PPA), the main focus is simplifying the next-state logic and reducing unnecessary transitions without changing the functional behavior.

Improvements:
1. Use one-hot encoding for states to potentially improve synthesis timing on FPGA or ASIC flows where one-hot FSMs can yield faster clocks, at the minor expense of area. However, since the state count is only 3, area increase is minimal.
2. Simplify next state logic by directly encoding transitions without redundant assignments.
3. Reduce toggling of the output signal z by asserting it only in the minimal logic condition, avoiding unnecessary recomputation.
4. Maintain the asynchronous negative edge reset as required.
5. Use blocking assignments in combinational logic for clarity and synthesis friendliness, non-blocking for sequential.

By employing one-hot encoding, the FSM uses three flip-flops, each representing one state. The transitions become simple assignments without extra decoding logic, which can reduce critical path delay and power switching activity.

This trade-off slightly increases area (three flip-flops vs two) but generally improves timing and can reduce glitching in logic. Given the small FSM, area increase is minimal.

Overall, the redesign trades slight area increase for better timing and power efficiency, which improves the PPA metrics while maintaining correctness and meeting all specs.
