The original design uses full adders for each bit with carry-lookahead logic. However, the full adders still compute carry-out internally, which is unused, causing unnecessary switching power and area. Also, the carry-lookahead logic is implemented as simple ripple carry on the carry signals, so not a true parallel carry computation.

To optimize for power, area, and performance, I will:
- Remove the FullAdder module entirely.
- Implement sum bits directly using XOR of propagate signals and carry-in.
- Compute carries fully in parallel using carry-lookahead equations based on generate and propagate signals, avoiding sequential carry dependency.
- The carries will be computed as:
    c[0] = 0 (initial carry-in)
    c[1] = g[0] | (p[0] & c[0])
    c[2] = g[1] | (p[1] & g[0]) | (p[1] & p[0] & c[0])
    c[3] = g[2] | (p[2] & g[1]) | (p[2] & p[1] & g[0]) | (p[2] & p[1] & p[0] & c[0])
    c[4] = g[3] | (p[3] & g[2]) | (p[3] & p[2] & g[1]) | (p[3] & p[2] & p[1] & g[0]) | (p[3] & p[2] & p[1] & p[0] & c[0])
- This parallel carry computation reduces critical path delay and avoids carry rippling.
- Directly compute sum bits as sum[i] = p[i] ^ c[i].
- Output the final carry c[4] as sum[4].
- This approach minimizes logic duplication, saves area and power by removing unused carry logic, and improves performance by reducing carry chain delay.

Overall, the design becomes a purely combinational carry-lookahead adder without explicit full adder modules.
