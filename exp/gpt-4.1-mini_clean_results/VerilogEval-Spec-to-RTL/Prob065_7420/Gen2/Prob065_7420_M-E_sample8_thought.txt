Instead of implementing the 4-input NAND gates by direct gate-level expression or dedicated modules, this design uses a behavioral approach by leveraging the built-in reduction AND operator in Verilog and inverting its result. This minimizes the module hierarchy and uses concise expressions that rely on the synthesizer's capability to infer optimized NAND gates. Additionally, it uses intermediate wires to hold the AND results before negation, which can improve readability and potentially help synthesis tools recognize shared sub-expressions if inputs overlap in a larger context. This approach also simplifies the design, making it easy to extend or modify with minimal code changes.
