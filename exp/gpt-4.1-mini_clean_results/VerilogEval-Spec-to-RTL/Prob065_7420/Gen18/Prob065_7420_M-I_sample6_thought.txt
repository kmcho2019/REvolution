The original design cleanly implements the 7420 chip using two instances of a dedicated 4-input NAND gate module, which is already minimal and synthesis-friendly. Given the simplicity and combinational nature, performance and area are essentially optimal. Power consumption is minimal due to direct NAND implementation.

However, one small optimization is to avoid an extra module instantiation by inlining the NAND gates directly in the TopModule. This eliminates module hierarchy overhead, which could slightly reduce area and power by allowing the synthesis tool to optimize the logic more aggressively. Moreover, we can leverage bitwise operations with concatenated inputs to slightly simplify the code.

Thus, my improvement strategy is:
- Inline the two 4-input NAND gates directly inside TopModule using continuous assignments.
- Use bitwise reduction AND operators (&) to evaluate all four inputs per gate, then invert for NAND output.
- Keep clear comments to maintain readability.
- This approach reduces module count, possibly improving area and power marginally without impacting timing.

Overall, this simplification will maintain functional correctness and slightly improve area and power metrics while preserving clarity and performance.
