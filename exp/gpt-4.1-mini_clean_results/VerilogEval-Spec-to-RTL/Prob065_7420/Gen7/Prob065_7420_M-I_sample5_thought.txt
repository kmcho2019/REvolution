The original design is already clean and modular, explicitly instantiating two 4-input NAND gates. To further optimize for PPA (performance, power, and area), we can simplify the code by eliminating the extra module and directly implementing the NAND functionality using a single assign statement per output. This reduces module overhead and synthesis complexity. Modern synthesis tools will infer optimal NAND gates from the direct logic expressions, likely matching or improving area and power compared to module instantiations.

This approach:
- Simplifies the design hierarchy for slightly reduced area.
- Keeps the logic purely combinational for maximum speed.
- Avoids the overhead of an extra module instantiation, which can help reduce routing and area.
- Is highly synthesizable and maps efficiently to standard-cell libraries.

No functional change occurs; just a more concise, synthesis-friendly implementation.
