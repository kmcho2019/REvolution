The original solution already implements the 7420 chip as two separate 4-input NAND gates with a minimal and clean design. Since the 7420 device internally consists precisely of two independent 4-input NAND gates, the design matches the functionality and structure exactly, leading to optimal area and timing.

However, to potentially optimize for power and area slightly further, we can merge the two 4-input NAND gates into a single module with parameterizable inputs or directly inline the NAND logic for minimal module overhead. Given that synthesis tools are highly efficient, removing the nand4 module and implementing the gates as direct continuous assignments in TopModule can reduce module instantiation overhead and slightly improve power by eliminating nets and module boundaries.

Therefore, the improved design will:
- Remove the intermediate nand4 module.
- Implement the two 4-input NAND gates directly inside TopModule using continuous assignments.
- This reduces hierarchy and simplifies the netlist, possibly reducing synthesis overhead and switching.
- The logic remains combinational and straightforward, retaining optimal timing and area.

This change trades off a small amount of modularity for potentially better power and area without affecting functional correctness.
