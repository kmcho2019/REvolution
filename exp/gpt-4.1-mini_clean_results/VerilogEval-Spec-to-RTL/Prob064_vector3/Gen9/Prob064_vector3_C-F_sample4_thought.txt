Fuse the clarity and modular concatenation approach of Example 2 with the explicit and clear slicing of Example 1. Create two intermediate 15-bit wires (first_half and second_half) from the six 5-bit inputs, then concatenate these with the two appended '1' bits to form a 32-bit combined wire. Instead of relying on a single combined-to-{w,x,y,z} direct assignment, explicitly slice the combined wire into four 8-bit output vectors (w, x, y, z). This fusion enhances readability, debuggability, and clarity on the output bit mapping, which is valuable for verification and synthesis reports. It keeps the design purely combinational and minimal, while structurally clean and maintainable.
