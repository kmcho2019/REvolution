Use a simple linear FSM with these states:  
- A: reset active, outputs low  
- B: one-cycle pulse f=1 immediately after reset release  
- C1, C2, C3: detect x sequence 1,0,1 step by step  
- D0, D1, D2: after sequence detected, g=1 and monitor y for up to two cycles (0,1,2)  
- E: permanent success (g=1)  
- F: permanent failure (g=0)  

This uses explicit states to track sequence and y-monitoring count instead of counters, simplifying logic and removing off-by-one errors.  

On reset (sync, active low), go to A, outputs 0. On release, go to B, f=1 one cycle. Then go to C1.  
In C states, track x sequence exactly.  
Upon sequence completion, go to D0 with g=1, check y each cycle D0→D1→D2. If y=1 in D0-D2, go E, else after D2 go F.  
Outputs depend only on state, synchronous.  

This explicit FSM avoids counters, is easy to understand, and ensures correct one-cycle pulses and timing.
