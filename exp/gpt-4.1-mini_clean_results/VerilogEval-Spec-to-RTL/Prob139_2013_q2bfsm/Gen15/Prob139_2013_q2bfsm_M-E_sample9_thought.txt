Use a multi-stage FSM with the following states:

- STATE_A: Initial/reset state, wait until resetn is de-asserted.
- STATE_B: One-cycle pulse of f=1 after reset de-assertion.
- STATE_C0, C1, C2: Three sequential substates to detect x=1, then 0, then 1 in consecutive clock cycles.
- STATE_D: One-cycle pulse of g=1 after pattern detected.
- STATE_E0, E1: Two-cycle window to monitor y input with g=1 asserted.
- STATE_F: g=1 permanent (y=1 detected within two cycles)
- STATE_G: g=0 permanent (y=1 not detected within two cycles)

This FSM directly encodes the sequence detection using explicit states instead of a shift register, ensuring precise input timing capture and avoiding off-by-one shifts.

Outputs f and g are registered signals driven by states.

Reset is synchronous active low.

The FSM transitions are carefully ordered so that:

- After resetn goes high, on next clock edge f=1 for one cycle (STATE_B),
- Then FSM waits for x=1 (STATE_C0), then x=0 (STATE_C1), then x=1 (STATE_C2),
- After sequence matched, g=1 pulse (STATE_D),
- Then g=1 while monitoring y in two states (STATE_E0, STATE_E1),
- Depending on y, transition to permanent g=1 (STATE_F) or g=0 (STATE_G).

This explicit state-by-state detection guarantees cycle-accurate behavior matching requirements and minimizes misalignment of outputs and inputs.
