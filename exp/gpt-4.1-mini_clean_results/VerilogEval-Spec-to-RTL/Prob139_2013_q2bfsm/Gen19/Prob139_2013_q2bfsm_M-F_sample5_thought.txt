The primary issue is improper handling of the pattern detection for input `x`: the FSM stops incrementing the sample count after 3 samples and thus does not detect the pattern on a rolling basis.

To fix this:

- Remove the sample count register altogether and always shift in the latest `x` sample every cycle during STATE_C. This allows continuous detection of the 3-bit pattern sliding over `x` inputs.

- Check for pattern `1,0,1` at every cycle once three samples are available (which we can assume after at least two shifts).

- Use a small flag `pattern_ready` to indicate when pattern detection can start (after 3 samples). To simplify, assume the shift register is valid after two clock cycles of shifting and simply start checking from then on.

- For the synchronous reset (active low), ensure FSM stays in STATE_A when `resetn` is low, transitions to STATE_B after release, where `f` pulses high for one cycle.

- Outputs are Moore type, driven purely by state.

- Keep counting `y_timer` during STATE_E to monitor `y` input for up to 2 cycles and determine permanent g=1 or g=0 state accordingly.

- All registers update on rising clock edge with synchronous active low reset.

This approach allows continuous pattern detection and correct output timing, fixing the main FSM logic causing the mismatch in `f` and `g` outputs.
