We can simplify the FSM by carefully defining fewer states that clearly match each step described:

- STATE_A: reset asserted, hold outputs low.
- STATE_B: after reset de-asserted, output f=1 for exactly one cycle.
- STATE_C: wait for x=1 to start sequence detection.
- STATE_D: wait for x=0 (second step in sequence).
- STATE_E: wait for x=1 (third step).
- STATE_F: output g=1 one cycle to indicate sequence detected.
- STATE_G0: first cycle monitoring y for 1.
- STATE_G1: second cycle monitoring y for 1.
- STATE_H: permanent g=1 after y=1 detected.
- STATE_I: permanent g=0 after y=1 not detected in two cycles.

By encoding the states simply with parameters and using Moore outputs, the FSM structure is very clear and minimal.

The reset is synchronous active-low: on resetn=0 FSM stays in STATE_A; on resetn=1 at next clock FSM moves to STATE_B to pulse f; then sequence detection happens in STATES C, D, E by checking x on each clock.

Once sequence 1,0,1 detected, FSM pulses g=1 for one cycle in STATE_F, then monitors y in G0 and G1 states. If y=1 in G0 or G1, FSM goes to permanent g=1 state H; else after 2 cycles FSM goes to permanent g=0 state I.

Outputs f and g are assigned based on current state. This clear separation eliminates extra pulses or ambiguity, minimizing mismatches.

This approach reduces the number of states, makes state transitions explicit and synchronous with input conditions, and cleanly separates output pulses from permanent output states.

Overall, it improves clarity, correctness, and should match the requirements exactly with fewer states and simpler logic.
