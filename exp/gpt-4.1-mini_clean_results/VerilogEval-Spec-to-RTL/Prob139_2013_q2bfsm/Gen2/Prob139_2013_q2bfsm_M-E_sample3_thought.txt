Use a Moore FSM with a minimal number of states by encoding the sequence detection and output logic within the state transitions. Instead of having separate states for each step of the x=1,0,1 pattern detection, implement a small shift register (3 bits) to hold the last three samples of x. When this shift register matches "101", trigger g=1 for one cycle and then enter a monitoring phase for y. During the y-monitoring phase, use a 2-bit counter to track up to two clock cycles: if y=1 within two cycles, stay in a permanent g=1 state; otherwise, switch to a permanent g=0 state. Use synchronous active-low reset to hold in initial state A. Output f is asserted one clock cycle immediately after reset deassertion and before starting pattern detection. This approach reduces states drastically (only a few states: IDLE, F_PULSE, MONITOR_Y, G_ON_PERM, G_OFF_PERM) and leverages a small shift register and counter for pattern matching and timeout. This simplification improves clarity, and the FSM outputs depend only on the state, fitting a Moore machine model, with all sequential logic clocked on posedge clk and synchronous active-low reset.
