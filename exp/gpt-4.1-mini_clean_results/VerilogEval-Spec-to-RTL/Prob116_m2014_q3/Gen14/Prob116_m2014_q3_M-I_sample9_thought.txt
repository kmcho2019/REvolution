The original solution correctly implements the K-map function by converting Gray-coded inputs to binary, then using a 16-entry case statement. However, this adds unnecessary complexity and area overhead.

Improvement strategy:
1. Since the input bits are given as x[3:0], and the Karnaugh map is defined with axes in Gray code, converting them to binary is correct but leads to a large case statement. Instead, we can directly derive the minimized boolean expression from the Karnaugh map, working directly on the inputs without Gray-to-binary conversion.
2. The Karnaugh map with don't-cares can be minimized using standard techniques to obtain a simpler expression in terms of input bits, avoiding the large case statement.
3. Implement this minimized boolean expression combinationally using standard logical operators, which reduces area, power, and improves performance by minimizing logic depth.
4. Since inputs are x[3:0], we need to confirm the mapping of bits to rows and columns per the Karnaugh map and directly write the expression.

From the Karnaugh map:

   x[1]x[2]
x[3]x[4]   00  01  11  10
    00 | d | 0 | d | d |
    01 | 0 | d | 1 | 0 |
    11 | 1 | 1 | d | d |
    10 | 1 | 1 | 0 | d |

Let's label bits for clarity as per the original:

x[3] = MSB, x[0] = LSB, i.e., x = {x[3], x[2], x[1], x[0]}

Mapping to Karnaugh map axes:
- Row: x[3] x[2]
- Column: x[1] x[0]

Use the given map directly with inputs as they are:

Simplify function f for inputs x[3], x[2], x[1], x[0]:

From the K-map, after careful minimization and using don't-cares, the minimal sum of products is:

f = (x[3] & ~x[2])             // row 10: covers 1's in columns 00,01
  | (x[3] & x[2])              // row 11: covers 1's in columns 00,01
  | (~x[3] & x[2] & x[1] & ~x[0])  // row 01, col 11: single 1
  | (~x[3] & x[2] & ~x[1] & ~x[0]); // row 01, col 00: 0 so not included, only 1 at col 11

But we must check the exact positions of 1's:

- Row 10 (x[3]=1, x[2]=0): f=1 for columns 00 (x[1]=0,x[0]=0), 01 (0,1), 0 in 11(1,1), d in 10(1,0)
  => So row 10 covers columns 00 and 01 with 1's → (x[3] & ~x[2]) & (~x[1] | x[0]) ?

- Row 11 (1,1): 1's at columns 00(0,0), 01(0,1), d's at 10 and 11 → (x[3] & x[2]) & (~x[1])

- Row 01 (0,1): 1 at column 11 (x[1]=1, x[0]=1), 0 at 00 and 10, d at 01

- Row 00 (0,0): all d's and 0 at column 01

We can write expressions:

- Group 1: Rows 10 and 11, columns 00 and 01: f=1 at these points

(x[3] & ~x[2] & ~x[1]) // row 10, col 00
(x[3] & ~x[2] & ~x[1] & x[0]) // row 10, col 01 - also 1
(x[3] & x[2] & ~x[1]) // row 11, col 00 and col 01

This simplifies to:

(x[3] & ~x[1]) // since rows 10 and 11 require x[3]=1 and x[1]=0 in columns 00 or 01

- Single 1 at row 01, col 11: ( ~x[3] & x[2] & x[1] & x[0] )

Combining:

f = (x[3] & ~x[1]) | (~x[3] & x[2] & x[1] & x[0])

This is minimal and covers all 1s in K-map.

This expression uses only AND, OR, and NOT, easy for logic synthesis and minimal area/power.

Hence, implement this boolean function directly without Gray-to-binary conversion or case statement.

This reduces logic depth, area, and power substantially.
