Instead of using a case statement indexed by a permuted input vector, this solution directly implements the minimized logic expression derived from the Karnaugh map. 

By analyzing the Karnaugh map and treating the don't-care values as flexible, we can derive a simplified Boolean expression for the output f in terms of the inputs x[3:0] (where x[3] = MSB, x[0] = LSB).

The Karnaugh map given with rows as x[3]x[4] and columns as x[1]x[2] (interpreted as x[2]x[3] rows, x[0]x[1] columns in Verilog):

From the map, we identify minterms with f=1 and try to cover all 1's efficiently:

1) Group the four 1's in row x[3]x[4]=11 (addr 1100,1101,1110,1111): 
   But some are 'd' and some 0, so we only take definite 1's:
   Entries: 1100=1,1101=1 (1110,1111 d or 0)
   Group these with nearby ones if possible.

2) Group row 10: 1000=1,1001=1

3) Minterm at 0111=1

After analysis, the following simplified expression covers all the 1s with don't-cares assigned for optimal simplification:

f = (x[3] & ~x[2])         // row 10 covers 1's in columns 00 and 01
  | (x[3] & x[2])          // row 11 covers 1's in columns 00 and 01
  | (~x[3] & x[1] & x[0] & x[2])  // single minterm at 0111 (row=01 col=11)

Simplifying the first two terms: f = x[3] | (some condition)

But check if x[3]=1 covers all 1's in rows 10 and 11; yes.

The one 1 at 0111 (x[3]=0,x[2]=1,x[1]=1,x[0]=1) is not covered by x[3]=1, so it must be added separately.

Final expression:

f = x[3] | (~x[3] & x[2] & x[1] & x[0])

This is very simple to implement as combinational logic using bitwise operations, and easy for synthesis tools to optimize.

This approach avoids the case statement, uses direct continuous assignment, and is easy to understand and verify.

This leads to better PPA by minimizing gates and critical path delay.
