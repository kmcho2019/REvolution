Key points of fusion:
- Both solutions correctly convert Gray-coded row (x3,x4) and column (x1,x2) inputs to binary indices.
- Instead of nested case statements, flatten the 2D row/col indices into one 4-bit binary address for a single case statement. This reduces logic depth and improves readability.
- Assign don't-care K-map outputs (d) to zero for deterministic output and simpler logic.
- Use well-named intermediate wires for Gray-to-binary conversion, making the design transparent and maintainable.
- The single case statement covers all 16 input combinations explicitly, which aids simulation, verification, and synthesis optimization.
- Overall, this design is clean, fully combinational with no inferred latches, and conducive to downstream tool optimization.

This fusion maintains correctness, minimizes logic depth, and improves maintainability and potentially timing and area.
