To optimize the given solution for better PPA metrics, we replace the ROM-like case statement with a minimized Boolean expression derived from the Karnaugh map, which reduces area and improves performance and power efficiency. 

First, we carefully analyze the Karnaugh map, interpreting 'd' (don't care) to allow the minimal expression.

Mapping bits:
- Row bits: x[3]x[4] → x[2]x[3] in Verilog
- Column bits: x[1]x[2] → x[0]x[1] in Verilog

The Karnaugh map (with don't cares replaced appropriately to minimize the function):

                x[1]x[2]
      x[3]x[4]   00  01  11  10
            00 | d | 0 | d | d |
            01 | 0 | d | 1 | 0 |
            11 | 1 | 1 | d | d |
            10 | 1 | 1 | 0 | d |

Convert the rows/cols in terms of x bits:

Row bits (x[3] x[4]) → x[2] x[3]:
- 00 → x[2]=0, x[3]=0
- 01 → x[2]=0, x[3]=1
- 11 → x[2]=1, x[3]=1
- 10 → x[2]=1, x[3]=0

Col bits (x[1] x[2]) → x[0] x[1]:
- 00 → x[0]=0, x[1]=0
- 01 → x[0]=0, x[1]=1
- 11 → x[0]=1, x[1]=1
- 10 → x[0]=1, x[1]=0

Analyzing the map and performing K-map minimization for f, the minimal expression can be found as:

f = (x[3] & x[2]) | (x[3] & x[1]) | (x[2] & ~x[1] & ~x[0]) | (~x[3] & x[1] & x[0])

We can further simplify:

- The pair (x[3] & x[2]) and (x[3] & x[1]) can be factored as x[3] & (x[2] | x[1])
- The term (x[2] & ~x[1] & ~x[0]) covers specific minterms
- The term (~x[3] & x[1] & x[0]) covers another specific minterm

This expression uses only AND, OR, and NOT gates with four inputs, minimizing logic depth.

This approach improves area by reducing logic gate count compared to a large case statement and decreases power by reducing toggling in unnecessary branches. It also enhances performance by decreasing combinational delay.

Finally, the output is declared as a wire and computed with a continuous assignment for better optimization by synthesis tools.

This yields a more efficient and clean design.
