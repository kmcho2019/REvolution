The key issue was the unclear mapping of the input bits to the Karnaugh map axes. The problem states the input x is 4 bits, and the Karnaugh map rows are indexed by x[3] and x[4], but x[4] does not exist. To fix this, we interpret x[3:0] as [x3, x2, x1, x0] and assign row bits as x[3] (MSB of row) and x[2] (LSB of row), column bits as x[1] (MSB of column) and x[0] (LSB of column).

Using this mapping:
- row = {x[3], x[2]}
- col = {x[1], x[0]}

Then the Karnaugh map is:

                x[1]x[0]
  x[3]x[2]   00  01  11  10
        00 | d | 0 | d | d |
        01 | 0 | d | 1 | 0 |
        11 | 1 | 1 | d | d |
        10 | 1 | 1 | 0 | d |

We list the minterms where f=1:

Row (x3,x2) | Col (x1,x0) | x3 x2 x1 x0 | binary
----------------------------------------------
  01 (0 1)  |    11 (1 1) | 0 1 1 1 = 4'b0111
  11 (1 1)  |    00 (0 0) | 1 1 0 0 = 4'b1100
  11 (1 1)  |    01 (0 1) | 1 1 0 1 = 4'b1101
  10 (1 0)  |    00 (0 0) | 1 0 0 0 = 4'b1000
  10 (1 0)  |    01 (0 1) | 1 0 0 1 = 4'b1001

We implement f=1 for these minterms.

Then, for better PPA, we derive a minimal SOP or POS expression by grouping ones on the Karnaugh map. After simplification, the expression reduces to:

f = (~x3 & x2 & x1 & x0)      // minterm 0111
  + (x3 & x2 & ~x1)           // covers 1100 and 1101 (x3=1,x2=1,x1=0)
  + (x3 & ~x2 & ~x1)          // covers 1000 and 1001 (x3=1,x2=0,x1=0)

This is minimal and avoids explicitly listing all minterms.

This logic is more efficient and directly corresponds to the Karnaugh map.
