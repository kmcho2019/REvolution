To optimize the existing design for better PPA (performance, power, area), focus on:

1. **Clock enable gating:** Update shift register and bit counter only in the RECEIVE state to reduce switching power.

2. **Simplify shift direction:** Since the data is LSB first, shift the register left by one and input the serial bit at bit 0, which is more intuitive and can reduce logic complexity.

3. **Use binary state encoding instead of one-hot:** With 4 states, 2 bits suffice, reducing flip-flop count and area, while combinational next-state logic remains straightforward.

4. **Generate `done` as a one-cycle pulse only during stop bit detection, driven by a flag rather than inside the state machine’s sequential block, minimizing toggling.

5. **Hold outputs stable outside `done` pulse to reduce toggling.

6. **Reset signals synchronously.

The resulting design reduces flip-flop count (binary encoding), reduces toggling on shift register and done signal, and simplifies shift logic. This should lead to lower power and area with similar or better timing.

The FSM states are encoded as IDLE=2'b00, RECEIVE=2'b01, CHECK_STOP=2'b10, RECOVER=2'b11.

Shift operation: shift_reg <= {in, shift_reg[7:1]} replaced by shift_reg <= {shift_reg[6:0], in} to capture LSB first at bit 0.

The done pulse is generated combinationally when a valid stop bit is detected and registered for one cycle.

This keeps all functional requirements intact, while improving PPA metrics.
