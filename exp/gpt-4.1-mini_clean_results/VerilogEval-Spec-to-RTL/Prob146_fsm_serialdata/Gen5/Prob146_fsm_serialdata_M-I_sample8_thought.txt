To improve timing (tns and wns) and reduce switching power, I will separate the combinational next-state logic from the sequential state and output register updates. This avoids mixing outputs and state transitions in one always block, easing synthesis optimization and potentially shortening the critical path.

I will implement a standard two-process FSM: one combinational block to compute next state and outputs based on current state and inputs, and one sequential block updating state and outputs on posedge clk with synchronous reset.

To reduce toggling of the done signal, it will be asserted only for one cycle when a valid stop bit is detected and held low otherwise, without unnecessary resets every cycle.

The shift register update will remain in the sequential process, but I'll use a simpler shift approach with an enable signal to minimize unnecessary changes outside the RECEIVE state.

State encoding remains binary for area efficiency; the combinational logic separation typically improves timing without area penalty.

These refinements should improve timing slack and reduce power by limiting unnecessary output toggling and making the FSM easier to optimize.
