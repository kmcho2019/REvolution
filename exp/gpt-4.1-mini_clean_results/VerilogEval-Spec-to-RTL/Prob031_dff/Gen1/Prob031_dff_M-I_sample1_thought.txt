The original design is already minimal and optimal in terms of logic, implementing a single D flip-flop triggered on the positive edge of the clock. Since the power consumption is very low and the area is minimal, the best opportunity to improve PPA (power, performance, area) is by adding clock gating to reduce dynamic power when the input 'd' does not change, thus preventing unnecessary flip-flop toggling.

The improvement strategy:
- Implement a simple clock gating cell inside the module to enable the clock only when the input 'd' is different from the stored value 'q'.
- This can reduce the power consumption by preventing unnecessary clock toggles to the flip-flop.
- This does not affect performance or area significantly, but reduces dynamic power in scenarios where 'd' is stable.

Note that the interface remains the same. Internally, a gated clock signal is generated.

This is a common low-level power optimization for flip-flops that can tolerate small additional clock gating logic.
