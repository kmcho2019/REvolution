The original solution cleanly instantiates a separate DFF module inside TopModule, providing good modularity and clarity. However, since the design is minimal—a single flip-flop without resets or other logic—this modularization adds an unnecessary instantiation layer and minor overhead. 

To optimize for power, performance, and area (PPA), I will inline the flip-flop logic directly inside TopModule. This removes the module instantiation overhead, reducing area slightly and potentially simplifying timing for synthesis tools. The use of non-blocking assignment on the positive clock edge remains, as it is the correct style for sequential logic.

This approach keeps the design minimal and straightforward, fully satisfying the problem specification with fewer lines of code, no unnecessary module boundaries, and equivalent functionality. As a single flip-flop, this is effectively the minimal implementation.

No resets or enables are added, adhering strictly to the original interface.

In summary: inline the DFF in TopModule to improve area and power marginally while maintaining clean, synthesizable, and minimal sequential logic.
