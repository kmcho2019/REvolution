Instead of using a separate module for the D flip-flop, I will integrate the flip-flop behavior directly inside the TopModule using a continuous assignment for the output driven by a registered intermediate signal. The approach will use a reg-type internal signal updated on the rising edge of clk to store the state. The output q is assigned via a continuous assignment from the registered signal. This reduces module hierarchy and isolates the sequential behavior inside the top-level module. The design remains synthesis-friendly and straightforward, but with a novel structure different from the example. No blocking assignments are used; all sequential logic is inside a single always block triggered on the positive edge of clk. This approach optimizes for minimal code size and avoids extra module instantiations while preserving clean timing semantics.
