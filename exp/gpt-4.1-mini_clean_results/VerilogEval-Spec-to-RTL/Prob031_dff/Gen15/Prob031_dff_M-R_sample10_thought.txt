The original design uses an always block with a nonblocking assignment to implement a positive-edge-triggered D flip-flop inside a submodule, which is clean and modular.

To refactor with a different structure yet the same core functionality, I will embed the flip-flop logic directly into the TopModule without a submodule. Instead of explicitly coding a sequential always block, I will use a continuous assignment with a clocked process implemented as a procedural block using an explicit register updated on the positive edge of the clock. This removes modular hierarchy but keeps clear and direct DFF behavior. 

Since DFF inherently requires sequential logic, it is not possible to replace the flip-flop with a purely combinational assign statement, but I can inline the register into TopModule as a reg output to avoid submodule instantiation. This refactor thus flattens the hierarchy and simplifies the design for synthesis while maintaining the exact functional behavior of a positive edge triggered D flip-flop.

This approach trades off modularity for simplicity and might better suit very small designs where hierarchy is unnecessary, while still maintaining synthesis friendliness.
