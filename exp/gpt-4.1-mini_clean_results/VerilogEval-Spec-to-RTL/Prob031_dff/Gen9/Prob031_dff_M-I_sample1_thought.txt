The initial design is clean and modular, but instantiating a separate DFF module adds slight overhead in terms of area and possibly power due to hierarchy and extra wiring. To optimize for power and area, I will remove the separate DFF module and embed the D flip-flop logic directly inside TopModule. This reduces hierarchy and can help synthesis tools generate more optimized flip-flops with less overhead. Also, I'll add an asynchronous reset input (optional but commonly used) to improve practical usability, but since the original spec didn't include reset, I won't add it to keep the interface minimal. Keeping only necessary signals reduces switching activity slightly. The code uses non-blocking assignment for sequential logic, preserving good style. Overall, flattening hierarchy and directly implementing the flip-flop in TopModule should yield better area and power metrics while maintaining the same performance.
