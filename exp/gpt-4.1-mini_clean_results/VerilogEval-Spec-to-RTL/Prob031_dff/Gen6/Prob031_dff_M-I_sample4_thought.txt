The existing design is already minimal and modular, with a single DFF instantiated in TopModule. Since there is only one flip-flop, the main way to reduce power is by adding clock gating or enabling the DFF only when needed. However, the interface only has clk, d, and q signals, with no enable input.

Given the constraints, the best optimization is to remove the separate DFF module and implement the DFF directly inside TopModule with a single always block. This eliminates one module instantiation, slightly reducing area and complexity. The non-blocking assignment at posedge clk ensures correct synchronous behavior.

If clock gating or enabling signals were allowed, we could add them for power reduction, but since inputs are fixed, this is not possible here.

Therefore, the improvement strategy is to inline the flip-flop logic directly into TopModule, simplifying the design and slightly improving area and potentially power due to reduced hierarchy overhead.

The design remains fully synthesizable and meets the exact specification.
