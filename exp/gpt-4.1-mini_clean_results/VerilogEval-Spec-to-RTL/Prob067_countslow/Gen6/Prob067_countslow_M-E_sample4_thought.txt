I will implement the decade counter using a two-stage approach: first, a small 3-bit counter that counts from 0 to 4, then a 2-bit high-order counter that increments every time the low-order counter wraps from 4 to 0. Together, they form a 5-bit count from 0 to 9. On reset, the entire count resets to zero. The increment only happens on the positive clock edge when slowena is high, otherwise the count holds. This modular counting splits the counting logic into smaller counters, potentially improving timing by reducing logic depth on the critical path, and enabling easier scaling or modifications. The 3-bit low counter resets to zero at 5 (counting 0 to 4), then triggers the high counter to increment from 0 to 1 (representing counts 5 to 9). When the high counter reaches 1 and the low counter reaches 4, the next increment resets both to zero. The output q is the combined count value, made by concatenating the high counter shifted left by 3 bits plus the low counter.
