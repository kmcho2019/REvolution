To fuse the strengths of both examples, I will use continuous assignment statements for purely combinational logic, which minimizes logic levels and reduces unnecessary registers, thereby improving timing and area efficiency. Additionally, I will keep the design simple and modular by using local parameters or intermediate wire signals (not registers) to clearly express the intermediate XNOR result. This provides clear code readability like in the first example but without the overhead of always blocks or reg variables. Using continuous assignments leverages synthesis tools' built-in support for bitwise XNOR and XOR operators, potentially leading to minimal and optimized gate-level implementation. This approach maintains clarity, improves PPA metrics (performance, power, area), and preserves full functionality.
