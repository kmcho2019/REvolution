Fuse the best ideas by keeping the per-bit next_state computation with explicit boundary conditions to maximize clarity and synthesis friendliness, while slightly optimizing logic fanout and potential routing by introducing local wiring to reduce the wide fanout of the large vector q. 

Specifically:
- Use a local wire register q_reg to hold q’s current state, breaking long vector fanout and easing timing.
- Compute next_state bits individually with a generate loop, using explicit left/right assignments with boundary check to keep logic simple.
- Keep the always block simple: on clk edge load q_reg from data or next_state, then drive output q directly from q_reg.
- This separates the storage and output, which can sometimes improve timing by preventing direct loading of output reg from combinational logic.
- The explicit boundary handling and per-bit logic enhances synthesis mapping to XOR gates.
- Output q is a register driven by q_reg, avoiding combinational loop concerns.
- This structure merges the clarity and simplicity of the earlier examples with a slight enhancement in signal flow and timing optimization.
