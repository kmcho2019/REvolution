To improve the design for better Power, Performance, and Area (PPA), I focus on reducing power and area without harming performance:

1. **Remove the expensive 64-bit comparator for update_en**:
   - The existing design prevents toggling when the state does not change by comparing q_out and next_state on every clock, but this comparator itself consumes significant power and area.
   - Since the Rule 90 operation will always produce changing patterns unless stable, this optimization often yields diminishing returns relative to comparator cost.
   - Removing update_en simplifies the clock enable logic and timing paths, improving frequency and reducing power consumed by comparison logic.

2. **Directly compute next_state with fixed boundary zeros, avoid extra boundary wires**:
   - Instead of passing boundary bits through wires and logic, hardcode left boundary for first bit = 0 and right boundary for last bit = 0 directly in the logic.
   - This simplifies wiring, reduces area and slight performance benefit by removing extra signal routing.

3. **Keep modular 64-bit blocks for manageable synthesis and maintainability**:
   - Use non-blocking assignments consistently.
   - Compute next_state as XOR of neighbors with boundary conditions applied inline, no clock gating.

4. **Simplify and flatten boundary connections between blocks**:
   - Use internal wiring to pass boundary bits without redundant signals.

Overall, these changes remove the update_en comparator overhead and reduce wiring complexity, which should improve timing and lower power while slightly reducing area. The design remains fully functional and correct per Rule 90.

No clock gating or enable logic: registers update on every clock cycle either loading data or next_state directly.

This tradeoff yields a cleaner and more efficient design.
