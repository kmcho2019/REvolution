To create a superior Rule 90 512-bit cellular automaton, I will fuse the best aspects of both previous designs:

- Maintain the modular approach with eight 64-bit blocks for scalability and manageable logic.
- Use clear boundary wiring with zero padding at edges for correctness and clarity.
- Implement combinational next-state logic per bit as XOR of left and right neighbors, which is simple and fast.
- Remove the complex clock enable (ce) comparator logic because:
  1. Rule 90 updates every cycle (unless loading).
  2. The ce logic adds considerable combinational delay, area, and power overhead due to wide XOR and OR reductions.
  3. The dynamic power saved by gating registers is offset by ce comparator toggling and complexity.
- Instead, update the registers on every clock cycle, loading data when load is asserted and otherwise updating to next_state.
- Use non-blocking assignments to avoid race conditions.
- The simpler clocked logic enhances timing, reduces area and power by eliminating ce comparator logic, and maintains clarity and correctness.
- The design remains fully synthesizable and easy to maintain.

This fusion retains modularity and scalability from both examples, boundary clarity from both, while reducing complexity and improving timing, area, and power by simplifying the register update logic.

Overall, it is a clean, modular, power- and timing-efficient Rule 90 512-bit automaton with load capability.
