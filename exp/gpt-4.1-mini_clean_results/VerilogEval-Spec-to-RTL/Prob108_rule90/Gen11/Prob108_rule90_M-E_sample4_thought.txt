Instead of partitioning into 64-bit blocks, I will implement the entire 512-bit Rule 90 state as a single shift-register style vector updated on each clock edge.  
To compute the next state, I will generate the next 512-bit vector in a purely combinational loop, where each bit is the XOR of its left and right neighbors with zero boundary bits at the edges.  
The entire state will be stored in one 512-bit register updated every cycle, or loaded with input data when `load` is high.  
This monolithic design eliminates inter-block boundary wiring and simplifies the dataflow, potentially improving timing by minimizing inter-block dependencies and reducing routing overhead.  
The combinational next state computation will be done with a generate loop producing a wire vector.  
Non-blocking assignment is used to update the register on the rising clock edge.  
The output `q` directly reflects the internal state register.  
This approach trades off modularity for simplicity and a clear combinational-next-state computation, possibly reducing area and power by avoiding duplicated block logic and unnecessary interconnect.
