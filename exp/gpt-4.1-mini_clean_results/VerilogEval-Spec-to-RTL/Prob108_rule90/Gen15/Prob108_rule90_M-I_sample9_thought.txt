To improve performance and reduce power while keeping area reasonable, I will remove the large clock enable comparator that checks the entire 64-bit next state vs current state. This comparator incurs a wide XOR reduction that hurts timing and adds dynamic switching from the comparator itself.

Instead, I will implement a simple clock enable that only gates the register update when `load` is asserted, and otherwise always update the state on each clock cycle. This removes the comparator, simplifying the critical path and reducing dynamic power caused by comparator toggling.

Although this slightly increases switching since we update the register every cycle (except when stalled by load=0), it greatly simplifies timing and removes the large wide logic.

Also, to keep area minimal, I will retain the 8-block partitioning of 64-bit modules with clear boundary inputs.

Overall, the design sacrifices some fine-grained clock gating to improve maximum clock frequency and reduce dynamic power caused by wide combinational comparators, balancing power, area, and performance.

This approach also maintains functional correctness and clean synchronous design.
