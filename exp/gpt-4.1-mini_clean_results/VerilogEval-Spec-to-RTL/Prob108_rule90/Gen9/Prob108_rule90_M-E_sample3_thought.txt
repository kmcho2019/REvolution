I will implement the 512-cell Rule 90 cellular automaton as a single 512-bit register updated each clock cycle. The key idea is to compute the next state for all bits simultaneously by shifting the register left and right with zero padding at the boundaries and then XOR-ing the two shifted values.

Specifically, since q[-1] and q[512] are zero, the left neighbors for q[511:1] are q[510:0], with q[-1]=0 zero-padded on the left. Similarly, the right neighbors for q[510:0] are q[511:1], with q[512]=0 zero-padded on the right.

We can do this by:
- left_shifted = {q[510:0], 1'b0}
- right_shifted = {1'b0, q[511:1]}
- next_state = left_shifted ^ right_shifted

On a positive clock edge, if load is asserted, load the input data into q; otherwise, update q with next_state.

This approach uses only one 512-bit register and simple vector operations, eliminating the need for multiple smaller blocks and boundary wiring. It should result in a compact, clean design with minimal combinational depth and straightforward timing. Also, power may be lower due to simpler logic and no inter-block connections.

The design is fully synchronous, and all operations are bit-vector manipulations, well supported by synthesis tools.
