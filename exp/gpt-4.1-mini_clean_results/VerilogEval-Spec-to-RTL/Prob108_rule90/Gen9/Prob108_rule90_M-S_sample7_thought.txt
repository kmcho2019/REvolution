I will simplify the design by removing the clock enable (ce) logic and always update the state register on each clock cycle. This reduces logic complexity by avoiding the comparison (q_out != next_state) and associated gating logic. Although this may slightly increase power due to more toggling, it significantly simplifies the design.

I will also flatten the design into a single module (TopModule) without multiple blocks. The 512-bit state vector is updated all at once using combinational logic to compute next_state from q, with zeros assumed outside the boundaries.

Each bit's next state is the XOR of its immediate neighbors: for bit i, next_state[i] = q[i-1] ^ q[i+1], with q[-1] and q[512] = 0.

On load, the state is loaded from input data; otherwise, it advances by Rule 90 on every rising clock edge.

This approach maintains correct functionality while simplifying code, making it more readable and easier to synthesize.
