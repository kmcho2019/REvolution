To improve synthesis friendliness and avoid large wide concatenations that might overwhelm some synthesis tools, we will break down the next state calculation into a generate-for loop that computes each bit's next state individually from its two neighbors. This replaces the wide vector shifts and XORs with element-wise combinational logic, which synthesis tools handle more efficiently. We will explicitly handle boundary bits (0 and 511) with zeros as neighbors and compute q[i]'s next state as q[i-1] ^ q[i+1]. On load, the full state is loaded. This approach keeps functionality intact but improves synthesizability and potentially synthesis runtime.
