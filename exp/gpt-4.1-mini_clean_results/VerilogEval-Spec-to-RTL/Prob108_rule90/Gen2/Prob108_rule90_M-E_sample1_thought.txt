Implement the Rule 90 automaton using a generate block with 512 one-bit flip-flops, each explicitly computing its next state from the XOR of its two neighbors' current states. This avoids wide shifts and XORs by localizing the combinational logic per bit, making the design more friendly to synthesis tools. Boundary conditions (neighbors out-of-range) are treated as zero. The module uses a combinational array to compute the next state bits from q's neighbors every clock cycle, and on the clock edge, either loads new data or updates to the next state. This approach distributes the logic and may produce better synthesis results at the cost of more code verbosity but clearer structure.
