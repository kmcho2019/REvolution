To improve PPA further, especially timing and power, the key insight is to reduce the fanout and logic depth on the large 512-bit vector `q`. The original design creates 512 XOR gates all driven by bits of `q` directly, resulting in wide fanout and large switching.

Improvement strategy:
1. Register the inputs `q` into two shifted versions representing left and right neighbors, which breaks the large 512-bit dependency into two narrower vectors shifted by one bit. This introduces a simple pipeline stage before XOR, reducing fanout and enabling better optimization.
2. Compute next_state as XOR of these two registered shifted vectors. This allows next_state XORs to be done on registered inputs, improving timing by shortening combinational logic.
3. This approach adds a one-cycle latency but is acceptable unless latency is strictly prohibited.
4. On load, reset the pipeline stages to the loaded data directly, avoiding extra cycles of delay.
5. The scheme reduces critical path to a single XOR after registered neighbors and reduces switching power by localizing transitions.

This pipelined approach maintains correctness, as the logic still corresponds exactly to Rule 90, and improves timing and power by reducing combinational fanout and long paths.
