Instead of explicitly inverting 'in2' and then ANDing with 'in1', I will directly instantiate a NAND gate with inputs 'in1' and 'in2', then invert the NAND output. This is logically equivalent to ANDing 'in1' with inverted 'in2' because:

out = in1 & ~in2 = ~(~in1 + in2) = ~(in1 NAND in2)'.

By using a NAND gate followed by an inverter, we emphasize structural logic and enable synthesis tools to potentially merge gates or use complex gates, optimizing implementation differently from the previous design. This approach uses a small gate-level primitive combination different from a simple AND plus inverter on input.
