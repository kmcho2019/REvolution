To implement the function out = in1 & ~in2, I can use a NAND gate with inputs in1 and in2 followed by an inverter. The NAND gate outputs ~(in1 & in2), and the inverter outputs the complement of that, which is in1 & in2. But since I want in1 & ~in2, this direct NAND-inverter approach would implement the wrong logic.

Instead, another approach is to use a NAND gate with one input inverted by exploiting De Morgan's theorem: out = in1 & ~in2 = ~(~in1 + in2).

Thus, implement out as a NOR gate fed by inverted in1 and in2:

- invert in1 -> inv_in1
- feed inv_in1 and in2 into a NOR gate: out = ~(inv_in1 + in2) = in1 & ~in2.

This uses two gates (one inverter and one NOR), which is the dual of the original solution but with different gate types.

This alternative structural design might help in technologies where NOR gates are cheaper or faster than AND gates and could provide a different optimization angle.
