To provide a distinctly different architectural approach for the same functionality, I will implement the XOR operation with three methods that share no intermediate signals to avoid data dependencies.

1. The assign statement will implement the direct XOR output combinationally.
2. The combinational always block will compute XOR from inputs independently and store the result in a reg output.
3. The clocked always block will implement a two-stage pipeline: first stage is a combinational XOR result stored in an intermediate register, and second stage registers this intermediate result at the clock's positive edge to produce the final delayed output.

This design introduces a pipeline register in the sequential path, making the delayed output depend on XOR computed in the previous clock cycle. It demonstrates a pipelined sequential design for a single-bit operation, contrasting from the original example’s immediate combinational to sequential data flow. This also avoids sharing wires or regs between the three outputs, emphasizing independent logic paths.
