The original design is already optimal for such a simple XOR function with three different implementations. However, a minor improvement can be made for the combinational always block by removing the intermediate wire and directly implementing XOR inside the always block to reduce logic duplication and minor netlist overhead. This saves one wire and one XOR gate duplication for the always block, slightly reducing area and power.

The out_assign uses a continuous assign statement as before, ensuring optimal combinational path. The sequential block remains unchanged with a flip-flop storing the XOR result. This approach yields the same functional behavior, lowers logic duplication, and keeps the code clear and synthesizable.

In summary:
- Remove the shared intermediate wire.
- Compute XOR directly in assign and always_comb blocks.
- Keep sequential always block intact using direct XOR.
- This reduces gate count slightly by avoiding the duplicated XOR wire and may reduce power and area marginally.
