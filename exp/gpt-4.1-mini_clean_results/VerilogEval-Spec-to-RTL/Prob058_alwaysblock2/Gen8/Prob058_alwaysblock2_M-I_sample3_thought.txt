To improve power and area without impacting performance, share the combinational XOR logic by computing it once in a wire and driving both combinational outputs from that wire. This avoids duplicating the XOR gate in the combinational domain, reducing logic area and switching power. The sequential logic remains registered separately as required, so its latency and behavior are unchanged. Using a shared wire for combinational logic minimizes redundant switching and logic duplication, yielding better area and power metrics.
