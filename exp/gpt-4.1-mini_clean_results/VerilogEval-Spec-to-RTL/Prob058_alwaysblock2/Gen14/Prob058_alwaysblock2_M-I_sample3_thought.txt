To optimize for power and area, I will share the XOR computation for the combinational outputs by computing it once in a wire. Then, I will drive both out_assign and out_always_comb from that wire, eliminating duplicated XOR logic. This reduces switching activity and area since the synthesis tool can implement the XOR only once for both combinational outputs.

The sequential output still requires its own registered copy with a flip-flop, so no change there.

This approach keeps performance the same but improves power and area due to logic sharing and reduced switching. The code remains clear and adheres to good coding style with proper blocking and non-blocking assignments.
