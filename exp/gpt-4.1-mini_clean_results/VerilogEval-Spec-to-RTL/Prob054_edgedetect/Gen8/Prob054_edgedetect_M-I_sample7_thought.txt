The original design is already minimal and synthesizable with clean timing. However, to slightly improve power and area, we can optimize by avoiding the explicit register for `pedge` and instead make it a combinational output derived directly from stored `prev_in` and current `in`. This reduces one register, thereby saving area and dynamic power associated with toggling the `pedge` register.

Since the requirement states "the output bit should be set the cycle after a 0 to 1 transition occurs," having `pedge` as combinational output based on the registered `prev_in` meets this condition naturally (because `prev_in` is updated on the rising edge, so `pedge` reflects the transition in the next cycle).

This change improves power and area by removing one register and associated clock toggling, and it slightly reduces logic depth by removing a register output. The latency and timing remain the same, as the critical path only involves reading `prev_in` and current `in`.

Summary of improvements:
- Remove the `pedge` register, make `pedge` a continuous assignment output
- Keep `prev_in` register only (minimal state)
- Keep logic to detect rising edges intact (~prev_in & in)
- This reduces power (less clocked register toggling) and area (one less register)
- Timing should improve slightly due to simpler output path
