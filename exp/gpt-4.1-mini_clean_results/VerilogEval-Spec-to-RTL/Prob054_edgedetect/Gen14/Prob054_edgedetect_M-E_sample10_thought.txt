Instead of using a 2-bit shift register or separate registers per bit, maintain a single 8-bit register to hold the previous cycle's input vector. On each clock, compare the current input vector with the previous one to detect bitwise transitions from 0 to 1 by checking which bits are '1' now and were '0' previously.

The pedge output is then registered (delayed by one clock) to meet the requirement of producing the positive edge detection one cycle after the transition occurs.

This approach minimizes storage (only one 8-bit register), avoids arrays, and registers the output for timing closure. The design is scalable, clean, and leverages bitwise operations for efficient edge detection.
