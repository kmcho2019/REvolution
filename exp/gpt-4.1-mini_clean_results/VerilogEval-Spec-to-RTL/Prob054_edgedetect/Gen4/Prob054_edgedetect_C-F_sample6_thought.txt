To fuse the best ideas, I will keep the clean separation of sequential and combinational logic from Example 2, which aids clarity and timing optimization. Specifically, previous input is registered in one always block, and edge detection is done combinationally outside the clock domain.

Thus, the fused design:
- Registers the previous input once,
- Detects edges combinationally by comparing current input and prev_in,
- Registers the detected edges immediately within the same clock cycle,
yielding a one-cycle delayed pedge pulse after a 0->1 transition, minimal registers (two: prev_in and pedge), minimal combinational logic, and clear structure.

This fusion preserves clarity, reduces register stages (so potentially better timing), and maintains output synchronization with minimal logic, combining the clean separation and minimal register philosophy.
