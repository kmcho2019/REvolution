Instead of storing the entire previous input vector, use individual bit flip-flops that store the inverse of the previous input bit. This inversion allows the positive edge detection to be expressed simply as an AND between the stored inverted bit and the current bit. Then register the output pedge to meet the timing requirement that it be asserted in the cycle after a 0→1 transition. This approach offers a different architectural perspective by emphasizing signal inversion storage and using AND gates for detection, which might be optimized differently by synthesis tools. The inversion storage can be thought of as a latch of the complement, allowing edge detection by a single-level AND, and then a registered output aligns the timing properly.
