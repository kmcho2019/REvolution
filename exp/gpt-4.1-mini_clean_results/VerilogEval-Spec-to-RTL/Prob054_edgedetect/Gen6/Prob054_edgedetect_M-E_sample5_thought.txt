Instead of tracking the previous input directly, the module uses a two-stage shift register (a pipeline of two registers) to delay the input signal by one and two cycles. The positive edge (0-to-1) detection is then done by comparing the delayed signals: the first delayed stage (in_d1) and the second delayed stage (in_d2).

The logic for detecting a 0-to-1 transition is: if the signal was 0 two cycles ago (in_d2 bit = 0) and is 1 one cycle ago (in_d1 bit = 1), it means the rising edge occurred in the cycle between in_d2 and in_d1 sampling. Since the output pedge must be asserted one cycle after the transition, we can directly assign pedge with this condition at the current clock.

This approach uses two registers for pipelining but gives a novel way to detect edges based on two previous samples and aligns the pedge output timing with the problem statement. It is a fresh structural approach compared to the single previous input approach.

This method inherently delays the output by two cycles total (due to two pipeline stages), but since the requirement is to assert pedge one cycle after a 0->1 transition, this pipeline technique effectively shifts input samples to realize that delay explicitly.
