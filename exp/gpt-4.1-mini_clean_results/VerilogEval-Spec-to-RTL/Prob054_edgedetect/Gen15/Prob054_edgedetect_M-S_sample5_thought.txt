Simplify the design by removing the explicit `prev_in` register and instead register the input `in` itself to serve as the previous input on the next cycle. This way, we directly use the registered `in` to detect positive edges by comparing the current input and its registered (previous) version. This reduces the number of registers and simplifies the logic to a single always block with one register for the previous input, and `pedge` assigned combinationally as the delayed pulse, improving clarity and compactness while maintaining functionality.
