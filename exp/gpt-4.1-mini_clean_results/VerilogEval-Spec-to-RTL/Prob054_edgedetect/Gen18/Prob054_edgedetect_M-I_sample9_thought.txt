To optimize the design for improved power and area without sacrificing performance, we can remove the explicit pedge register by directly driving pedge combinationally from the stored prev_in and current input inside a synchronous block. Since pedge is only needed one cycle after the 0-to-1 transition, we can avoid registering pedge and instead use a combinational assignment within the always block, reducing the number of flip-flops from 16 bits to 8 bits (only prev_in stored). This decreases area and power because pedge toggles combinationally and the output register is removed. Performance remains high as only a simple combinational AND and NOT occur. This approach leverages synchronous sampling of prev_in but no registered output for pedge, simplifying the design.
