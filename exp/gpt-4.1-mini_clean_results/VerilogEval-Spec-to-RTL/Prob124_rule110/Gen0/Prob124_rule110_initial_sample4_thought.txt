Implement a 512-bit wide register q to hold the current state of the 512 cells. On each positive clock edge, if load is high, q loads the input data. Otherwise, q updates to the next state calculated per the Rule 110 cellular automaton rules. 

To calculate the next state for each cell i, look at three bits: left (i+1), center (i), and right (i-1). For boundary cases:
- left neighbor of cell 511 is zero
- right neighbor of cell 0 is zero

Compute the next state bit by bit using the rule 110 truth table, which can be implemented combinationally as:
next_state = (left & center & right == 111) ? 0 :
             (left & center & ~right) ? 1 :
             (left & ~center & right) ? 1 :
             (left & ~center & ~right) ? 0 :
             (~left & center & right) ? 1 :
             (~left & center & ~right) ? 1 :
             (~left & ~center & right) ? 1 :
             (~left & ~center & ~right) ? 0;

An equivalent and simpler way is the known bitwise expression of Rule 110 next state:
next_state = (left & center & right == 1) ? 0 else if in other combos 1 or 0 accordingly.

However, an algebraic form of Rule 110 is:
next = (left & center & right) ? 0 :
       (left & center & ~right) ? 1 :
       (left & ~center & right) ? 1 :
       (left & ~center & ~right) ? 0 :
       (~left & center & right) ? 1 :
       (~left & center & ~right) ? 1 :
       (~left & ~center & right) ? 1 :
       0;

We can implement this as a truth table lookup or use the boolean expression from the truth table. The known boolean expression for Rule 110 next bit is:
next = (~left & center) | (center ^ right) | (left & ~center & ~right);

This can be used bitwise for each cell.

Perform the update simultaneously for all bits and update q synchronously on clock edge if load=0.

Output q directly as current state.
