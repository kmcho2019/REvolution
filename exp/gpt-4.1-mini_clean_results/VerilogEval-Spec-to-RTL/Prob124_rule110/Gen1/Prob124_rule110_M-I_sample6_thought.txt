To optimize the original design for better PPA (performance, power, and area):

1. **Reduce combinational critical path**: Instead of computing all 512 next states combinationally each cycle, pipeline the next-state logic by splitting the calculation into multiple cycles or by using a more efficient combinational expression. However, to keep the design synchronous and simple, we maintain a single-stage next state update but optimize the rule implementation.

2. **Simplify Rule 110 computation**: Replace the 8-case function with a simple boolean expression derived from the rule. Rule 110 can be expressed as:
   next = (left & ~center) | (center ^ right) | (center & ~right)
   or equivalently:
   next = (left & ~center) | (center & ~right) | (~center & right)

   This boolean logic uses fewer gates and no case statements, reducing area and delay.

3. **Handle boundary conditions by zero-padding q**: To avoid boundary muxes inside the loop, internally extend q with zero bits at indices -1 and 512. Do this by creating a 514-bit wire with zeros padded at the ends. Then, neighbors can be accessed as q_ext[i+2], q_ext[i+1], q_ext[i], where i in [0..511]. This removes if-else muxes for boundary, simplifying combinational logic.

4. **Use a generate block for combinational logic**: A generate-for or for-loop block unrolled by synthesis will efficiently build the next_state, now simpler due to no boundary mux and simple boolean function.

5. **Maintain synchronous load and update**: On load high, load input data; otherwise, update q with next_state.

Overall, these changes reduce mux complexity at boundaries, simplify combinational logic (no function case statement), reduce critical path delay, and reduce area and power.

This approach balances clarity and efficiency without changing the overall one-cycle next-state update behavior.
