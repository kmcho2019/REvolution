Use a shift-register like approach with parallel next-state computation: Store the 512 cells in a register `q`. On each clock cycle, if `load` is high, load `data` into `q`. Otherwise, compute the next state for all 512 cells in a combinational way by explicitly building three 512-bit vectors representing the left, center, and right neighbors for all cells simultaneously. 

Specifically:
- Left neighbors: q shifted left by 1 bit, with zero at LSB.
- Right neighbors: q shifted right by 1 bit, with zero at MSB.
- Center neighbors: current q.

Then for all bits, apply Rule 110 simultaneously using a bitwise formula derived from the truth table, exploiting vectorized operations without loops.

This vectorized approach avoids for-loops and local variables inside always blocks, improving synthesis friendliness. The combinational next state is calculated in one line, and the 512-bit register updates on clock edge.

The key is to encode Rule 110 logic as a boolean formula of the three neighbors (left, center, right), and apply it vector-wise over all bits.

Assuming neighbors outside bounds are zero means shifting and padding zeros is valid.

This approach is structurally simple, synthesizable, and efficient.
