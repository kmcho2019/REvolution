Implement the Rule 110 automaton using a circular buffer approach. Keep only the 512-bit state register q and compute the next state on each clock cycle from q without an explicit padded register. For boundary conditions (cells -1 and 512), assume zero. For each cell i, extract its left, center, and right neighbors from q, substituting zero if the neighbor is out of range (i.e., i=0's left neighbor and i=511's right neighbor). Compute the next state bits combinationally and then update q synchronously. This removes the need for a padded register, reduces area and power by avoiding large wide registers, and simplifies the design. The combinational logic is directly derived from the original q, incorporating boundary zeros via conditional checks.
