Optimize the original FSM implementation by encoding the states using one-hot encoding instead of binary. Although one-hot encoding may slightly increase area in large FSMs, for a four-state machine it can reduce combinational complexity and improve timing (lower TNS and WNS). The one-hot encoding eliminates the need for decoding logic in the next-state logic and output logic, thus simplifying the logic cones. This can lead to better performance (higher clock frequency) and potentially lower dynamic power due to simpler combinational paths, since fewer logic levels are involved. The asynchronous reset remains active high and resets to state A encoded as one-hot. The output logic remains Moore style and is generated directly from the current state register bits. This tradeoff generally favors timing improvement in small FSMs.
