To optimize the FSM for better timing (tns and wns) and power while maintaining area efficiency, I will convert the binary state encoding to one-hot encoding. One-hot encoding uses one flip-flop per state, simplifying next state logic to mostly simple gate logic, which can improve timing and reduce logic levels. This can also reduce glitches and switching activity in the next state combinational logic, thus lowering power. 

Additionally, I will keep the asynchronous reset as specified and use non-blocking assignments properly for sequential logic. The output logic will remain a simple combinational assignment since it only depends on the current state (state D). This change balances improved timing and power without significant area increase, suitable for a small 4-state FSM.

This strategy is commonly used in small FSMs where timing is more critical than minimal area, as the area increase (4 flip-flops vs. 2) is negligible. The clearer and simpler next state decoding logic often yields better synthesis results for speed and power.
