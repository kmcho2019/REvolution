Fuse the best elements of both solutions by maintaining the simple 2-bit FSM with clearly defined WAIT_SYNC, BYTE2, and BYTE3 states, and by using a shift-register style approach for out_bytes to consolidate register updates and minimize toggling:

- Use a single 24-bit shift register (out_bytes) that shifts left by 8 bits and loads the new byte in the least significant byte position each cycle a valid byte is accepted.
- Accept the first byte (with in[3]=1) to initialize out_bytes by loading it into the top byte and zeroing lower bytes, then shift subsequent bytes in at BYTE2 and BYTE3 states.
- Assert done only for one cycle immediately after the third byte is received (i.e., at BYTE3 state).
- This reduces multiplexing and partial byte register writes, minimizing toggling and glitch power, improving timing, and reducing area.
- Synchronous reset sets state and out_bytes to zero.
- Clear done every cycle except when asserting it for the third byte.
- Keep combinational next-state logic straightforward.
- This design leverages the concise FSM and done gating from both examples and replaces partial byte updates with an efficient shift register method to optimize power, timing, and area.

Overall, this fusion simplifies datapath logic, lowers register switching, and enhances maintainability without losing functionality.
