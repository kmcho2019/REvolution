To optimize the original FSM design for better PPA while preserving functionality:

- Use one-hot encoding for the FSM states. Although it uses more flip-flops, it often reduces combinational logic complexity, improving max frequency (timing) and sometimes power due to simpler decoding.
- Add clock-enable signals to the byte registers and out_bytes so they only update when needed, reducing unnecessary toggling and saving power.
- Generate 'done' as a registered pulse asserted only for one cycle, controlled by state and counters, minimizing redundant assignments.
- Combine the byte registers into a single 24-bit shift register that loads bytes in sequence, simplifying datapath and reducing registers.
- Ensure all sequential logic is in one always @(posedge clk) block for clarity and better synthesis.
- Keep synchronous active-high reset.
- Preserve output valid cycle on done assertion and correct byte ordering.

These changes should improve timing by simplifying next-state logic, reduce power by gating register updates, and maintain or reduce area slightly due to simpler registers and clearer logic.
