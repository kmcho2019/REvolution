The issue arises from the synchronous internal reset, which may cause the flip-flops to initialize incorrectly relative to simulation or synthesis expectations. To guarantee that the flip-flops start at zero before any clock edge and avoid dependence on initial values, I will implement an asynchronous reset signal internally generated at power-up.

Since the problem disallows any reset input, the best approach is to define an asynchronous reset with a reg initialized to 1, then de-asserted after the first clock edge. This ensures that at simulation start (time zero), the flip-flops are reset asynchronously, matching typical FPGA reset behavior.

The asynchronous reset signal is asserted at initialization (value 1), and is de-asserted synchronously on the first positive clock edge by the same always block. The flip-flops will be reset asynchronously to zero at time zero, and then normal operation resumes after reset is cleared on the first clock.

This approach respects the interface constraints, ensures zero initial state for all flip-flops before the FSM begins, and fully implements the required logic.
