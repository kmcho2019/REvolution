The primary issue is the non-standard internal reset scheme which may cause incorrect initial states or timing mismatches in simulation and synthesis. To fully comply with the requirement "flip-flops are initially reset to zero before the machine begins" without adding external reset ports, I will use Verilog's synthesis-friendly initial block to explicitly initialize the flip-flops to zero. This method is widely supported by modern synthesis tools and simulators to set initial register values.

This approach cleanly and reliably initializes the three flip-flops to zero before simulation starts or hardware powers up, ensuring correct initial states without extra inputs or complicated reset logic.

The rest of the logic remains the same:
- Each flip-flop output feeds back as a second input to its respective gate with the correct polarity.
- On each positive edge of the clock, flip-flops update according to their respective gate outputs.
- Output z is the NOR of all three flip-flop outputs.

This solution removes the non-standard internal reset mechanism, guaranteeing deterministic initialization and avoiding timing mismatches.

The design remains compact using a 3-bit register to store all flip-flop states.

This final design respects the original interface, maintains clean and readable code, and fulfills the functional requirements robustly.
