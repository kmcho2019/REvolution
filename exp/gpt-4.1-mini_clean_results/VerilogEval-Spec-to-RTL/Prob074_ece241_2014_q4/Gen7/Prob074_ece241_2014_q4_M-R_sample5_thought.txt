The key behavioral requirements are to implement three D flip-flops driven by the outputs of XOR, AND, and OR gates fed by input x and respective flip-flop outputs (or their complements), and then combine their outputs through a 3-input NOR to produce z.

To avoid the asynchronous reset and initial block complexity, I will implement the flip-flops using synchronous logic without any explicit reset input. According to the problem statement, we can assume that flip-flops are initialized to zero before the FSM begins (e.g., through FPGA power-up conditions), so no reset port or reset logic is required in the code.

I will use a single always block triggered on the positive clock edge to update three registers q_xor, q_and, q_or without any reset. Their D inputs are continuously assigned by combinational logic using assign statements. This approach is synthesizable and simpler.

The D input signals (d_xor, d_and, d_or) will be wires computed by the respective logic gates as described:
- d_xor = x ^ q_xor
- d_and = x & (~q_and)
- d_or  = x | (~q_or)

Flip-flops will update q_xor, q_and, q_or with d_xor, d_and, d_or at every clock's rising edge.

Finally, the output z is the NOR of q_xor, q_and, and q_or.

This structure is straightforward, has no asynchronous resets, and matches the problem statement's assumption that flip-flops start at zero.

This refactor removes the asynchronous reset and initial block but maintains the correct functional behavior.
