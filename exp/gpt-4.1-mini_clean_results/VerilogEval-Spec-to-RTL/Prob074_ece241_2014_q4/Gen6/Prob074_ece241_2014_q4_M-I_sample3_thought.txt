The initial solution attempts to implement an internal reset controller, which complicates the design and causes the FSM to start with indeterminate states for several clock cycles. The problem states "Assume that the D flip-flops are initially reset to zero before the machine begins," which can be cleanly handled by initializing the registers to zero in an initial block for simulation and synthesis tools that support it. This eliminates the need for any reset signal or counter, simplifying the design and avoiding startup glitches.

Therefore, the improvement strategy is:
- Remove the reset controller and internal reset signals entirely.
- Use an initial block to initialize q_xor, q_and, and q_or to zero at time zero.
- Keep the sequential always block triggered on posedge clk with no reset.
- Calculate the D inputs combinationally from current flip-flop outputs and x, as per the specification.
- Update flip-flops on every clock with these inputs.
- Compute z as the NOR of all three flip-flop outputs.

This approach is simpler, fully meets the problem requirements, and ensures that the FSM starts from a known zero state, avoiding any mismatch in initial outputs. It is also synthesizable in most modern FPGA and ASIC flows.

Additionally, separate combinational signals for the D inputs enhance readability.
