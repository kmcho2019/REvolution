To meet the requirements that shift_ena is asserted for exactly 4 clock cycles *starting from the cycle where reset is active* (including that reset cycle) and then deasserted forever until the next reset, I will implement a small FSM-like approach with a dedicated state register counting the remaining enable cycles.

- When synchronous reset is asserted, load a 3-bit counter with 4, indicating 4 cycles of enable including the current.
- While the counter is not zero, assert shift_ena and decrement the counter each clock cycle.
- When counter reaches zero, deassert shift_ena permanently until the next reset.
- This ensures shift_ena=1 is active immediately during reset clock, lasts for 4 cycles total, then 0 forever.
- By explicitly counting down from 4 including reset cycle, no delays or off-by-one errors occur.
- I use combinational assign for shift_ena and a simple synchronous always block for counter update, changing the structure but keeping the functionality.

This design is simple, meets synchronous reset behavior, and provides deterministic 4-cycle enable periods aligned with reset assertion.
