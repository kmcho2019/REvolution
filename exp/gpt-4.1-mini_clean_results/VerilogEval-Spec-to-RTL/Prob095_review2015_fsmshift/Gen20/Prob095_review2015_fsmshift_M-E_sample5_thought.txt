To meet the requirement of asserting `shift_ena` for exactly 4 clock cycles whenever reset is asserted synchronously, and then remain deasserted until the next reset, I propose a different approach using a shift register as a cycle counter.

Design idea:
- Use a 4-bit shift register (4 stages) to represent the enable period.
- On reset, load the shift register with all ones: 4'b1111, which corresponds to enabling `shift_ena` for 4 cycles.
- On each subsequent clock cycle, shift right by one, effectively shifting out one '1' bit per cycle.
- When the shift register becomes zero (all zeros), `shift_ena` is deasserted.
- Since reset is synchronous, the load of all ones happens exactly on the reset clock edge.
- This ensures `shift_ena` is asserted starting from the reset cycle and remains asserted for 4 cycles total.

Advantages:
- Simple shift register logic is intuitive and straightforward.
- It guarantees exact 4 cycles assertion with no off-by-one errors.
- No need for explicit counter arithmetic.
- The state is clear and easily extendable if needed.
- Easily extendable later to include other conditions that load the shift register (e.g., on pattern detection), but as per current spec, reset behavior is implemented.

No initial value needed since the shift register is synchronously loaded on reset.

This approach fulfills the requirement with an alternative architectural style from counters.
