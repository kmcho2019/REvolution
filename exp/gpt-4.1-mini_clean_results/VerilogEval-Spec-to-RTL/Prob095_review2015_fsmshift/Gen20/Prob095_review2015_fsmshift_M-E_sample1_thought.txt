Implement a shift enable controller using a simple up-counter that runs only immediately after reset, without an explicit FSM. On synchronous reset, start counting from 0 to 3 on each clock cycle, asserting shift_ena when the count is less than 4. After counting 4 cycles, stop incrementing and keep shift_ena deasserted until the next reset. This approach treats shift_ena as directly derived from a timer counter gated by reset, making the control logic simpler and eliminating separate FSM states.

The counter resets to zero on synchronous reset and increments on each clock cycle while less than 4. shift_ena is driven high when the counter is within the 4-cycle window. After 4 cycles, the counter saturates and shift_ena remains low indefinitely until the next reset.

This solution guarantees exactly 4 cycles of shift_ena assertion after reset, clean synchronous reset behavior, and minimal logic without the need for explicit state encodings.
