I will refactor the design to use a simple synchronous counter-based approach without explicit FSM states. On synchronous reset, the counter is loaded to 4, then decremented each clock cycle until zero. The shift_ena output is asserted whenever the counter is non-zero. This makes the assertion length explicit, and output is registered with the counter to ensure proper timing.

This approach simplifies the logic and avoids combinational output derived from FSM states, preventing glitches or timing mismatches. The synchronous reset directly initializes the counter to 4 cycles of enable, then the output shift_ena is '1' while the counter is not zero, achieving exactly 4 clock cycles of enable after reset.

This implementation keeps synchronous reset and positive edge triggered sequential logic. The counter width is 3 bits (enough to hold 4) and shift_ena is assigned based on the counter state.

No FSM states or next state combinational logic is used here, making the design concise and robust for the required behavior.
