Refactor the design by using a simple 2-state FSM to control shift_ena instead of directly counting down with a counter register. The FSM has two states: IDLE and SHIFT_ENABLE. On synchronous reset, transition to SHIFT_ENABLE state and assert shift_ena. While in SHIFT_ENABLE, count 4 clock cycles using a separate cycle counter; once 4 cycles have elapsed, transition back to IDLE and deassert shift_ena. This separates control flow (FSM state) from counting cycles and makes the design more explicit.

This approach uses an explicit FSM state register and a small cycle counter. Both registers are updated synchronously on the clock edge. shift_ena is asserted when the FSM is in SHIFT_ENABLE state. This differs structurally from the original by separating state and counting, improving clarity and modularity while meeting the same timing requirements.

Also, initialize cycle counter to zero on reset and start counting up to 4 when in SHIFT_ENABLE. After 4 cycles, return to IDLE.

This eliminates off-by-one errors, since the count starts at zero immediately after reset and increments to 4 while shift_ena is high.

Finally, using enumerated states improves readability.
