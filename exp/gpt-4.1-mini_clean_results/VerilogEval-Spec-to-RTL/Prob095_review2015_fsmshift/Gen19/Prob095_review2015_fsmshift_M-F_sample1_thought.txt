The previous design initialized the counter to 4 on synchronous reset and asserted `shift_ena` while the counter was non-zero. This made `shift_ena` high for 4 cycles starting immediately after reset.

However, feedback indicates a timing mismatch, possibly because the counter initialized to 4 causes `shift_ena` to be asserted for cycles counting from 4 down to 1 inclusive, which is correct logically but can cause confusion if reset assertion timing is ambiguous.

To fix this, I will:

- Initialize the counter to 3 on reset instead of 4. This means that on the cycle reset is detected, counter = 3, and `shift_ena` is asserted while counter is not zero.

- The counter will decrement each clock cycle until zero.

- The `shift_ena` signal will be high whenever counter is greater than zero.

This approach ensures `shift_ena` is asserted for exactly 3 cycles *after* the reset cycle, so total asserted cycles including the reset cycle is 4.

Also, to avoid any unknown initial states, explicitly initialize the counter to zero at declaration.

This small off-by-one fix aligns the assertion timing with the expected waveform:

- Cycle when reset is high: counter loaded to 3, `shift_ena` high.

- Next 3 cycles: counter counts down 2,1,0 with `shift_ena` high for counter=3,2,1, and then low at zero.

This satisfies the requirement for asserting `shift_ena` exactly 4 cycles starting synchronously on reset.

All logic remains synchronous, uses non-blocking assignments, and is simple.

No dangling inputs or outputs are introduced.

This addresses the timing issue and mismatch in the original implementation.
