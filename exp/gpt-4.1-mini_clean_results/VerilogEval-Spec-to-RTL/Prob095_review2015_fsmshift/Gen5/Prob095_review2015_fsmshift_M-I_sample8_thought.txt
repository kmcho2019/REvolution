The problem states that shift_ena should be asserted for exactly 4 clock cycles immediately after a synchronous active-high reset, and then stay low indefinitely until the next reset. The original solution incremented a counter while reset was high, enabling shift_ena for counts less than 4. However, if reset remains high beyond 4 cycles, the counter keeps incrementing unchecked, and shift_ena goes low after 4 cycles of reset, which is correct per spec but may cause unnecessary large counters and unclear code semantics. Moreover, the intent is to enable shift_ena for 4 cycles immediately *after* reset assertion, not necessarily during reset if it is held high for long.

To handle this cleanly and precisely:

1. Use a separate enable counter that triggers when reset transitions from low to high (detect reset rising edge synchronously).
2. On reset assertion (detected by previous reset sample), start the 4-cycle shift_ena enable cycle.
3. shift_ena stays high for exactly 4 cycles, counted by a small 3-bit counter.
4. After 4 cycles, shift_ena is cleared and remains low until the next reset.
5. The counter saturates to prevent overflow.
6. This approach works correctly regardless of the duration reset is held high, asserting shift_ena exactly 4 cycles after reset assertion (including the cycle of reset assertion).
7. The counter and previous reset state are updated synchronously at clk rising edge.

This solution gives clean, robust control over shift_ena timing aligned to reset assertion and precisely 4 cycles enable.

This approach ensures:
- No counter overflow.
- shift_ena asserted for exactly 4 cycles upon reset assertion.
- shift_ena low forever after that until next reset.
- Handles reset held high for longer without extending shift_ena beyond 4 cycles.

Implementation includes:
- A register to store the previous reset state for edge detection.
- A counter to count the shift_ena cycles.
- Logic to assert shift_ena and increment the counter on the 4 cycles following reset rising edge.
