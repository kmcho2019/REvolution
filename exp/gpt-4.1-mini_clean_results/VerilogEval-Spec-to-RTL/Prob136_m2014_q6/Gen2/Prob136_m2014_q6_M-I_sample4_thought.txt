To optimize the FSM for better Power, Performance, and Area (PPA), I will implement one-hot encoding instead of binary state encoding. One-hot encoding simplifies next-state decoding logic to mostly AND/OR gates, which often results in faster combinational paths and potentially reduced power due to fewer toggling bits in state register. 

Though one-hot encoding uses more flip-flops, the FSM size (6 states) is small, so area increase is minimal and timing usually improves. Additionally, separating the output logic into a separate combinational block clarifies design and may help synthesis tools optimize output timing.

Also, I will use non-blocking assignments in sequential blocks and blocking assignments in combinational blocks for clarity and best practices. The asynchronous reset will remain synchronous as originally specified.

This change trades slightly higher area for better timing and possibly lower dynamic power due to simpler next-state logic and fewer glitches.
