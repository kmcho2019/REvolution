To optimize the FSM for better performance and power without significantly increasing area, I will convert the state encoding from binary to one-hot. One-hot encoding reduces the complexity of next-state logic since only one flip-flop is set at a time, minimizing combinational logic levels and improving timing. This will especially help the critical path as the case statements become simple AND-OR combinations.

I will also register the output z to avoid glitches and reduce dynamic power. Registering the output aligns output changes with clock edges, improving timing predictability.

Although one-hot encoding increases flip-flop count (from 3 to 6), the simpler next-state logic and registered output can improve overall PPA by improving timing (reducing worst negative slack and total negative slack) and lowering power due to reduced glitching.

The FSM will use synchronous reset, and next-state logic will be in combinational always block with simple case statements. Output z will be a registered signal asserted only in states E and F.

This approach balances a modest area increase with improved timing and power characteristics.
