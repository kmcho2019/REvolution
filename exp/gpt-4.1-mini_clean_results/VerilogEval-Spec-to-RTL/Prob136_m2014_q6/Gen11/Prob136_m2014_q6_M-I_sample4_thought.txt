To improve the existing FSM design for better performance, power, and area, I will:

1. Use a combinational `always @*` block with a `case` statement for next-state logic instead of nested ternary operators. This is often synthesized more efficiently and is clearer.
2. Use a `case` statement for output logic for clarity and potential area optimization.
3. Consider using a one-hot encoding for states because it can simplify next-state decoding, reduce logic depth, and possibly lower power by reducing toggling.
4. Keep the synchronous reset and state register with non-blocking assignment for reliable sequential logic.

With six states, one-hot encoding requires 6 flip-flops but can simplify combinational logic, potentially improving timing and power. I will implement one-hot encoded states and update transitions accordingly.

The output `z` is '1' in states E and F, so it becomes a simple OR of these bits.

This approach should reduce combinational path delay (improving timing), simplify logic (potentially reducing area), and reduce switching activity (power).
