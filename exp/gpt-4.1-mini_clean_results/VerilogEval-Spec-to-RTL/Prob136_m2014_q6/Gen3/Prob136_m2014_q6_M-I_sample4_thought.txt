To improve PPA, I will switch from one-hot encoding to binary state encoding. This reduces flip-flop count from 6 to 3, decreasing area and power significantly. Although combinational next-state logic becomes slightly more complex, it remains manageable with 6 states.

I will assign binary codes to states A-F (3 bits) and implement the next-state and output logic using a combinational case statement. This change will reduce area (fewer flip-flops), power (fewer toggles), and may improve timing by reducing fan-out and capacitance.

Output z remains high only in states E or F, easily detected from encoded states. The code remains synchronous, reset sets state to A.

This binary-encoded FSM is a common, more PPA-efficient approach and should improve the given metrics.
