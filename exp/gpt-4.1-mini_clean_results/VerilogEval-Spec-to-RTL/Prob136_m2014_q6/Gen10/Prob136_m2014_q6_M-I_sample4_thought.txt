To optimize the given FSM for better timing and potentially reduced area, I will adopt one-hot encoding for the states instead of binary encoding. One-hot encoding simplifies next-state logic since only one flip-flop is active per state, enabling faster transitions due to simpler combinational logic. Although it increases flip-flop count, the critical path for next-state logic often reduces, improving slack and timing. 

I will keep separate combinational logic for next-state computation using case statements, now with one-hot encoded states. The output logic will be simplified by OR'ing the relevant state flip-flops directly.

This approach should improve worst negative slack and total negative slack by reducing combinational depth in next state logic and simplifying output logic, as suggested by PPA metrics. Power and area trade-offs are justified for timing improvements.
