Instead of using a single vectorized register, implement the TopModule with eight independent single-bit D flip-flop modules instantiated individually. Each DFF module will have its own always block with synchronous active-high reset triggered by the positive edge of the clock. This approach increases modularity at the bit level, allowing for potential individual bit-level modifications or test insertion in future expansions.

This design offers:
- Bit-wise modularity, which is good for per-bit debugging or scan insertion.
- Clear separation of each DFF logic and reset handling.
- Simplified logic inside each DFF which may help synthesis tools optimize individual flip-flops separately.
- The tradeoff is a slight increase in area and instance overhead compared to the vectorized approach, but the modularity is beneficial for complex designs that need fine-grained control.

This bottom-up modular design suits scenarios where each bit's behavior might diverge or require special treatment later while maintaining synchronous reset and edge-triggered behavior consistent with requirements.
