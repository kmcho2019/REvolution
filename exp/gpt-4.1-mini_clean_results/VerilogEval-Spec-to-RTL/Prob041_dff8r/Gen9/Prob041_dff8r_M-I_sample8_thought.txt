The original design is functionally correct and modular, but instantiating eight individual D flip-flops with separate synchronous reset logic introduces some area and power overhead due to duplicated reset handling circuitry. To optimize for better area and power (while maintaining timing), I will consolidate the register into a single always block handling the entire 8-bit vector. This eliminates redundant reset logic inside each flip-flop instance, reduces flip-flop overhead, and is a typical industry practice for small registers. The synchronous reset and positive edge clock behavior are preserved. This approach improves area by sharing reset logic and likely reduces dynamic power by minimizing reset signal fanout and logic. Timing remains optimal since there's no additional combinational logic in the data path.
