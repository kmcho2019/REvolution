To improve PPA (performance, power, area) over the original balanced 4-to-1 mux tree, reduce the number of logic levels by increasing the mux radix at higher levels. For instance, use a mix of 8-to-1 and 4-to-1 muxes to decrease the overall mux tree depth from 4 levels to 3. This reduces combinational delay (improves performance) and cuts down intermediate wires and number of modules (improves area). Specifically:
- Level 0: Use 8-to-1 muxes selecting among 8-bit slices with sel[2:0].
- Level 1: Use 8-to-1 muxes selecting among 8 outputs from level 0 with sel[5:3].
- Level 2: Use a single 8-to-1 mux selecting final output with sel[7:6] plus one extra bit (sel[8]) not present, so only 8 entries.
Since sel is 8 bits, max index 255, 8 bits for 8-to-1 mux addressing means 3 bits per level.

We actually only need to cover 256 inputs: 8 bits sel for 256 inputs, split into 3 levels of 8-to-1 muxes:
- 8-to-1 mux (3 bits of sel) picks one bit out of 8 bits.
- After level 0, 256/8=32 outputs.
- Level 1: 8-to-1 mux with sel[5:3] picks one out of 8 from 32 outputs → produces 4 outputs.
- Level 2: final 4-to-1 mux with sel[7:6] selects final output from 4 outputs.
This leads to 3 levels total with fewer intermediate signals, fewer muxes, and lower depth than original 4-level 4-to-1 mux tree.

This hybrid approach reduces gate count and delay while keeping code clean and modular. Also keep Mux8to1 and Mux4to1 modules simple and index-based for synthesis friendliness.

This reduces total muxes from 85 to 32+8+1=41 muxes, cutting area roughly by half and improving timing due to fewer levels.
