To improve PPA while maintaining correctness and modularity, I will reduce the mux tree depth from 2 levels of 16-to-1 muxes to a balanced 3-level tree of 4-to-1 muxes only. This results in lower fan-in and potentially better delay than the previous approach that used 16-to-1 muxes implemented as 4-to-1 muxes in two levels internally.

Specifically:

- Implement the top 256-to-1 mux as a three-level tree of 4-to-1 muxes.
- Level 0: 64 instances of Mux4to1 select 1 bit from each 4-bit chunk of input (sel[1:0] controls selection).
- Level 1: 16 instances of Mux4to1 select among Level 0 outputs, controlled by sel[3:2].
- Level 2: 4 instances of Mux4to1 select among Level 1 outputs, controlled by sel[5:4].
- Level 3: 1 instance of Mux4to1 selects among Level 2 outputs, controlled by sel[7:6].
  
This provides a fully balanced 4-level tree of 4-to-1 muxes, each with low fan-in and shorter propagation delay per level, typically leading to better timing and lower power than any large fan-in mux.

The hierarchy is clear and regular, enabling efficient synthesis and mapping to FPGA or standard cell muxes. Also, the mux depth is fixed at 4 stages, each with only 4-to-1 muxes, preventing large fan-in mux overheads.

A FLAT parameter is maintained for direct indexing for environments where flat logic is preferred.

This improved tree reduces the maximum fan-in from 16 to 4 per mux and balances delay evenly across levels, likely improving the effective clock period and reducing power and area by leveraging smaller muxes without extra intermediate wiring overhead from 16-to-1 mux modules.

The existing Mux4to1 module is reused and the top-level tree is instantiated hierarchically using generate loops for clarity and scalability.

Overall, this balances the trade-offs between logic depth, fan-in, and modularity for optimized PPA.
