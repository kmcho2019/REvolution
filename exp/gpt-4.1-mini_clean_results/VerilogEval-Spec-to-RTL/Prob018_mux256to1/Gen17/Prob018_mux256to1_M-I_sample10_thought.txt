The original solution uses a balanced 4-level tree of 4-to-1 muxes, which is modular and clear but has a mux depth of 4 and 85 total small muxes. To improve PPA (performance, power, area), we can reduce the mux tree depth by increasing the mux radix.

Specifically, replacing the 4-to-1 muxes with 16-to-1 muxes arranged in a 2-level tree reduces the logic depth from 4 mux stages to 2 mux stages, thus potentially improving timing. Using 16-to-1 muxes also reduces the total number of muxes instantiated, improving area and power.

We can implement 16-to-1 muxes by indexing 16-bit inputs with 4-bit selects (sel bits). We can split the 8-bit sel into two 4-bit parts:

- Level 0: 16 instances of 16-to-1 muxes, each takes 16 bits from 'in' selected by sel[3:0].
- Level 1: 1 instance of 16-to-1 mux, selects among 16 outputs of level0 using sel[7:4].

This halves the mux depth from 4 to 2, reduces number of muxes from 85 to 17, which should improve performance and area, and likely reduce power due to fewer active switching elements.

The Mux16to1 module is simple and uses indexing to select one bit. The TopModule instantiates 16 Mux16to1 modules for level0, and a final Mux16to1 at level1.

This approach maintains clarity and modularity, while significantly improving PPA metrics compared to the original hierarchical 4-to-1 mux tree.
