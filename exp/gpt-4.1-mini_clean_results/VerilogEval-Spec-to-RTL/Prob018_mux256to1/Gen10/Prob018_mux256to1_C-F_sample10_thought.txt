Fuse the clarity and modularity of explicit small mux modules (like Mux8to1 and Mux4to1) from Example 2 with the hierarchical 16-to-1 mux concept from Example 1 to balance fan-in, mux levels, and synthesis friendliness. 

Fusion Strategy:
- Use three levels of muxing, but with uniform 16-to-1 mux modules to keep mux fan-in moderate, minimizing large fan-in delay and power.
- Level 0: 16 instances of Mux16to1 selecting bits within 16-bit chunks (using sel[3:0])
- Level 1: 16 instances of Mux16to1 selecting among 16 outputs of level 0 (using sel[7:4])
- Level 2: 1 Mux16to1 selecting among 16 outputs of level 1 (using sel[11:8]) — here sel[11:8] would be unused because sel is only 8 bits. So we must fix the levels accordingly.

Since sel is only 8 bits, indexing from 0 to 255, and 8 bits corresponds exactly to 256 entries, and 256 = 16 * 16, two levels of 16-to-1 muxes suffice (as in Example 1). To improve timing and power, we can decompose the 16-to-1 mux further into smaller 4-to-1 mux trees internally for each Mux16to1 module. This reduces large fan-in gates, improving delay and power.

Therefore, the fusion approach is:

- Define a parameterized Mux4to1 module.
- Build a Mux16to1 module using a 4-to-1 mux tree internally (two layers of 4-to-1 muxes).
- At the TopModule, implement two levels of Mux16to1:
  - Level 1: 16 Mux16to1 muxes selecting from in[0:255] in chunks of 16 bits, using sel[3:0]
  - Level 2: 1 Mux16to1 mux selecting from the 16 outputs of level 1, using sel[7:4]

This keeps the hierarchical structure clean, reduces the maximum mux fan-in from 16 to 4 at each gate level, improves synthesis quality, and leverages modular reusable building blocks.

Additional notes:
- Using parameterized modules improves code reuse.
- Explicit tree decomposition inside Mux16to1 improves PPA compared to direct large fan-in muxing.
- The output is directly the final selected bit as requested.

This design balances depth (2 levels of 16-to-1 muxes) with low fan-in (internal 4-to-1 trees), improving delay and power while maintaining area efficiency and code clarity.
