The original solution uses a four-level tree of 4-to-1 multiplexers, which is functionally correct but replicates many small muxes, increasing area and power due to routing overhead and switching on intermediate signals.

To optimize for better power and area without sacrificing performance too much, I propose a flattened approach that directly selects the output bit with a single indexed assignment using the 8-bit `sel` as an index into the 256-bit `in` vector. This eliminates all intermediate mux wires and stages, thereby reducing area and switching activity.

Modern synthesis tools efficiently infer large multiplexers from indexed bit selections, often resulting in optimal implementation. This approach reduces mux count to one, dramatically cutting routing and gate count. Since the effective clock period is not critical here, the potentially slightly higher logic depth (large 256-to-1 mux) is acceptable and typically well handled by synthesis optimizations.

Thus, the design improves PPA by lowering power (less switching on intermediate mux outputs) and area (no replicated mux instances and wiring). It's also simpler and more readable.

If needed, the code can easily be extended to hierarchical versions again if timing constraints tighten.

In summary, I replace the hierarchical 4-to-1 mux tree with a flat indexed multiplexing via vector indexing for better power and area, leveraging synthesis tool optimizations.
