To optimize for better PPA while keeping correctness and scalability, we can replace the explicit mux tree hierarchy with a direct indexed selection. Most synthesis tools efficiently infer optimized multiplexers from such indexed vector accesses, often mapping directly to dedicated mux primitives or optimized LUT structures. This approach drastically reduces area and power and minimizes delay, as it flattens the selection logic into a single operation instead of multiple sequential 2-to-1 muxes.

This design uses:
- A single always_comb or continuous assignment to select the bit from the input vector `in` indexed by `sel`.
- This style yields the simplest RTL description, which synthesis tools can optimize much better than explicit hierarchical mux trees.

Overall, this will yield:
- Significant area reduction by eliminating many mux instances.
- Lower power due to reduced switching and logic complexity.
- Improved timing because the selection is a direct vector index with minimal logic depth.
- Simpler, cleaner, and easier-to-maintain code.

Given the problem's clear spec and that synthesis tools handle indexing efficiently, this is the best trade-off for PPA optimization.
