Implement the 256-to-1 multiplexer using a hierarchical two-level approach combining a tree of 8-to-1 multiplexers at the first level and a final 32-to-1 multiplexer at the second level. 

Key ideas:
- First, slice the 256 inputs into 32 groups of 8 bits.
- For each group of 8 bits, use an 8-to-1 multiplexer controlled by sel[2:0].
- This produces 32 intermediate outputs.
- Then, select among these 32 outputs using a single 32-to-1 multiplexer controlled by sel[7:3].

To efficiently build the 8-to-1 mux, instantiate two 4-to-1 muxes followed by a 2-to-1 mux (this decomposes the 8-to-1 mux into smaller fan-in blocks, improving timing and power).
For the 32-to-1 mux, instantiate eight 4-to-1 muxes to reduce 32 inputs to 8 outputs, then two 4-to-1 muxes to reduce 8 to 2 outputs, and finally a 2-to-1 mux to select the final output. This tree structure keeps the mux fan-in small at every stage.

This approach:
- Uses smaller fan-in muxes hierarchically for better synthesis and timing.
- Has a depth of 3 mux stages in the final mux (8-to-1 mux depth is 2 stages, 32-to-1 mux depth is 4 stages, total 6 mux stages), but with small fan-in each stage.
- Reduces fan-in complexity compared to a direct large 256-to-1 mux.

This novel tree structure differs from the previous design by using 8-to-1 muxes at the bottom and a multi-stage 32-to-1 mux at the top, decomposed into 4-to-1 and 2-to-1 muxes for better modularity and synthesis friendliness.
