To optimize for better PPA, we focus on reducing dynamic power and minimizing combinational logic delays. Since the original design is already minimal, further area reduction is limited. To reduce toggling and glitching, I will remove the separate combinational output logic block and instead register the output directly from the state flip-flop, making the output a direct mapping of the registered state. This avoids redundant combinational logic and ensures the output only changes on clock edges, reducing glitches and dynamic power. Also, combine next state and output assignment in one synchronous block to minimize logic and possible delay. This will slightly increase latency by one cycle for the output but fits Moore FSM definition and improves power and timing. The asynchronous reset remains unchanged for fast reset.

Overall, this approach simplifies the design by having output directly driven by state register, cutting out combinational logic, lowering power, and improving timing margins.
