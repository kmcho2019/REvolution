To implement the same logic function q = (a OR b) AND (c OR d), I will use a structurally different approach by explicitly declaring intermediate wires for the OR operations and then combining them with an AND gate. This modular approach can improve code readability and facilitate potential reuse of the intermediate signals in an extended design. It also clearly reflects the two-stage logic structure of the circuit: first compute the ORs, then the final AND.

By separating the logic into intermediate signals:

- We explicitly capture the partial results (or_ab and or_cd).
- This aids debugging and testability by allowing probing of these intermediate signals.
- The overall logic remains purely combinational.
- The final assign statement remains simple and clear.

This structural style might slightly increase area and power due to the extra signals but provides clarity and extensibility advantages, which can be valuable in more complex designs.
