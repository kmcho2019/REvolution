The original logic q = (a | b) & (c | d) is minimal and functionally correct. However, we can try rewriting the logic to use only NAND gates, which are often more power- and area-efficient in CMOS technology due to transistor sharing and fewer transistor stacks.

Using De Morgan's laws:
q = (a | b) & (c | d) = !(! (a | b) | ! (c | d))
                      = !((!a & !b) | (!c & !d))
                      = NAND(NAND(a,a), NAND(b,b)) & NAND(NAND(c,c), NAND(d,d))

But this expression is complex. Instead, we can express q as:
q = (a | b) & (c | d)
  = !(!(a | b) | !(c | d))
  = NAND(NAND(a,b), NAND(c,d))

So implementing q = NAND(NAND(a,b), NAND(c,d)) uses only NAND gates.

This approach can reduce transistor count and thus area and power, possibly improving PPA while maintaining the exact same logic and timing.

Hence, we implement q as q = NAND(NAND(a,b), NAND(c,d)).
