The original implementation q = (a | b) & (c | d) is logically minimal and optimal in gate count and depth. To improve power slightly, we can apply De Morgan's law and implement it as q = ~((~a & ~b) | (~c & ~d)), which avoids the direct OR gates and may reduce simultaneous switching if inputs have specific toggling patterns. This form is functionally identical but can offer minor dynamic power improvements by changing switching activity patterns. Also, replacing the OR-AND with NOR-AND inverted form can benefit certain standard cell libraries. Area and timing remain essentially the same.
