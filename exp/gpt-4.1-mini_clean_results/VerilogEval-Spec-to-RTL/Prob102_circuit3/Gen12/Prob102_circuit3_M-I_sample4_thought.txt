The existing logic q = (a | b) & (c | d) correctly implements the function matching the waveform. To optimize for power, area, and performance further, I will consider logic factoring and De Morgan's laws.

By De Morgan's law:
q = (a | b) & (c | d) = ¬(¬(a | b) | ¬(c | d)) = ¬((¬a & ¬b) | (¬c & ¬d))

This can be rewritten as:
q = ¬( (¬a & ¬b) | (¬c & ¬d) )

Implementing q in this form uses NOR and NAND gates, which can lead to potentially simpler and lower power logic in CMOS as NOR/NAND are basic gates. 

Using this form may reduce the number of transitions and gate count slightly because it merges the OR and AND operations into a single NOR operation of intermediate NANDs, which tends to be efficient in standard cell libraries.

Therefore, I will implement the circuit using intermediate negations and a final NOR, reflecting this optimized form.

This restructuring may offer improved power and area due to gate merging and can provide a small performance benefit from reduced logic depth.

Also, this design remains combinational and minimalistic without added complexity.
