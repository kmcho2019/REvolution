The original implementation uses q = (a & (c|d)) | (b & (c|d)), factoring out (c|d). Since (a & (c|d)) | (b & (c|d)) = (a|b) & (c|d), we can simplify further by sharing two common OR gates rather than AND-OR. This reduces the logic depth and gate count from three gates to two gates (two OR gates and one AND gate), improving performance and area slightly.

By expressing q = (a | b) & (c | d), we can implement with just two OR gates feeding a single AND gate, which typically is faster and consumes fewer resources than the original factoring.

Hence, the improved solution:
- Compute a_or_b = a | b
- Compute c_or_d = c | d
- Output q = a_or_b & c_or_d

This reduces logic depth, gate count, and toggling paths (two OR gates feeding an AND), improving performance and power.

It also aligns perfectly with the waveform and is functionally equivalent.

Thus, this approach improves PPA by minimizing logic levels and shared signals, and makes the code clearer by directly matching the function.
