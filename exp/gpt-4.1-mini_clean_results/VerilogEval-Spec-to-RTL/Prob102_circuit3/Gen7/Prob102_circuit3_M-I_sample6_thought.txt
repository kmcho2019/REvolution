The original solution implementing q = (a | b) & (c | d) exactly matches the waveform and is logically minimal. However, further improvement for PPA can be achieved by factoring the expression to reduce logic depth and possibly switching activity:

q = (a & (c | d)) | (b & (c | d))

This suggests sharing the (c | d) term and implementing the logic as:

wire cd_or = c | d;
assign q = (a & cd_or) | (b & cd_or);

This enables sharing the OR gate output for c|d, reducing duplicated logic if the synthesis tool isn't already optimizing it. Similarly, we reduce fanout and possibly improve power.

This factorization is functionally equivalent to (a | b) & (c | d), but explicitly showing sharing may help synthesis for area and power.

Also, we can declare intermediate wires for better readability and possible synthesis benefits.

Thus, the improved code defines an intermediate wire for (c|d) and uses it twice, which might reduce total gate count and toggling, improving power slightly, with no increase in delay.

No flip-flops or clocked logic is needed since this is combinational.

Hence, the improvement is explicit factoring and clarity, possibly helping synthesis for area and power.
