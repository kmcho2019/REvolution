Analyzing the waveform, output q is 1 if either:
- a=1 and (c=1 or d=1), OR
- b=1 and (c=1 or d=1)

This can be rewritten as q = (a & (c | d)) | (b & (c | d))

Alternatively, factoring differently: q = (c | d) & (a | b)

To create a novel architectural idea, instead of directly combining all inputs with a single expression, we can break the logic into intermediate signals representing partial conditions, then combine them. For example, generate two intermediate signals: p = a & (c | d), and r = b & (c | d). Then OR these partial results to get q.

This modular approach is logically equivalent but represents a different structure using intermediate wires, thus providing a different architectural style than the previous single-line assignment.
