The existing design is already minimal and optimal for a 1-bit 2-to-1 mux in terms of area, power, and performance, using a continuous assignment and a ternary operator. There is no clock or sequential logic that could be optimized further. Since the mux is a fundamental combinational primitive, improvements at this granularity are unlikely.

However, to marginally optimize for power and possibly reduce glitching, we can implement the mux using a structural approach with AND, OR, and NOT gates, which synthesis tools often recognize and can optimize well at the gate level. This approach can help reduce dynamic power by limiting unnecessary switching at the output node.

Additionally, explicitly declaring internal wires can sometimes give synthesis tools more control over mapping the logic efficiently. Also, adding synthesis directives (like `(* keep = "true" *)` or equivalent) is not necessary here, as the design is simple.

Overall, the strategy is:
- Use a structural description: out = (~sel & a) | (sel & b).
- This is functionally equivalent but may synthesize into standard logic gates directly, potentially reducing glitches and power.
- Maintain clear port declarations and module comments.

This change should not increase area or delay significantly but can help marginally on power by ensuring simpler gate-level implementation.

Note: Depending on synthesis tool and flow, the difference may be negligible, but this is a commonly accepted minimal optimization for a simple mux in Verilog.
