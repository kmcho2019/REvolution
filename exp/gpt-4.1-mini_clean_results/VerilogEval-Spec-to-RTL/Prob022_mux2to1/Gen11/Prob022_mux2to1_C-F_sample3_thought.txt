To fuse the best ideas from both examples, I will implement the 1-bit 2-to-1 multiplexer using a continuous assignment with a ternary operator, as it is the simplest and most power- and area-efficient form. This approach avoids the complexity of always blocks and unnecessary default assignments, reducing logic and switching activity.

From Example 1, the idea of clear documentation and explicit port declarations will be retained to maintain code readability and style.

From Example 2, the explicit wire declarations on inputs and outputs help clarify signal directions and types.

Therefore, the final implementation will:
- Use continuous assign with ternary operator: `assign out = sel ? b : a;`
- Declare inputs as wire explicitly (though optional, it aids clarity)
- Declare output as wire
- Provide clear documentation for maintainability

This design is minimal, synthesizable, and optimal for power, performance, and area without sacrificing readability.
