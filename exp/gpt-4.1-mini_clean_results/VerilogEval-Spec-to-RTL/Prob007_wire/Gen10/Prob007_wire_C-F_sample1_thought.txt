To fuse the strengths of both examples, I will create a minimal and reusable design that maintains modularity while ensuring the synthesis tool will optimize it to a pure wire.

From Example 1, we get the benefit of modularity and parameterization via a reusable WirePassThrough module. From Example 2, we see that a direct continuous assignment is optimal for power and area.

My fusion strategy is:

- Keep the WirePassThrough module as a minimal wrapper with a single continuous assignment, parameterized for bit-width to allow future reuse.
- Instantiate this minimal module inside TopModule to maintain hierarchy, clarity, and modular reuse.
- Use the parameter to fix the bit-width to 1 for this use case, avoiding unnecessary complexity.
- The continuous assignment inside WirePassThrough ensures synthesis tools can easily optimize it away as a pure wire without added delay or area.
- This yields a clear, reusable, and power/area optimized design that also provides clean hierarchy.

Thus, the final design combines modularity, minimal logic, parameterization for reuse, and direct assignment for optimal synthesis results.
