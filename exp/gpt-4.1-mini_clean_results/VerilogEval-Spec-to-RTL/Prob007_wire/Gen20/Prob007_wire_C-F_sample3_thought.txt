To fuse the best ideas, I will implement a minimalistic, parameterized wire-passthrough module using a continuous assignment for clarity, modularity, and synthesis-friendly design. Unlike the separate primitive buf gate, the parameterized module with a simple assign statement offers better flexibility for reuse with various bit widths and better synthesis optimization potential. At the same time, the module is straightforward enough that synthesis tools will optimize it down to a direct net connection, incurring no performance, power, or area penalty.

I will then instantiate this module within TopModule, preserving hierarchy and modular clarity without overhead. The parameterization is retained to encourage reuse and future extensibility without sacrificing the simplicity or efficiency of a direct wire.

This approach balances modular clarity, flexibility, and PPA efficiency by:

- Defining a simple parameterized wire-passthrough module using assign (no primitives).
- Avoiding unnecessary gate primitives that could complicate synthesis.
- Instantiating it in TopModule for clear hierarchy and reuse.
- Relying on synthesis tools to optimize the hierarchy to a minimal wire connection.

Overall, this fusion promotes best-in-class modularity and reuse with minimal PPA impact, making it a scalable and clean solution for both single-bit and potential wider applications.
