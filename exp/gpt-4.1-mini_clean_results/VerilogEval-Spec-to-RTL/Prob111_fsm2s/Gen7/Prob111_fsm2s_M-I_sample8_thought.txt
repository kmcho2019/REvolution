The initial design is already very optimal for such a simple two-state Moore FSM in terms of performance, power, and area: it uses a single bit for state encoding, minimal combinational logic, and a synchronous reset. However, slight improvements can be made to reduce glitching on combinational signals, which can indirectly reduce power consumption.

One common technique is to use a registered output rather than combinational logic for the Moore output. This stores the output in a flip-flop, ensuring stable outputs without glitches and reducing dynamic power consumption caused by output toggling in combinational logic.

Given that the output is strictly a function of state, we add an output register updated at clock edge, making the output a registered Moore output. This adds one flip-flop but can reduce power and improve timing stability, often desirable in FPGA or ASIC design flows.

All other logic is kept minimal and synchronous reset remains active high and synchronous. This adjustment is a good trade-off for stable outputs and slight power improvement at the cost of a single flip-flop, which is negligible here.

Hence, the improvement strategy is to register the Moore output rather than drive it directly from combinational state logic.
