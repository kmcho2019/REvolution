The original design is already minimal and clean for a two-state Moore FSM. However, a slight improvement can be made by removing the explicit next_state register to reduce the register count by one, combining the next state logic and state update in a single always block. This reduces area slightly and potentially improves timing by reducing the logic levels between registers. Also, assigning the output directly from the registered state instead of next_state maintains correct Moore semantics, and may reduce glitches since the output directly reflects the stable state register rather than a combinational signal. We keep synchronous reset and non-blocking assignments. Overall, this trims one flip-flop (next_state) and simplifies the design while preserving functionality and timing.
