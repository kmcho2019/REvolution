To optimize for better PPA, the design can be simplified by encoding the FSM into a single 2-bit state register rather than separate direction and falling bits. This reduces register count and logic complexity slightly. By encoding states explicitly as four states representing walking left/right and falling left/right, the direction is implicitly preserved when falling, eliminating the need for separate direction register.

This approach helps in:
- Minimizing flip-flop count from 2 to 2 bits but combined as one register, potentially enabling simpler next-state logic.
- Simplifying combinational next-state logic by a case statement rather than separate variables and conditionals.
- Potentially improving timing due to more straightforward state decoding.
- Avoiding separate direction and falling signals reduces interdependent signals and can help synthesis optimizations.
- Moore outputs are derived directly from the state bits.

The asynchronous reset will still reset to walking left.

This encoding is:

2-bit state:
00 = walking left
01 = walking right
10 = falling left
11 = falling right

Transitions:
- When walking and bumped on either side (or both), switch walking direction.
- When ground disappears (ground=0) while walking, go to falling same direction.
- While falling, bump inputs ignored; when ground reappears (ground=1), resume walking same direction.
- aaah is asserted when in falling state (states 10 or 11).
- walk_left when walking left state (00), walk_right when walking right state (01).

This approach reduces separate signal overhead and helps synthesis to merge logic efficiently.

The rest of the logic (async reset, posedge clock) remains the same.
