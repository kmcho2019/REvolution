Refactor the design by splitting the FSM state and the walking direction into separate registers. Use a 1-bit direction register (0 = left, 1 = right) to remember walking direction. Have a separate falling state register (1 = falling, 0 = walking). On reset, start walking left and not falling.

On each clock, if falling, remain falling until ground returns; output aaah=1, no walking signals asserted. Bumps during falling do not affect direction.

When not falling and ground disappears, go to falling.

When not falling and ground present, bumps control direction: bump_left sets direction to right, bump_right sets direction to left; bump_left and bump_right together also change direction accordingly (if both, direction flips). We implement bump logic to precisely set direction as per problem description.

Outputs are combinational: walk_left = walking and direction==0, walk_right = walking and direction==1, aaah = falling.

This separation simplifies state and direction management and clarifies bump direction handling, avoiding the pitfalls of encoding direction in multi-bit states and next state logic with ambiguous bump conditions.

The asynchronous reset will clear falling and direction to walk left.

This implementation meets the problem requirements with clearer state management and explicit handling of bump inputs.
