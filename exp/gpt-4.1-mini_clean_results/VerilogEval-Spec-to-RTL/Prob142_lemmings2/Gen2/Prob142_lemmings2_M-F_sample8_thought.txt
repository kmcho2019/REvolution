The key fix is to align ground edge detection properly with the clock domain and simplify the next state logic to avoid redundant and potentially conflicting checks.

- Store the previous ground signal synchronously (`ground_d`) updated at clock edge.
- Detect edges combinationally as `ground_falling_edge = ground_d & ~ground` and `ground_rising_edge = ~ground_d & ground`.
- Use these edges in the next state combinational logic to distinguish the cycle when ground changes.
- When ground falls (either detected by `ground_falling_edge` or `ground == 0` but ground stable), immediately go to FALLING state.
- When falling and ground rises (`ground_rising_edge` or stable ground=1), resume previous walking direction stored in `walking_dir`.
- Update `walking_dir` only when entering a walking state, using the actual next_state.
- Ignore bumps during ground edges (`ground_falling_edge` or `ground_rising_edge`) and during FALLING state.
- Handle bumps only in walking states when ground is stable (no edge).
- Remove redundant condition checks to clarify priorities: 
   1) If ground just fell or ground=0, go to FALLING.
   2) If ground just rose from FALLING, resume walking_dir.
   3) Else, if walking and bumps occur and no ground edge, switch walking direction.
- Initialize `ground_d` on reset as the actual input `ground` to avoid mismatch after reset.
- This approach prevents bumps during ground transitions from affecting direction and keeps walking_dir consistent with walking states, eliminating the mismatch on walk_left and walk_right outputs.

The outputs remain purely Moore: determined by current state.

This simplification and correct edge synchronization aligns the FSM outputs with the specification and fixes the direction mismatch issue.
