The original solution was almost correct but had subtle issues with ground edge detection and walking_dir updates, which caused direction mismatches during walking states.

To improve correctness and clarity:

1. **Synchronize ground edge detection:**
   - Sample ground into a register `ground_d` on each clock to detect edges.
   - Use these synchronous edges only within the clocked always block to determine next state, avoiding combinational logic dependence on asynchronous signals.
   - This guarantees edges align with FSM transitions without glitches.

2. **Walking direction update:**
   - Instead of updating walking_dir based on `next_state`, update walking_dir based on actual current state and bump inputs when ground is stable.
   - Maintain walking_dir during FALLING state.
   - This prevents walking_dir from toggling unexpectedly due to transient next_state values.

3. **Simplify next state logic inside clocked block:**
   - Use a single clocked always block for state transitions.
   - On reset, initialize to WALK_LEFT and walking_dir=left.
   - On ground falling edge or ground=0, transition to FALLING (ignore bumps).
   - While in walking states, if ground stable, check bumps and flip direction accordingly.
   - On ground rising edge, resume walking in stored walking_dir.
   - Ignore bumps on edges or during falling.

4. **Outputs are determined by state only (Moore FSM).**

This approach ensures bump inputs affect walking direction only when ground is stable, edges are detected cleanly synchronized to clock, and walking_dir is updated predictably. It reduces combinational complexity and improves timing and PPA while fixing the previous corner cases.

This design strictly adheres to the problem statement and avoids glitches that caused mismatch errors in walking signals.
