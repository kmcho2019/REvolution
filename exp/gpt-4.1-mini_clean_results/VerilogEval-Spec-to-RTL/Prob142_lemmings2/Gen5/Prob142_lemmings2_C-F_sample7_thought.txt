Fuse the best features from both examples by keeping a simple two-bit FSM state that encodes direction and falling in one register to reduce the total number of flip-flops to just one 2-bit state register, instead of two separate 1-bit registers.

Encode the state as:
- 2'b00: walking left
- 2'b01: walking right
- 2'b10: falling left (falling while previously walking left)
- 2'b11: falling right (falling while previously walking right)

On asynchronous reset, set the state to walking left (2'b00).

At each clock cycle:
- If falling (state[1] = 1), stay falling until ground returns. When ground returns, revert to walking in the direction preserved in state[0].
- If walking (state[1] = 0), and ground disappears, transition to falling with same direction.
- If walking and on ground, update direction depending on bump inputs:
  - bump_left = 1: set direction to right (1)
  - bump_right = 1: set direction to left (0)
  - both bumps = flip direction (~state[0])
- Bump inputs during falling or during transitions are ignored.

Outputs are Moore-style based on state:
- aaah = state[1] (falling bit)
- walk_left = (~aaah) & ~state[0]
- walk_right = (~aaah) & state[0]

This fusion reduces registers to a single 2-bit state, ensuring minimal area, slightly simpler control logic, and preserving the clear direction/falling semantics. It also strictly separates walking and falling sub-states and handles bumps precisely. The asynchronous reset and Moore outputs are maintained.

The combined logic simplifies direction and falling together in one state register while maintaining readability, correctness, and efficiency.
