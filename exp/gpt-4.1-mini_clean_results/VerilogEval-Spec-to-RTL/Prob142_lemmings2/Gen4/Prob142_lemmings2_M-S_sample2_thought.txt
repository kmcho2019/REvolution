Use a simple two-bit state machine with three states: WALK_LEFT, WALK_RIGHT, and FALLING. Store the walking direction implicitly in the walking states and keep the current walking direction remembered during falling. 

On bumps in walking states, change direction according to which bump is active: if bump_left=1, switch to walk_right; if bump_right=1, switch to walk_left; if both bumps are active simultaneously, switch direction as if either bump occurred (both bumps trigger a direction change). Ignore bumps during falling.

When ground=0, go to falling state remembering current direction. When ground=1 again, resume walking in remembered direction.

Use a synchronous process with asynchronous reset on posedge of areset to update the state and direction.

Output walk_left, walk_right, and aaah based directly on the state, making outputs Moore-type.

This simplifies the code by avoiding a separate direction register for falling and keeps direction tied to state except when falling, improving readability and correctness.
