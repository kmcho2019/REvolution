This design models the Lemming's behavior using two separate flip-flops: one for direction (walk_left or walk_right) and one for whether it's falling (aaah). The direction flip-flop is updated only when the Lemming is on the ground (ground=1), and bumps cause direction to flip regardless of which side or both sides are bumped simultaneously.

When the ground disappears (ground=0), the falling flip-flop is set to 1 (aaah=1) to indicate falling; during falling, direction remains constant, unaffected by bumps. When the ground reappears (ground=1), falling is cleared and the previous direction resumes.

This architecture splits the state into two independent flip-flops which can be clock-gated or optimized separately. The direction state transitions only on bumps when ground is present, ensuring bumps during falling do not change direction.

Asynchronous reset forces the Lemming to walking left (direction=0) and not falling (fall=0).

Outputs are directly mapped from these flip-flops: walk_left = direction==0 and falling==0; walk_right = direction==1 and falling==0; aaah = falling.

This approach is conceptually simpler and uses minimal logic, potentially improving clarity and some synthesis tools' ability to optimize direction and falling state separately.
