Fuse the clarity and minimal state approach of Example 1 with the explicit state encoding clarity of Example 2 to form a hybrid FSM.

- Keep a single 1-bit 'falling' register to indicate falling state.
- Keep a 1-bit 'direction' register to indicate walk direction (0=left, 1=right).
- On asynchronous reset, start walking left (direction=0) and not falling.
- When falling, ignore bumps and remain falling until ground returns; output aaah=1; no walking outputs asserted.
- When not falling, bumps change direction per problem statement:
   - bump_left causes direction = 1 (walk right)
   - bump_right causes direction = 0 (walk left)
   - both bumps simultaneously flip direction (~direction)
- On ground going low while not falling, start falling without changing direction.
- When ground returns from falling, resume walking in the previous direction.
  
This fusion keeps state and direction separately for simplicity and minimal registers (as in Example 1), but borrows the explicit logic for updating direction on bumps from Example 2's clear conditional expressions. It avoids a 3-state FSM encoding, reducing area and power, and simplifies critical paths. The asynchronous reset remains for immediate initialization.

Outputs are simple combinational expressions from falling and direction registers, maintaining Moore FSM style.

This fusion combines the minimal register count and clean direction logic from both examples, aiming to improve area, power, and timing while fully meeting the specification.
