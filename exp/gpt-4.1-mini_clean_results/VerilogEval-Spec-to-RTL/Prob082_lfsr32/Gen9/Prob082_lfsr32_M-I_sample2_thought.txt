To optimize the design for better power and performance without changing the LFSR functionality, I will implement the Galois LFSR with explicit XOR feedback on the tapped bits during the shift. This approach updates each bit based on the LSB and the taps, avoiding a full 32-bit XOR operation each cycle. It will reduce switching activity and may slightly reduce logic complexity and critical path delay.

Specifically, in a Galois LFSR, bits shift right, but the feedback (from the LSB) is XORed only into the tapped bits' next values. Non-tapped bits simply shift down without XOR. This can be implemented by shifting q right by 1 and XORing the feedback bit only into the tapped bits.

I will:
- Remove the full vector XOR with the TAP_MASK.
- Instead, implement the update logic by shifting right and XORing the LSB with the tapped bits individually.
- This reduces the combinational XOR fanout, potentially lowering power and improving timing.
- Keep synchronous reset and same interface.

This explicit per-bit update aligns with the classic Galois LFSR and should improve PPA metrics.

Additionally, I will declare the next state variable (`next_q`) outside the always block for clarity.
