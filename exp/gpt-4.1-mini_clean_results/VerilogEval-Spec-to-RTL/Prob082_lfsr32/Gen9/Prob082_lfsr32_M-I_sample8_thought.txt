To optimize the existing Galois LFSR implementation for better PPA, we focus on reducing combinational logic delay and potential dynamic power. By explicitly coding the LFSR feedback bit as q[0], and updating each bit based on whether it is tapped, we can avoid a wide XOR operation with the full tap mask in one expression, potentially reducing critical path delay.

Additionally, we unroll the XOR operations and use generate loops or explicit combinational assignments to minimize gate fan-in. Since taps are sparse, this approach clarifies the logic and potentially allows synthesis tools to optimize better.

We keep the synchronous reset and non-blocking assignments as is.

This explicit approach may yield a slight improvement in timing and power by reducing the XOR fan-in and spreading the logic across multiple bits rather than a single large XOR, without increasing area significantly.
