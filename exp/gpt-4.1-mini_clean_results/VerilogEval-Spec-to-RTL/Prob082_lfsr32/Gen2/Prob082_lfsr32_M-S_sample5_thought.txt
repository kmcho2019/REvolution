To simplify the Galois LFSR implementation, first extract the feedback bit (q[0]). Then compute the next state of q as a 32-bit vector by shifting right one bit and XORing feedback into the tapped bit positions (bits 31, 21, 1, and 0). The entire next state can be computed combinationally and assigned to q in a single non-blocking assignment on the clock's positive edge. This avoids partial slice assignments and in-block wire declarations, making the code clearer and syntactically correct.
