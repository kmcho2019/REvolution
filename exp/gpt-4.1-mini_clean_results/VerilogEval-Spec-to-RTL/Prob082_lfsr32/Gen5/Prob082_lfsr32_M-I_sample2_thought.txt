The existing design is already efficient and clean, using a single right shift and conditional XOR with the TAP_MASK masked by q[0]. To optimize for better power and timing (PPA), we can reduce toggling by minimizing the XOR gates switching when q[0] is zero and simplifying logic further. 

A way to do this is to implement the LFSR as a shift register where the next MSB bit is computed first as the XOR of q[0] and q[31], then other bits are shifted with conditional XORs at the tapped positions. This avoids applying a broad XOR mask on all bits simultaneously, potentially reducing switching activity and improving timing by localizing XOR gates instead of a big wide XOR. 

In this "conditional Galois" approach, each tapped bit position toggles based on q[0], while non-tapped bits just shift. It preserves the same LFSR polynomial and behavior. The synchronous reset remains as before. 

This approach may reduce gate switching on non-tapped bits, potentially improving power and slightly improving timing by localizing combinational logic depth. Area should be similar or slightly less since fewer wide XORs are needed.

We will write the shift update explicitly for each bit, unrolling the logic, which can help synthesis tools optimize better. This is a classical Galois LFSR update style.

The taps at bit positions 32, 22, 2, and 1 translate to bits 31, 21, 1, and 0 zero-based in Verilog. The MSB next bit is XOR(q[0], q[31]). Bits 30 down to 22 shift from q[31:23] (no tap). Bit 21 toggles XOR with q[0]. Bits 20 down to 2 shift from q[21:3]. Bit 1 toggles XOR with q[0]. Bit 0 toggles XOR with q[0].

This design explicitly enumerates all 32 bits' next values and should optimize power by limiting XOR toggling to tapped positions only.
