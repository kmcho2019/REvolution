Refactor the Galois LFSR by separating the combinational next-state logic from the sequential update. Compute the next state of the register as a wire by first shifting right and conditionally XORing with the tap mask based on the current LSB. Then update the register q with this next state in a clocked always block. This approach improves clarity by isolating combinational logic, can ease formal verification, and keeps the same synchronous reset and tap logic. The behavior remains identical but the separation may also assist synthesis tools in optimizing timing and power.
