To fuse the strengths of both designs, I will keep the explicit per-bit XOR feedback approach from Example 1 to minimize switching activity and reduce power by XORing only the tapped bits, not the entire vector. This reduces toggling in non-tapped bits and lowers unnecessary logic toggling compared to Example 2's replicated mask.

At the same time, I will simplify the combinational logic by using a wire for the feedback and computing the next state combinationally outside the clocked block, improving code clarity and synthesis friendliness.

I will:
- Declare feedback wire = q[0].
- Compute next_q as a shifted q with XOR applied only to tapped bits.
- Assign next_q to q synchronously on clock edge with synchronous reset.
- Use a compact tapped bits mask and selectively XOR tapped bits, similar to Example 1.
- Avoid creating a full 32-bit replicated vector from q[0], thus minimizing power.
- Keep code clean, synthesizable, and maintain performance.

This fusion reduces power over Example 2 by avoiding a full mask AND and reduces logic complexity compared to explicit blocking assignments inside the always block, improving readability and possibly timing.
