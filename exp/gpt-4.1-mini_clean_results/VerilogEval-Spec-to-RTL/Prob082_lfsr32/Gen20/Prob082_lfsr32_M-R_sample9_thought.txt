Refactor the 32-bit Galois LFSR implementation by separating the next-state combinational logic from the sequential register update. Use a combinational always block to compute the next state (next_q) based on the current q and the feedback bit (q[0]). Then, in a synchronous always block triggered by clk and reset, update q with next_q or reset to 1. This structure cleanly separates combinational feedback logic and synchronous state storage, easing timing analysis and improving readability. Additionally, unroll the XOR taps explicitly with bit-by-bit assignments, preserving clarity and enabling synthesis optimization without multiple partial assignments.
