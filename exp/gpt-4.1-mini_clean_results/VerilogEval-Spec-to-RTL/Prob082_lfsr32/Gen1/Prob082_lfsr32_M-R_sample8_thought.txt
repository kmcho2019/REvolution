Refactor the Galois LFSR implementation by computing the next state combinationally before the clock edge, then assigning it to the register on the positive clock edge. Use a wire `next_q` to hold the next state value based on the current state `q` and the feedback bit `q[0]`. For each bit position `i` from 31 down to 1, if `i` is tapped (31, 21, 1, 0 in 0-based indexing correspond to taps at bits 32, 22, 2, and 1 respectively), then `next_q[i] = q[i-1] ^ q[0]`; otherwise, `next_q[i] = q[i-1]`. The LSB `next_q[0]` simply gets the old `q[0]` shifted out to `q[1]` (non-tapped) or stays as old `q[1]` value without XOR. Implement synchronous reset to 32'h1 on the rising clock edge. This clean separation of combinational and sequential logic avoids partial assignments and improper ordering, ensuring correct LFSR behavior.
