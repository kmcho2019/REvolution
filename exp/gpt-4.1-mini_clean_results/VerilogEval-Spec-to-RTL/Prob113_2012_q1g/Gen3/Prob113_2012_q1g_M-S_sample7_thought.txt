To simplify the function f, analyze the Karnaugh map and derive a minimized boolean expression instead of enumerating all minterms.

Given row = {x[2], x[3]} and col = {x[0], x[1]} with f=1 in the K-map:

            x[0]x[1]
x[2]x[3]  00  01  11  10
  00     | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
  01     | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
  11     | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
  10     | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 |

From the map, group adjacent ones to find simpler product terms:

- Group 1: all four corners (positions where x[1]=0 and x[3]=0 or 1) suggest terms with ~x[1].
- Group 2: rows with x[2]=1 have many ones; observe that when x[2] = 1 and (x[1]=0 or x[3]=0), f=1.
- Group 3: A larger combined group can be expressed as x[2] | (~x[1] & ~x[3]) | (x[0] & x[1] & x[2] & x[3]) (the last one addresses the isolated 1 at row=11 col=11).

But a better simplification is:

f = (~x[1]) | (x[2] & ~x[3]) | (x[2] & x[3] & x[1]) 

Explanation:
- ~x[1] covers all columns where x[1]=0 (col=00,10), which already includes many 1's.
- x[2] & ~x[3] covers row 10, the third row with many 1's.
- x[2] & x[3] & x[1] covers the remaining 1's in row 11 col 01 and 11.

This expression greatly reduces terms and literals, improving PPA while preserving correctness.
