Instead of implementing the function via a LUT, derive the minimized boolean expression from the Karnaugh map and implement it using combinational logic gates.

Given the Karnaugh map:

             x[0]x[1]
x[2]x[3]  00  01  11  10
  00     | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
  01     | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
  11     | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
  10     | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 |

First, note the 1's at addresses (row, column):

- (00,00) → x[2]=0,x[3]=0,x[0]=0,x[1]=0 → f=1  
- (00,10) → x[2]=0,x[3]=0,x[0]=1,x[1]=0 → f=1  
- (10,00) → x[2]=1,x[3]=0,x[0]=0,x[1]=0 → f=1  
- (10,01) → x[2]=1,x[3]=0,x[0]=0,x[1]=1 → f=1  
- (10,10) → x[2]=1,x[3]=0,x[0]=1,x[1]=0 → f=1  
- (11,00) → x[2]=1,x[3]=1,x[0]=0,x[1]=0 → f=1  
- (11,01) → x[2]=1,x[3]=1,x[0]=0,x[1]=1 → f=1  
- (11,11) → x[2]=1,x[3]=1,x[0]=1,x[1]=1 → f=1  
- (11,10) → x[2]=1,x[3]=1,x[0]=1,x[1]=0 → f=0  
- (01,xx) → all zero  

Finding implicants from the map:

- Group1: Row 11, columns 00,01,11 (f=1), corresponds to x[2]=1,x[3]=1,x[0] any except possibly 10, x[1] varies. For these 3 cells, we can simplify as x[2] & x[3].

- Group2: Column 00 (x[0]=0, x[1]=0) with rows 00,10,11 (three 1's) corresponds to x[0]=0 & x[1]=0 & (x[2]=0,x[3]=0 or 1,x[3]=1) → simplifying to ~x[0] & ~x[1].

- Group3: At row 10, columns 01 and 10 are 1, which are (x[2]=1,x[3]=0,x[0]=0,x[1]=1) and (x[2]=1,x[3]=0,x[0]=1,x[1]=0). This pattern suggests an XOR of x[0] and x[1], masked by x[2]=1 & x[3]=0.

Deriving final expression:

f = (x[2] & x[3])         // Group1  
  | (~x[0] & ~x[1])       // Group2  
  | (x[2] & ~x[3] & (x[0] ^ x[1])) // Group3

This is a compact logic expression covering all 1s in the map.

This combinational expression can be implemented using AND, OR, NOT, and XOR gates without any LUT.

This approach reduces area and power, potentially improves timing by avoiding a wide LUT.
