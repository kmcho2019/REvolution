The fusion strategy combines the explicitness and direct mapping of minterms from Example 1 with the compact, factored form from Example 2. Example 1 provides full coverage and clarity by listing all minterms explicitly, which is perfect for verification and understanding. Example 2 offers a more compact, minimal, and easily synthesizable expression using a factored form and an XNOR logic term that covers multiple minterms efficiently.

Our superior solution will:
- Use the factored structure of Example 2 for compactness and efficiency.
- Replace the XNOR with its equivalent AND-OR form as done in Example 2 to avoid XOR gates, benefiting some synthesis flows.
- Include an additional minterm from Example 1’s explicit minterm list that is not covered by the base Example 2 expression, if any, to ensure full correctness.
- Double-check that all minterms from the Karnaugh map are covered.
- Use internal wires for readability and easy maintenance.
- Achieve a minimal logic depth with balanced gate usage.

By doing this, we get the clarity and correctness of explicit minterms, and the performance and area benefits of factoring and minimal gate usage. This approach balances readability, synthesis friendliness, and optimal PPA characteristics.

The final logic formula is:
f = (~x2 & ~x3 & ~x1) 
  | (x2 & (~x0 | ( (x1 & x3) | (~x1 & ~x3) )))

This covers all Karnaugh map 1s correctly and uses only AND/OR/NOT gates, suitable for most synthesis tools and technologies.
