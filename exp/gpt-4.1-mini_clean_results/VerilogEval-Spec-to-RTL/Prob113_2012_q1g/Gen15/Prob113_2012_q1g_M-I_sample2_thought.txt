The original design is already minimal and clear, but further optimizations can improve area and power slightly by reducing intermediate wires and sharing complements more aggressively. 

Improvement strategy:
- Factor out common terms in the expression to reduce redundant logic:
  From the original:
  f = (~x3 & ~x1)
    | (~x3 & x2 & x1 & ~x0)
    | (x3 & x2 & (~x0 | x1))

  Notice (~x3) is common in first two terms:
    (~x3 & (~x1 | (x2 & x1 & ~x0)))
  Also, (~x0 | x1) appears only once.

- Merge the first two terms under one AND with OR:
  f = (~x3 & ( ~x1 | (x2 & x1 & ~x0) )) | (x3 & x2 & (~x0 | x1))

- Reduce intermediate wires by combining logic inside the always small expressions.
- Use minimal complements: only generate complements once and reuse.
- This reduces the number of gates and nets, helping area and power.
- Keep the decomposition to two-input AND gates for synthesis friendliness but minimize unnecessary wires.

This approach improves power by fewer nets toggling and reduces area by removing extra intermediate nets, while maintaining the original function and timing characteristics.
