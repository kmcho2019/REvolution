Instead of using a LUT to directly map the input to the output, we can implement the logic function by decomposing the Karnaugh map into minimal sum-of-products terms.

Given the K-map, we will derive the minimal SOP expression for f in terms of x[0], x[1], x[2], x[3]:

From the K-map:

Rows (x[2] x[3]) and Columns (x[0] x[1]):

| x[2]x[3] \ x[0]x[1] | 00 | 01 | 11 | 10 |
|---------------------|----|----|----|----|
| 00                  | 1  | 0  | 0  | 1  |
| 01                  | 0  | 0  | 0  | 0  |
| 11                  | 1  | 1  | 1  | 0  |
| 10                  | 1  | 1  | 0  | 1  |

We write the minterms (inputs where f=1):

- (x[2], x[3], x[0], x[1]) = 
  - 0,0,0,0 (addr 0)
  - 0,0,1,0 (addr 2)
  - 1,1,0,0 (addr 12)
  - 1,1,0,1 (addr 13)
  - 1,1,1,1 (addr 15)
  - 1,0,0,0 (addr 8)
  - 1,0,0,1 (addr 9)
  - 1,0,1,0 (addr 10)

From here, by analyzing the K-map, we find that the ones cover these implicants:

1. x[2]=1 and x[3]=0 (row 10), columns 00, 01, 10 with output 1 except for 11 (addr11=0)
2. x[2]=1 and x[3]=1 (row 11), columns 00, 01, 11 outputs 1 except for 10 (addr14=0)
3. x[2]=0 and x[3]=0 (row 00), columns 00 and 10 outputs 1

Minimal SOP form can be:

f = (~x[2] & ~x[3] & (~x[0] & ~x[1] | x[0] & ~x[1]))   // row 00, columns 00 and 10
  | (x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[1])                              // row 10, columns 00, 01, 10: ~x[1] covers 00(0,0), 01(0,1), 10(1,0)
  | (x[2] & x[3] & ( ~x[0] | (x[0] & x[1]) ))          // row 11, columns 00, 01, 11

Simplify:

- The first term: ~x[2] & ~x[3] & (~x[0]&~x[1] | x[0]&~x[1]) = ~x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[1] & (~x[0] | x[0]) = ~x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[1]

- The second term: x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[1]

- The third term: x[2] & x[3] & (~x[0] | (x[0] & x[1])) = x[2] & x[3] & (~x[0] | x[0]&x[1]) 

Rewrite (~x[0] | x[0]&x[1]) = (~x[0]) + (x[0] & x[1]) = (~x[0]) + (x[0] & x[1])

This expression is a sum; in boolean logic, it means:

  (~x[0]) + (x[0] & x[1]) = (~x[0]) + (x[0] & x[1]) = (~x[0]) + (x[0] & x[1]) = The minimal expression equals (~x[0] + x[1])

Because (~x[0] + x[1]) covers both cases (if x[0]=0 or x[1]=1).

So, simplify:

f = (~x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[1])
  | (x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[1])
  | (x[2] & x[3] & (~x[0] | x[1]))

Combining first two terms since both have ~x[1]:

f = ~x[1] & (~x[3] & (~x[2] | x[2])) | (x[2] & x[3] & (~x[0] | x[1]))

As (~x[2] | x[2])=1, so:

f = ~x[1] & ~x[3] | (x[2] & x[3] & (~x[0] | x[1]))

This is a compact form.

Hence,

f = (~x[1] & ~x[3]) | (x[2] & x[3] & (~x[0] | x[1]))

This expression is compact and can be implemented with a few gates.

We will implement this expression directly in combinational logic.

This reduces area and power compared to the LUT, while ensuring the logic matches the Karnaugh map function exactly.
