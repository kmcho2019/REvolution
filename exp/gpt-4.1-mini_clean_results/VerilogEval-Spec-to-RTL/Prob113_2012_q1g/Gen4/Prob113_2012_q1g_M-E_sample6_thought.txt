Instead of using a single LUT indexed by a specific bit ordering, we can derive and implement a minimal boolean expression for the function f by analyzing the Karnaugh map.

From the K-map:

| x[2]x[3]\x[0]x[1] | 00 | 01 | 11 | 10 |
|-------------------|----|----|----|----|
| 00                | 1  | 0  | 0  | 1  |
| 01                | 0  | 0  | 0  | 0  |
| 11                | 1  | 1  | 1  | 0  |
| 10                | 1  | 1  | 0  | 1  |

Grouping the 1's to get a minimal SOP:

- Group1: x[2]' x[3]' x[0]' x[1]' = (row=00 col=00) alone, but since isolated, look for bigger groups.

- Observe groups in row 10 and 11:
    - For x[2]x[3] = 11 (3), f=1 for col=00,01,11 (x[0]x[1]=00,01,11)
    - For x[2]x[3] = 10 (2), f=1 for col=00,01,10
  This suggests some terms involving x[2] = 1.

- Analyzing common patterns:
    - When x[2] = 1 and x[1] = 0, f=1 (covers many ones)
    - Also, when x[3]' x[0]' x[1]' (row=00, col=00) = 1
    - Also row=00 col=10 (x[0]=1,x[1]=0) = 1

Final minimal SOP (from inspection):
f = (~x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[0] & ~x[1])   // (00,00)
  | (~x[2] & ~x[3] & x[0] & ~x[1])    // (00,10)
  | (x[2] & ~x[1])                    // rows 10 and 11 where x[1]=0 and x[2]=1

This covers all 1's.

Thus, the boolean expression is:

f = (~x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[0] & ~x[1]) | (~x[2] & ~x[3] & x[0] & ~x[1]) | (x[2] & ~x[1])

We can implement this as combinational logic with AND, OR, and NOT gates directly, removing the LUT. This approach reduces area and switching and improves clarity.
