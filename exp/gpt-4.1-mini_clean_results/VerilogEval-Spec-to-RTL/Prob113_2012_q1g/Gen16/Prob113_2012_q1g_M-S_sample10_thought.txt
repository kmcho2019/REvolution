Examining the K-map and previous expression, the simplified form still has some complex terms, especially the last one with an OR inside a product. We can revisit the Karnaugh map and derive a minimal sum-of-products expression directly.

Marking minterms with f=1:

- Row x3x2=00: columns x1x0=00 (minterm 0), 10 (minterm 2)
- Row 01: none
- Row 11: 00 (12), 01(13), 11(15)
- Row 10: 00(8), 01(9), 10(10), 11(11)

The minterms where f=1 are: 0,2,8,9,10,11,12,13,15

Grouping:

- Group of four: minterms 8,9,10,11 (x3=1,x2=0 or 1, x1 varies, x0=0 or 1)
- Group of two: minterms 12,13 (x3=1,x2=1,x1=0,x0=0 or 1)
- Group of two: minterms 0,2 (x3=0,x2=0,x1=0,x0=0 or 1)
- Single minterm: 15 (x3=1,x2=1,x1=1,x0=1)

Simplify terms:

- Group 8,9,10,11: x3=1 and x2=0 or 1, x1 varies, x0 varies; but since all four, only x3=1 remains constant.
- Group 12,13: x3=1,x2=1,x1=0,x0=don't care (0 or 1) → x3 & x2 & ~x1
- Group 0,2: x3=0,x2=0,x1=0,x0=don't care → ~x3 & ~x2 & ~x1
- Minterm 15: x3=1,x2=1,x1=1,x0=1

Combining:

f = (x3)                       // covers minterms 8-11 and 12-15 partly
  | (~x3 & ~x2 & ~x1)          // covers 0,2
  | (x3 & x2 & ~x1)            // covers 12,13 specifically

Note: x3=1 covers minterms 8-11 and also 12-15, but not minterm 12 and 13 explicitly because x1=0 for them, so the term x3 alone includes minterms 8-11 and 12-15, which may cover the necessary ones.

Double-check:

- Minterm 12 (1100) with x3=1,x2=1,x1=0,x0=0: x3=1 → covers it

- Minterm 13 (1101): x3=1 covers it

- Minterm 15 (1111): x3=1 covers it

- Minterm 0 (0000): ~x3 & ~x2 & ~x1 → covers

- Minterm 2 (0010): ~x3 & ~x2 & ~x1 → covers

All ones are covered.

So the simplified expression is:

f = x3 | (~x3 & ~x2 & ~x1);

This is simpler, with only two product terms and a single OR gate.

This reduces complexity drastically, uses fewer literals, and should maintain functionality.
