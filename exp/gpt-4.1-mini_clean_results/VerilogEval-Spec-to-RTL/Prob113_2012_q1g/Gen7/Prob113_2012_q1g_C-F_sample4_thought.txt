To combine the strengths of both examples, I will implement the function f as a concise combinational expression derived from Karnaugh map simplification, capturing common implicants from the minterms rather than enumerating them or using a raw LUT.

This approach benefits from:
- The clarity and minimal area of direct combinational logic,
- Avoiding an explicit LUT to reduce area and potentially power,
- Reduced switching activity compared to enumerating individual minterms,
- A single assign statement for clean, synthesis-friendly code.

First, analyze the Karnaugh map to find a minimal expression:

K-map (rows: x[2]x[3], cols: x[0]x[1]):

         x[0]x[1]
x[2]x[3]  00  01  11  10
  00       1    0    0    1
  01       0    0    0    0
  11       1    1    1    0
  10       1    1    0    1

Minterms with output 1 (input x3x2x1x0, assuming x[3]=MSB, x[0]=LSB):
- 0000 (x=4'b0000)
- 0010 (4'b0010)
- 1000 (4'b1000)
- 1001 (4'b1001)
- 1011 (4'b1011)
- 1100 (4'b1100)
- 1101 (4'b1101)
- 1110 (4'b1110)

By Karnaugh map grouping and simplification, a minimal expression is:

f = (!x[2]) & (!x[1])                // covers row 00 col 00 and 10
  | x[3] & !x[1]                    // covers rows 10 and 11, col 0
  | x[2] & x[1] & !x[0];           // covers parts of row 11, col 01

This expression compactly covers all 1’s from the map. It is easy to implement as combinational logic.

Hence, implement `f` as a single assign with this minimal expression for best area, power, and performance trade-offs.
