To optimize the logic for better power and area, we first derive the minimal sum-of-products expression from the Karnaugh map. Analyzing the K-map:

           x[0]x[1]
    x[2]x[3] 00 01 11 10
      00     1  0  0  1
      01     0  0  0  0
      11     1  1  1  0
      10     1  1  0  1

By mapping x = {x3, x2, x1, x0}:

- Minterms with f=1:
  - 0000 (0), 0011 (3:0b0011?), careful check of bits order:
  
Note: The given K-map indices: rows = x[2]x[3], columns = x[0]x[1]

Meaning to access K-map cell f for input x:

row_index = {x2, x3}, column_index = {x0, x1}

Example: x=4'b0000 (x3=0,x2=0,x1=0,x0=0) => row=00, col=00 => f=1

Double check to assign correctly:

Therefore, the function f = f(x3,x2,x1,x0) = f(row,col) with row = {x2,x3}, col = {x0,x1}

Rearranged inputs for clarity:

row = {x2, x3}, col = {x0, x1}

So f is 1 at:

- row=00 (x2=0,x3=0), col=00(x0=0,x1=0) => x=0000 -> f=1
- row=00, col=10(x0=1,x1=0) => x=0010 -> f=1
- row=11(x2=1,x3=1), col=00 => x=1100 -> f=1
- row=11, col=01 => x=1101 -> f=1
- row=11, col=11 => x=1111 -> f=1
- row=10(x2=1,x3=0), col=00 => x=1000 -> f=1
- row=10, col=01 => x=1001 -> f=1
- row=10, col=10 => x=1010 -> f=1
- row=00, col=00 and 10: x=0000 and 0010

From the K-map grouping, a simplified expression is:

- Group 1: Covers entire row 11 col= 00,01,11 (x2=1,x3=1)
- Group 2: Covers row 10 col= 00,01 (x2=1,x3=0,x0=0)
- Group 3: Covers row 10 col=10 (x2=1,x3=0,x0=1,x1=0)
- Group 4: Covers row 00 col=00 and 10 (x2=0,x3=0,x0=0,x1=0 or x0=1,x1=0)

The minimal sum-of-products expression can be:

f = (x2 & x3) | (x2 & ~x3 & ~x1) | (~x2 & ~x3 & ~x1 & ~x0)

But checking more carefully:

- From the map, whenever x2=1 and x3=1, f=1 regardless of x0,x1
- When x2=1, x3=0 and x1=0, f=1 (covering row 10 cols 00,01,10)
- When x2=0, x3=0 and x1=0 and x0=0 or 1? No, only col=00 and col=10 have f=1 here, so need to specify x0 don't care except as in the map.

Re-expressing as:

f = (x2 & x3) | (x2 & ~x3 & ~x1) | (~x2 & ~x3 & ~x1 & ~x0) | (~x2 & ~x3 & ~x1 & x0)

Note the last two terms can be combined as (~x2 & ~x3 & ~x1), since x0=0 or 1

Thus simplified further:

f = (x2 & x3) | (x2 & ~x3 & ~x1) | (~x2 & ~x3 & ~x1)

= (x2 & x3) | (~x1 & ((x2 & ~x3) | (~x2 & ~x3)))

= (x2 & x3) | (~x1 & ~x3)

This is minimal and matches the K-map:

- If x2 and x3 both 1 -> f=1
- Else if x1=0 and x3=0 -> f=1
- Else f=0

This expression uses only ANDs, ORs, and inverts, thus efficient in area and power.

Implementing this with an assign statement is straightforward and efficient.

This implementation reduces logic levels and literals compared to case statements enumerating minterms, improving timing, power, and area.
