The original solution uses a LUT to implement the function directly from the Karnaugh map. This is correct and simple but may not be optimal in terms of power and area due to the 16-bit LUT.

To optimize for better power and area, derive the minimal sum-of-products expression from the Karnaugh map. 

From the K-map:

            x[0]x[1]
x[2]x[3]  00  01  11  10
  00     | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
  01     | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
  11     | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
  10     | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 |

Let's analyze the 1's:

Address = {x[2],x[3],x[0],x[1]} as rows x[2]x[3], columns x[0]x[1]

Positions of 1s (row, col):

- (00,00) = addr=0 -> 1
- (00,10) = addr=2 -> 1
- (10,00) = addr=8 -> 1
- (10,01) = addr=9 -> 1
- (10,10) = addr=10 ->1
- (11,00) = addr=12 ->1
- (11,01) = addr=13 ->1
- (11,11) = addr=15 ->1

Try to find groups to simplify:

1) Four 1s at row=11 and row=10 with col=00 and 01:

- These four correspond to rows where x[2]x[3] = 1x1x or 10 and columns 00 or 01, meaning:

x[0] = 0, x[1] = don't care

and

x[2]=1, x[3] = 1 or 0 (so x[2] = 1), x[1] don't care, x[0] = 0

=> Group1: x[2] & ~x[0]

2) Two 1s at (00,00) and (00,10):

- row=00: x[2]=0,x[3]=0

- columns: 00 and 10

- columns 00 and 10 means x[1]=0 (since 00=0 0, 10=1 0), x[0] = either 0 or 1 (since bits are x[0]x[1], with x[0] MSB in addr)

- So columns 00 (x[0]=0,x[1]=0) and 10 (x[0]=1,x[1]=0) => x[1]=0

Group2: ~x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[1]

3) One remaining 1 at addr=15: (11,11)

- row=11: x[2]=1, x[3]=1

- col=11: x[0]=1, x[1]=1

Can we include it in any group?

Look at addr=10 (10,10): 1

addr=11 (10,11):0 no

addr=14 (11,10):0 no

So no adjacent 1s to addr=15 except itself.

So term for addr=15:

x[2] & x[3] & x[0] & x[1]

Now check addr=9 (10,01): 1

addr=8 (10,00): 1

addr=10 (10,10):1

Columns 00,01,10 in row=10

x[2]=1, x[3]=0

Columns 00=0 0, 01=0 1, 10=1 0

Is there a way to group these three?

They differ in x[0] and x[1], so:

- x[2] & ~x[3] & (some expression for columns 00,01,10)

But because addr=11 (10,11) is 0, can't cover all four.

Group 3: x[2] & ~x[3] & (~x[1] | ~x[0])

Because columns 00 (x[0]=0,x[1]=0), 01 (0,1), 10 (1,0)

Looking at minterms:

(10,00): x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[0] & ~x[1]

(10,01): x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[0] & x[1]

(10,10): x[2] & ~x[3] & x[0] & ~x[1]

Covering these with: x[2] & ~x[3] & (~x[1] | ~x[0])

This covers the three 1s at (10,00),(10,01),(10,10)

The 1 at (11,11) cannot be combined with others.

Summary terms:

- T1: ~x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[1]          (Group2)

- T2: x[2] & ~x[0]                  (Group1)

- T3: x[2] & ~x[3] & (~x[1] | ~x[0]) (Group3)

- T4: x[2] & x[3] & x[0] & x[1]     (single minterm)

Now simplify T2 and T3:

T3 expands to x[2]&~x[3]&~x[1] + x[2]&~x[3]&~x[0]

Add T2: x[2]&~x[0]

Note T2 covers x[2]&~x[0], and T3 includes x[2]&~x[3]&~x[0]

Since T2 is more general (doesn't require ~x[3]), T2 covers T3's second term.

So we can simplify:

- Use T2: x[2]&~x[0]

- Use T3 reduced to x[2]&~x[3]&~x[1] (since x[2]&~x[0] already included)

Hence final expression:

f = (~x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[1])    // T1

  | (x[2] & ~x[0])             // T2

  | (x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[1])    // T3 simplified

  | (x[2] & x[3] & x[0] & x[1]) // T4

We can check if T4 is covered by previous terms:

Check (11,11): x[2]=1,x[3]=1,x[0]=1,x[1]=1

- T2: x[2]&~x[0] → x[0]=1 → no

- T3: x[2]&~x[3]&~x[1] → x[3]=1 no (need ~x[3])

- T1: ~x[2]&~x[3]&~x[1] → no (x[2]=1)

So T4 is necessary.

Implementing these 4 terms will reduce area and power compared to 16-bit LUT.

This implementation uses only AND, OR, NOT gates in small numbers.

Hence the design plan is:

- assign f = (~x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[1]) | (x[2] & ~x[0]) | (x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[1]) | (x[2] & x[3] & x[0] & x[1]);

This reduces LUT size and switching activity.

This expression is minimal or near-minimal from the K-map.

Implement this combinational logic for better PPA.
