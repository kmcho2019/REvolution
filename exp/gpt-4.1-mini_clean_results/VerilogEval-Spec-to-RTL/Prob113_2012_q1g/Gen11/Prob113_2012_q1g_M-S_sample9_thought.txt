By carefully reviewing the Karnaugh map and the previous expression, we can simplify the logic further by grouping minterms to cover the required ones with fewer literals and terms.

From the K-map:

- The first row (x3x2=00) has ones at x1x0=00 and 10 -> minterms 0 and 2
- The third row (x3x2=11) has ones at 00,01,11 -> minterms 12,13,15
- The fourth row (x3x2=10) has ones at 00,01,10 -> minterms 8,9,10

One way to simplify:

1. For x3=0,x2=0 (first row), ones at x1x0=00 and 10: (~x3 & ~x2 & ~x1 & ~x0) + (~x3 & ~x2 & x1 & ~x0) = (~x3 & ~x2 & ~x0) (covers both minterms 0 and 2)

2. For x3=1,x2=1 (third row), ones at x1x0=00,01,11:
   - (~x3=0,x2=1) is false; here x3=1,x2=1 means (x3 & x2)
   - The ones are at x1x0=00,01,11 (0,1,3 decimal)
   - So (x3 & x2 & ( (~x1 & ~x0) | (~x1 & x0) | (x1 & x0) )) simplifies to (x3 & x2 & (~x1 | x0))

3. For x3=1,x2=0 (fourth row), ones at 00,01,10 (x1x0 = 00,01,10):
   - (x3 & ~x2) & ( (~x1 & ~x0) | (~x1 & x0) | (x1 & ~x0) ) = (x3 & ~x2 & (~x1 | ~x0))

Putting together:

f = (~x3 & ~x2 & ~x0)
  | (x3 & x2 & (~x1 | x0))
  | (x3 & ~x2 & (~x1 | ~x0));

This expression has three terms, each with at most three literals, is simpler, and covers all required minterms exactly.

This reduces complexity by avoiding redundant terms and nested OR conditions, making it more straightforward for synthesis and possibly reducing gate count and delay.
