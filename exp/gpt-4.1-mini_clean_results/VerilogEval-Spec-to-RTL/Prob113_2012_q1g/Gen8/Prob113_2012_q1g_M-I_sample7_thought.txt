The current implementation is already optimized by factoring common terms and reducing product terms from five to three, which is good for power and area. However, the expression `(x3 & x2 & (~x0 | (x1 & x0)))` can be simplified further by Boolean algebra:

`(~x0 | (x1 & x0)) = ~x0 + (x1 x0)`  
This is equivalent to: `(~x0) + (x1 x0)`

Using consensus theorem or distributive law, it can be expressed as:

`(~x0) + (x1 & x0) = (~x0) + (x1 & x0) = (~x0 + x1) & (~x0 + x0) = (~x0 + x1) & 1 = ~x0 + x1`

Since `(~x0 + x1)` is simpler than the original OR expression.

So the term reduces to: `(x3 & x2 & (~x0 + x1))`

Now the final expression is:

`f = (~x3 & ~x1) | (~x3 & x2 & x1 & ~x0) | (x3 & x2 & (~x0 + x1))`

This slightly reduces gate count and logic depth.

Also, we can combine the first two terms:

`(~x3 & ~x1) | (~x3 & x2 & x1 & ~x0)`

These don't factor well further without increasing complexity, so keep them separate.

For coding style, we can also remove individual wire declarations for clarity and less verbosity.

This small algebraic simplification reduces literals and likely improves area and power marginally without affecting timing.

Therefore, the improved expression is:

`f = (~x3 & ~x1) | (~x3 & x2 & x1 & ~x0) | (x3 & x2 & (~x0 | x1));`

In Verilog, OR inside parens is replaced with `|` or `+`.

Hence this is the improved implementation.
