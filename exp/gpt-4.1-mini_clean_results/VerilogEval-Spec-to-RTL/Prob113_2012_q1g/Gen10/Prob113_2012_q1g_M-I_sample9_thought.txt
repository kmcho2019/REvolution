The previous solution is functionally correct and logically minimal; however, it contains some redundant terms that can be merged to reduce logic complexity further.

Notably, the term (x[2] & ~x[0]) covers some parts of (x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[1]) because when x[2] = 1 and x[0] = 0, f=1 regardless of x[3] and x[1]. However, since the term (x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[1]) covers cases where x[0]=1, we cannot eliminate it entirely. But we can rewrite the terms to reduce the total logic by factoring.

Also, (x[2] & ~x[0]) and (x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[1]) can be combined as:
x[2] & ( ~x[0] | (~x[3] & ~x[1]) )

This removes one AND gate.

Hence the function f can be expressed as:

f = (~x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[1])  // term1
  | x[2] & ( ~x[0] | (~x[3] & ~x[1]) )
  | (x[2] & x[3] & x[0] & x[1])  // term4 remains

This factoring reduces AND gate count and might slightly improve power and area.

Also, by sharing inverted signals efficiently and grouping sub-expressions, the code will be cleaner and possibly more efficient.
