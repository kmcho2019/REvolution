To optimize the implementation for better PPA (power, performance, area), we first translate the Karnaugh map into a boolean expression and then simplify it using Karnaugh map grouping to minimize the number of terms and literals.

From the Karnaugh map, groupings are:

- Group 1: x[2]=1 row, col=00 and 01 (covers four cells with x[2]=1, x[3]=0 or 1, x[0]=0, x[1]=0 or 1)
  -> This simplifies to x[2] & ~x[0]

- Group 2: corners where row=00 & col=00 or 10, and row=10 & col=10 (cells (00,00), (00,10), (10,10))
  -> The three cells: (x[2], x[3], x[0], x[1]) =
     - 00 00 = ~x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[0] & ~x[1]
     - 00 10 = ~x[2] & ~x[3] & x[0] & ~x[1]
     - 10 10 = x[2] & ~x[3] & x[0] & ~x[1]
  This can be combined as (~x[3]) & x[0] & ~x[1] + (~x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[0] & ~x[1]) which simplifies to (~x[3] & x[0] & ~x[1]) | (~x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[0] & ~x[1]). But the cell (00,00) is already covered in group 3, so maybe better to handle separately.

- Group 3: row=11 (x[2]=1,x[3]=1) col=00,01,11 => covers three 1s:
  (x[2] & x[3]) & ( ~x[0]&~x[1] | ~x[0]&x[1] | x[0]&x[1]) = (x[2]&x[3]) & (~x[0] | x[1])

  This can be further simplified: Since col values are 00, 01, 11 (i.e. ~x[0] or x[1])

Putting it all together:

- f = (x[2] & ~x[0])      // covers row=11 and 10 for col=00 and 01
   + (~x[3] & x[0] & ~x[1])  // covers cells with x[3]=0, x[0]=1, x[1]=0
   + (~x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[0] & ~x[1]) // cell at row=00, col=00 (top-left corner)

This reduces the number of product terms and literals compared to the minterm sum.

Implementing this reduced form improves area (fewer gates), performance (shorter logic depth), and power (less switching due to fewer terms).
