The original solution directly enumerates all minterms where the function f is 1, resulting in a lengthy sum-of-products expression with many terms. This causes more area and power usage due to multiple AND gates and OR gates and deeper logic levels.

To optimize for better power, performance, and area (PPA), the function should be minimized using Karnaugh map grouping and boolean simplification.

Given the Karnaugh map, we identify groups of 1's to find simplified product terms. For instance, looking at the map:

       x[0]x[1]
x[2]x[3] 00 01 11 10
  00     1  0  0  1
  01     0  0  0  0
  11     1  1  1  0
  10     1  1  0  1

We note the following groups:

- Group 1: All the 1's in the first column (col=00) for rows 00, 11, 10 => (col=00) => x[0]=0, x[1]=0
  - So: ~x[0] & ~x[1]

- Group 2: All 1's in row 11, columns 00, 01, 11 => (row=11) => x[2]=1, x[3]=1
  - So: x[2] & x[3]

- Group 3: 1's in row 10, columns 00 and 01 => x[2]=1, x[3]=0, x[1]=1 (col=01)
  - Actually columns 00 (00) and 01 (01) in row 10:
    (x[2]=1, x[3]=0) & (~x[0]&~x[1] OR ~x[0]&x[1]) => x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[0]

- Group 4: The 1's in row 00, columns 00 and 10 (cells at 00 and 10)
  - row=00 => ~x[2] & ~x[3]
  - columns 00 and 10 => x[1] is 0 in both (col 00 = 00, col 10 = 10 => x[1]=0)
  - columns differ in x[0] => so group: ~x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[1]

Collecting these:

f = (~x[0] & ~x[1]) 
  + (x[2] & x[3]) 
  + (x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[0]) 
  + (~x[2] & ~x[3] & ~x[1])

This is much simpler than the full sum of minterms.

Implementing this expression reduces gate count, switching, and levels, improving PPA.
