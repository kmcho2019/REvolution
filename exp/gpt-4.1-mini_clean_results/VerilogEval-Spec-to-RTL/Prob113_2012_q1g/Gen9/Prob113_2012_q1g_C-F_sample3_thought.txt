The fusion strategy combines the clearer, minimal sum-of-products structure from Example 2 with the factoring efficiency in Example 1 to reduce overlapping product terms and literals.

From Example 2, the minimal terms are:

- term1: ~x2 & ~x3 & ~x1
- term2: x2 & ~x0
- term3: x2 & ~x3 & ~x1
- term4: x2 & x3 & x0 & x1

Example 1 shows that terms involving x3 and x2 can be factored to reduce logic complexity, particularly the terms with x3 & x2 and the terms involving ~x3 & ~x1.

Looking at term1 and term3:

- term1: ~x2 & ~x3 & ~x1
- term3: x2 & ~x3 & ~x1

They share the literals ~x3 & ~x1 and differ only in x2 being 0 or 1, which covers both cases of x2.

Hence, these two can be combined as:

- ~x3 & ~x1

Similarly, term2: x2 & ~x0 remains, and term4: x2 & x3 & x0 & x1 is a specific minterm.

Therefore, the final simplified function can be expressed as:

f = (~x3 & ~x1) | (x2 & ~x0) | (x2 & x3 & x0 & x1)

This expression merges the shared parts, reduces total literals and product terms, and yields a simpler logic implementation with fewer AND terms and less logic depth.

This fusion improves power and area by reducing redundant logic and improves clarity and maintainability compared to both examples.

Implementing this final expression ensures the logic precisely matches the Karnaugh map outputs.
