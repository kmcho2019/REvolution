The original design uses one-hot encoding with three flip-flops for four states, causing moderate area and power overhead, and slight timing pressure (WNS ~ -0.22 ns). To improve PPA, we can:

- Use a 2-bit binary encoding for the four FSM states, reducing flip-flop count from 3 to 2.
- This reduces area (fewer FFs) and switching power.
- The priority logic can be implemented in a simple combinational block; although slightly more complex than one-hot, the smaller state register size benefits timing.
- Output logic maps states to g outputs accordingly.
- Keep synchronous active-low reset and posedge clock.
- Use non-blocking assignments in sequential block and blocking in combinational block for clarity.
- Ensure default states in combinational always block for safe synthesis.

This approach is the minimal encoding FSM design that should reduce area and power, while potentially improving timing due to fewer flip-flops and simpler register outputs.
