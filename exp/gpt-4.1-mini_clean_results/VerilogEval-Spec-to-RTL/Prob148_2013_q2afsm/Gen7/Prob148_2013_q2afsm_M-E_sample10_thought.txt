This solution uses binary encoding of states with a 2-bit state register. The four states are:

- 2'b00: A (idle)
- 2'b01: B (grant device 0)
- 2'b10: C (grant device 1)
- 2'b11: D (grant device 2)

Using binary encoding reduces the number of flip-flops from 3 to 2 compared to one-hot encoding, potentially improving area and power.

The FSM logic:
- In idle (A), the next state depends on the highest priority request. Priority order is r[0], then r[1], then r[2].
- In grant states (B, C, D), remain if the respective request remains asserted; otherwise go back to idle (A).

Outputs g are derived combinationally based on current state: g[0] = 1 in state B, g[1] = 1 in C, g[2] = 1 in D, else 0.

Active-low synchronous reset sets state to A.

Separate always blocks are used for state register and next-state logic. Outputs are assigned combinationally with a combinational always block for clarity.

This approach balances simplicity, area, and power by minimizing flip-flops and straightforward logic.
