To optimize for power and area while keeping the FSM simple and maintaining performance, switch from one-hot to binary state encoding. With four states (A, B, C, D), two bits suffice.

Use:
- 2'b00 for A (idle)
- 2'b01 for B (grant device 0)
- 2'b10 for C (grant device 1)
- 2'b11 for D (grant device 2)

This reduces the number of flip-flops from 3 to 2, lowering power and area. Next-state and output logic will map the binary states to grants and transitions.

Priority logic remains the same: from idle (A), check r[0], then r[1], then r[2]. From grant states, stay in the grant state if the corresponding request holds, else return to idle.

Implement synchronous active-low reset to idle.

This approach reduces flip-flops and simplifies hardware while keeping the design clean and easy to understand.
