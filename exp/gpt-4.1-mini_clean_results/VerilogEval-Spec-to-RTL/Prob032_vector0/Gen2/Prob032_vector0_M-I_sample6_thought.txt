The original design is already minimal, purely combinational, with direct wiring from input to outputs. Since it's only routing signals without any logic or registers, the performance and area are nearly optimal. Power is minimal as well. 

To optimize slightly for PPA, we can:
- Use continuous assignments as before for clarity and synthesis optimization.
- However, since o2, o1, o0 are just bits of the same vector, we can declare them as wires indexed from the vector directly. This could help synthesis tools better recognize that these signals are slices of the vector and possibly share buffers or reduce routing.
- Also, order the outputs and input bits consistently (vec[2] = o2, etc.) which is already done.

This subtle rewrite uses wire assignments instead of multiple assigns for scalar outputs and may marginally improve area and power due to clearer intent. Performance remains optimal.

Overall, no major PPA improvement is possible for such trivial combinational wiring, but this style may yield slightly cleaner netlists.

No changes to functionality or interface.
