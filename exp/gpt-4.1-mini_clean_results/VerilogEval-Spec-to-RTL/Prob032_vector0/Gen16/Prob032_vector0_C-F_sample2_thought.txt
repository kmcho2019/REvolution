The best fusion strategy combines modularity, clarity, and minimal logic overhead by directly assigning the input vector `vec` to the output vector `outv` and also directly slicing the bits of `vec` to assign to `o0`, `o1`, and `o2` outputs within the TopModule. This removes the need for an intermediate BitSplitter submodule and any extra signals, thus flattening the logic hierarchy. 

This approach improves power and area by eliminating redundant nets and module instances while maintaining perfect readability and clean, direct bit extraction. It also avoids concatenation or reconstruction of vectors, preventing unnecessary logic layers. 

In summary:
- No submodule instantiation (fewer nets, less area/power).
- Direct bit slicing from `vec` for `o0`, `o1`, `o2`.
- Direct assignment of `outv` to `vec`.
- Simple and flat combinational logic ensures minimal delay.

This yields a simple, elegant, low-overhead implementation optimized for performance, power, and area without sacrificing code clarity or modularity where not needed.
