Implement a line-buffered approach using three 16-bit shift registers to store three consecutive rows of the grid. At each clock cycle, we shift in the new row (current q row or loaded data) to the line buffers and compute the next row's state in a streaming fashion.

This approach treats the grid as a toroidal buffer and processes one row per cycle, effectively pipelining the update over multiple clock cycles. The outputs accumulate into a 256-bit register after 16 cycles.

We use modulo-16 counters for rows and columns with wrap-around indexing. The neighbor count for each cell is computed using the three line buffers by summing 8 neighbors, taking care of toroidal wrapping.

The module has internal state: the current grid q, three 16-bit registers representing previous, current, and next rows for neighbor calculation, and counters for row and column.

On load, the entire 256-bit input is loaded and the computation counters reset. Then each clock cycle advances the game one row at a time, updating q progressively.

This reduces combinational complexity per cycle, dividing the workload into 16 cycles per generation, which should improve clock frequency and timing at the cost of latency. It also minimizes simultaneous logic by handling one row of updates at a time.

The design is self-contained, synchronous, and produces identical results to the fully combinational neighbor counting approach but with a different architectural style.
