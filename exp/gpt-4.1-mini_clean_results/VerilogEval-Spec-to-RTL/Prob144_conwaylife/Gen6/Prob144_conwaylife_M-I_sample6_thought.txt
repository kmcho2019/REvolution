To optimize the original design for better performance and area, I will reduce the combinational logic depth and fanout by restructuring the neighbor counting. Instead of summing all 8 neighbors in a single large expression per cell, I will precompute horizontal partial sums for each row (sum of left, center, right neighbors), and then sum these vertical partial sums to get each cell's neighbor count.

This approach creates a two-step summation:
1. Horizontal 3-cell sums for each cell's column neighborhood in a row.
2. Vertical summation of three neighboring rows' horizontal sums to get 8 neighbors per cell.

This decomposition balances combinational logic and reduces large 8-input adders to smaller 3-input adders, which synthesize more efficiently.

Also, I will replace the signed arithmetic with explicit modulo addition using `(val + 15) % 16` for decrement and `(val + 1) % 16` for increment to avoid synthesis overhead from negative numbers and signed operations.

I will keep the same synchronous load and state update logic. This redesign reduces the critical path depth in combinational neighbor counting and should improve timing (WNS) and possibly reduce area.

The interface and functional correctness remain identical; only the internal summation structure changes.
