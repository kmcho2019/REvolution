To improve PPA, especially timing and power, we can pipeline the combinational neighbor counting logic by splitting the computation into two stages. In the first stage, we compute partial sums of neighbors row-wise, and in the second stage, we sum those partial sums to get the total neighbors per cell. This reduces the logic depth per clock cycle, improving timing. The pipeline registers also reduce glitching and switching activity, helping power. We keep the original load logic synchronous and add pipeline registers between combinational stages and before the output register update, maintaining a 2-cycle latency from input to output. This tradeoff improves clock frequency and power at the expense of latency, which is acceptable here as the "game" state update is naturally iterative and continuous.
