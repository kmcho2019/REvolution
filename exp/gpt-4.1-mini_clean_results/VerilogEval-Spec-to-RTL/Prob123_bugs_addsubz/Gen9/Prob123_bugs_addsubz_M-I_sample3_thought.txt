The original fixed solution uses an explicit ripple carry adder implemented via bitwise operations in a single always block, correctly fixing the zero flag and the subtraction logic. However, this approach incurs area and power penalties due to manual carry chain logic and the explicit loops.

To optimize for better PPA while preserving correctness and synthesis-friendliness, we can:
- Use Verilog's built-in `+` and `-` operators instead of manually implementing the ripple carry adder. This lets synthesis tools infer optimal carry chains (e.g., dedicated adder hardware).
- Implement two’s complement subtraction by using the built-in `-` operator conditionally, simplifying the logic and reducing combinational complexity.
- Use a simple combinational always block with blocking assignments to maintain the reg outputs.
- Fix the zero flag by using reduction NOR `~|out` explicitly and use an `else` branch to avoid inferred latches.
- This reduces area and power by delegating carry logic to optimized primitives inside synthesis tools.
- The slight tradeoff in having two operators (+ and -) is offset by the synthesis tool’s optimization capabilities.
- This approach also improves readability and maintainability.

Overall, this leads to a leaner, cleaner, and more efficient design without manual carry propagation logic, reducing area and power while maintaining the same functionality and correct zero flag generation.
