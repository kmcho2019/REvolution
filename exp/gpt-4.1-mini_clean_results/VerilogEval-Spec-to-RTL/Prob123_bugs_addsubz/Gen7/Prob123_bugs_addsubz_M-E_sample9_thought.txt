Instead of using a conditional case or conditional inversion plus carry-in to implement the subtract operation, I will explicitly perform addition and subtraction using separate combinational adders and then use a multiplexer to select the correct result based on do_sub. This avoids relying on XOR and carry-in trickery and may improve clarity for some synthesis tools.

For zero flag detection, I will use the reduction NOR operator on the output for concise and efficient logic.

The implementation steps:
- Compute add_result = a + b
- Compute sub_result = a - b
- Use a MUX on do_sub to select out = add_result or sub_result
- Compute result_is_zero as the NOR reduction of out.

This approach clearly separates addition and subtraction paths, ensuring correctness and ease of understanding and verification. Although it may duplicate some hardware compared to conditional inversion, it makes the design explicitly support addition and subtraction operations, which some synthesis tools might optimize well.

All operations are combinational and use blocking assignments in an always_comb or always @(*) block, maintaining readability and synthesis friendliness.
