To improve the previous solution, I aim to optimize for performance (reduce critical path delay) and power by avoiding a full ripple carry adder and leveraging the Verilog built-in addition operator for synthesis optimization. Modern synthesis tools map '+' operators efficiently to carry-lookahead or carry-select adders, reducing delay and switching activity.

The improvement strategy is:
- Use the built-in '+' operator for addition and subtraction with conditional inversion and carry-in, which synthesis tools will optimize.
- Implement subtraction by adding the two's complement of b (invert and add 1) when do_sub is high.
- Calculate the zero flag using a simple reduction NOR of the output, which is combinational and efficient.
- Keep output ports as reg with a single always_comb block, to maintain the interface and synthesis compatibility.
- This reduces manual gate-level carry chain logic, letting synthesis optimize the adder.

This approach results in smaller critical path delay and potentially less power by letting synthesis use efficient adder architectures. It also reduces code complexity and improves readability.
