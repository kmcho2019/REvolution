The original design implements the function as out = (~c & a) | (c & ~b) | (c & a). By factoring c, this is equivalent to (~c & a) | (c & (~b | a)). Since (~b | a) is a common term, we can reduce logic by combining c & (~b | a) into one term instead of two separate ANDs followed by an OR. This reduces gate count and switching activity, improving power and area. We keep the separate ~c & a term and then OR with the combined term. Also, by sharing the or-term (~b | a), we avoid redundant gate usage. This is a minimal change that simplifies the logic and reduces the overall gate count, optimizing PPA while preserving correctness.
