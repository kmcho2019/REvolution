The original solution implements the function as out = (~c & a) | (c & ~b) | (c & a). We can factor the last two terms as c & (~b | a), reducing from three AND gates to two AND gates and one OR gate inside. This shares the common factor 'c', reducing gate count and thus area and power.

Also, we keep the inverter for ~c and ~b, but since ~b is used only once, that is minimal. The logic now becomes:

out = (~c & a) | (c & (~b | a))

This simplification reduces the total number of product terms and gates, improving area and power while keeping the logic simple and performance optimal.
