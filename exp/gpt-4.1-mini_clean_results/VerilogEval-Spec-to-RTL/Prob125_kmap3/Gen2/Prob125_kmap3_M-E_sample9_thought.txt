To provide a novel solution, let's explore a different approach by implementing the Karnaugh map directly using conditional logic reflecting the map structure. Instead of relying on algebraic simplification, we can write the output based on selected minterms.

Analyzing the K-map:

- When (c,d) = 00 and (a,b) = 10 or 11, out=1; also for (a,b)=01, out=d (don't-care), we can treat them as zero or one.

- When (c,d) = 11 or 10 and (a,b) = 01, 10, 11, out=1

- At other positions, out=0 or don't-care

Let's choose to set don't-care terms to 1 where it simplifies the expression.

With that, the output is 1 for these combinations of (a,b,c,d):

- c=0,d=0 and (a,b)=10 or 11 => c=0,d=0,a=1,b=0 or b=1

- c=1,d=1 or d=0 and (a,b) in 01,10,11

Thus, the logic can be written as:

out = (~c & ~d & a) | (c & (b | a))

But since d is don't-care, let's integrate it as a selector only where needed.

We can implement a 4-to-1 MUX with select lines c and d selecting the output for the corresponding (a,b) input.

Specifically:

- For (c,d)=00 => out = a & ~b (from map: 01=don't care, 00=0, 10=1, 11=1, so if a=1 and b=0 or 1, output is 1)

- For (c,d)=01 => out=0 (from map row 01: mostly 0)

- For (c,d)=11 => out=1 if (a,b) in 01,10,11, else 0; So if (a=1 or b=1), out=1

- For (c,d)=10 => out=1 if (a,b) in 01,10,11, else 0; same as above

This leads to the logic:

out = (~c & ~d & a & ~b) | (c & (a | b)) | (~c & d & 0) | (c & d & (a | b))

Since the map for (c,d)=01 is zero, no need to add logic for that.

The expression simplifies to:

out = (~c & ~d & a & ~b) | (c & (a | b))

To implement this, we will use AND, OR, and NOT gates reflecting the logic directly.

This is a different architectural approach: implementing minterms directly from K-map interpretation rather than algebraic minimal expression.

This approach:

- Handles don't-care d explicitly as a control signal.

- Reflects map rows as conditions.

- Could be useful if d control is used in further logic expansions.

- Provides an alternative way to map logic.

Hence, the module implements the logic:

out = (~c & ~d & a & ~b) | (c & (a | b))
