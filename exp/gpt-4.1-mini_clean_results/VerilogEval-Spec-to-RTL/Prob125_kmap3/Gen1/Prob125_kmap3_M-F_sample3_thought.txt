The previous simplification incorrectly incorporated the don't-care input 'd' as an actual controlling variable in the output expression, leading to mismatches.

Since 'd' is a don't-care input, it can be ignored or assigned in a way that simplifies the expression without causing output errors on the specified outputs.

The Karnaugh map can be redrawn with 'd' as don't-care (ignoring its effect), and only inputs a, b, and c considered for the expression.

From the map:

- For c=1 (regardless of d), output is 1 when (a,b) is not 01 (a=0,b=1), else 0. That is, for c=1, out=1 except when (a=0,b=1).
- For c=0, when d=0:
  - out=1 when a=1 and b=0 or b=1 (from positions (c,d,a,b)=(0,0,1,0),(0,0,1,1))
- For c=0, d=1, outputs are 0 or don't-care.

Considering 'd' as don't-care, we can choose to ignore it in logic.

An expression that matches all the 1's and zeros while treating 'd' as don't care is:

out = (c & (a | ~b)) | (a & ~c)

Explanation:

- (a & ~c): covers the rows where c=0 and a=1 (regardless of b), which are 1's at (c=0,d=0 or 1,a=1,b=0 or 1).
- (c & (a | ~b)): covers c=1 rows where output=1 except when a=0,b=1.

This expression is simpler and respects the don't-care conditions on d.
