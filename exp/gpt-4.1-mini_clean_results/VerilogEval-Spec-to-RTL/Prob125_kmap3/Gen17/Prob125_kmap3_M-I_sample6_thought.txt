The existing solution implements the logic as out = (~c & a) | (c & ~b) | (c & a). Notice that the last two terms share c as a common factor and can be combined: c & (~b | a). Thus, the expression simplifies to out = (~c & a) | (c & (~b | a)). This reduces the gate count and inverter usage by sharing the 'c' and the OR term (~b | a).

Implementing this factored form reduces the number of AND gates and inverters from 3 ANDs and 2 NOTs to 2 ANDs and 1 OR and 2 NOTs (same number of NOTs but less ANDs and OR gates total). Also, sharing common subexpressions reduces switching power and area.

Therefore, implement the output as:
  wire not_c = ~c;
  wire not_b = ~b;
  wire or_term = not_b | a;
  assign out = (not_c & a) | (c & or_term);

This is a more optimized and clean implementation that maintains correctness.
