The existing design is already minimal and cleanly implements the simplified Boolean expression derived from the Karnaugh map. It uses minimal gates and reuses complemented signals effectively. However, one minor optimization can be applied to reduce the number of inversions from two to one by factoring the expression differently.

Original: out = (~c & a) | (c & (~b | a))

Rewrite the expression to factor 'a':

out = a | (c & ~b & ~a)    // but since 'a' already covers 'a=1', the second term only applies when a=0.

Simplify carefully:

- out = a | (c & ~b & ~a)
- out = a | (c & ~b & ~a)
- Since (a | (c & ~b & ~a)) = a + c~b~a = a + c~b~a
- Because a + anything including ~a = a + c~b~a, this expression covers all required ones.

Thus, 'out' is true if 'a' is 1 or if c=1, b=0, and a=0.

This reduces the logic to:

out = a | (c & ~b & ~a)

We only need one inverter for b, and a few AND and OR gates.

Implementation:

- Generate not_b and not_a.
- Compute c_and_notb = c & not_b
- Then c_and_notb_and_nota = c_and_notb & not_a
- Finally, out = a | c_and_notb_and_nota

This reduces the inverter count from two to one and potentially reduces power by fewer inversions and simpler OR structure.

Overall, this expression is functionally equivalent and minimal, possibly better in gate-level PPA.

Note: 'd' remains don't-care and is ignored.
