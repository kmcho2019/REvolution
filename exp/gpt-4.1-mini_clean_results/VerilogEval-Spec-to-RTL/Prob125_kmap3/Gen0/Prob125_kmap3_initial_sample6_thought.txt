First, assign inputs to variables matching the Karnaugh map rows (cd) and columns (ab). The Karnaugh map has 4 variables (a,b,c,d), with d being don't care. The map is given with cd as row indices and ab as column indices in Gray code order: 01,00,10,11 for columns and 00,01,11,10 for rows. We map each cell value with d=don't care and assign either 0 or 1 to minimize the expression. Then, derive the simplified logic expression and implement it in Verilog.

Analyzing the K-map with given values (where d is don't care):

K-map (rows cd, cols ab):

        ab
cd   01  00  10  11
00 | d | 0 | 1 | 1 |
01 | 0 | 0 | d | d |
11 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
10 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 |

Converting columns and rows to variable assignments:

Columns (ab):
01 = a=0,b=1
00 = a=0,b=0
10 = a=1,b=0
11 = a=1,b=1

Rows (cd):
00 = c=0,d=0
01 = c=0,d=1
11 = c=1,d=1
10 = c=1,d=0

With d as don't care, we focus on essential 1s to get minimal SOP.

By inspection, from the rows with c=1 (11 and 10), for columns 00,10,11 the output is 1 regardless of b and d (since don't care). So, for c=1 and a=1 or b=1, output 1.

Also, for cd=00 row, columns 10 and 11 are 1, meaning when c=0,d=0,a=1,b=0 or 1. So, output is 1 when c=0,d=0,a=1.

Considering these, a possible simplified expression is:

out = (c & (a | b)) | (a & ~c & ~d)

Implement this logic in Verilog.
