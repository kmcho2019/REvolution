To optimize the existing 4-bit synchronous counter for improved PPA (performance, power, and area), I will leverage the property that a simple binary counter can be implemented using flip-flops with toggle enable signals derived from the lower bits. This is typically more power-efficient because higher bits only toggle when the lower bits roll over, reducing unnecessary switching activity. 

Specifically, I will replace the straightforward addition (q <= q + 1) with a ripple toggle approach where:
- q[0] toggles every clock cycle,
- q[1] toggles when q[0] transitions from 1 to 0,
- q[2] toggles when q[1] and q[0] are both about to rollover,
- q[3] toggles when q[2:0] roll over.

This reduces combinational logic complexity per clock cycle, minimizing the toggling on higher bits and potentially reducing power consumption. The synchronous active-high reset remains intact, setting the counter to zero on reset.

This approach should preserve performance and potentially reduce power and area due to simpler combinational logic and fewer toggling bits per cycle.
