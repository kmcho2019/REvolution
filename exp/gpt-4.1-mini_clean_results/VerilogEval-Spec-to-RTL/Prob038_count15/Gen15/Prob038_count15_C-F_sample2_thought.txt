To fuse the best ideas from both examples, I will keep the clear and explicit separation of combinational logic and sequential update from Example 1, which uses an always @(*) block to compute the next state, making the design easy to understand and modify. However, from Example 2, I adopt the simplicity of continuous assign statements for the increment logic, which can be synthesized efficiently and reduces the number of registers (no intermediate reg needed).

The fused strategy:
- Use continuous assignments for next_q bits as combinational logic (clear and concise).
- Use a single always @(posedge clk) block for synchronous reset and register update.
- Maintain ripple carry increment logic with XOR and AND gates for minimal delay and area.
- Ensure synchronous active-high reset sets counter to zero.
- Output q is registered as required.

This approach combines clear separation of logic, simple implementation, and leverages synthesis tools' ability to infer efficient hardware from continuous assignments, achieving slightly better performance and area than Example 1, while maintaining clear structure and minimal logic from Example 2.
