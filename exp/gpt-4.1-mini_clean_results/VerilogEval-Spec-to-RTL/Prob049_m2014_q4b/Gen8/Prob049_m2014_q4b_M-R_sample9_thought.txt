Refactor the asynchronous reset D flip-flop using a combinational reset signal driving the flip-flop enable. We use a two-process style: one combinational block to determine the next state based on reset and input, and a clocked always block to update q. This separates reset logic from data sampling and still models the asynchronous reset behavior by including reset in the sensitivity list for combinational logic and clocked always block. The reset asynchronously forces q to 0 by overriding the next state, making the design functionally equivalent but stylistically different.
