To implement a positive edge-triggered D flip-flop with asynchronous reset differently from the standard always block approach, I will architect the design using lower-level primitives to explicitly show the asynchronous reset logic separated from the D latch and clock gating. Specifically, I'll implement the flip-flop as a transparent latch followed by a clocked latch (master-slave style) with asynchronous reset applied to the latches.

This approach decomposes the flip-flop into two latches in series:
- A transparent latch enabled by clk (positive level)
- A latch clocked by inverted clk (the slave), which captures output of the master latch on clk falling edge

The asynchronous reset will asynchronously clear the output of the slave latch immediately when asserted.

This master-slave latch configuration replicates the D flip-flop behavior with asynchronous reset, but structurally is distinct from a single always block.

Advantages:
- Explicit separation of asynchronous reset logic and clocked data storage
- Illustrates internal latch-level design useful for certain synthesis or educational contexts
- Clearly demonstrates asynchronous reset overriding internal latch states

Disadvantage:
- Slightly increased area and complexity
- Potentially longer delay paths due to two latches

This architecture meets the specification and provides a novel structural design different from the canonical always @(posedge clk or posedge ar) block.
