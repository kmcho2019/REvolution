Simplify the design by aligning the shift register so that Q[0] maps to shift_reg[7] (MSB) and Q[7] maps to shift_reg[0] (LSB), shifting in S at shift_reg[7] on each enable clock edge. Instead of computing an inverse index, directly use the 3-bit input {A,B,C} as an index to select the output Z from the shift register bits, since ABC=0 selects Q[0] (shift_reg[7]) and ABC=7 selects Q[7] (shift_reg[0]). To do this, reverse the shift direction: shift left by one bit with S entering at MSB (shift_reg[7]) so that the indexing matches naturally without subtraction. This avoids extra arithmetic and makes the multiplexer selection direct and clearer.

The shift operation becomes shift_reg <= {shift_reg[6:0], S}; but since Q[0] is MSB, and we want it at shift_reg[7], shifting left means Q[0] moves left in the register. To get Q[0] at shift_reg[7], shifting left with S at LSB would be wrong. Instead, shift right and keep mapping Q[0] = shift_reg[7], and just use the inverse index as before but implement the multiplexer manually with a simple case to avoid variable part-select or arithmetic. This reduces complexity at the expense of an 8:1 mux written with case.

Alternatively, rename Q[0] to shift_reg[0] as MSB and shift left. Then output Z = shift_reg[{A,B,C}]. This is the simplest: shift left by 1 with S shifted into LSB (shift_reg[0]), Q[0]=shift_reg[0], Q[7]=shift_reg[7]. But problem requires Q[0] to be MSB, so keep Q[0]=shift_reg[7], shift right by 1 with S shifted into MSB (shift_reg[7]) and implement mux as case for Z with input ABC. This avoids subtraction and variable indexing.

Hence final simplified design:
- shift_reg[7] is Q[0] MSB, shift_reg[0] is Q[7] LSB.
- shift right by one on enable, shift in S at MSB (shift_reg[7]).
- output Z is selected by ABC via case statement, mapping ABC=0 to shift_reg[7], ABC=1 to shift_reg[6], ... ABC=7 to shift_reg[0].

This simplifies indexing logic and avoids arithmetic or variable part-select in output mux.
