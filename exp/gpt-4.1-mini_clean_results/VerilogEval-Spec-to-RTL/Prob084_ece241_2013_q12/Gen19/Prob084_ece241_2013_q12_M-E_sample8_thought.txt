Instead of implementing a linear shift register shifting from MSB to LSB, we can implement the 8-bit shift register as a circular shift register shifting the data left on each enable clock, shifting in the new bit S at LSB (shift_reg[0]). This means Q[0] corresponds directly to shift_reg[0], Q[1] to shift_reg[1], ..., Q[7] to shift_reg[7]. This direct mapping means the input ABC can be used directly as an index to select the output Z from the shift register without any bit reversal or subtraction.

This approach has a simple data flow: at each clock, if enable is high, the bits shift towards the higher index (left shift), and the new input bit S is inserted at the LSB. The outputs Q[0..7] are directly the shift_reg bits from 0 to 7 respectively.

The multiplexing for Z is a straightforward 8-to-1 MUX indexed by {A,B,C}, selecting shift_reg[{A,B,C}]. No extra reversal wire or subtraction needed. The circuit is cleaner, smaller in combinational logic, and the indexing is intuitive.

This differs architecturally from the original by changing the shift direction and mapping of bits to outputs, removing extra combinational logic and potentially improving timing and area.
