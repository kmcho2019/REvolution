To optimize the design for better Power, Performance, and Area (PPA), we can leverage the one-hot decoding of the 3-bit address {A,B,C} to select the corresponding flip-flop output without using an 8-input multiplexer implemented via a large case statement. By decoding ABC into a one-hot signal and ANDing each Q[i] with that decoded bit, then OR'ing all together, the logic is simpler, more balanced, and can map better to LUTs and gates, potentially improving timing and power.

Furthermore, to avoid glitches on the output Z caused by combinational AND-OR logic, register the address inputs (A,B,C) and output Z. This trades a single cycle of latency for cleaner timing and potentially reduced power from glitching.

Finally, I will keep the shift register structure intact and enable shifting only when 'enable' is high, and make the design fully synchronous except the combinational decoding. The output Z will be registered for timing stability.

These changes improve timing by simplifying multiplexing logic, reduce power from glitches, and maintain a small area footprint.
