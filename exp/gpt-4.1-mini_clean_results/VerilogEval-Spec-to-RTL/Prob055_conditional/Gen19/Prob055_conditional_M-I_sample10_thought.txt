The current solution uses two min2 module instantiations and a procedural block for the final comparison, which is clear and modular but could be optimized slightly for area and potentially power by removing the module instantiations and expressing the entire logic combinationally with continuous assignments. 

My improvement strategy:
- Inline the min2 functionality using simple conditional operators instead of separate module instantiations.
- Use purely combinational continuous assignments without any registers or always blocks, making the logic flatter and easier for synthesis to optimize and potentially share logic.
- This removes module instantiation overhead and might reduce the area and power slightly by synthesizing fewer separate comparators and muxes.
- The balanced binary-tree comparison remains, preserving the minimal logic depth and critical path.
- The final output is driven by a wire, not a reg, since no sequential logic is needed, which suits the purely combinational function.

This yields a simpler, flatter combinational circuit that synthesis tools can optimize well and reduces module instantiation overhead, potentially improving area and power while maintaining performance.
