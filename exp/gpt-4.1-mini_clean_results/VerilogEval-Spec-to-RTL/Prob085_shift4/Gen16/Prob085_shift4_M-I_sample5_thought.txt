To optimize the existing solution for better performance, power, and area (PPA), the key is to minimize the combinational logic complexity and switching activity. The original design uses a combinational always block generating a next state that depends on the current state `q` and inputs `load` and `ena`. Although this is clean and synthesizable, we can slightly improve by directly updating `q` inside the sequential block, removing the intermediate `next_q` signal and associated combinational logic block. 

This reduces combinational logic by merging it into the sequential block, which can reduce setup time and power due to fewer transitions in the combinational path.

Additionally, replacing explicit bit slicing with the right shift operator (`>>`) for shifting can help synthesis tools optimize area and performance better.

Clock gating is generally not handled here as it is typically done at a higher level or by synthesis tools; the enable signal effectively controls register updates, reducing switching when idle.

Thus, the improved design merges the combinational next-state logic into the sequential block, prioritizes load over enable, and uses the shift operator to simplify shifting logic.

This should slightly reduce logic levels, power, and area, and marginally improve timing while maintaining functionality and asynchronous reset behavior.
