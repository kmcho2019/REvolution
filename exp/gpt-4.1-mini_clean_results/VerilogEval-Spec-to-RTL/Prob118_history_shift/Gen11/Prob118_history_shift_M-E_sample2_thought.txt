To provide a different architectural approach, I will implement the 32-bit global history register as a shift register built from a 32-element array of flip-flops, each holding one bit of the history. Instead of using a single concatenation with shifting, I will explicitly shift bits through the array on each prediction valid update. When a misprediction occurs, I will overwrite the entire history register with train_history concatenated with train_taken. This explicit bitwise shifting and loading approach is more structural and can aid in pipelined or fine-grained control flows and potentially better map to FPGA or ASIC registers. The asynchronous reset will clear all bits in the array. This design also clearly prioritizes misprediction over prediction by checking train_mispredicted first.
