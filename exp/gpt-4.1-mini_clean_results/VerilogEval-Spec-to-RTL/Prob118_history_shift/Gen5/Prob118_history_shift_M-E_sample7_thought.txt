Implement the global branch history register as two separate registers for better modularity and simpler update logic:

1. Maintain a 31-bit register to hold the older history bits (history[31:1]) and a 1-bit register for the youngest bit (history[0]).

2. On predict_valid, shift the entire history right by one bit, placing predict_taken into the youngest bit.

3. On train_mispredicted, directly load the full 32-bit history formed by concatenating train_history[31:1] and train_taken as the youngest bit.

4. The priority is misprediction update over prediction update.

5. Asynchronous reset resets both registers to zero.

6. predict_history is continuously output by concatenating the two registers.

Splitting the history into two registers clarifies control logic and might facilitate better synthesis optimization, reducing combinational muxing and shift logic within a single vector. It also cleanly separates the youngest bit from the rest, matching the problem's focus on the LSB as the newest branch.

All sequential logic is triggered on the clock's positive edge or asynchronously reset on areset.
