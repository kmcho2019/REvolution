Instead of implementing a traditional shift register, I will implement the 32-bit history as a circular buffer with a pointer indicating the position of the youngest branch bit. On each valid prediction, the pointer increments (wrapping around 32) and the new prediction bit is stored at the pointer position. On a misprediction, the history is restored by writing the 33-bit concatenation of {train_history, train_taken} into the buffer and resetting the pointer to 31 (pointing to the youngest bit). This approach turns the shift operation into a pointer increment and selective memory writes, potentially improving timing since it avoids wide shifting of the entire register. The output is assembled by reading bits starting from the pointer backward (wrapping around) so that predict_history[0] is the youngest bit at the pointer. Asynchronous reset clears the buffer and resets the pointer to zero. This circular buffer approach decouples shifting from physical bit movement, which can reduce routing and improve performance.
