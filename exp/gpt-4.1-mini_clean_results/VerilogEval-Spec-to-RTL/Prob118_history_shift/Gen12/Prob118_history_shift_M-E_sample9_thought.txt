Instead of a single 32-bit shift register that directly shifts in new bits each clock, I will implement the global history as two smaller 16-bit shift registers, combining their outputs to form the 32-bit predict_history. Each 16-bit shift register will be updated separately to reduce the combinational path length of shifting 32 bits at once. On a prediction update, the lower 16-bit shift register will shift in the new bit and the upper 16-bit register will shift in the LSB of the lower register from the previous cycle. On misprediction, I will load both 16-bit registers from train_history and train_taken accordingly. This "split shift register" approach can reduce shift logic fanout and improve timing by parallelizing shift operations. The module still asynchronously resets both shift registers to zero. This also ensures the misprediction update takes priority over prediction update.
