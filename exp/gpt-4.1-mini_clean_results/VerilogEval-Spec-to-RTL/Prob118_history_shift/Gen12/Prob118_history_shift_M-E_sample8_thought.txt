To take a different architectural approach, I will implement the 32-bit global history as a register updated by a small finite state machine (FSM) to explicitly handle the two update cases: prediction shift-in and misprediction reload. The FSM will have two states: IDLE and UPDATE. On each clock, if a misprediction occurs, it immediately loads the new history value composed from train_history concatenated with train_taken. Otherwise, if a prediction is valid and no misprediction, it shifts in the predicted bit by concatenating. The FSM separates update control from data logic and enforces priority for misprediction updates over prediction updates naturally by state transitions. This also allows easier insertion of future pipeline controls or stalls. Asynchronous reset clears the history and returns FSM to IDLE. The shift uses vector concatenation to minimize combinational logic and maximize efficiency.
