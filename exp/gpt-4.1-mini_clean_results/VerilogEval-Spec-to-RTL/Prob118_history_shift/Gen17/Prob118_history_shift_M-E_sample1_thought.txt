I will implement the 32-bit global history as a circular shift register with a pointer indicating the current youngest bit position. Instead of physically shifting the entire 32-bit register every update, I will update only the bit at the pointer and advance the pointer. This reduces the shifting complexity and may improve timing and power by eliminating a large barrel shifter or wide shift logic. On a predict_valid, the pointer advances by 1 and the bit at that position is updated with predict_taken. On a misprediction, the entire history is replaced by train_history concatenated with train_taken, and the pointer resets to zero (meaning the youngest bit is at position zero). The predict_history output will be a rotated version of the stored register aligned so that index 0 corresponds to the youngest branch. The asynchronous reset clears the register and pointer. This pointer-based circular buffer approach maintains correct history ordering with simple logic, offers a novel perspective different from a conventional shift register, and optimizes the critical path by avoiding wide shifts.
