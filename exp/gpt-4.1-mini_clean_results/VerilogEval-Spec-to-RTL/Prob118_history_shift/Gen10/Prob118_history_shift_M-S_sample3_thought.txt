Use a 32-bit shift register to directly represent the global history, where bit 0 is the most recent branch outcome. On a misprediction, load the shift register with {train_taken, train_history[31:1]}, effectively restoring the history with the actual branch outcome at the youngest bit and the older bits shifted right. On prediction, shift right by one and insert predict_taken at bit 0. This keeps the history register 32-bit wide and simplifies the rollback logic by adjusting the history bits accordingly. Asynchronous reset clears the history to zero. Misprediction update takes precedence over prediction.
