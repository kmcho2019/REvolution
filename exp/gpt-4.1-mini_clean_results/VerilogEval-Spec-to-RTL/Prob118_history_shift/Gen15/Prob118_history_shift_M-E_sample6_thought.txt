Instead of using a single 32-bit shift register updated every cycle, I will implement the global history as a 32-bit register and explicitly shift its contents on each clock cycle when predict_valid is high. To handle misprediction rollback, I will include a separate rollback register that directly captures the concatenation of train_history and train_taken and loads it into the history register on misprediction. This approach makes the state update logic more modular and separates rollback loading from normal shifting, avoiding conditional concatenation in a single expression. The priority is preserved by using if-else in the sequential block, with asynchronous reset clearing both registers. This architectural style emphasizes clear sequential operations, separating normal prediction update and rollback handling explicitly.
