Use a 32-bit register to hold the global history. On the positive edge of clk or areset, asynchronously reset history to zero if areset is asserted. On a misprediction (train_mispredicted=1), directly assign the updated history by concatenating the upper 31 bits of train_history and train_taken as the LSB (since train_taken is the newest branch result). Otherwise, if predict_valid=1 and no misprediction, shift the current history to the right by one and insert predict_taken at the MSB side. This differs from the first solution by shifting towards MSB and treating predict_history[0] as the youngest branch at LSB, so after the shift, the youngest branch remains at LSB. Output the register as predict_history. Misprediction update takes priority over prediction. This approach ensures clear prioritization and a simple direction of shifting, potentially improving clarity and synthesis results.
