Fuse the two solutions by implementing a 32-bit global history shift register with an asynchronous positive reset. Use non-blocking assignments to update the register on the positive clock edge or areset. Prioritize the misprediction update to load {train_history[30:0], train_taken} because this explicitly matches the 32-bit width and ensures the bit positions align correctly. When a prediction is valid (and no misprediction), shift left by one inserting predict_taken at the LSB side. To reduce switching and save power, gate the updates with an enable signal that is high only when either train_mispredicted or predict_valid is asserted, so the register does not toggle unnecessarily on cycles without updates. This avoids unnecessary shifting and loading and reduces power. The output predict_history is registered to hold the current state.

This design blends the explicit slicing from example 2 for clarity and correctness, the shift register structure of example 1, and adds clock enable gating to reduce switching power. It keeps the combinational logic minimal and clean for good timing and area. Using a clock enable in FPGA or ASIC synthesis typically results in gated clock logic or an equivalent enable signal. This offers better PPA compared to always updating on every clock cycle with multiplexers.
