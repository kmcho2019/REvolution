Instead of using a single 32-bit shift register, implement the global history register as a circular buffer with a 5-bit write pointer that tracks the insertion index. When a prediction occurs, write predict_taken at the current pointer and advance it modulo 32. On a misprediction, reload the history register directly with train_history plus train_taken at the last position and reset the pointer accordingly. This eliminates continuous shifting, reducing switching activity and potentially improving timing by avoiding a full 32-bit shift on each update. The pointer indicates the youngest branch's position, and the predict_history output is rearranged so that predict_history[0] is always the youngest branch by re-indexing accordingly. Asynchronous reset clears the entire register and pointer. This novel circular buffer approach can optimize timing and power by replacing wide shift operations with indexed writes.
