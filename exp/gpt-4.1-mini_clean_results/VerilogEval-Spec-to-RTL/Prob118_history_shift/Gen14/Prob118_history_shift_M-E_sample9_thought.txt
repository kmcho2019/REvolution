I will implement the 32-bit global history shift register with a design that uses a single always block triggered on the positive clock edge and asynchronous reset, but without a separate combinational next-state logic or explicit update enable signal. 

Instead, the logic will be prioritized and incorporated directly inside the sequential always block: on each clock edge, if a misprediction occurs, load the shifted train_history and train_taken; else if predict_valid is asserted, shift in predict_taken; else retain the current state.

This simplifies the design, avoids the 32-bit equality comparison required for gating updates, potentially reducing critical path and area.

The asynchronous reset will clear the history to zero. The output simply reflects the current history.

This approach is straightforward, with good clarity, and avoids redundant combinational logic, relying on straightforward sequential updates with clear priority, which is often favored for better timing and simpler synthesis.
