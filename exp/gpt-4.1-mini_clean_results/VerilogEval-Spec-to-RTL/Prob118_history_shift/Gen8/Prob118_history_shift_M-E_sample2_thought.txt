Implement a single 33-bit shift register where the top bit represents the branch outcome of the oldest branch in history, and the bottom 32 bits represent the 32-bit global branch history. On a prediction (predict_valid), shift left by one, inserting predict_taken at bit 0. On misprediction (train_mispredicted), load the 33-bit register with {train_history, train_taken} directly, thus restoring history including the actual branch result. The 32 least significant bits of this register form predict_history. Use asynchronous reset to clear the entire shift register to zero. This approach simplifies rollback by incorporating the actual branch bit directly into the shift register, removing the need for separate pre-prediction buffering, and uses a wider register to streamline concatenation and state updates.
