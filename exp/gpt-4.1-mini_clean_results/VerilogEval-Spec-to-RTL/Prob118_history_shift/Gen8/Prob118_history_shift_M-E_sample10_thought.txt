Implement the 32-bit global history using a circular shift register approach with an explicit index pointer indicating the youngest branch bit position (LSB). The history is stored in a 32-bit register, but instead of shifting bits every cycle, we rotate the view logically by updating the pointer. On a prediction, we update the bit at the current pointer, then increment the pointer to point to the next youngest bit, effectively achieving a shift-in without physically shifting the entire register. On a misprediction, we reload the history directly, resetting the pointer to zero, since the corrected history represents the true order. This approach reduces the shifting logic and leverages bit-masking and pointer arithmetic for history management, potentially improving timing and power by avoiding a full 32-bit shift every cycle.
