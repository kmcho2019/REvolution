I will implement the 32-bit global history register as a shift register updated on each positive clock edge, with asynchronous reset. To handle the rollback on misprediction, I will maintain two separate registers: the main history register (predict_history) and a shadow register holding the last stable history state before the current predicted branch. On each prediction (predict_valid), I shift in predict_taken into the main history and save the previous main history into the shadow register. If a misprediction occurs (train_mispredicted), I restore the history by loading {train_history[30:0], train_taken} into both the main and shadow registers, effectively rolling back the prediction pipeline state. The priority of misprediction over prediction is naturally handled by conditional if-else logic. This architecture simplifies rollback by having a dedicated shadow register to hold the previous state and allows clean pipeline flush recovery without complicated combinational muxing inside the shift register.
