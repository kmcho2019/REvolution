To improve the PPA of the existing design, I will leverage a built-in shift register primitive if available (e.g., a dedicated shift register or FPGA SRL primitives) to reduce combinational delay and area from manual concatenation. Since a direct primitive may not be universally portable in Verilog, I'll implement a register with a mux selecting the update source to avoid wide concatenation logic in a single cycle, which can improve timing.

Also, I will use clock gating to reduce power by only updating the register when an update event (predict_valid or train_mispredicted) occurs. To safely implement clock gating with asynchronous reset, I will create an enable signal and use an enable register with a simple clock gating style.

This approach reduces switching activity and combinational logic in the critical path, improving timing and power without increasing area significantly.

The asynchronous reset remains positive edge triggered to meet the spec.

Summary of changes:
- Use a registered enable signal to gate updates and reduce unnecessary toggling.
- Use a mux to select the new value to load (mispredicted vs predict update) instead of concatenation in the sequential always block.
- Keep asynchronous reset as is.
