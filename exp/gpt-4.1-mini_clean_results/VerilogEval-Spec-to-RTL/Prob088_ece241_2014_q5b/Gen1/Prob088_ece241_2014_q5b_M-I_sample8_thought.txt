The original design uses one-hot encoding with 2 bits for two states, which is functionally correct but can be optimized in area and power by using a binary encoding (1 bit) since there are only two states. This reduces flip-flop count from 2 to 1 and the associated combinational logic complexity, improving area and power.

Additionally, keeping the asynchronous active-high reset as is maintains functional correctness. The output logic is simple and Mealy-style, dependent on both state and input, which we preserve.

Therefore, the improvement strategy is:
- Change state encoding from one-hot (2 bits) to binary (1 bit).
- Maintain asynchronous active-high reset.
- Keep output combinational as Mealy FSM.
- This reduces state register size, potentially reduces toggling and critical path, improving timing, power, and area.

This approach balances correctness and PPA improvements while keeping the FSM logic clear and simple.
