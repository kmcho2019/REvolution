Since the design only has two states and already uses one-hot encoding minimally (two flip-flops), the biggest lever for optimization is to simplify state encoding further while preserving correctness. Changing to binary encoding reduces the number of flip-flops from 2 to 1, decreasing area and power and potentially improving timing by reducing register-to-register delay.

We keep the asynchronous active-high reset and Mealy output logic intact. The output logic is simple and is optimized with minimal logic operations.

Additionally, to optimize combinational logic for synthesis and clarity, we use simple ternary operations and avoid case statements where possible.

This reduces state register area and switching activity, simplifies next state and output logic, and leads to improved PPA metrics while preserving exact functionality.
