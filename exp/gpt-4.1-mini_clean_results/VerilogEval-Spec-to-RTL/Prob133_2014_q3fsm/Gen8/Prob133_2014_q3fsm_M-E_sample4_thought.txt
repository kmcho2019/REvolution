Instead of using explicit states A and B, we implement a simple FSM with three main logical states encoded as a 2-bit state machine: IDLE (waiting for s=1), COUNTING (counting w inputs for 3 cycles), and OUTPUT (asserting z based on the count). We use a shift register to store the last three w values, enabling a sliding window to determine if exactly two of them are 1. After receiving three samples, we check the count of 1's using a popcount function, then assert z for one cycle before restarting counting. This approach uses a small state machine but leverages a shift register and combinational popcount to simplify output logic and avoid maintaining explicit counters for number of ones. It separates counting of cycles and w storage cleanly and keeps logic modular.
