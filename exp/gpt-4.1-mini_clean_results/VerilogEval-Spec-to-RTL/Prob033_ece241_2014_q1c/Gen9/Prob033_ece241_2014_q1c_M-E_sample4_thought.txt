I will implement the 8-bit adder using a modular hierarchical structure that splits the inputs into two 4-bit blocks. Each block will use a simple 4-bit ripple carry adder internally, and then the carries between blocks will be handled at the block level to reduce complexity and provide a balanced tradeoff between speed and structural clarity.

Specifically:
1. Create a reusable 4-bit ripple carry adder module that computes sum and carry out.
2. Instantiate two of these 4-bit adders in the top module for the lower and upper halves.
3. Connect the carry-out of the lower 4-bit adder as the carry-in of the upper 4-bit adder.
4. Calculate the final 8-bit sum by concatenating the sums of each 4-bit adder.
5. Detect overflow by checking the carry into and carry out of the most significant bit, using the fact that overflow in signed addition is when the sign of the inputs are the same but differ from the sum sign; equivalently, overflow = carry into MSB XOR carry out of MSB.

This hierarchical approach improves code reuse, simplifies debugging, and balances performance by reducing the ripple length to 4 bits per block rather than 8. It is different from the previous carry-lookahead approach but still efficient for an 8-bit adder.
