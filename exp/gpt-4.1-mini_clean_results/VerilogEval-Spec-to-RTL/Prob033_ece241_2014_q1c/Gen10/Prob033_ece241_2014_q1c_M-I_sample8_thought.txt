The current ripple-carry adder design is clean and area-efficient but has linear carry propagation delay and moderately higher dynamic power due to carry chain switching. To optimize PPA, a carry-select adder structure can be used. Carry-select breaks the addition into two halves (lower 4 bits and upper 4 bits). It computes the upper 4-bit sums and carries assuming carry-in = 0 and 1 in parallel, then selects the correct sum and carry based on the actual carry-out from the lower half.

This reduces carry propagation delay from 8 stages to roughly 4 + 1 stages, improving performance. Although it uses some redundant logic, the increase in area is minimal due to small 4-bit blocks and selective multiplexers. Power may improve since carry signals do not ripple through all 8 bits but select between precomputed results, reducing switching activity in critical carry chain. 

Overflow logic stays the same: XOR of carry into and out of MSB.

Key points:
- Use two 4-bit ripple carry adders for lower and upper halves.
- For upper half, compute sums and carry assuming carry-in=0 and carry-in=1.
- Select correct upper sum and carry based on carry out from lower half.
- Retain simple ripple-carry within 4-bit blocks.
- Generate output sum and overflow as before.

This yields a balanced tradeoff, improving timing significantly, with a small area overhead and likely lower dynamic power on carry signals.
