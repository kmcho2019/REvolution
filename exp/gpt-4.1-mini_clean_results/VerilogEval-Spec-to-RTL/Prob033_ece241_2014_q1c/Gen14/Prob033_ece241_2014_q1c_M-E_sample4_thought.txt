I will design a simple, flat 8-bit signed adder using behavioral Verilog to sum the inputs `a` and `b` as signed values and assign the result to `s`. Overflow detection in 2's complement addition can be done by checking if the sign of the sum is incorrect relative to the operands' signs: overflow occurs if the sign bits of `a` and `b` are the same but differ from the sign bit of `s`.

This approach uses concise Verilog behavioral constructs without explicit carry chains or complex modular adders. It relies on Verilog's signed arithmetic, enabling clear, efficient code with minimal logic, reducing area and power compared to structural carry-based implementations.

This design yields:

- A straightforward implementation leveraging Verilog's signed arithmetic.

- Simple and fast overflow detection by checking the sign relationships.

- Minimal structural logic and routing for improved power and area efficiency.

- Clear and maintainable code without the need for intermediate carry wires.

This contrasts with previous hierarchical or ripple-carry adder designs by directly treating the operands as signed numbers and focusing on sign-bit logic for overflow.
