The original design is functionally correct and uses carry-lookahead logic for better performance than ripple carry. However, it still implements the carry signals sequentially in a generate loop with a linear chain of assignments, which is essentially ripple carry in logic, only more readable. Although it computes carries with propagate and generate signals, it does not implement a true hierarchical or parallel carry-lookahead structure that can reduce delay by computing carries in parallel groups.

For 8-bit addition, a fully hierarchical carry-lookahead might be overkill, but a small improvement can be made by computing group propagate and generate signals for the lower 4 bits and upper 4 bits, and then combining those for the final carries. This reduces the carry chain delay from 8 to 2 stages.

Also, using a classic carry-lookahead approach for groups will slightly reduce critical path delay, and marginally reduce switching power as carry signals propagate faster and do not toggle unnecessarily.

Area impact should be small, as it mainly adds a few additional gates for group signals.

Summary of improvement strategy:
- Compute bit-level propagate and generate signals.
- Compute group propagate and generate signals for the lower and upper nibble.
- Use group signals to compute carry into upper nibble faster.
- Compute final carries for each bit using the hierarchical carry-lookahead formulas.
- Keep sum and overflow logic the same.
This hierarchical carry-lookahead reduces carry propagation delay and can marginally improve power by limiting the carry chain length.

This approach trades slightly increased area for better timing and power, improving the PPA.

We implement this hierarchical carry-lookahead in pure combinational Verilog using assign statements and generate loops.
