Use a parallel adder based on the carry-select adder (CSLA) technique for the 8-bit addition. Split the 8-bit inputs into two 4-bit groups: lower and upper. 

1. The lower 4 bits are added with a normal ripple-carry adder, starting with carry-in = 0.
2. The upper 4 bits are added twice in parallel: once assuming carry-in = 0, and once assuming carry-in = 1.
3. After the lower group sum and carry-out are computed, the correct upper sum and carry-out are selected based on the lower carry-out.

This approach reduces the carry propagation delay compared to a ripple-carry adder over 8 bits because the upper bits are precomputed for both carry possibilities, thus "selecting" the result based on the actual carry-in from the lower bits.

For overflow detection in 2's complement addition, use the standard overflow logic: overflow occurs if the carry into the MSB and the carry out of the MSB differ. The carry into MSB is the carry into bit 7, which is the carry out of bit 6 in the upper group. We can get these signals from the selected upper sum.

This approach provides a balanced tradeoff between complexity and speed, speeding addition compared to pure ripple carry but simpler than full carry lookahead logic. It also clearly separates concerns using two ripple-carry adders and multiplexers.

The module will instantiate a 4-bit ripple carry adder as a reusable submodule.
