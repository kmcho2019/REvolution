The waveform shows q = 1 whenever either b=1 or c=1, and q = 0 otherwise. The inputs a and d do not affect the output.

Both examples implement q = b | c but use additional gates or a mux unnecessarily, which increases area and power without performance benefit.

The best approach fuses both by:
- Directly assigning q = b | c, eliminating redundant modules.
- Keeping a clean, simple, single line combinational logic.
- This reduces area, power, and simplifies timing without losing clarity or modularity.

Thus, the fused design is a straightforward assign statement implementing the exact function derived from the waveform: q = b | c.
