The given solution is correct but uses a case statement with an output reg, which can lead to slightly less optimal hardware due to inferred multiplexers. Since q is a purely combinational output determined by inputs a, b, and c, and d is unused, it can be simplified to a straightforward boolean expression to minimize area and power. The truth table indicates q = (b OR c), regardless of a and d, except for the case a=0, b=0, c=0 (q=0), and a=1, b=0, c=0 (q=0). Both correspond exactly to q = b OR c.

Hence, the simplest and lowest power implementation is to assign q as a continuous assignment: q = b | c. This reduces gate count, power, and complexity.

Also, declaring q as an output wire (default for assign) instead of reg allows direct combinational logic inference without flip-flops or latches.

This optimization improves area, power, and simplifies the design without changing functionality.
