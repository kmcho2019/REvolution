The original design is clean and functionally optimal for a simple decade counter. To optimize for better power and possibly reduce area slightly, I will introduce a clock enable (ce) signal inside the always block that disables counting when the counter reaches 9, so the counter resets to 0 on the next cycle instead of incrementing further. This saves toggling bits on cycles when the count wraps around.

While clock gating is the best power optimization, it depends on external control signals and is environment specific. Here, an internal clock enable avoids unnecessary increment operations on the wrap-around cycle, potentially reducing switching.

To optimize timing (reduce critical path), the comparison remains but is done once per cycle; keeping the comparison as q == 4'd9 is straightforward and minimal.

This modification will keep the counter logic simple, synchronous, and synchronous reset intact.

Overall:
- Introduce internal clock enable logic to reduce toggling at wrap-around.
- Maintain synchronous reset.
- Keep single always block and non-blocking assignments.
