I will fuse the strengths of both examples by implementing a clean, minimalistic 4-bit binary decade counter with synchronous active-high reset. The counter increments on each positive clock edge and rolls over to 0 after 9. 

To optimize power and avoid unnecessary toggling, I will implement a clock enable (ce) signal that disables counting when reset is asserted or when the counter has reached 9, preventing any counting beyond that point before rollover. This ce gating is synthesized as simple combinational logic and avoids complex condition chains in the always block, improving clarity and slightly reducing switching activity.

The always block handles synchronous reset and counting only when enabled, avoiding redundant conditions and multiple comparisons. This approach balances simplicity, readability, power awareness, and minimal logic.

Summary of the fusion strategy:
- Use a single clock enable signal that is active only when not in reset and counter is less than 9.
- Use a synchronous reset inside the always block.
- On counter equal to 9 (when ce=0), load 0 in the next cycle.
- The always block only has reset and ce conditions, avoiding an explicit check for q==9.
- This reduces combinational complexity and switching.
- The design is easy to synthesize, minimal in area, and optimized for low power.

This implementation fuses the clean synchronous reset and modular counting with effective clock enable gating, as seen in both examples, for a superior design.
