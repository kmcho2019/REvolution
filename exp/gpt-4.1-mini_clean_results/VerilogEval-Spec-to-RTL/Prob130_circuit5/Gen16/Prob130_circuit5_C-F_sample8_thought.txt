To fuse the best ideas of both examples, I will implement a clean, single case statement multiplexer to minimize logic depth and area, improving timing and area metrics. Instead of multiple one-hot decodes and OR gates, I use a direct case over c for clear selection, which synthesis tools can optimize into an efficient MUX.

To reduce dynamic power caused by switching on all inputs simultaneously, I will still gate the selected input only by enabling its path within the case statement and assign the output combinationally. This approach avoids redundant switching on other inputs.

For c > 3, output is forced to constant 4'hF.

This combined approach achieves:
- Minimal logic depth and gate count (improving area and timing),
- Reduced switching activity by selecting and driving only the required input (improving power),
- Clarity and ease of maintenance by straightforward case logic.

Thus, the design balances performance, power, and area (PPA) effectively by combining the simplicity and efficiency of a multiplexing case statement with selective gating to limit input toggling.
