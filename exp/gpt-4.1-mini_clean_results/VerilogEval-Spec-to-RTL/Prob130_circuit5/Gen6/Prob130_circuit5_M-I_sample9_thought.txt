The original design correctly selects q based on c, but it uses multiplication and shifting on a wide concatenation, which can increase area and power. To optimize, I will replace the multiplication by 4 with an explicit shift-left operation (idx << 2), which synthesis tools recognize more efficiently as a hardware shift. Also, instead of concatenating a wide vector, I will implement q using a simple combinational case statement keyed by c. This approach generates a small mux with 5 input options and a default, which typically results in smaller area, lower power, and better timing. Since c is 4 bits, for c >= 5, output q should be 4'hF as per the waveform. This reduces complexity and switching activity compared to the wide shift method.
