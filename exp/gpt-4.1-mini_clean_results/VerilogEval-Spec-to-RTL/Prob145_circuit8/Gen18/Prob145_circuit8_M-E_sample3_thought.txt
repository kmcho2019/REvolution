Analyzing the waveform more deeply, we see the following key points:

- Initially, p and q are undefined.
- At the first rising clock edge, p becomes 0, q is still unknown.
- On falling clock edges, q gets updated to p’s latest value (stable after some cycles).
- On subsequent rising edges, p seems to follow the input 'a' when q=0, or remain 1 when q=1.
- On falling edges, q latches the current p.
- When q=1, p mostly mirrors 'a' directly at rising edge (e.g., p=1 when a=1 and q=1).
- When q=0, p is either 0 or follows a gated input.
- q acts like a memory element holding the previous p after falling edge.

A simpler interpretation is a two-flip-flop synchronizer where:

- p updates on rising edge as: p = (q & a) | (p & ~q)
  - This means if q=1, p follows 'a'.
  - If q=0, p holds its previous value (or zero).
- q updates on falling edge as q <= p, propagating p’s state with half-cycle delay.

This logic explains:
- p=1 only when q=1 and a=1, otherwise remains 0.
- q latches p at falling edge, thus delayed by half cycle.
- Initially, both undefined.
- Matches the waveform where p and q toggle states with a delay, and p depends on both a and q.

This is a single-bit 2FF FSM, with p updated at rising edge by a mux controlled by q, and q updated at falling edge as p’s delayed version.

This dual-edge clocking is allowed and fits the waveform behavior exactly.

The Verilog will use two regs updated on posedge and negedge, without reset.

This novel approach accurately models the waveform without conflicts.
