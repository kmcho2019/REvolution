The waveform indicates that p and q behave as a two-bit shift register with feedback from input 'a' and the current state.

- The output 'p' updates on the rising clock edge and depends on input 'a' and current q.
- The output 'q' updates to the old p value on the same rising clock edge.

To capture this behavior correctly, I will:

1. Use two internal registers p_reg and q_reg to hold the current state.
2. Compute next state values p_next and q_next combinationally:
   - p_next = a | q_reg
   - q_next = p_reg
3. Update p_reg and q_reg synchronously on the posedge of clock with nonblocking assignments.
4. Drive outputs p and q from p_reg and q_reg respectively.

This way, the state updates correctly reflect the delay and dependence as per the waveform, and the sequential logic is clear and matches expectations.

No reset is needed as initial 'x' states in simulation are acceptable.

This fully utilizes the next-state registers and aligns with proper sequential design style.
