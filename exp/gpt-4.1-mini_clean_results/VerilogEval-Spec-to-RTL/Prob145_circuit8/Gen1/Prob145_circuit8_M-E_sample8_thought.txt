The waveform shows complex behavior where outputs p and q do not simply track a on clock edges or shift directly between p and q. Instead, q changes mostly on falling edges and stays at 1 for some time, while p changes mostly on rising edges, sometimes holding its value or resetting based on a.

A plausible interpretation:
- On the rising edge of clock, p updates based on input a and possibly the current state of q.
- On the falling edge of clock, q updates based on the current state of p and/or a.
- Both p and q seem to form a two-bit state machine whose next state depends on input a.
- The initial values are undefined, then both become 0 at early clock cycles.
- When a=1 during rising edges, p sets to 1.
- q sets to 1 at falling edges only if p is 1.
- If a=0 at rising edge and q=1, p resets to 0.
- At some point (around 175ns), both reset to 0, probably triggered by a=1 on falling edge or by a reset condition implied by inputs.

To capture this, implement a two-process state machine with p and q as state bits. On rising edge, update p based on a and q; on falling edge, update q based on p. For example:

- posedge clock: p <= a | (p & ~a & ~q); // p is set by a or hold/reset logic
- negedge clock: q <= p;                // q follows p at falling edge

This matches the observed behavior that q lags p by half a clock and stays high while p is high.
