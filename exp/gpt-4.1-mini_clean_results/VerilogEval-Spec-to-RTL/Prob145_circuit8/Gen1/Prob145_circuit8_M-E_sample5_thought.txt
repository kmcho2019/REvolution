From the waveform, the outputs p and q depend on both the clock and input a with a sequential relationship:

- At initial times, p and q are unknown, indicating reset is implied at start.
- When clock=1, p tracks the input 'a'.
- On the falling edge of the clock (clock=1->0 transition), q captures the last p value.
- When clock=0, p outputs q’s stored value.
- This behavior means p acts as a multiplexer output selecting between a (when clock=1) and q (when clock=0).
- q is a register updated at the falling edge of clock to capture p.
- On reset (before first clock edge), p and q can be initialized to zero.

Implementing this:

- Use synchronous reset at start to set p=0 and q=0.
- Combinationally set p = (clock) ? a : q;
- On negedge clock, update q <= p;

This creates a data path where p is input-driven on clock high and output-driven by stored q on clock low. q holds the last sampled p at clock falling edges, capturing the data stable during clock=1.

This matches the waveforms where p and q values follow the input a when clock is high, and p mirrors q when clock is low, with q updating on falling edges.

This approach avoids combinational loops and respects the sequential behavior implied by the waveform.
