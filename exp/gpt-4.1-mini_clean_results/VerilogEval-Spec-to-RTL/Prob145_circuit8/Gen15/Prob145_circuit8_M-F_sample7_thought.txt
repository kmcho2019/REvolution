The previous design implemented 'p' and 'q' as a simple two-stage shift register with p <= a | q and q <= p, which does not match the waveform behavior. The waveform shows that 'q' changes only at certain times and that 'p' depends not only on 'a' and 'q' but also on prior states.

Careful analysis suggests 'q' is a state bit updated on the falling edge of the clock, and 'p' updates on the rising edge depending on 'a' and 'q'. Specifically:

- At rising edge of clock: p <= a & ~q; (p is asserted when 'a' is high and q is low)
- At falling edge of clock: q <= p; (q takes the value of p, introducing a delay)

The initial values for p and q should be zero.

This dual-edge clocked behavior matches the waveform transitions better:

- p depends on current 'a' and previous 'q' (updated at rising edge)
- q captures the 'p' value at falling edge, delaying p by half a clock cycle

This design uses two always blocks with opposite clock edges and nonblocking assignments to properly capture the timing and sequential dependencies shown in the waveform.

This approach matches the waveform more closely and resolves the mismatch issues by correctly timing updates of p and q.
