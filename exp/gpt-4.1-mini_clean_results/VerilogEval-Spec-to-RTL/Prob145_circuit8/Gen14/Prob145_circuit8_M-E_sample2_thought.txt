Analyzing the waveform, the output signals p and q behave as a two-bit state machine that transitions on the rising edge of the clock and holds values otherwise.

Key observations:
- At the initial rising clock edge, p is set to 0.
- At clock rising edges, p updates mostly to 'a' ORed with previous p when q=1, and to zero when q=0.
- q updates only at falling clock edges and seems to latch p's previous value, but with some delay or condition.
- p seems to reflect a "set" condition driven by 'a' and current q.
- q holds the previous p value but only updates at the falling edge.
- At clock falling edges, q <= p, and at clock rising edges p <= a | (p & q), effectively a latch with enable.
- Using two flip-flops updated on opposite edges but with slightly different logic can capture the sequential pattern.

Hence, implement:

- On posedge clock: p <= a | (p & q);
- On negedge clock: q <= p;

This approach creates a "memory" effect on p controlled by a and q, and q tracks p with one half clock cycle delay, matching the observed waveforms. The OR with (p & q) ensures p can stay high once set while q=1, and resets when q=0 and a=0.

This design is simple, uses dual-edge triggered flip-flops to capture timing of updates from waveform, and logically matches the signal progression in the waveforms.
