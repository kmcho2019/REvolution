The previous design attempted to update p and q on different clock edges, causing synchronization and timing mismatches with the given waveform.

The waveform indicates that:
- p is updated on the rising edge of the clock, reflecting the input a and possibly some stored internal state.
- q changes only at the falling edge or when clock is low, and seems to latch or hold some previous state of p or related logic.

However, the waveform also shows q is stable and does not toggle within a single clock cycle, indicating q is likely updated synchronously at the clock's rising edge, but reflecting a different state variable.

To avoid the complexity and uncertainty of dual-edge triggered logic, we can implement a synchronous sequential circuit with one clock edge (rising edge).

By analyzing the waveform:

- p goes high on rising clock edges when input a is high, or when p is already high and q is low.
- q goes high on the next clock cycle after p becomes high.
- q resets only when p resets to zero.

This behavior can be modeled by two registers updated at the rising clock edge:

- p_next = a | (p & ~q);
- q_next = p | q;  (q stays high once set, as the waveform shows q remains at 1 until p drops)

Initialize both p and q to zero.

This model respects the waveform transitions, and updates both outputs synchronously on the rising edge, which resolves timing issues and matches the observed state transitions.

This single clock edge approach will produce stable outputs aligned with the waveform.
