The initial implementation treated p and q as simple latches dependent on the input 'a' and q, but the waveform shows a more complex sequential behavior. Notably:

- p goes high on rising clock edges when 'a' transitions to 1 while q is low, but also p remains high for multiple cycles when a=1 and q=1.
- q appears to latch the previous value of p when clock is low, and updates only at clock rising edges.
- At clock falling edges, q follows p, indicating a two-phase behavior: q tracks p at the falling edge and holds or updates at rising edge.
- The waveform suggests that q updates at the falling clock edge to p's current value, and p updates at the rising edge depending on a and q.
- Also, at the very first clock rising edge, p initializes to 0 and q is undefined but then stabilizes.
- Between clock edges, the signals appear to hold their values or update on opposite clock edges.

Therefore, a double-edge triggered behavior is implied:

- p updates on rising clock edge depending on a and q
- q updates on falling clock edge following p

This approach matches the waveform where q changes state at falling edges, tracking p from the previous rising edge, and p depends on a and q at rising edges.

Implementing this with two always blocks triggered on posedge and negedge of clock:

- On posedge clock: p <= a & ~q;
- On negedge clock: q <= p;

Outputs are registered to reflect the internal state changes matching the waveform.

This design captures the sequential pattern and state progression indicated by the waveform and reduces mismatches compared to previous implementation.
