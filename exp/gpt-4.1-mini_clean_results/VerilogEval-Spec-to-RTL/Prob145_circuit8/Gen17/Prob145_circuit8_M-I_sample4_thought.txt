The previous design uses dual-edge triggered flip-flops which complicate timing and does not exactly match the observed waveform. The waveform shows that p and q transition in a phased manner, but only on the rising edge of clock, with q taking on previous p values delayed by one clock cycle.

By analyzing the timing, the outputs can be viewed as two state bits updated synchronously on the rising clock edge:

- p_next = a | q (next value of p)
- q_next = p (next value of q)

Both p and q update only on posedge clock, meaning q essentially lags p by one cycle, and p depends on input a and the previous q.

This synchronous one-clock edge update avoids metastability issues and matches the waveform transitions better. No need for negedge clock sensitivity.

Initial outputs are unknown, and after first clock edges, p and q settle to correct values according to the input and prior state.

This approach is simpler, clean, and reliable for synthesis and timing closure.

Hence, the improved design:
- Use one always block triggered on posedge clock.
- Update p and q simultaneously as per next-state logic.
- p = a | q;
- q = old p;

This matches the pattern seen in the waveform and avoids dual-edge triggered logic.
