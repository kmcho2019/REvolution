The waveform indicates a sequential circuit with outputs p and q changing only on clock edges. At time zero, p and q are unknown, then after the first rising edge of clock, p is zero and q is unknown, and after the first falling edge of clock, q becomes zero, implying q updates on falling edges, and p updates on rising edges.

Since dual-edge triggered logic is not recommended, the best approach is to use a single clock edge but a state machine with two registers updated at different clock edges.

To match the waveform:

- On the rising edge of clock, update p based on input a and current q (likely p = a & ~q).
- On the falling edge of clock, update q with the current value of p.

This produces a half-cycle delayed q with respect to p, matching the waveform where q changes after falling edge, and p after rising edge.

Implement separate always blocks for posedge and negedge clock:

- At posedge clock: p <= a & ~q;
- At negedge clock: q <= p;

Initialize p and q to unknown ('x') at simulation start to match waveform initial conditions.

This model directly follows the waveform transitions and captures sequential behavior over both clock edges, accurately reflecting the timing of p and q updates.
