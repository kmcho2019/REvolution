The waveform shows that outputs p and q are unknown ('x') initially and only become defined after the first rising edge of clock when a=0, at which point p=0 and q is still 'x'. Then at the next falling edge of clock, q becomes 0 while p remains 0. This suggests that p is updated on the positive edge of the clock and q is updated on the negative edge, each depending on input a and/or previous states.

The key insight is that p updates on posedge clock and q updates on negedge clock, implementing a two-phase state machine. The transitions of p and q occur on opposite clock edges, which explains the stable states of q during clock=0 shown in the waveform.

Further, p at the posedge clock takes the value of input a; q at the negedge clock takes the previous value of p. This matches the delayed behavior of q relative to p and the waveform timing.

Initially, both p and q are unknown. At the first posedge clock, p <= a (which is 0), q remains unknown. Then at the first negedge clock after that, q <= p (which is 0), making q 0. There is no need for an 'initialized' flag; we keep p and q as reg initialized to 'x', and let the clock edges drive them.

This approach aligns with the waveform behavior:

- p updated at posedge clock: p <= a

- q updated at negedge clock: q <= p

We must implement two always blocks triggered on posedge and negedge of clock, respectively, using nonblocking assignments and initialize p and q to 1'bx for correct simulation startup unknowns.

This will fix the output mismatches by accurately modeling the two-edge-triggered sequential behavior observed.

Hence, implement:

- reg p, q initialized to x

- always @(posedge clock): p <= a

- always @(negedge clock): q <= p
