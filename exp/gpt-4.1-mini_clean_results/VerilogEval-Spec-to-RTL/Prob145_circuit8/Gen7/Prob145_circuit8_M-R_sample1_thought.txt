To improve the design and avoid potential race conditions with separate always blocks on opposite edges, 
refactor the module to use a single clocking domain with registers updating synchronously on the positive edge of the clock. 

The output 'p' captures input 'a' on the posedge clock, and 'q' captures the previous value of 'p' 
(i.e., delayed by one clock cycle). This effectively models the two-flip-flop behavior but synchronously, 
simplifying timing and matching the waveforms where 'q' follows the delayed 'p'.

This also simplifies initialization by using a reset-like initial block for simulation purposes.
