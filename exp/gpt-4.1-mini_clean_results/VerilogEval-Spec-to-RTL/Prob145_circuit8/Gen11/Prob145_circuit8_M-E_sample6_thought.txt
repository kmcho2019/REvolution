The waveform suggests a two-bit state machine implemented with flip-flops p and q that update on clock edges. Initially, p and q are undefined. After reset (implicit at start), the outputs take values depending on input a and prior state.

Observing the pattern:

- On rising edges of clock, p updates based on input a and possibly the current state of q.
- On falling edges, q updates based on the current p.
- There is a phase shift: p updates on posedge, q updates on negedge, capturing p.

A practical approach is to interpret p and q as a 2-bit shift register or state register with feedback influenced by input a.

To implement that:

- On posedge clock: p <= a | q;  (p captures input a OR current q)
- On negedge clock: q <= p;     (q captures p, introducing delay)

This logic explains the sustained ones in q and p values influenced by both input and previous q state.

Also, undefined initial states (x) are natural without reset and the outputs will stabilize after clock transitions.

This design uses a two-edge triggered circuit, but with p driven by input and previous q, making the state evolution richer and more closely matching the waveform pattern.

This approach avoids simple direct sampling and introduces feedback, making output states depend on the sequence of a and clock edges.
