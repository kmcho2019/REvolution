The previous approach used both posedge and negedge clock triggers, which causes timing and synchronization issues. The waveform suggests that both outputs p and q update synchronously on the rising edge of the clock, with p depending on the current input 'a' and some internal state, and q depending on p from the previous cycle or on internal state.

From the waveform, p is initially 0 after the first clock rising edge when a=0, then p toggles to 1 on rising clock edges only when a=1 at certain times, while q seems to track a delayed version of p, but not just a one-cycle delay. q becomes 1 after several cycles and remains stable despite a changing.

A good interpretation is a two-bit state register {q,p} updated synchronously on rising edge clock:

- p updates to a logical function of input 'a' and current q (e.g., p <= a & ~q or similar).
- q updates to the old p (q <= p).
  
Analyzing the pattern:

- At times when a=1 and clock rises, p goes high.
- q updates to previous p.
- On falling edge, outputs remain stable.
  
Hence, implementing a 2-bit shift register updated on the rising clock edge:

- q <= p;
- p <= a;

This matches the waveforms where q always lags p by one clock cycle.

Also, this avoids using negedge clock, simplifying timing and synthesis.

The initial unknown states (x) are natural due to uninitialized reg outputs.

Therefore, the improved design will update p and q on posedge clock, where p <= a and q <= previous p.

This aligns with the waveform's data:

- At first rising edge, p = a = 0, q is unknown.
- At next rising edge, q = old p, p = a.
- This produces the shifting pattern observed.

This approach simplifies timing and fixes synchronization errors.
