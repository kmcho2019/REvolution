Instead of modeling the design as a classic FSM with encoded states, use two flip-flops (p and q) directly updated on the rising edge of the clock based on combinational logic of input 'a' and the current outputs p and q. Analyze the waveform to deduce the next values of p and q on each clock edge:

- At reset (initial time), outputs are undefined.
- On first clock edge with a=0, p=0, q=x (q undefined).
- Later when a=1 at clock edge, p becomes 1, q remains 0.
- q becomes 1 only after some cycles when p=1 is stable.
- When a=1 and p=0, p sets to 1.
- When a=0 and p=1 and q=0, q sets to 1.
- When a=0 and p=1 and q=1, both reset to 0.

From these observations, the circuit behaves as a 2-bit shift-register-like sequence with conditional logic:  
- p sets to 1 on a=1 input if not already set.  
- q sets to 1 if p=1 and a=0.  
- Both clear to 0 if p=1, q=1 and a=0.

Implement this logic with two registers p and q updated synchronously on clock edges without explicit reset (outputs start undefined as shown). Outputs are driven directly from these registers.

This approach is simple, matches the waveform pattern, and requires no complex state encoding or asynchronous resets.
