From the waveform, the outputs p and q depend on previous states and input a sampled on clock edges. The outputs p and q update only on the rising clock edges.

- Initially p and q are unknown.
- On the first rising clock with a=0, p=0, q is unknown (x).
- When clock is low and a changes, p and q hold their values.
- On rising clock edges:
  - If q=0, then p is set to input a, q remains 0.
  - If q=1, then p is set to input a, q stays 1.
- On falling clock edges:
  - p and q do not change.
- At time 115ns and after, q becomes 1 on falling edge and remains 1.
- After that, on rising edges, p follows input a when q=1, and q stays 1.
- On the falling edges q toggles to 0 when p=1 and a=1?
  Actually q changes from 1 to 0 at 175ns falling edge when a=1. But at 180ns q=0 and p=0.

From this data, the simplest consistent implementation is:
- Registers p and q updated on rising clock edge.
- q set to 1 when p and a are both 1 on rising edge.
- On falling edge q resets to 0.
- p copies input a on rising edge.
This matches most waveform behavior:
- On rising edge p = a
- On rising edge if p and a =1 then q=1 else q remains previous
- On falling edge q reset to 0

Since the waveform is not fully deterministic, this solution fits well with the given data.

We'll implement with a positive edge clock process updating p and q. Additionally, on negative clock edge, q is cleared.

The q is a state that remains 1 after detecting p=a=1 at rising edge, and clears at falling edge.
