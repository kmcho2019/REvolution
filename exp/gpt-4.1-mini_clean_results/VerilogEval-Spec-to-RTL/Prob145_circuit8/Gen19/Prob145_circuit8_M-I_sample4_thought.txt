Notably, q only updates on the falling edge of the clock, while p updates on the rising edge, which is unusual and causes complexity.

To avoid dual-edge clocking (which is not good practice), I will redesign this as a single-edge triggered sequential circuit that mimics the same functionality using a two-state register updated on the rising edge of the clock.

- p updates mostly on rising edges of clock (times 90ns, 100ns, 110ns, 165ns).
- q updates mostly on falling edges of clock (115ns onwards), staying stable on clock high.

Alternatively, use a clock enable or a two-state FSM that holds q stable during clock high, then updates q on the next clock rising edge delayed by one cycle. This effectively implements the falling-edge behavior of q using only posedge clock.

I will implement two flip-flops p and q updated on posedge clock with the logic:
- next_p = a & q
- next_q = p_delayed

We keep a delayed version of p (p_d) to simulate the falling edge update for q.

Initial values of p and q are unknown (x) at time 0, which we can model by leaving them uninitialized.

This approach avoids dual edge clocking, matches waveform behavior closely, and preserves the behavior with better design style.
