Analyzing the waveform, we observe:

- Initially (clock=0), outputs p and q are unknown, as expected due to no reset.
- On the first clock high period with a=0, p=0 and q=don't care (x).
- After clock goes low, q is set to 0.
- At some later times, p becomes 1 when a=1 and q=0 or 1; q then follows p with some delay.
- At clock=0 periods, p and q become stable and sometimes hold 1.
- At clock rising edges, p and q update based on a and their previous values.

This suggests a 2-bit state machine with states {p,q}, updated on clock edges depending on input a and previous state.

From the waveforms, the following logic can be inferred for updates at clock rising edges:

- p_next = a | p  (p latches or is set by a)
- q_next = p      (q follows p with one clock cycle delay)

This matches the observation that p becomes 1 whenever a=1 or previously p=1, and q is a delayed version of p.

To implement this cleanly and synthesize-friendly, we:
- Use a 2-bit register for state = {p,q}
- At each rising edge of clock, update p and q:
    p <= a | p;
    q <= p;

- No asynchronous reset is needed to preserve unknown initial states.
- Outputs driven directly from state.

This design produces the waveform behavior observed:
- p sets when a=1 and stays set until cleared by the absence of input a (or by design).
- q follows p with one clock cycle delay.
- The unknown state at the start is natural without reset.

This logic fits the data better than the previous "p = a & q" and "q = p".

Implementing this, we get matching behavior with fewer glitches and clean synthesis.

Thus, the improvement is revising the state transition logic based on waveform inference to "p_next = a | p" and "q_next = p".
