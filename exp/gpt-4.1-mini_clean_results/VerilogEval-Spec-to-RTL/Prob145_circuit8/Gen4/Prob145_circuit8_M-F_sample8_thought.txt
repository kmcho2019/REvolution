Re-examining the waveform, the outputs p and q both behave like a 2-bit shift register capturing the input 'a' synchronized with the clock edges but with distinct update edges: 

- p updates on the rising edge of clock, capturing the current 'a'.
- q updates on the falling edge of clock, capturing the previous value of p.

This means p directly reflects the input 'a' sampled at the rising clock edge, and q holds p's value sampled at the previous rising edge but updated at the falling edge.

There is no complex logic holding p at 1 once set; instead, p follows 'a' at the rising edge. Also, q effectively latches p at falling edges.

Implementing this as two sequential processes:

- On posedge clock, p <= a;
- On negedge clock, q <= p;

Initial values of p and q can be zero or 'x' (unknown) to match the waveform at simulation start.

This approach aligns with the waveform where p changes to the value of a on rising clock edges and q changes to the previous p value on falling edges.
