The previous logic for state transitions (next q = p, next p = a | (p & q)) does not match the given waveform. Analyzing the waveform carefully, we observe:

- Initially, both p and q start as unknown (x), then at first posedge clock (25ns) with a=0, p=0 and q=x, then q becomes defined at next negedge clock.
- At the clock rising edges when a=1, p becomes 1 and q stays 0 or 1 depending on the previous state.
- At the falling edges of clock, q follows p, indicating q updates on falling edge, while p updates on rising edge.
- The outputs p and q do not update simultaneously at clock edges; p updates on posedge clock, q updates on negedge clock by capturing p.

From this pattern, we deduce a two-process approach:

- On posedge clock: p <= a | (p & q) (or some similar logic depending on a and previous p,q)
- On negedge clock: q <= p

This dual-edge triggering is consistent with the waveform showing q changing only on falling edges of clock.

To implement this, we use two always blocks:

1. On posedge clock, update p synchronously with the logic:

   p <= a | (p & q);

2. On negedge clock, update q as:

   q <= p;

We initialize p and q to zero in an initial block to avoid unknowns.

This two-edge approach fits the timing pattern seen in the waveform, ensuring q updates after p.

This matches the waveform patterns:

- At posedge clock (e.g. 90ns), p updates to 1 if a=1 or (p&q) was 1.
- At negedge clock (e.g. 115ns), q updates to the value of p just captured.

Hence, the corrected approach uses two flip-flops clocked on different edges.
