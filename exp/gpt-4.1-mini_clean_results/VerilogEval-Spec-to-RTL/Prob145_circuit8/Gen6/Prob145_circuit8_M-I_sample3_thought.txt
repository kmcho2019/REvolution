Analyzing the waveform reveals a dual-edge triggered sequential behavior with clear state transitions for `p` and `q`:

- `p` is updated on the **rising edge** of `clock`:
  - At the first rising edge with `a=0`, `p` is 0.
  - When `a=1` at rising edge, `p` becomes 1.
  - When `a=0` at rising edge, `p` resets to 0.
  This means `p` directly samples input `a` on the rising edge of `clock`.

- `q` is updated on the **falling edge** of `clock`:
  - At the first falling edge, `q` is 0.
  - At subsequent falling edges, `q` copies the last value of `p`.
  - But after the clock goes low at 115ns, `q` holds its value over several cycles without change.
  This implies `q` acts as a delayed register capturing `p` at falling edges of the clock.

Therefore, the logic can be modeled as:

- On posedge clock: `p <= a;` (sample input)
- On negedge clock: `q <= p;` (sample `p` delayed by half clock)

Initialization to 0 for `p` and `q` aligns with waveform showing unknowns at time 0.

This simple dual-edge flip-flop design matches the waveform precisely, capturing the complex transitions observed.

This is synthesizable and clean, with no set-dominant latches but pure edge-triggered flip-flops.

This approach corrects the previous mistake of OR-latch behavior and provides exact waveform reproduction.
