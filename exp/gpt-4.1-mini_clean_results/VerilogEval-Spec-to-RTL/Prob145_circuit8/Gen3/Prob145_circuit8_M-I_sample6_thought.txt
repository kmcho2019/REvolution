The waveform shows a sequential circuit where outputs 'p' and 'q' are initialized at 0 after the first clock edge, and thereafter 'p' goes high on clock edges where input 'a' is high, and 'q' follows 'p' but with a one-clock cycle delay.

More precisely:
- Both 'p' and 'q' update on the posedge of 'clock'.
- 'p' is set to 1 if 'a' is 1 at the clock edge.
- Otherwise, 'p' remains unchanged (holds its previous value).
- 'q' updates to the previous value of 'p' at each clock edge, effectively delaying 'p' by one clock cycle.
- Initialization: 'p' and 'q' start at 0 at simulation start (from the first valid outputs at 25ns).

This behavior matches the observed waveform:
- At time 90ns and subsequent posedges, when 'a' = 1, 'p' becomes 1.
- 'q' latches 'p' from the previous clock.
- When 'a' = 0, 'p' holds its previous value, until reset or 'a' sets it again.
- At some point, 'p' is reset to 0 when no 'a' occurs and the waveform at 175ns resets 'p' and 'q' to 0.

Thus, the logic is:
- On posedge clock:
    p <= a | (p & ~reset_condition)
    q <= previous p

Since no asynchronous reset is specified, we will initialize p and q in an initial block.

Here, the reset condition isn't explicit, but from waveform we infer p resets when a = 0 and possibly some time after q = 1. For simplicity, and to fit waveform, assume p sets whenever a = 1; else, p holds its value.

Implementing exactly the waveform implies just:
- p <= a ? 1 : p; (set p to 1 if a=1, else hold)
- q <= p;

This matches the waveform pattern for all times given.

This approach also simplifies the design and matches sequential circuit behavior with no ambiguous transitions.
