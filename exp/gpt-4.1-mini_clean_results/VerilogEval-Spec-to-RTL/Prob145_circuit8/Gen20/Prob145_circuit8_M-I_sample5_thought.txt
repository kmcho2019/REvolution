The previous solution attempted a dual-edge triggered design, but the waveform indicates that both outputs `p` and `q` update only on the positive clock edge, with `q` holding the previous `p` value. Furthermore, `p` only changes to `1` when `a` is asserted and remains `0` otherwise; `q` seems to follow `p` but delayed by one clock cycle. Also, at falling clock edges, outputs `p` and `q` remain stable and do not update, which means only positive-edge triggered behavior exists.

Thus, to match the waveform, implement a positive edge clocked sequential logic where:
- `p` updates to `a` at each rising clock edge.
- `q` updates to the previous value of `p` (i.e., one-cycle delayed `p`).

This results in `q` being a one-clock-cycle delayed version of `p`.

This correctly matches all waveform data points:
- Initially unknown (`x`).
- At first clock edges, p becomes 0 because a=0.
- Later when a=1 at a clock edge, p becomes 1, and q takes the previous p value.
- No updates on falling edge.
  
This is a straightforward dual flip-flop pipeline with no asynchronous resets, consistent with the waveform.

This also avoids dual-edge triggered logic, improving synthesis and simulation reliability.
