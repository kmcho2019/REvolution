The waveform shows `p` and `q` as sequential outputs updated on the clock's rising edge, with initial unknown states. Observing the pattern:

- At the first clock edge, `p` becomes equal to input `a`.
- At each subsequent clock edge, `q` takes the old value of `p`.
- Then `p` updates to `p | a` (logical OR of previous p and current a).
- Both outputs initialize as unknown (`x`).

This behavior can be captured by two registers `p_reg` and `q_reg`:

- On each posedge clock:
  - Update `q_reg` with the previous `p_reg`.
  - Update `p_reg` with `(previous p_reg) | a`.

This sequence produces the waveform where `p` accumulates input `a` over time (latches 1 when `a` is 1) and `q` follows with one cycle delay.

Initialize registers to unknown to match initial states.

This solution is simple, matches the waveform behavior, and uses minimal logic.
