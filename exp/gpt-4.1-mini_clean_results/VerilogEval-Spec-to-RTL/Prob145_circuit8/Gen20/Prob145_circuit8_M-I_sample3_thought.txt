The waveform suggests outputs p and q are updated on opposite clock edges: p updates on the rising edge of clock based on input a and current q, while q updates on the falling edge of clock, tracking p. This indicates a dual-edge triggered behavior: p updates on posedge clock, q updates on negedge clock.

To correctly model this, I will use two always blocks: one triggered on posedge clock to update p, and another on negedge clock to update q. Both registers hold state.

From the waveform, p is zero at the first posedge clock with a=0, and then p = a & previous q at subsequent posedges. q copies p on the negedge clock.

Initial values of p and q are unknown (x), so no reset is used.

This dual-edge design perfectly fits the timing of the waveform.

This approach directly matches the waveform and avoids hacks or delays with a single clock edge.

If dual-edge is undesirable in some FPGA flows, this can be further refined, but for correctness matching the given waveform, this is the appropriate method.
