To better mimic the waveform, where p updates on the rising edge of clock and q updates on the falling edge, we can explicitly use two always blocks: one triggered on posedge clock to update p, and another on negedge clock to update q.

This captures the behavior of the waveform more accurately without relying on tricky delayed registers within a single always block.

- On posedge clock, p <= a & q (using the old q value)
- On negedge clock, q <= p

This cleanly models the shifting effect and separate timing for p and q as in the waveform.

Initial values remain undefined as the waveform shows 'x' at time 0.

This approach avoids race conditions and timing ambiguities present in a single always block and better matches the dual-edge sequential behavior in the original waveforms.
