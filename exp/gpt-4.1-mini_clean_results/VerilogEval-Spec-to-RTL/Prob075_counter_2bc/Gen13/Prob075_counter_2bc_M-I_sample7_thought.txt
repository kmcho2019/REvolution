To optimize the existing design for better PPA, the focus is on further reducing unnecessary toggling and simplifying the comparator logic for update_enable. Instead of comparing next_state and current state (which needs a 2-bit comparator), we can determine update_enable more simply by checking train_valid only because saturating increment/decrement saturate at the bounds and if train_valid=1 but the state is already at max/min, the state does not change anyway.

Thus, removing the explicit comparator (next_state != state) reduces combinational logic complexity and delay. We rely on saturating arithmetic to keep state stable at boundaries, so unnecessary updates cause no change, minimizing toggling inherently.

Additionally, changing update_enable to just train_valid results in always updating on valid training, but because the counter saturates, this won't cause incorrect behavior or excessive toggling.

Finally, keep the asynchronous positive edge reset as specified, as it is required.

This approach simplifies logic, improves timing slightly, and trades off minor increase in toggling (negligible) for less complex comparison logic, which can enhance critical path and area efficiency.
