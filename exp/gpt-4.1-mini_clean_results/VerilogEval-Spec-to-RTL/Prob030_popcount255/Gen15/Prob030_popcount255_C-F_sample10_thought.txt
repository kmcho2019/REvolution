Fuse the recursive balanced popcount structure and efficient base popcount8 module of Example 2 with the top-level balanced partial summation strategy and fixed partitioning of Example 1 to optimize timing, power, and area.

Key design points:
- Partition the 255-bit input into 5 equal chunks of 51 bits, as in Example 1, to limit recursion depth and ensure manageable, balanced sub-block sizes.
- Apply the recursive popcount module (from Example 2) on each 51-bit chunk, splitting recursively down to ≤8-bit base cases using the structural popcount8 with zero-padding.
- At the top level, sum the 5 partial 6-bit counts (max 51) with a balanced adder tree of minimal depth (2-3 stages) rather than a full recursive summation. This reduces recursion depth and logic depth in final addition compared to fully recursive approach.
- Use $clog2(WIDTH+1) for all bit widths, ensuring minimal bitwidths for adders and reducing area/power.
- This hybrid approach limits recursion levels to ~4 inside each 51-bit chunk (instead of 8 if done flat), and limits the top-level adder stages to 3 stages, reducing overall critical path.
- Zero-pad only at base cases in popcount8, preserving clean sizing.
- This fusion improves timing by reducing recursion depth on large width and reducing adder tree depth on final sum, improving area and power due to smaller adder sizes and fewer intermediate signals.

Overall, this design balances synthesis-friendly modular recursion on smaller blocks with a shallow, efficient fixed top-level summation, resulting in better PPA while maintaining modularity and clarity.
