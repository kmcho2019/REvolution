Implement a wide parallel popcount by hierarchically partitioning the 255-bit input into 32 groups of 8 bits and 1 group of 7 bits. Use a highly regular popcount8 module for each 8-bit group and a special popcount7 module for the 7-bit remainder. Sum all 33 partial counts (each 4 bits wide for popcount8 and 3 bits wide for popcount7) using a balanced binary adder tree to minimize logic depth and maximize speed.

This approach leverages wide parallelism and uniform small-width counting units to keep combinational paths short, avoiding recursive parameterization. Using a balanced adder tree over 33 partial results, we reduce fan-in and keep additions at small bit widths until final sum, improving timing and power. The popcount8 module is explicitly balanced with minimal add stages. The popcount7 module is adapted similarly. The balanced adder tree is implemented with generate loops, summing pairs at each stage until a single output remains. This ensures modularity, predictable timing, and efficient synthesis, different from recursive designs.

Final output width is 8 bits (since max 255 ones require 8 bits). This parallelized, flat architecture trades increased area (more small popcount8 blocks) for significantly improved performance and power efficiency.
