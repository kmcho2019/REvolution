Instead of a strictly hierarchical tree of adders for fixed 17-bit chunks, I will partition the 255-bit input into 5 groups of 51 bits. For each 51-bit group, I will implement a population count using a parallel prefix popcount technique that recursively splits the input into halves, sums the counts of each half, and concatenates the results. This approach reduces the number of explicit adders by efficiently halving the input recursively until small base cases (e.g., 3 or 2 bits) are counted directly.

Then, the five 6-bit partial sums (since max 51 ones fit into 6 bits) are summed in a straightforward 3-stage adder tree:
- sum first two,
- sum next two,
- then sum those two sums with the fifth partial count.

This novel approach reduces the number of small adders and better uses recursion and generate constructs, achieving a scalable and elegant parallel prefix population count. The recursive design avoids unbalanced trees by splitting roughly evenly at each level.

The final output is 8 bits to cover up to 255 ones. All combinational and no loops other than generate and localparam recursion.

This approach differs from fixed 17-bit chunking by choosing 51-bit chunk size (larger chunk reduces number of partial sums), and differs from flat balanced trees by recursive prefix decomposition inside popcount51, enabling simpler synthesis for large inputs.
