To optimize for better power and area without sacrificing performance or clarity:

1) Use one-hot encoding for the FSM states to simplify next-state logic and reduce combinational complexity.
2) With one-hot encoding, only one flip-flop is set per state, potentially reducing switching activity and power.
3) The next-state logic becomes a simple combinational gate per flip-flop.
4) Use synchronous reset to initialize the one-hot encoded state.
5) Drive output z directly from the state flip-flops that correspond to the output=1 states (S3 and S4), eliminating combinational decoding logic and potentially reducing glitches.
6) This approach often leads to simpler logic and smaller critical path, which could improve timing and power, especially in FPGA targets.

This should lower power by minimizing logic depth and toggles, reduce area by removing wide muxes, and keep timing favorable due to simple AND/OR logic for next state.
