To optimize the FSM for better performance, power, and area, I will apply one-hot encoding instead of binary encoding for states. One-hot encoding often improves timing because the next-state logic becomes simple combinational logic without multi-bit comparisons, reducing critical path delay. It also simplifies output logic and may reduce overall toggling power due to fewer logic levels in next-state decoding. Since the FSM has only 5 states, one-hot encoding only requires 5 flip-flops, which is acceptable for area and can reduce complexity. The output z logic becomes a simple OR of two state bits. I will keep the synchronous active-high reset and non-blocking assignments for robustness. The next state logic will be expressed using simple combinational logic assignments rather than a case statement to further ease synthesis optimizations and potentially lower area and power. This approach should improve timing and possibly power while maintaining clear and maintainable code.
