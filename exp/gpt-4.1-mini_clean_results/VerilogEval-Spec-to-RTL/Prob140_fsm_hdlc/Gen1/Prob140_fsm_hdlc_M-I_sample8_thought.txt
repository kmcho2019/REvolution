The original design correctly uses a Moore FSM with states counting consecutive ones and additional output states for asserting disc and flag signals after detecting the patterns. However, it delays asserting the err output until a zero input appears after 7 or more consecutive ones, violating the requirement that err be asserted immediately one cycle after detecting 7+ ones.

To fix this, the FSM must assert err starting exactly one cycle after the 7th consecutive one is detected, i.e., on entering an err output state immediately after detecting 7 ones. Also, the err output should remain asserted while in the err output state. For continued 1s after that, the FSM remains in the err output state to continuously signal the error, until a zero input resets the FSM.

Thus, the improved FSM will:

- Count consecutive ones from 0 to 6 in states s0 to s6.
- On seeing the 7th consecutive one (in s6 and in=1), transition to err_out state immediately, which asserts err output.
- While input remains 1, stay in err_out to continuously assert err.
- On input zero from err_out, go back to s0.
- The disc and flag outputs and transitions remain the same.
- This removes the intermediate 'error' state and merges error signaling into err_out to ensure immediate and continuous err assertion once error detected.

This approach satisfies all problem requirements, maintains Moore outputs asserted one full cycle after detection, and provides continuous err signaling during the error condition.
