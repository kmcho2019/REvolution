To improve the PPA (timing, power, area), the FSM can be simplified by encoding states as a simple counter of consecutive ones with only 3 output states indicated by extra flags, avoiding multiple explicit output states. This reduces the state encoding and the combinational next_state logic complexity.

We encode the count of consecutive ones with a 3-bit register (0 to 7+ for error), and use separate output registers to generate one-cycle pulses for disc, flag, and err. This removes the need for 4-bit state encoding and complicated case matching on {state,in}.

The next_state logic becomes simple increment/reset on input bits, and outputs are generated from transitions with a one-cycle delayed pulse, ensuring Moore output timing.

This approach reduces state bits from 4 to 3, simplifies combinational logic, and reduces output logic complexity. The outputs are registered signals that pulse high for exactly one cycle after detection, meeting requirements.

The reset state is count=0 (no previous ones), as required, and synchronous reset is preserved.

This simpler and more compact design improves timing by minimizing combinational logic depth and reduces area. Power is reduced by simplified logic and fewer bit toggles in state register.

Overall, this approach improves PPA while preserving correctness and Moore outputs with one-cycle delayed pulses.
