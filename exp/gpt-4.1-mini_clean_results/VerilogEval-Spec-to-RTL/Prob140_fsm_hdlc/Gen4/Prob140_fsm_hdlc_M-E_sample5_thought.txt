Implement the HDLC bit sequence detector as a shift register that tracks the last 8 bits to directly detect the patterns for DISC (bit to discard), FLAG (frame boundary), and ERROR (7 or more consecutive ones). 

The input stream is sampled synchronously on clk with synchronous reset. We keep a 8-bit shift register shifting in new bits every cycle. The patterns to detect are:

- DISC (bit to discard): the pattern `111110` (five 1s followed by 0) starting at bit 7 down to bit 2 (i.e., bits 7 to 2 are `111110`).
- FLAG: the full flag byte `01111110` which is `0b01111110` (0x7E).
- ERROR: seven or more consecutive ones, i.e., `1111111` (7 ones) or `11111111` (8 ones).

To detect these, we use bitwise comparisons on the shift register. Once we detect a condition on the current cycle (based on the shifted bits), we assert the corresponding output one cycle *later* (Moore style), so outputs are registered signals that get asserted for one full clock cycle after detection.

This avoids complex state machines and relies on a simple sliding window with registered outputs that cleanly match the timing requirements.

Outputs disc, flag, and err are registered signals asserted for one cycle after detection, and then cleared.

This approach leverages parallel pattern matching, simplifies verification, and cleanly separates detection and output timing.

Reset clears the shift register to all zeros (previous bit was 0), and clears outputs.

This design is fully synchronous, easy to modify, and explicitly handles all signals without complicated state transitions.
