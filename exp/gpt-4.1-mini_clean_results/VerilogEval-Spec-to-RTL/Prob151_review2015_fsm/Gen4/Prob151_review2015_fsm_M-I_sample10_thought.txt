To optimize the FSM for better PPA:

- Move the pattern detection out of the FSM states into a small shift register plus comparator logic. This reduces the FSM states to only four: IDLE, SHIFT, COUNT, and DONE.
- Use one-hot encoding for the FSM states to simplify next-state logic and minimize combinational complexity.
- Implement shift_ena assertion for exactly 4 cycles using a 2-bit counter similar to before.
- Since pattern detection is separate, the FSM just waits for a pattern_detected signal to start shifting, which reduces state transitions and glitching on next state signals.
- Outputs are combinational from state, keeping timing simple.
- This reduces the FSM area (fewer states), power (less toggling in FSM logic), and may improve timing as combinational next state logic is simplified.

This clean separation of pattern detection and FSM control leads to a more modular, lower toggle, and potentially faster implementation.
