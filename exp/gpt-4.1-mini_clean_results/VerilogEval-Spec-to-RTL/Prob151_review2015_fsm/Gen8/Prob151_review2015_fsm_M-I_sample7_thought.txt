To optimize the original FSM for better power and area without sacrificing performance, move pattern detection out of the FSM by implementing a 4-bit shift register that continuously captures incoming data bits. Use a combinational comparator to detect the 1101 pattern. This simplifies the FSM states, reducing states from 7 to 4 by removing the SEARCH states.

The FSM states become:

- IDLE: waiting for pattern detection (pattern_detect signal from the shift register logic)
- SHIFT: shift_ena asserted for exactly 4 cycles (counted by a 2-bit counter)
- COUNT: counting asserted until done_counting is high
- DONE: done asserted until ack is received

This approach reduces combinational logic complexity in the FSM and eliminates the multiple SEARCH states, thus saving area and reducing switching activity in the FSM.

Additionally:

- Use one-hot encoding for FSM states to minimize combinational decoding logic.
- The 2-bit shift_count counter remains but only toggles during SHIFT state, minimizing switching.
- Outputs remain combinationally derived from states.
- Synchronous reset sets FSM to IDLE state.

Overall, this offloads pattern detection to a small dedicated logic block, simplifying FSM and reducing dynamic power and area.
