The key issue in the initial design is the handling of pattern detection and its synchronization with FSM transitions. To improve reliability and avoid multiple triggers on the same pattern occurrence, I propose:

- **Integrate pattern detection directly into the FSM transition logic** rather than using an auxiliary latch (`start_pattern_detected`). This avoids synchronization and glitch issues.

- **Implement the pattern shift register and matching combinationally, but only advance the FSM when pattern matches in the IDLE state**. This way, the FSM moves into SHIFT only on a clear, registered detection of the pattern, without needing an extra latch.

- **Use a simple 4-bit shift register updated every clock cycle**, continuously looking for pattern "1101". When the pattern is detected and the FSM is in IDLE, transition to SHIFT.

- **Count exactly 4 cycles in SHIFT state** (0 to 3) while enabling `shift_ena`.

- Outputs (`shift_ena`, `counting`, `done`) will be synchronous and directly driven by the current FSM state for clean timing.

- Ensure synchronous reset sets the FSM to IDLE and clears counters and shift register properly.

This approach simplifies and robustifies the FSM and pattern detection interface, ensuring deterministic and glitch-free behavior, correctly matching the required sequence of states and outputs.

Additionally, using simple FSM state encoding with `localparam` and a minimal counter will optimize area and timing.

Overall, this solution is simpler, more reliable, and better aligned with the problem requirements.
