To improve the original FSM in terms of timing, power, and area, I will apply one-hot encoding for states. One-hot encoding reduces the complexity of next-state combinational logic and typically improves timing and power because only one flip-flop toggles per state transition. It can slightly increase area due to more flip-flops, but since we only have 10 states, this is acceptable.

I will keep the same states including the 4 separate SHIFT substates, but encode each state as a separate bit. The next-state logic will become simpler as it relies on individual state bits. Outputs remain Moore style, driven by the state bits. The synchronous reset will set the FSM to the SEARCH0 state.

This approach should reduce combinational logic delay and switching activity, improving timing and power, with a modest area increase from extra flip-flops, trading area for performance and power benefits.
