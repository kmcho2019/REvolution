Implement the FSM using one-hot encoding for states to simplify combinational next-state decoding and speed up timing. Use a shift register for pattern detection embedded in the FSM by tracking the last 4 bits serially shifted in, allowing direct comparison for the 1101 pattern. After detecting the pattern, move to a shift state where shift_ena is asserted for 4 clock cycles via a separate 3-bit shift counter. Then go to a counting state waiting for done_counting, assert done and wait for ack. Outputs are registered (synchronous) signals updated in the sequential block to minimize glitches and improve power. The synchronous reset initializes the FSM to the initial state and clears counters. This design trades a small increase in area for simpler and faster next state logic and more stable outputs, which is beneficial for timing and power.
