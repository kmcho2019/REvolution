To improve PPA (performance, power, area), we adopt one-hot state encoding which typically speeds up state decoding and reduces combinational logic delay, benefiting timing. Since the FSM is small (7 states), the area overhead is modest. We also split outputs into registered signals updated on clock edge to reduce glitches, trading a small latency for more stable outputs and improved timing. The shift counter remains a simple 2-bit counter enabled only in SHIFT state to minimize switching. We keep synchronous active-high reset and positive-edge triggered registers for robustness. This one-hot FSM with registered outputs reduces combinational path complexity, lowers glitching on outputs, and improves timing margin, while power and area remain reasonable for the given complexity.
