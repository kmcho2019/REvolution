To create a superior solution, we combine the modular clarity and potential scalability of the per-bit edge detectors from Example 1 with the area and power efficiency of the vectorized register and XOR approach from Example 2. We use a single 8-bit register to hold the previous input vector, performing a parallel XOR to detect edges, ensuring minimal register count and combinational logic depth for performance and area.

At the same time, we keep the logic within a single always block to reduce dynamic power by minimizing clocked elements and avoid multiple always blocks firing in parallel. This fusion keeps the design compact and synthesizes efficiently, while remaining modular at the bit-level for easy future extension by parameterization if needed.

Also, to further reduce unnecessary toggles, we can gate updates to the delayed register and output by detecting whether the input has changed at all. If no change, skip registering again. This clock-enable style logic reduces dynamic power and switching activity.

This balanced solution leverages the strength of both approaches:
- Vectorized registers and XOR for minimal area and power.
- Single always block for simplified control and reduced switching.
- Optional clock enable for power optimization without sacrificing performance or correctness.

Thus, we achieve a solution that is clean, compact, efficient, and power aware.
