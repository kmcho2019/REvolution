Instead of storing previous input bits in registers and XORing on each clock edge, we can implement edge detection by leveraging a pair of registers that form a two-stage shift register for the entire 8-bit input vector. By comparing these two delayed samples of input, we detect changes that happened between the two cycles. To generate the output one cycle after the edge, we then register the edge detection result in a third register stage. This three-stage pipeline approach creates a neatly timed output delay, enables clear separation of timing stages, and offers a fully vectorized implementation minimizing per-bit logic duplication. Additionally, this pipeline-style structure is easy for synthesis tools to optimize and can scale well for larger vectors.
