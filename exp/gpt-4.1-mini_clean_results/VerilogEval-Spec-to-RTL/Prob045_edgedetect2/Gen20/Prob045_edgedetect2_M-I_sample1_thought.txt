To optimize for power and area while maintaining performance, we consolidate the edge detection logic into a single always block that handles all bits simultaneously using vectorized registers and bitwise XOR operations. This reduces the number of flip-flops from 8 separate single-bit registers to one 8-bit register, cutting down clocked elements and logic duplication. It also reduces switching activity by minimizing the number of separate sequential elements clocked. The logic remains straightforward with one clocked register holding previous input and combinational XOR to detect edges, with the output registered synchronously to produce the one-cycle delayed pulse per bit. This approach improves power and area efficiency and keeps timing fast due to minimal logic depth.
