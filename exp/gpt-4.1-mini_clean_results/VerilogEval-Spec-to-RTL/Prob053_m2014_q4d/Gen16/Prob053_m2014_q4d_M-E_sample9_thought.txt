Instead of using the flip-flop output directly in the XOR input, use a separate internal register to hold the previous state of 'out' and drive the output from this registered state. This way, the combinational XOR only depends on 'in' and a stable registered value (previous state), avoiding combinational feedback directly on an output register. On each positive clock edge, the internal register updates with the XOR result, and the output is assigned from this internal register. This also isolates the sequential logic from the output port, improving clarity and reducing simulation startup ambiguity.
