To improve the existing design for better synthesis compatibility and PPA metrics, I will remove the `initial` block since it is typically ignored in synthesis and can cause simulation-synthesis mismatch. Instead, I will add an explicit asynchronous reset input to initialize the flip-flop output to a known state. This aligns with good design practices and ensures consistent behavior between simulation and synthesis.

The reset is active high and asynchronous, allowing a deterministic startup state. If the problem explicitly disallows reset, it should be documented that initial output is undefined after power-up, but typically including a reset is preferable.

This modification improves robustness and avoids unknown states. The rest of the design remains the same: the flip-flop captures the XOR of `in` and the current `out` on the positive clock edge. This approach keeps area minimal and has no performance penalty.

If the reset is strictly disallowed by requirements, this design can still be synthesized without it by tying reset inactive, but having it provides a clean initialization method and is preferred in practical designs.
