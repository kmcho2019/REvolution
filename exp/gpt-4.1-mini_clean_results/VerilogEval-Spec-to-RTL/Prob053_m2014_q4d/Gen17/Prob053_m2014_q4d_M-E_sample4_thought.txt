Instead of implementing the XOR and D flip-flop directly in a single always block, separate the combinational XOR logic from the flip-flop register. Create a wire for the XOR output of 'in' and the current 'out' value, then feed that wire into the D input of the flip-flop (which updates on the positive clock edge). This separation clarifies the datapath and can avoid any confusion about evaluation order in simulation. The 'out' register holds the flip-flop output. Although functionally equivalent, explicitly separating the combinational and sequential logic improves readability and might aid synthesis tools.
