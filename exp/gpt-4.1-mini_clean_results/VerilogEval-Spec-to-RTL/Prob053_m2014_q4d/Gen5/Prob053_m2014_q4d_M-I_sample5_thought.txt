The existing design correctly models the required behavior with a D flip-flop and XOR feedback. To improve clarity, synthesis friendliness, and potentially avoid synthesis mismatches due to implicit combinational feedback, I will explicitly separate the output register from the combinational logic input and avoid naming output as the register directly.

Also, I will eliminate the 'initial' block since it only affects simulation and may mask issues during synthesis, favoring purely synthesizable constructs as no reset is allowed.

To further assist synthesis tools, I will:

- Use a distinct output register separate from the output port (i.e., assign output from the register).

- Keep the feedback and logic clearly separated.

This ensures the design is fully synchronous, with no unintended combinational loops on the netlist level, and outputs are stable.

Since no reset is allowed, the initial state at power-up remains undefined in hardware but clearly defined in simulation without initial blocks.

This design is optimal for PPA given constraints, and compatible with synthesis tools.
