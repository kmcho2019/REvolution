Instead of directly feeding the XOR output as the D input of the flip-flop, we can split the logic into a combinational stage that stores the intermediate XOR result in a wire. Then, the flip-flop captures this wire on the positive edge of clk. This creates a clean separation between combinational logic and storage. Additionally, we can define the output as a wire driven by the register to clarify the data flow and maintain the single-bit output port as a wire connected to the internal register. This approach explicitly models the XOR output and flip-flop input separately, potentially improving readability and modularity.
