To provide a novel architectural approach, instead of directly using a single register for 'out', I can split the state storage and combinational XOR into two separate registers. One register will hold the previous state of the flip-flop output (named 'q_reg'), and another register will compute the next state (named 'next_out'). On the positive edge of 'clk', 'q_reg' updates to 'next_out', which is combinationally assigned as the XOR of 'in' and 'q_reg'. Finally, 'out' is assigned from 'q_reg'. This creates a registered datapath with explicit separation of combinational and sequential logic. Functionally this is equivalent to the original, but architecturally different, highlighting the intermediate combinational stage and state register separation.
