I will refactor the design by removing the intermediate combinational register and using a continuous assignment to directly implement the XOR logic feeding the flip-flop's D input. This approach uses a wire for the XOR output computed combinationally, and the flip-flop is only in the sequential always block.

This structure is more compact and typical for simple combinational-to-sequential data flow: combinational logic expressed by an assign statement and the sequential register capturing that result on the clock's rising edge. It simplifies the code, potentially improving synthesis and timing by eliminating one always block and intermediate signal storage in a reg.

The functional behavior remains identical: the D flip-flop stores the XOR of 'in' and 'out' each clock cycle, with no reset. This design is also synthesizable and clean.
