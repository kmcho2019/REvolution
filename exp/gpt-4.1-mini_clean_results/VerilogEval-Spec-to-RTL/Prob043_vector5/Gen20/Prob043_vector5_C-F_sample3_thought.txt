To reduce power and area, I will compute only the upper triangle and diagonal elements explicitly using XNOR gates, then assign the corresponding lower triangle bits by directly referencing those computed signals (outputs), avoiding duplicating logic.

This approach effectively halves the number of XNOR gates, saves power and area by sharing results of symmetric comparisons, and maintains a single explicit computation per unique pair.

- Pack inputs as inputs[0]=a ... inputs[4]=e for natural indexing.
- Compute out[24-(i*5+j)] for i <= j using XNOR inputs[i] and inputs[j].
- For i > j, assign out[24-(i*5+j)] = out[24-(j*5+i)] (mirror assignment).
- This output-to-output assignment is safe because the referenced out bits are computed combinationally in the same cycle (and static). This reduces duplicated logic without complicated wiring.

This design achieves:
- Only 15 XNOR gates (sum of 5 diagonal + 10 upper triangle pairs).
