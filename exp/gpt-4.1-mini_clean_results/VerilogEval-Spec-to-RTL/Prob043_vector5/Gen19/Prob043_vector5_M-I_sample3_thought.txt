The original design cleverly uses symmetry by assigning lower-triangle elements as direct references to the corresponding upper-triangle outputs, saving logic for equality checks. However, this results in signal fanout and potential routing congestion since outputs depend on other outputs, which may increase delay and power.

To improve PPA, I will eliminate output-to-output assignments by computing only the upper triangle including diagonal outputs with XNOR gates, and for the lower triangle, directly compute their equality again instead of referencing output bits. Although this duplicates some small logic, it simplifies routing, reduces fanout of outputs driving outputs, and can improve timing and power due to reduced net capacitance and signal bounce.

Additionally, I will reverse the input packing to keep inputs[0]=a down to inputs[4]=e, so the indexing of inputs aligns naturally with i,j (i.e., inputs[i], inputs[j]), making the code cleaner and easier to maintain.

This approach trades a few more gates for improved timing and power by reducing critical path fanout and routing complexity, which typically yields better overall PPA, especially for small bitwidth and simple logic.

Finally, I will keep the output index mapping as per the spec (out[24 - (i*5 + j)]).

This design keeps all 25 comparisons explicitly implemented, which is acceptable given the small size (25 XNORs is minimal), and avoids output-to-output signal dependencies that may degrade PPA.

Summary:
- Pack inputs as inputs[0]=a ... inputs[4]=e for natural indexing.
- For all (i,j), assign out[24-(i*5+j)] = ~(inputs[i]^inputs[j]) directly.
- No output-to-output assignments.
- Keep code simple and fully combinational.

This should yield cleaner timing, improved power (by reduced net fanout), and possibly better area (due to simpler routing and synthesis optimization), suitable for small circuits.
