A different architectural approach is to express the output q as a multiplexed selection based on one of the inputs, for example input 'a', which acts as a select line choosing between two functions of the other inputs.

By examining the truth table, we notice that when 'a' is 0, q depends on a certain parity pattern of b, c, d, and when 'a' is 1, q follows a complementary parity pattern of b, c, d.

Specifically:

- For a=0, q = ~(b ^ c ^ d) (even parity of b,c,d)
- For a=1, q = (b ^ c ^ d) (odd parity of b,c,d)

So q can be implemented as a 2-to-1 multiplexer:

q = a ? (b ^ c ^ d) : ~(b ^ c ^ d);

This solution differs from the simple total parity approach by isolating 'a' as a selector, allowing potentially modular or pipeline-friendly logic blocks, which could be advantageous in larger designs.

This decomposition also offers a clear pathway for optimizing timing or area in specific conditions, since the parity of b,c,d can be reused in different ways depending on a.
