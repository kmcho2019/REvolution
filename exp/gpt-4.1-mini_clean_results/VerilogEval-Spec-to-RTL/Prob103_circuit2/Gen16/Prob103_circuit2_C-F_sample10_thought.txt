Both solutions implement the even parity of inputs a, b, c, and d, producing q as the inverse of their XOR. The first solution uses XOR followed by a negation, while the second uses a chain of XNOR operators. Both are logically equivalent and minimal in logic depth and gate count.

The fusion strategy is to explicitly express the output as the XNOR (even parity) of all inputs using the reduction XNOR operator (^~), which is synthesizable and directly expresses the parity with inversion in a clear and concise way. This combines the readability of the first solution’s inversion with the power and area efficiency of the second solution’s XNOR chain, but condensed using a Verilog reduction operator.

This approach enhances code clarity, reduces gate count by avoiding separate NOT gate insertion, and leverages synthesis optimizations for reduction operators, yielding optimal PPA without compromising simplicity or maintainability.

Thus, I assign q using the reduction XNOR operator as:
assign q = ^~{a, b, c, d};
