The given solution uses a chain of XNORs to produce even parity over four inputs. Since all inputs are single bits, XOR is associative and commutative, so the output q is simply the parity bit. The even parity output q can be rewritten as the negation of the XOR of all inputs. Instead of chaining multiple XNORs, we can compute the XOR of all inputs and then invert the result with a single NOT operation.

This reduces the expression to:
q = ~(a ^ b ^ c ^ d);

This simplifies the expression by having one XOR tree and one inversion, which is equivalent in logic to the XNOR chain but expressed more directly and arguably simpler. It is functionally identical and potentially more readable and straightforward for synthesis tools.

Hence, the simplified implementation uses an XOR of inputs followed by a NOT to produce even parity.
