The initial implementation uses a straightforward 4-input XNOR for even parity detection, which is minimal and optimal in logic depth and area for combinational parity calculation. However, XOR and XNOR gates tend to have higher dynamic power consumption due to frequent toggling and multiple transistor stacks.

To optimize power further without increasing area or critical path delay, I propose restructuring the expression to use fewer XOR/XNOR gates in a balanced manner, or rewriting as a sum of products that may map better to simple AND/OR gates depending on the technology.

Given the 4-input parity function q = ~(a ^ b ^ c ^ d), it can also be expressed as the equivalence of the parity of two 2-input parities: q = (a XNOR b) XNOR (c XNOR d).

This can be beneficial because 2-input XNOR gates are simpler and may reduce internal node toggling and power.

Thus, by implementing q as the XNOR of (a XNOR b) and (c XNOR d), we can potentially improve power consumption due to gate-level balancing while maintaining the same delay and area roughly.

This restructuring keeps the logic combinational and minimal, and no blocking assignments are used. The code reflects the balanced XOR tree explicitly, which can sometimes help synthesis tools optimize gates better.

No area or delay penalty is expected; power may improve slightly due to balanced toggling.

If a synthesis tool is highly optimized, this is mostly an equivalent alternative, but it is a recommended good practice for parity computation for potential power gains.
