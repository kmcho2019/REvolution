The original solution implements the circuit as a 4-input even parity (XNOR) function using a pairwise XNOR decomposition. This is functionally correct and quite clear. 

To optimize for PPA:
- We can eliminate intermediate wires to reduce area slightly.
- Use a single continuous assignment combining all inputs directly with XOR reduction and negate the result. This yields the same output with minimal logic depth and gate count.
- Using Verilog's reduction XOR (^) operator is optimal for synthesis tools, which recognize this pattern as a parity generator.
- The reduced logic uses one XOR-reduction followed by one inversion, minimizing gates and switching nodes, thus improving area and power.
- Timing is also likely improved due to a single logic stage with inversion, compared to multiple XNOR gates and intermediate nets.

Hence, the improvement strategy is to implement q as the negation of the XOR of all four inputs, i.e., q = ~(a ^ b ^ c ^ d), in a single assign statement with no intermediate nets.
