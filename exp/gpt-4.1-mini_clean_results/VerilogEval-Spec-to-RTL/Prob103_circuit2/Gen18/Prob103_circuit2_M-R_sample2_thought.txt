The original implementation directly computes the even parity as a chain of XNOR operators in a single assign statement. To refactor while preserving the same core logic, I will use an intermediate wire to hold the XOR of inputs a and b, another wire for XOR of c and d, and then combine these two with an XNOR to produce q. This decomposes the logic into smaller parts and changes the expression structure without altering the functionality.

This approach keeps the combinational logic style with assign statements, avoids always blocks (to keep it purely combinational), and provides better clarity on how the parity is computed step-by-step. It can also help synthesis tools to optimize or map gates more explicitly, or facilitate debugging or reuse of intermediate signals.

In summary:
- Compute ab_xor = a ^ b;
- Compute cd_xor = c ^ d;
- Compute q = ~(ab_xor ^ cd_xor) = ab_xor ~^ cd_xor;

This implements the same logic (even parity with inverted XOR) but in a decomposed manner.
