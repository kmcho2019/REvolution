Instead of directly using XOR and negation to produce the parity output, implement the logic through a combination of simpler gates by decomposing the parity function stepwise.

1. Calculate partial XORs for (a,b) and (c,d) separately.
2. Compute q as the XNOR of these two partial XORs, because q is 1 when the overall parity is even.
3. Express XNOR as an equality check: q = ~( (a^b) ^ (c^d) ) = ((a^b) == (c^d)).

This approach uses intermediate wires and comparators, reflecting a different architectural style while producing the same functionality.
