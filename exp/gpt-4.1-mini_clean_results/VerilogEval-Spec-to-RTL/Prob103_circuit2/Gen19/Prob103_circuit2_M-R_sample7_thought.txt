The original implementation uses a single assign statement with the reduction XNOR operator to produce the even parity output. To refactor this while keeping the core logic the same, I will use a combinational always block with an intermediate wire that computes the XOR of all inputs and then assign q to the inversion of this XOR result. This approach explicitly shows the parity calculation as an XOR followed by a negation, which is logically equivalent to the original reduction XNOR but structured differently. It also demonstrates the use of an always block and intermediate signals for combinational logic, providing an alternative coding style that may help in some synthesis or verification environments.
