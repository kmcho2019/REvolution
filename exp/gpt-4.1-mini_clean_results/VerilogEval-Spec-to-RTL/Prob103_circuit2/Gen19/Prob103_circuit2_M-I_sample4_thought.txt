The original design implements the logic as q = ~(a ^ b ^ c ^ d), which is minimal and straightforward for a 4-input even parity function. XOR gates tend to have moderate power and delay due to their transistor count and switching activity, but this is optimal at the RTL level for this parity check.

To further optimize for power and area, we can consider implementing the same logic using only NAND and NOR gates, which are simpler and generally have better drive strength and lower power in CMOS technology. Since the XOR is more complex, decomposing the logic into NAND/NOR gates can reduce the transistor count and switching nodes, thereby lowering power and area slightly.

Using De Morgan’s and Boolean algebra, the logic for q = ~(a ^ b ^ c ^ d) is equivalent to q being high when an even number of inputs are high. Implementing this exactly with only NAND/NOR gates would increase complexity, but replacing the final inverter with a NAND with tied inputs or using the fact that q can be computed as an XNOR of pairs and combining them via a NOR might yield improvements.

Alternatively, since XORs are associative, and q = XNOR(a,b,c,d) = XNOR(XNOR(a,b), XNOR(c,d)), this approach breaks the 4-input XOR into two 2-input XNORs and then XNOR of these results. Two-input XNORs can be implemented as ~(a ^ b) = (a & b) | (~a & ~b), which can be implemented with simple gates.

This may reduce switching activity because the intermediate signals reduce the depth of XOR chain and possibly share smaller gates.

Hence, implementing q as q = XNOR(XNOR(a,b), XNOR(c,d)) using simple gates may provide a power and area optimization.

I will implement the 2-input XNORs as (~a & ~b) | (a & b) and combine them.

This trades off minimal increase in logic for potentially reduced toggle activity and slightly better area and power.

Note: The delay will still be minimal and similar to single XOR chain.
