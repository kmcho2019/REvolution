Partition the 16-bit register into two separate 8-bit registers, each with its own byte-level enable (byteena[1] for upper byte, byteena[0] for lower byte). Implement each byte as an 8-bit synchronous register with synchronous active-low reset, driven by its corresponding byte enable signal. This approach leverages the inherent byte enables as individual clock enables for simpler and more efficient flip-flop implementations (in synthesis, often mapped to enable pins in registers), reducing combinational masking logic and improving timing, power, and area. The outputs of the two registers are concatenated to form the full 16-bit output.
