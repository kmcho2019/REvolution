Implement the 16-bit register as two separate 8-bit registers (upper and lower bytes), each with its own enable controlled by byteena[1] and byteena[0]. This modular approach splits the logic cleanly, potentially improving timing by reducing mux complexity and allowing synthesis to optimize each byte independently. On the positive edge of clk, if resetn is low, both registers reset synchronously. Otherwise, each byte updates only if its corresponding enable is asserted; if disabled, that byte retains its previous value.

This approach uses two 8-bit always blocks for clarity and potential better area and timing optimizations, explicitly associating enables with each byte and avoiding bitmask operations. It also simplifies the combinational logic feeding the flip-flops by decoupling upper and lower byte enables and data paths.
