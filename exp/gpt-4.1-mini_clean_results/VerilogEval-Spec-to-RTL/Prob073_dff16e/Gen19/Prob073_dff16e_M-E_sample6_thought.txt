Implement the 16 D flip-flops as two separate 8-bit registers, one for the upper byte and one for the lower byte, each with its own clock enable derived from the byte enable signals. On each clock edge, if resetn is low, both registers reset to zero synchronously. Otherwise, each 8-bit register updates only when its corresponding byte enable is asserted. This avoids combinational masking logic and reduces toggling inside the registers, potentially improving power efficiency and timing by using clock enables rather than masking the data inputs.

This design modularizes the byte enables directly into clock enables for each byte group of flip-flops, leveraging built-in flip-flop enable signals and minimizing unnecessary data toggling. The output q is formed by concatenating the two 8-bit registers. This approach differs structurally from masking logic by partitioning the register and controlling updates at the register enable level.
