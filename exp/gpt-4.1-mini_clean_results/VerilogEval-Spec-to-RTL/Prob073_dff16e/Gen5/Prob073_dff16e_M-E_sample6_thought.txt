Instead of implementing the 16 DFFs as a single 16-bit register with a combinational mux per byte, partition the register into two 8-bit registers, each with its own byte-enable control. Each byte register has its own always block triggered on the posedge of clk and synchronous active-low reset. This architectural separation allows for more explicit control and potentially easier timing and power optimization on each byte independently. The outputs are then concatenated to form the full 16-bit output q. This modular approach can simplify byte-level enable logic and help synthesis tools optimize each byte segment separately.
