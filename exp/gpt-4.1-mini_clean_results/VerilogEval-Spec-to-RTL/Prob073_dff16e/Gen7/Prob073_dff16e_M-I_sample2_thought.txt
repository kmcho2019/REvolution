To optimize for better performance, power, and area, I will merge the two separate 8-bit registers into a single 16-bit register controlled by a byte-enable mask. This reduces the number of always blocks from two to one, simplifying the clock tree and control logic, which may improve timing and area. The synchronous active-low reset remains as specified, applied in the same always block. The output q will be a registered output driven directly by this single 16-bit register, eliminating any combinational concatenation to reduce glitching and switching power on the output nets. By using a mask derived from byteena for conditional updating, unnecessary toggling of unaffected bytes is avoided, which also helps reduce dynamic power. This single-block approach is cleaner and more resource-efficient while preserving functional correctness.
