The original design is already minimal and optimal in terms of functionality, timing, and area for a single-bit D flip-flop with synchronous reset. Given the trivial logic and near-zero critical path, the only modest improvement for power could be to prevent unnecessary toggling of the output when the reset signal is continuously asserted. By checking if q is already zero before assigning it in the reset condition, we can avoid unnecessary output toggles, slightly reducing dynamic power consumption. This optimization does not affect functionality or timing but can reduce switching activity when reset is held asserted over multiple cycles.
