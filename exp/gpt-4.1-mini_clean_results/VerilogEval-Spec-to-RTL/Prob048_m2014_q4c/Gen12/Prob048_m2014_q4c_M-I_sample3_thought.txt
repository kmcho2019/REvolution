The original design is already minimal and efficient for a synchronous reset D flip-flop. To optimize for power, we can reduce unnecessary toggling by gating the clock only when the input or reset changes, thus preventing the flip-flop from capturing the same value and toggling the output unnecessarily. This clock gating can reduce dynamic power consumption at the cost of slightly increased area and complexity. Since the design is simple, the gating logic is straightforward: enable the clock only when `d` differs from `q` or when reset is asserted. This approach balances power savings with minimal area and timing impact.
