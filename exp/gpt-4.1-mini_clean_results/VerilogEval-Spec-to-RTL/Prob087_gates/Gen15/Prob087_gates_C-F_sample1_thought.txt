To achieve an optimal balance of clarity, modularity, and PPA efficiency, we fuse the best ideas from both examples as follows:

- Define simple gate modules only for fundamental two-input gates that directly implement their logic without intermediate inversion (AND, OR, XOR, ANDNOT).
- Implement NAND, NOR, and XNOR outputs in the top module using direct continuous assignments by inverting the shared basic gate outputs (AND, OR, XOR) instead of instantiating separate modules. This reduces module overhead, area, and power while maintaining readability.
- Compute shared intermediate signals (and_ab, or_ab, xor_ab) once to avoid redundant logic and minimize switching activity.
- Keep the design fully structural and modular for easy verification and potential reuse.
- Use concise inline inversion for inverted outputs to minimize extra nets and gates.

This fusion leverages the modular clarity and reusability of basic gate modules with the efficiency of direct assignments for complementary gates, resulting in a clean, efficient, and synthesis-friendly design with minimal area and power, while preserving explicit structural style and easy testability.
