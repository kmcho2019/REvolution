To combine the strengths of both examples, I will fuse the clarity, modularity, and power/area efficiency of using shared intermediate signals (from Example 1) with the centralized logic grouping style of Example 2 that organizes all outputs together. This fusion keeps the logic in one combinational always block to localize the combinational logic, as in Example 2, which aids readability and potential optimizations in certain synthesis flows, while leveraging shared intermediate signals as named variables inside the block to avoid duplicated computations, thereby improving power and area efficiency. The outputs remain individual one-bit wires assigned from the reg vector bits, maintaining explicit output interfaces. This approach combines centralized logic control with efficient reuse of common intermediate results, resulting in a clean, efficient, and maintainable design.
