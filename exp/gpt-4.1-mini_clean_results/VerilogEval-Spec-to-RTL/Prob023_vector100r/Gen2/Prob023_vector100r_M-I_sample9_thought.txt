The existing implementation is already optimal in terms of timing and area, with direct bitwise connections minimizing logic depth and area overhead. To further optimize power slightly, I propose using a single continuous assignment with vector slicing and concatenation instead of a generate loop. This reduces the number of continuous assignments and might help synthesis tools better optimize the routing and reduce net count slightly.

By writing `assign out = {in[0], in[1], ..., in[99]}` in reverse order directly, the synthesis tool can recognize this as a bit reversal pattern and may apply efficient wiring optimizations internally. This reduces syntactic complexity and improves readability.

Since the vector size is fixed at 100, I'll unroll the reversed bits in one concatenation expression without the generate loop.

This will maintain the same timing profile and area footprint but may slightly reduce power by lowering net switching and complexity in the netlist.

If truly synthesizable and practical, this is the minimal and cleanest RTL for bit reversal.
