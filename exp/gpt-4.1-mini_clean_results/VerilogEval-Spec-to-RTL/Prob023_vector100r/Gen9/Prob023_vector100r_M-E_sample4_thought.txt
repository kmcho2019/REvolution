An alternative architectural approach is to implement the bit reversal using a binary tree of 2-to-1 multiplexers that progressively swap bits at different granularity levels. This method structurally uses stages where each stage swaps pairs of bits separated by powers of two distances, effectively implementing a bit reversal as a series of bit swaps controlled by fixed signals.

Key aspects:
- Use a fixed series of 7 stages (since 2^7 = 128 > 100), each stage performs swapping of bit pairs separated by 2^stage bits.
- At each stage, bits are conditionally swapped or passed through.
- Since the swapping control signals are constants (1 or 0), the swaps can be hardwired without runtime controls.
- The design is fully combinational but expresses the reversal as layered bit permutations.
- This hierarchical approach may help synthesis tools optimize routing and timing by structuring the reversal in stages.
- The output is connected after the final swapping stage.
- Inputs less than 128 bits are handled by zero-extending in wiring but output only uses 100 bits.

This approach offers a different structural viewpoint compared to direct indexing and may facilitate better physical design due to staged localized bit-swaps.
