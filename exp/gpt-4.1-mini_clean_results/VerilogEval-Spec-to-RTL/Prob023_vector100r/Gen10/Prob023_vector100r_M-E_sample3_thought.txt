To implement a bit-reversal of a 100-bit input without explicitly enumerating each bit, I will leverage Verilog's vector slicing and concatenation in a hierarchical manner:

- Divide the 100-bit input into 10 chunks of 10 bits each.
- Reverse the order of these 10 chunks (i.e., swap chunk 0 with chunk 9, chunk 1 with chunk 8, etc.).
- Within each 10-bit chunk, reverse the bit order.
- This approach exploits the power of smaller building blocks, making the logic easier to manage.
- Implement a helper module BitRev10 that reverses a 10-bit vector.
- Instantiate 10 BitRev10 modules for the chunks and concatenate their outputs in reverse chunk order.
- This hierarchical structure supports reuse, is easy to scale, and synthesizes efficiently without an explicit generate loop over the entire 100 bits.
- The top module will instantiate these components and connect everything accordingly.

This method reduces the complexity by chunking and applies simple reversal in two stages: chunk order reversal and intra-chunk bit reversal.
