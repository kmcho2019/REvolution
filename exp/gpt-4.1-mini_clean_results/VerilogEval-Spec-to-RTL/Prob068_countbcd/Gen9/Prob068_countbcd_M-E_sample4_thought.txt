I will implement the 4-digit BCD counter using a single 16-bit register representing all four digits together. Instead of incrementing each digit separately, I will treat the entire 16-bit BCD value as a packed number and increment it by one every clock cycle. After the increment, if any digit exceeds 9, I will subtract 10 (BCD correction) from that digit and propagate the carry to the next digit, similar to how BCD addition works.

To implement this efficiently:
- I will use a synchronous process to increment the entire 16-bit counter as a binary number (straight +1).
- Then, I will run a combinational "BCD correction" step to adjust each digit and generate carries.
- The enable signals (ena) for digits 1-3 will be generated based on whether the previous digit caused a carry (i.e., needed correction).

This method avoids nested if-else chains and multiple increment function calls, reduces combinational path depth by splitting increment and correction, and keeps carry propagation explicit but simple. It leverages the common BCD increment approach via correction after binary increment, which can have better timing and area characteristics.

The synchronous reset will initialize the whole counter to zero.

Finally, I will output the 16-bit counter and ena signals accordingly.
