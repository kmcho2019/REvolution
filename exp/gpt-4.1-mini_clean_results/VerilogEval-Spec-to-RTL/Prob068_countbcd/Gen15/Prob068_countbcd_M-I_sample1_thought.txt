The original solution is functionally correct but the nested if-else carry propagation inside a single clock cycle creates a long combinational path, which hurts performance (timing) and increases power due to large switching activity.

To optimize PPA:
- Break down the carry propagation into two sequential steps across clock cycles (a ripple carry through pipeline stages), shortening the critical path per cycle.
- Implement the counter as a 2-stage pipeline: first increment ones and generate carry to tens, then in the next cycle increment tens (if carry), propagate carry to hundreds, and so on. This reduces the max combinational delay per cycle.
- Use separate registers per digit to reduce logic complexity and help synthesis optimize better.
- Register the enable signals to reduce glitches and switching.
- Use combinational logic only to detect carry conditions, avoiding a function inside always block.
- Keep synchronous active-high reset.
  
This modification slightly increases latency by one clock cycle but greatly improves max frequency (smaller critical path), reduces power from shorter combinational paths, and may reduce area since logic is simpler per stage.

The output q combines the four digits. The ena outputs are registered signals indicating when the next digit should increment. The ena signals update one cycle after the digit reaches 9, matching carry conditions cleanly.

Overall, this staged approach balances performance and power while maintaining correct BCD counting with synchronous reset.
