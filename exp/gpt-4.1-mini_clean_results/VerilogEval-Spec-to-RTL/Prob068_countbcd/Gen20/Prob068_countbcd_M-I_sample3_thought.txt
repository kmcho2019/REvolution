To improve the PPA (timing, power, area) of the original 4-digit BCD counter design, I will focus primarily on timing and power without compromising readability or correctness:

- Timing improvement: The original design increments all digits in a single clock cycle through nested if-elses, creating a long carry chain and a critical path. To shorten this critical path, I will split the increment process into two clock cycles using a simple two-phase counting scheme.

  Specifically, I will:
  1. Increment the ones digit every clock cycle.
  2. Use enable signals to trigger incrementing the upper digits in subsequent cycles, so that only one digit increment (and carry propagation) happens per clock cycle.
  
- This approach effectively pipelines the counting and breaks the long combinational carry chain, improving timing.

- Power improvement: Because only one digit increments per cycle, toggling activity is reduced. The enable signals control whether higher digits increment, so digit registers toggle only when needed.

- Area impact: Slightly more control logic is added but is minimal and well worth the timing and power benefit.

- The synchronous reset is maintained, and outputs remain the same.

- The enable signals remain combinational as in the original spec to indicate when the upper digits are about to increment.

This staged incrementing is a common tradeoff to achieve better timing by spreading carry operations over multiple cycles and minimizing logic depth per cycle.
