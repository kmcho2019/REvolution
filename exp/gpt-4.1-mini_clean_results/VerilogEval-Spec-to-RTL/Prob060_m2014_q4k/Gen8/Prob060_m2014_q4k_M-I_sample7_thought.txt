To optimize the original design for better power, performance, and area (PPA), I will focus on simplifying the implementation while maintaining clarity and functionality. The original uses a separate DFF module with synchronous active-low reset and a generate loop for instantiation. This modularity is good, but it adds wiring overhead.

Improvement strategy:
- Combine the shift register logic into a single always block using a 4-bit register vector, which reduces wiring and synthesis complexity.
- Use synchronous active-low reset on the vector register.
- This approach eliminates the generate loop and separate DFF module, reducing code overhead and potentially improving area and power due to fewer signals and less interconnect.
- Output the MSB of the shift register as the output.
- Keep the same interface and synchronous reset semantics.
- Non-blocking assignments remain consistent for reliable sequential logic.

This combined vector approach leverages synthesis optimizations for shift registers and should reduce area and power slightly while maintaining timing performance, as only a single 4-bit register is instantiated instead of 4 separate flip-flops and wiring.

Overall, this design is more compact and can be slightly more efficient in synthesis due to reduced wiring and module overhead.
