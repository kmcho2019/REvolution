Fuse the modularity and clear structure of individual D flip-flop instantiations from Example 1 with the vectorized and parameterized shift register approach from Example 2 for an optimal balance of clarity, maintainability, and synthesis efficiency.

Key points of fusion:
- Define a single, reusable DFF module with synchronous active-low reset for clean modularity and clear reset semantics.
- Inside ShiftReg, implement the 4-bit shift register as an array of four DFF instances connected in series, rather than a vector register in one always block. This keeps modular clarity, aids potential FPGA technology mapping to flip-flop primitives, and makes reset distribution explicit.
- Keep the parameterization from Example 2 to support scalability.
- Avoid using a single vector always block reset to maintain explicit per-flip-flop control and modularity; this can help in designs where control per flip-flop might be needed later.
- Shift logic handled by wiring data between DFF instances, taking 'in' as the first input and 'out' from the last DFF output.
- The TopModule instantiates ShiftReg cleanly, maintaining a neat top-level interface.

This fusion results in a highly maintainable, parameterized, modular shift register design with clear synchronous active-low reset, suitable for synthesis with good PPA characteristics and easy future extensions.
