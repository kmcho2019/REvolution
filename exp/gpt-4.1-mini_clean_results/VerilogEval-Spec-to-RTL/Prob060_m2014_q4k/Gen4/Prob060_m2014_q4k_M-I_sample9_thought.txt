To optimize the existing design for better power and timing while keeping correctness and clarity, I will introduce a clock enable signal to prevent unnecessary shifting when the input does not change, thus reducing dynamic power. This clock enable can be implemented by comparing the current input with the last stored bit in the shift register and only shifting when the input differs or when reset is asserted. 

Also, instead of instantiating individual DFF modules, I will implement the 4-bit shift register as a single always block with a synchronous active-low reset and a clock enable signal. This reduces hierarchical overhead, potentially improving timing and area by leveraging compact register inference by synthesis tools. The design remains simple, clear, and synthesizable.

This approach retains synchronous reset semantics and positive edge triggering, while minimizing toggle rates in the flip-flops and reducing unnecessary data movement along the shift chain, thereby improving power consumption and potentially timing.

Summary of improvements:
- Add clock enable to reduce shifting when input does not change.
- Flatten hierarchy into a single always block for the 4-bit shift register.
- Retain synchronous active-low reset and positive-edge clocking.
