To fuse the best ideas from both examples, I will implement a modular, parameterized, and clean 4-bit shift register that balances explicitness, scalability, and code clarity:

- Use a single reusable DFF module with synchronous active-low reset and non-blocking assignment.
- Instead of a generate loop, I will manually instantiate four DFFs with explicitly named instances and signals to combine the clarity and debugging ease of the first example with the modularity of the second.
- This gives explicit wiring and easier readability and debugging, while keeping modular reusable flip-flops and clean code.
- The synchronous active-low reset is handled inside each DFF, maintaining reset semantics.
- Output is taken from the last flip-flop.
- This design is concise but explicit, provides clear hierarchical modularity and easy extension (like adding scan or enable later), and is friendly for synthesis and PPA optimization.
