To create a superior solution, I will fuse the modular clarity of using a parameterized single-bit DFF with the area and power efficiency of vectorized design. 

The fusion strategy is:
- Define a single-bit DFF module with synchronous active-high reset and negative-edge clock trigger, parameterized by its reset value.
- In TopModule, instantiate a single 8-bit register with synchronous reset triggered on the negative edge of clk.
- Use an always block for the entire 8-bit vector rather than instantiating multiple single-bit modules or many module instances, reducing area and wiring overhead.
- Assign the reset value as the 8-bit constant 0x34 in the synchronous reset block.
- This approach combines the modular reset clarity (with a well-defined reset value) and vectorized register efficiency, improving area and power without losing modularity clarity.
- The code is clean, concise, synthesizable, and easy to maintain or extend.
- Negative edge triggering is maintained per requirement, but the single vector always block helps timing and area.

This fusion results in better area and power than 8 instantiated bit-level modules, while being clearer and more modular than a monolithic always block with hardwired reset value.
