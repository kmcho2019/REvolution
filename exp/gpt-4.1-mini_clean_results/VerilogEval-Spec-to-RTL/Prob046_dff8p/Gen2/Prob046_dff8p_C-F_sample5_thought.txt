Fuse the modular bit-level DFF instantiation from Example 1 with the simplicity and clarity of synchronous reset to 0x34 from Example 2 by creating a parameterized DFF module without an unused reset input and directly implementing synchronous reset inside each DFF. This removes the unnecessary mux logic on the data input from Example 1 and avoids redundant or unused reset signals, improving area and power. At the same time, using individual DFFs instantiated via generate improves modularity and scalability over a monolithic register.  
Each DFF triggers on negedge clk, has synchronous reset that loads its respective bit from 0x34, and otherwise loads d[i]. This design preserves the reset semantics, is easily synthesizable, and clearly separates each flip-flop, facilitating timing and power optimization.  
This fusion combines modularity, clear synchronous reset logic, minimal mux delay, and no unused inputs, achieving good performance, area, and power balance.
