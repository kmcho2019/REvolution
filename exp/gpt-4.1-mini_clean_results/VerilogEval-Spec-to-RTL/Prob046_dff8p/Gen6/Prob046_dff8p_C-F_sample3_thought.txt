Fuse the advantages of both examples by implementing a single 8-bit register with synchronous reset triggered on the negative edge of clk. Instead of instantiating eight separate single-bit DFF modules, use one always block that directly models eight D flip-flops as a vector. On reset, the entire register synchronously loads the constant 8-bit reset value 0x34, otherwise it captures the input vector d. This reduces module instantiations, interconnect complexity, and overhead of passing reset bits per DFF, improving area and power efficiency while maintaining clarity and modularity. Using non-blocking assignment and a single always block triggered on negedge clk aligns well with synthesis and timing goals.
