Fuse the clarity and modularity of Example 1's clean vectorized synchronous negative-edge triggered reset flip-flop design with Example 2's power-saving clock enable logic that suppresses unnecessary updates. 

Specifically:
- Use a parameterized 8-bit DFF module triggered on the negedge clk with synchronous active-high reset setting q to 0x34.
- Add a clock enable signal derived from a simple XOR reduction (d ^ q) to update q only when d changes or reset is asserted, minimizing toggling and power.
- Keep the design simple and clear with non-blocking assignments.
- Retain modularity by encapsulating the 8-bit register with reset and clock enable in a single module, then instantiate it in TopModule.
- Avoid extra storage for past inputs or complex comparators to keep area and timing minimal.
- This approach balances performance, power, and area well by combining straightforward synchronous negative edge reset logic with an effective, lightweight clock enable for power optimization.
