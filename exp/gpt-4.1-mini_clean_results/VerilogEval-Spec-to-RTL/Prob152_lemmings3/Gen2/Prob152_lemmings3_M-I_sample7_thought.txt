To improve correctness and align strictly with the problem specifications, the FSM must strictly prioritize fall > dig > bump for state transitions and ignore bumps in the same cycle as ground change or when falling/digging.

The key subtlety is: bumps must be ignored if ground is lost or regained this cycle or if currently falling or digging. This means bumps only affect direction switch when walking on solid ground (ground=1), and no ground transition occurs in this cycle.

To capture this precisely, we introduce a registered 'dir' bit to store the walking direction to keep direction stable during fall and digging, as the problem specifies direction is preserved through these states.

The states become simpler: WALKING, FALLING, DIGGING. Direction is a separate register.

Outputs depend on both state and direction.

Transitions:

- On reset: walk left
- Fall if ground=0 and was walking or digging
- When falling, wait for ground=1 to resume walking in stored direction
- When walking and ground=1, dig if dig=1
- When walking and bump(s) and no ground change, switch direction
- Bump has no effect in fall or dig, or when ground changes this cycle (i.e., ignore bumps during ground transitions)
- Priorities enforced strictly: fall > dig > bump

We implement edge detection of ground signal by keeping previous ground value to detect ground changes (fall or landing).

This approach reduces states and code complexity, makes direction explicit and stable, and avoids output mismatches. It also simplifies encoding (2-bit state plus 1-bit direction).

This design meets Moore FSM requirements and solves the direction/output mismatch problem.

For optimization: fewer states (3 states) and one direction bit reduce area; direction stored explicitly avoids combinational glitches on outputs; asynchronous reset as required.

The code is clean, modular, and matches all specifications.
