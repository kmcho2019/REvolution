Use a two-process FSM approach splitting the concerns of direction and mode separately to simplify state tracking and combinational complexity.

Implement a 1-bit direction register (0=left, 1=right) updated only on bumps while walking (not falling or digging).

Implement a 2-bit mode register with three states:
  - WALK (00)
  - FALL (01)
  - DIG (10)

Make mode transitions prioritized as per spec: falling if ground lost, then digging if commanded on ground and walking, otherwise walking.

Use synchronous reset to initialize direction to left and mode to WALK.

Use separate combinational logic to compute next_mode and next_direction with clear priority and no nested conditionals, minimizing logic depth.

Outputs derive from direction and mode:

- walk_left/walk_right asserted only when mode is WALK and digging is not active (mode DIG means digging active)
- aaah asserted only in FALL mode
- digging asserted only in DIG mode

This clean separation of direction and mode reduces combinational logic depth by avoiding combined state bits and avoids orthogonal flags outside of mode, improving clarity and potentially timing.

Because bump handling affects only direction when walking, direction updates are simple. Mode handles behavior transitions with priority.

This approach also naturally encodes the priority and mutual exclusivity of modes without complex nested conditions.
