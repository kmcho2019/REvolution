Implement a two-bit FSM encoding with explicit states for walking left/right, falling left/right, and add a separate digging flag. Instead of encoding digging inside the state bits, keep it orthogonal to preserve clarity. 

Use a fully enumerated state encoding (4 states):
- WALK_LEFT
- WALK_RIGHT
- FALL_LEFT
- FALL_RIGHT

Define state transitions as follows on each clock edge (with asynchronous reset to WALK_LEFT and not digging):

- Priority 1: If ground==0 and not already falling, transition to FALL_* corresponding to current direction and stop digging.
- Priority 2: If falling and ground==1, transition back to WALK_* in the same direction, digging off.
- Priority 3: If walking and digging==0 and ground==1 and dig==1, start digging (digging flag set), keep walking state.
- Priority 4: If digging and ground==1, continue digging.
- Priority 5: If digging and ground==0, transition to FALL_* with digging cleared.
- Priority 6: If walking and bump(s) occur and not digging or falling, change direction:
  - bump_left -> WALK_RIGHT
  - bump_right -> WALK_LEFT
  - both bumps -> flip direction (from walk left to walk right or vice versa)
- Priority 7: Otherwise stay in the same state and keep digging flag.

This design cleanly separates state and digging flag for simple logic. Outputs are directly derived from the state (walking or falling) and digging flag. Walking outputs are only asserted when in walking state and not digging. Falling state asserts aaah, digging asserts digging output.

This approach is a straightforward Moore FSM with a separate digging flag and clear priority, but the difference is the explicit 4-state FSM rather than bit-field encoded direction and mode. This may simplify synthesis and debugging and supports clear state naming.
