To fix the mismatches on walk_left and walk_right outputs, the key is to ensure the walking direction is fully encoded in the state itself, not kept separately in a `dir` register. This avoids any inconsistencies between direction and state during transitions.

We will encode the walking direction as part of the four states: Walking Left (WL), Walking Right (WR), Falling Left (FL), Falling Right (FR), Digging Left (DL), and Digging Right (DR). This leads to a 3-bit or larger state encoding. For simplicity, use a 3-bit state encoding to include direction in every state.

State encoding:

- WL (walking left)
- WR (walking right)
- FL (falling left)
- FR (falling right)
- DL (digging left)
- DR (digging right)

The FSM transitions now carry the direction explicitly, so no separate `dir` register is needed.

Outputs are assigned purely based on the current state: walk_left = (state == WL), walk_right = (state == WR), aaah = (falling states), digging = (digging states).

State transitions:

- When walking and ground disappears: go to falling state with current direction
- When walking and dig=1 and ground=1: go to digging state with current direction
- When walking and bump(s) detected and ground=1 and not digging: switch walking direction state
- When falling and ground=1: resume walking state with stored direction
- When digging and ground=0: transition to falling state with same direction
- When digging and ground=1: remain digging
- When falling and ground=0: remain falling

Because direction is included in state, no mismatch between direction and state can occur, eliminating output glitches.

Use an asynchronous reset to WL.

This approach cleans up the design and matches the Moore FSM requirement with outputs depending only on the current state.
