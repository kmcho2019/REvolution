Use a two-part FSM design separating the "action" and "direction" information to cleanly handle direction retention and state priorities. 

We define three main action states: WALKING, FALLING, DIGGING. The walking direction is tracked separately in a reg (dir), which can be LEFT (0) or RIGHT (1).

On reset, the Lemming starts walking left. The FSM prioritizes transitions as follows:

- If ground=0, Lemming FALLS, and direction is preserved.
- Else if dig=1 and currently walking on ground, Lemming starts DIGGING.
- Else if bumped (bump_left or bump_right) and currently walking, direction changes accordingly:
  * If bumped on left (bump_left=1), direction set to RIGHT
  * If bumped on right (bump_right=1), direction set to LEFT
  * If both bumps, direction reverses
- While falling or digging, bumps and dig signals are ignored.
- When ground returns while falling, transition to walking, keeping original direction.
- When ground disappears while digging, transition to falling.
- When ground is present and digging, continue digging.

Outputs depend on the current action and direction:

- walk_left/walk_right = 1 during walking or digging in that direction.
- aaah = 1 during falling.
- digging = 1 during digging.

This architectural separation cleanly isolates direction changes from the action state, simplifying direction retention and update logic and ensuring correct precedence and signal masking.
