To fuse the best ideas from both examples, I will keep the clear three-state FSM (WALK, FALL, DIG) with a 2-bit state register and a single direction bit. I will improve timing and clarity by separating the next-state and next-direction combinational logic into a pure combinational always block, and the state/direction registers updated in a separate sequential always block with asynchronous reset.

This separation reduces combinational path complexity and makes the design easier to analyze and optimize by synthesis tools. 

I will keep the optimized bump detection gating only in the WALK state, reducing unnecessary switching and logic toggling. Direction updates will only happen when walking and bumps occur, with combined bump conditions handled succinctly.

The priority order of fall > dig > bump direction change is strictly followed in the next-state logic.

Outputs remain Moore type, directly derived from registered state and direction.

This fusion yields a clean, efficient, and easy-to-maintain FSM with minimal logic complexity, improved timing potential, and controlled switching activity to reduce power.
