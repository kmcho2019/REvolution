The original design uses a structural approach with basic gates to build a 2:1 MUX, implementing f = (~x3 & x2) | (x3 & x1). While this is functionally correct and modular, it introduces extra gate delays, area, and power due to multiple intermediate signals and instantiations.

To optimize for better PPA (performance, power, and area), we can replace the structural MUX with a simple continuous assignment using a conditional operator (ternary operator) directly implementing f = (x3 ? x1 : x2). This reduces logic depth to one level, removes unnecessary intermediate nets and gate instances, and yields minimal area and power usage.

This optimized solution is simpler, faster (one combinational level), consumes less power, occupies less area, and is fully synthesizable and functionally equivalent to the original truth table.
