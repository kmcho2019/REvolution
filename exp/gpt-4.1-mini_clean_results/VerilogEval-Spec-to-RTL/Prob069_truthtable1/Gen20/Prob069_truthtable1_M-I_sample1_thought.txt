The original design uses structural gate-level implementation with basic gates and a 2:1 MUX to express f = (~x3 & x2) | (x3 & x1). While modular and clear, this approach introduces multiple gate delays, interconnect overhead, and switching activity that increase power and delay.

To improve PPA, I will simplify the design by using a single continuous assignment with a ternary operator:

- This reduces the logic to one-level mux behavior internally, allowing the synthesis tool to map directly to optimal gates.
- Eliminates the overhead of multiple module instantiations and additional nets.
- Minimal code size improves readability and synthesis efficiency.
- Functionally identical, maintaining perfect correctness according to the truth table.

This approach reduces gate count, power, and delay and likely slightly decreases area due to less wiring and module overhead.

Hence, I implement the TopModule with a single line combinational assignment: f = x3 ? x1 : x2.
