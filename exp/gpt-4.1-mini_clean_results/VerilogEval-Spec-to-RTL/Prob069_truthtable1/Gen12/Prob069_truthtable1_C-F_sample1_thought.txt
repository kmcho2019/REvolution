To create a superior solution, we combine the modular clarity from Example 1 with the concise and efficient combinational logic of Example 2. We use a simple continuous assignment implementing the minimized boolean expression from the truth table: f = (~x3 & x2) | (x3 & x1). This expression is minimal and directly corresponds to the required logic.

Instead of separate AND and OR module instantiations, or a verbose case statement, a single assign statement gives the cleanest, most efficient hardware with minimal gate levels, reducing area and power overhead and improving clarity.

This approach keeps the modular reasoning of the two minterms from Example 1 but implements them in a single line for compactness and performance.

Thus, the design is:
- A single module TopModule with inputs x3,x2,x1 and output f.
- f implemented as a continuous assign with the simplified boolean expression.

This design provides the best tradeoff of clarity, maintainability, minimal logic levels, and efficient synthesis.
