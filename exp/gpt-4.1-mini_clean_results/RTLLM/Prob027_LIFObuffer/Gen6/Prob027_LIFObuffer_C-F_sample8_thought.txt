Fuse the upward-counting stack pointer approach (from Example 2) with the clean synchronous logic of Example 1, but optimize and simplify initialization and pointer width. Use a single always block for all sequential logic including reset, push, and pop to ensure atomic operations per clock. Initialize stack_mem in the reset block by a simple for-loop, avoiding multiple always blocks for reset. Keep SP as a 2-bit register (since max count is 4) for minimal area and simpler arithmetic.

On reset, clear SP to 0 (empty) and clear stack_mem entries and dataOut. On push (RW=0), if not full, store dataIn at stack_mem[SP] and increment SP. On pop (RW=1), if not empty, decrement SP and output stack_mem[SP-1]. Flags EMPTY and FULL are combinationally derived from SP.

This design balances clear semantics (SP points to next free slot), minimal pointer width, clear reset logic, and synchronous operation. dataOut updates only on pop to avoid unnecessary toggling. The single always block reduces complexity and aids synthesis, while the for-loop reset inside the always block is a widely synthesizable pattern. This fusion gives a clean, performant, and compact solution suitable for timing, power, and area optimization.
