Fuse the memory-array-based approach from Example 2 with the shift-register simplicity of Example 1 to optimize timing, power, and area while maintaining clear code and efficient operations.

Fusion strategy:
- Use a small 4x4-bit memory array (stack_mem) like Example 2 for lower power on writes and no large shifts.
- Use a 2-bit stack pointer (SP) counting entries 0 to 4 to track stack depth.
- On push (RW=0, EN=1, not full), write dataIn at stack_mem[SP], increment SP.
- On pop (RW=1, EN=1, not empty), decrement SP, read dataOut from stack_mem[SP - 1].
- To remove combinational delay of variable index for pop dataOut read, implement a registered pipeline:
  * Capture pop event on clock edge, decrement SP synchronously.
  * Register dataOut after SP update, using stack_mem[SP-1] read before decrementing SP.
- Avoid clearing memory on reset or pop to reduce switching.
- Initialize SP and dataOut on reset.
- Generate FULL and EMPTY flags combinationally from SP.
- Ensure mutually exclusive push and pop via EN and RW and full/empty checks.
- Keep dataOut registered on pop, unchanged otherwise.
- This merges the small memory and pointer approach with the cleaner timing separation and low switching of Example 2, while avoiding large shift registers.

This design improves timing by avoiding wide shifts and complicated arithmetic on variable indices by pipelining SP decrement and dataOut update, reduces power by eliminating unnecessary clears, and keeps area low with a small memory and 2-bit pointer.
