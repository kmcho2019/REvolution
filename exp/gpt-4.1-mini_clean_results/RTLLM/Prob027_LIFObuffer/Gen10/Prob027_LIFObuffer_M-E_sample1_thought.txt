Implement the 4-entry 4-bit LIFO buffer as a true stack with a 2-bit stack pointer counting upwards from 0 (empty) to 4 (full). Unlike the previous approach that decrements SP on push, this design increments SP on push and decrements on pop, making SP directly represent the number of stored elements.

Key architectural differences and improvements:
1. SP ranges from 0 (empty) to 4 (full), simplifying push/pop pointer updates and index calculations.
2. On a write (push), dataIn is stored at stack_mem[SP], then SP increments.
3. On a read (pop), SP decrements first, then dataOut is loaded from stack_mem[SP-1].
4. The memory is not cleared on pop to reduce unnecessary switching; only the SP controls buffer content.
5. RESET synchronously clears SP to zero, resets dataOut, and zeroes stack_mem to keep known state.
6. EMPTY and FULL flags are combinational signals derived from SP.
7. Separate enable signals for push and pop avoid spurious operations, reducing switching activity.
8. Operations occur only if EN is asserted, ensuring no unintended stack changes when disabled.
9. dataOut is updated only on pop; otherwise, it holds its value.
10. Use non-blocking assignments consistently in sequential logic.

This approach offers clearer pointer semantics, easier indexing, and potentially better timing and power due to simplified SP usage and indexing.
