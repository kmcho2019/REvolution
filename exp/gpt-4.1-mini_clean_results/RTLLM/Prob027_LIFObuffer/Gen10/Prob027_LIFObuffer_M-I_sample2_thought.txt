To optimize the LIFObuffer design for better PPA:

1. Separate pointer update and data output for the pop operation to avoid read-after-write hazards and improve timing. Implement a two-cycle pop process: on the first cycle decrement SP, on the next cycle output data at the new SP. This pipelining eases timing on stack_mem access and dataOut update.

2. Use a registered "pop_data" buffer to hold the popped data, updated after SP changes, then drive dataOut from this register. This avoids combinational read of stack_mem[SP-1] immediately after SP decrement.

3. Keep push operation as single cycle for simplicity and low latency.

4. To reduce switching, clear stack_mem entries on reset only, but do not clear on pop. This reduces toggling during normal operation.

5. Retain EN gating and synchronous reset.

6. Add an FSM-like approach with a pop state to separate pointer decrement and dataOut update over two cycles only for pop. Push remains combinationally simple.

This pipeline approach trades an extra cycle latency on pop for better timing and easier synthesis, potentially improving WNS and power. The design remains fully synchronous and correctly flagged.

The pointer SP still points to the next free slot (0 = empty, 4 = full).

Flags EMPTY and FULL remain combinational from SP.

DataOut updates only after pop operation completes, holding valid popped data stable.

This approach is widely used in hardware stacks to handle timing constraints and memory read delays.
