The current design is functionally correct and compact for a 4-entry 4-bit LIFO buffer. To further optimize for better power, performance, and area (PPA), I will:

1. Simplify the reset logic by removing the for-loop inside the synchronous reset. The stack memory content upon reset is generally don't-care for functionality, especially since empty/full flags and SP fully define the buffer state. This reduces reset critical path and logic area.

2. Implement a gated write-enable signal for stack_mem to avoid unnecessary writes, reducing dynamic power. Writes only happen on push (RW=0), EN=1, and when the buffer is not full.

3. Cleanly separate the dataOut update for pop (RW=1) operations, updating it from the stack_mem with minimal timing dependency.

4. Keep SP encoding as a simple binary counter (3 bits), counting the number of elements (0 to 4), with combinational flags EMPTY (SP==0) and FULL (SP==4).

5. Use non-blocking assignments consistently and update SP before reading stack_mem on pop to ensure correct timing.

6. Remove clearing of stack_mem on pop to reduce toggling.

These changes simplify the design, reduce area by eliminating unneeded reset clearing, reduce power by gating memory writes, and maintain or improve timing by reducing logic on critical paths.

This strategy balances all three PPA factors effectively for a small LIFO buffer.
