To optimize for better PPA (performance, power, and area), I will make the following improvements:

1. Performance:
   - Modify the pop (read) operation so that `dataOut` is updated combinationally from the top stack element instead of registering it after decrementing SP. This reduces read latency by providing `dataOut` directly without waiting for the next clock edge.
   - Use the stack pointer (SP) as the index of the next free slot, where SP=0 means empty, SP=4 means full. Push writes at SP, then increments SP. Pop decrements SP, but `dataOut` is always driven from `stack_mem[SP-1]` combinationally if not empty. This avoids a clock cycle delay in output and improves performance.

2. Power:
   - Prevent unnecessary writes to stack_mem on push when full, and no writes during pop.
   - Clear stack_mem locations only on reset, not on every pop (already done).
   - Since `dataOut` is combinational, no extra clock toggling occurs there.
   - Enable signal (EN) gating operations remains.

3. Area:
   - Keep SP as a 3-bit counter.
   - Use a generate loop for stack_mem clearing on reset to potentially help synthesis tools optimize.
   - Reduce logic in sequential block by shifting `dataOut` update outside the clocked block to a combinational block.

Overall, this approach removes the cycle latency on pop output, potentially improving timing (lower WNS), saves power by preventing unnecessary dataOut register toggling, and keeps the area minimal.

This also aligns with typical stack behavior where the top of stack data is accessible immediately after push or pop.

Note: We still follow synchronous push/pop controlled by EN and RW with proper boundary checks.
