The superior design merges the clarity and power optimizations from both examples:

1. Use a single always_ff block for synchronous logic with non-blocking assignments for SP, dataOut, and stack_mem, ensuring clean and safe register updates.
2. Retain the SP counting scheme from 4 (empty) down to 0 (full), which is intuitive and minimal in bit width (3 bits).
3. Calculate push and pop operations combinationally, gating stack_mem writes only on push operations, preventing unnecessary toggling.
4. Update dataOut only on pop to minimize switching.
5. Avoid clearing stack_mem on pop for power saving.
6. On reset, clear stack_mem, SP, and dataOut synchronously, using a loop for initialization as standard practice; synthesis tools handle this efficiently.
7. Combine push and pop conditions into a single valid_op signal to gate SP updates and reduce unnecessary toggling.
8. Minimize combinational arithmetic depth by precomputing next_SP clearly.
9. Use clear, descriptive comments.
10. Maintain simple combinational EMPTY and FULL flags derived from SP for minimal logic.

This fusion achieves a balance between performance (short critical path), power (minimal toggling and gated writes), and area (simple pointer logic and small memory). The design is modular, clean, and synthesis-friendly.
