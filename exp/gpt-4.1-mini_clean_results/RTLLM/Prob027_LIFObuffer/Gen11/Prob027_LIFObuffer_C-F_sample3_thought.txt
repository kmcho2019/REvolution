Fuse the two designs by adopting the clear and power-efficient control signals and minimal reset logic of Example 2 with the safer and more timing-friendly dataOut update approach of Example 1. Specifically:

- Use a stack pointer (SP) counting the number of elements (0 to 4), as in both examples.

- On reset, initialize SP to 0 and dataOut to zero, but skip clearing stack memory to reduce reset critical path and power.

- Use gated push and pop enables derived combinationally to avoid unnecessary writes and pops.

- Perform push operations by writing to stack_mem[SP] then incrementing SP.

- Perform pop operations by first decrementing SP then reading from stack_mem[SP], updating dataOut accordingly. This prevents reading from stack_mem using a pointer updated in the same cycle, avoiding potential read-after-write or combinational timing hazards.

- Avoid clearing stack_mem on pop to reduce toggling and power.

- Update EMPTY and FULL flags combinationally from SP.

- Use non-blocking assignments consistently.

This fusion maintains low power, clean control, and safer timing, offering an optimized design well suited to a small 4-entry LIFO buffer.
