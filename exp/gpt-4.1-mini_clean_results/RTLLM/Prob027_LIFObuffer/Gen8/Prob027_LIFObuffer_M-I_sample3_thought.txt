Replace the single 16-bit shift register with a 4-entry 4-bit register array to store stack elements, accessed directly by a 2-bit stack pointer (SP). SP now tracks the number of elements (0 to 4), incrementing on push and decrementing on pop. This eliminates costly 16-bit shifts on every operation, improving timing and power. 

On push (RW=0), if not full, store dataIn into stack_mem[SP], then increment SP. On pop (RW=1), if not empty, decrement SP first, then output stack_mem[SP]. This ensures dataOut is the top element, and we do not need to clear the popped element explicitly as SP controls valid entries.

EMPTY and FULL flags are combinational based on SP. The reset sets SP to 0 and clears all stack_mem entries to zero. DataOut holds last popped value, zero on reset or if pop attempted on empty. 

This approach reduces switching activity, area (no wide shifts), and critical path delay, thus improving PPA. All operations remain synchronous on Clk with enable and active-high reset.
