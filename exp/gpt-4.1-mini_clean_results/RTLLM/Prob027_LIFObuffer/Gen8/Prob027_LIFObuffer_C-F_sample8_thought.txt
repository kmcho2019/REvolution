Fuse the indexed register array approach (Example 2) with careful pointer management and explicit clearing of popped stack locations (as requested), while ensuring that dataOut is updated synchronously on pops only. Use a 2-bit SP that points to the current top of the stack, initially at 4 (empty). On push (RW=0), decrement SP first then write dataIn at SP location (stack grows downward). On pop (RW=1), output data from SP location, clear that location, then increment SP. This aligns with the user request that SP=4 means empty, and memory locations are cleared on reset and pop. The EMPTY flag is asserted when SP==4, FULL when SP==0. This avoids shifting a large register, simplifies control logic, and keeps dataOut synchronous with pops. Clearing popped locations reduces power due to avoiding residual data. Flags are combinational from SP. This approach merges Example 1's SP=4 empty convention and Example 2's indexed memory, providing clear, easy-to-verify, and efficient stack logic.
