I will refactor the frequency divider by using a single counter driven on the positive edge and derive clk_div1 and clk_div2 as combinational signals based on the counter value instead of registering them in separate flip-flops. This reduces register count and potential glitches by avoiding separate always blocks for clk_div1 and clk_div2.

For clk_div2, instead of sampling delayed counter on negedge, I will generate it combinationally from the counter, offset by HALF_DIV, with wraparound, creating a phase-shifted version of clk_div1.

The final clk_div is then an OR of clk_div1 and clk_div2 as combinational signals, all derived from one registered counter on the posedge clk.

This approach simplifies the design by eliminating clk_div1 and clk_div2 registers, improving glitch robustness by avoiding asynchronous toggling, and keeps the asynchronous reset just on the counter.

The counter still counts modulo NUM_DIV as before.

Compile-time odd check and parameterization remain unchanged.

This refactoring trades a tiny duty cycle asymmetry for cleaner, glitch-free output with less state holding.
