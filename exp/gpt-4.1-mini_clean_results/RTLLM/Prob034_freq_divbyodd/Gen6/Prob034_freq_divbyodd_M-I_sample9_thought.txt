The previous design correctly implements the odd-frequency divider principle by toggling two flip-flops (clk_div1 and clk_div2) on opposite clock edges and combining their outputs by OR to get the final divided clock. The key was toggling at the halfway point of the count to ensure correct phase alignment.

To improve:

1. **Reset synchronization:** Use synchronous reset for better timing closure instead of asynchronous reset, which can cause metastability or glitches.

2. **Remove initial block:** The initial block is non-synthesizable in many tools; instead, enforce the odd NUM_DIV check via a generate-time assertion.

3. **Optimize counters width:** Use `$clog2(NUM_DIV)` for counter width, but also handle the special case where NUM_DIV=1, which requires no division.

4. **Add a localparam for the toggle count:** It's more intuitive to toggle at half + 1 counts (i.e., (NUM_DIV+1)/2) to maintain proper odd division.

5. **Add comments and neatly format:** Improve readability and maintainability.

6. **Reduce combinational OR glitches:** Since clk_div1 and clk_div2 toggle on opposite edges, glitches are unlikely. To further ensure no glitches, implement clk_div as a registered output toggling on the input clock edges or use the OR as is with proper timing.

7. **Parameter validation with synthesis-friendly assertions:** Use SystemVerilog `assert` with generate block.

This results in a fully synthesizable, glitch-free, well-documented, and stable odd-frequency divider.

Note: For NUM_DIV=1, the output clk_div should be same as clk. The implementation handles any odd number >=1.
