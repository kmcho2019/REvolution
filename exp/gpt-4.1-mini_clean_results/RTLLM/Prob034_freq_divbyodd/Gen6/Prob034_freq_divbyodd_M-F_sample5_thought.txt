To fix the odd frequency divider and produce the correct output waveform, the key idea is to toggle the output clocks twice per division cycle at specific counts to maintain frequency division and balanced duty cycle.

For an odd divisor NUM_DIV:
- Define HALF = (NUM_DIV - 1) / 2, which corresponds to the number of clocks per half period minus one.
- Each counter (cnt1 on posedge clk, cnt2 on negedge clk) counts from 0 to NUM_DIV-1.
- Toggle clk_div1 on cnt1 reaching HALF and also on cnt1 reaching NUM_DIV-1.
- Similarly, toggle clk_div2 on cnt2 reaching HALF and on cnt2 reaching NUM_DIV-1.
- Reset counters to 0 after they reach NUM_DIV-1 to start next counting cycle.
  
This produces two output clocks clk_div1 and clk_div2 toggled on alternating edges with two toggles per count cycle, ensuring the total toggling frequency is input clock divided by NUM_DIV. The OR of clk_div1 and clk_div2 forms clk_div with approximate 50% duty cycle.

Additional improvements:
- Synchronize reset for both edges properly.
- Initialize clk_div1 and clk_div2 to 0 for deterministic startup.
- Compile-time assertion to enforce NUM_DIV is odd.
- Use $clog2 to size counters properly.

This approach fixes the toggling misalignment and ensures toggling twice per division cycle on each edge, which was the main cause of failure in the previous design.
