To divide the input clock frequency by an odd number NUM_DIV, a common challenge is achieving a 50% duty cycle. The idea is to generate an output clock that toggles at appropriate intervals to produce a frequency equal to clk_in / NUM_DIV, and having a near 50% duty cycle.

A novel approach:

- Use a single counter that counts from 0 up to NUM_DIV - 1 and wraps around.
- Define toggle points for the output clock based on this counter:
  - Toggle the output clock at count == 0 (start of the period)
  - Toggle again at count == (NUM_DIV - 1) / 2 + 1 (midpoint)
- Because NUM_DIV is odd, the period of the divided clock is NUM_DIV input clocks.
- By toggling twice per period (once at start, once at midpoint), we get a divided clock frequency of clk_in / NUM_DIV with an approximate 50% duty cycle.
- This design uses only the positive edge of clk, avoiding complex dual-clock domain synchronization.
- The output clk_div is registered and synchronous.
- The counter and clk_div reset asynchronously with active low rst_n.

This approach simplifies the logic, removes dual edge complexities, and ensures proper frequency division and duty cycle for any odd NUM_DIV parameter.

We include a compile-time check for oddness of NUM_DIV.

We use nonblocking assignments and parameterizable counter width.

This method aligns toggling strictly with counter values and yields a clean and stable frequency division by odd numbers.
