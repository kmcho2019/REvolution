The original solution tries to generate a divide-by-odd clock by splitting the division across the posedge and negedge of the input clock, using two counters and toggling outputs at half intervals. However, the toggling condition is off because for an odd divisor N, the half interval is not an integer; simply using NUM_DIV/2 (floor) and toggling at `HALF_DIV-1` causes uneven pulse widths and misalignment.

Improvement strategy:
1. Use two toggle points: one at `(NUM_DIV + 1)/2` counts for the high period and one at `(NUM_DIV - 1)/2` counts for the low period. This way, the output clock is high for `(N+1)/2` cycles and low for `(N-1)/2` cycles, producing a 50% duty cycle as close as possible.
2. Implement the divider as a state machine or counters that toggle the output clk_div after counting these two intervals, using a single posedge clock.
3. The dual-edge approach can be replaced by a single-edge design to avoid timing mismatches and glitches.
4. Keep the active low reset to initialize counters and output.
5. Include a check to ensure NUM_DIV is odd.
6. Using a single counter reduces complexity and improves timing, leading to better PPA.
7. The output clk_div toggles after count reaches the current interval, alternating between high and low periods.

This approach ensures that the output clock frequency is clk divided by the odd NUM_DIV, and duty cycle is as balanced as possible for an odd divisor.
