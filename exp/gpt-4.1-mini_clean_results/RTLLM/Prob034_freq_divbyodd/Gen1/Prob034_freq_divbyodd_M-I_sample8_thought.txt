The original design concept is sound for an odd frequency divider using two counters triggered on opposite clock edges. However, the implementation has critical issues:

1. Counter width: The counters need to have a width of `$clog2(NUM_DIV)` bits, not `$clog2(NUM_DIV)-1`, to count up to `NUM_DIV-1` correctly.

2. Counter and toggle sequencing: The toggling of clk_div1 and clk_div2 must happen precisely at the half and full counts, but incrementing the counter at the same time as toggling can cause off-by-one errors. It's cleaner to increment the counters first and then check for toggling conditions.

3. Initialization: Initial values of clk_div1 and clk_div2 on reset should be complementary to produce a 50% duty cycle overall on clk_div. Typically, one starts at 0 and the other at 1 to avoid a prolonged low output at startup.

4. Output clk_div: ORing clk_div1 and clk_div2 produces a roughly 50% duty cycle output with frequency divided by the odd number.

Improvement strategy:

- Correct the counter width to fully accommodate counting to NUM_DIV -1.
- Rewrite the always blocks to increment counters first, then toggle outputs when counters reach the required counts.
- Initialize clk_div1 = 0 and clk_div2 = 1 on reset to improve the initial duty cycle of clk_div.
- Keep counters separate and symmetric for posedge and negedge.
- Retain compile-time assertion that NUM_DIV must be odd.
- Maintain use of localparam HALF = NUM_DIV / 2.

This provides an accurate and clean frequency divider for odd divisors.
