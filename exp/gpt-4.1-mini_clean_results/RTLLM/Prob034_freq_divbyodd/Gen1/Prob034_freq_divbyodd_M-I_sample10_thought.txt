The original approach uses two counters toggling clk_div1 and clk_div2 on both HALF_DIV and NUM_DIV-1 counts, causing double toggles per cycle and timing issues. Also, cnt1 and cnt2 are incremented independently on posedge and negedge clk, which can cause misalignment and glitches.

To correct this, we simplify the logic: each counter counts up to NUM_DIV-1; on reaching NUM_DIV-1, the respective clk_div signal toggles and the counter resets. This ensures a single toggle per full count cycle.

Importantly, to achieve a duty cycle close to 50% for odd division, we toggle clk_div1 on the positive edge when the counter reaches HALF_DIV, and toggle clk_div2 on the negative edge when its counter reaches HALF_DIV. This staggers toggling events evenly across the input clock period.

Thus, clk_div1 toggles at posedge clk at count = HALF_DIV, cnt1 resets at NUM_DIV-1; similarly, clk_div2 toggles at negedge clk at count = HALF_DIV, cnt2 resets at NUM_DIV-1.

The final clk_div is the OR of clk_div1 and clk_div2, generating an odd division output with close to 50% duty cycle.

Also, counters and toggles are carefully synchronized, avoiding multiple toggles per cycle. The reset initializes all registers.

This approach respects the odd divisor, ensuring one toggle per half cycle on posedge and negedge respectively and results in a clean divided clock output.
