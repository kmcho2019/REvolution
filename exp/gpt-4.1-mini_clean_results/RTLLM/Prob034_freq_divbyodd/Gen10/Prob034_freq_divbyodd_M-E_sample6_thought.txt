A clean way to generate a frequency divider by an odd number (NUM_DIV) with a good duty cycle is to toggle the output clock only once per NUM_DIV input clock cycles, but with a carefully timed pulse width that balances the duty cycle.

One known approach is:
- Use a single counter counting from 0 to NUM_DIV-1.
- Toggle the output clock on specific count values to form a "high" pulse duration of (NUM_DIV+1)/2 counts and a "low" pulse duration of (NUM_DIV-1)/2 counts.
- This produces a frequency division by NUM_DIV with a duty cycle close to 50% (exactly 50% impossible for odd division but as close as possible).

Implementation details:
- On posedge clk, increment the counter; reset on reaching NUM_DIV-1.
- When the counter is 0, toggle clk_div to start a pulse.
- When the counter equals (NUM_DIV-1)/2, toggle clk_div again to end the pulse.
- This creates a pulse of length roughly half the divisor and a complementary low time for the remainder.
- The active low reset asynchronously initializes clk_div and the counter.

This solution only uses a single counter and clock domain, minimizing complexity and synchronization issues. It naturally produces a stable frequency division by any odd number with a well-defined waveform and better duty cycle than simple divide-by-N with a single toggle.

We also include a compile-time check ensuring NUM_DIV is odd.
