To correctly divide the input clock by an odd number NUM_DIV, the key is to generate a divided clock with toggles spaced so that the output frequency is input frequency divided by NUM_DIV and the duty cycle is as close to 50% as possible.

For odd division, we use two toggling signals clk_div1 and clk_div2, toggled on opposite clock edges, to combine into the final output clk_div = clk_div1 | clk_div2.

Correct implementation involves:

1. Using a single counter that counts from 0 to NUM_DIV-1 on the rising edge of clk. This counter tracks the full period.

2. Generate clk_div1 toggled once per full period on the rising edge of clk at counter == NUM_DIV-1.

3. Generate clk_div2 toggled once per full period on the falling edge of clk at counter == HALF (where HALF = (NUM_DIV - 1)/2).

4. Both clk_div1 and clk_div2 start from 0 on reset.

5. This approach ensures that toggles are evenly spaced: clk_div1 toggles at the end of the count period, clk_div2 toggles halfway through, on the opposite clock edge, resulting in a combined output with frequency divided by NUM_DIV and near 50% duty cycle.

6. Both counters are synchronized in count index (counting from 0 to NUM_DIV-1). The toggling occurs only at these two points.

7. This eliminates glitches and misalignments because clk_div1 and clk_div2 toggle at fixed, complementary positions in the counting cycle.

By simplifying to one counter on rising edge, and toggling clk_div1 on rising edge at count max and toggling clk_div2 on falling edge at count HALF, the resulting OR of clk_div1 and clk_div2 gives the correct divided clock output.

Synchronous resets and nonblocking assignments ensure stable initial conditions.

This corrected approach resolves previous toggle timing and glitch issues while fulfilling the odd division with good duty cycle and frequency accuracy.
