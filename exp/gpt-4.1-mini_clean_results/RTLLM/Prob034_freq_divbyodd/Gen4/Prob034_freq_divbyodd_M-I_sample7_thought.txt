The original design uses two counters counting up to NUM_DIV-1 on opposite clock edges and toggles their respective clock divider outputs when counters reach HALF_COUNT. However, toggling when cnt == HALF_COUNT inside the same clock edge that increments the counter causes a timing offset (since cnt updates after the toggle condition is evaluated), resulting in a phase misalignment between clk_div1 and clk_div2. This causes the final clk_div (OR of clk_div1 and clk_div2) to have glitches or incorrect timing, failing the functional checks.

Improvement strategy:

1. Change counters to count from 0 to NUM_DIV-1 but toggle clk_divx output *after* the counter reaches NUM_DIV-1. This ensures that each clk_divx toggles once per full count cycle, ensuring stable and well-aligned edges.

2. On each clock edge (pos or neg), increment counter and when it reaches NUM_DIV-1, toggle clk_divx and reset the counter. This aligns toggling with counter rollover, avoiding mid-cycle toggling that causes misalignment.

3. Use half_count only to determine toggle point if generating 50% duty cycle clock, but with odd dividers, toggling on counter wrap-around generates the correct odd division frequency.

4. To improve duty cycle, use the two toggling outputs clk_div1 and clk_div2 staggered by half cycle on posedge and negedge, and OR them. Because toggle occurs every NUM_DIV cycles on posedge and negedge separately, the combined clk_div will have the input clock divided by NUM_DIV with near 50% duty cycle.

5. Ensure asynchronous reset resets counters and outputs synchronously on posedge and negedge accordingly.

6. Validate parameter remains an odd number greater or equal to 3.

This approach improves timing alignment, removes mid-cycle toggling glitches, and better matches expected simulation results.
