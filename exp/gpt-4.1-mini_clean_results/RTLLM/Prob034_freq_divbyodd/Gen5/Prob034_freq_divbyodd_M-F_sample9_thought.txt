The root problem lies in toggling clk_div1 and clk_div2 twice per cycle (at HALF_DIV-1 and NUM_DIV-1), causing glitches and incorrect timing. The solution is to toggle each clk_divX exactly once per count cycle to maintain proper phase and timing.

For an odd divisor NUM_DIV, the full period of the output clock is NUM_DIV input clock cycles. To achieve a 50% duty cycle as close as possible, we split the period into two half-periods:
- On rising edge domain: toggle clk_div1 after HALF_DIV counts
- On falling edge domain: toggle clk_div2 after HALF_DIV + 1 counts

Specifically, on posedge clk, cnt1 counts up to HALF_DIV, toggling clk_div1 when cnt1 == HALF_DIV. It then resets to 0.
On negedge clk, cnt2 counts up to (NUM_DIV - HALF_DIV - 1), toggling clk_div2 when cnt2 == (NUM_DIV - HALF_DIV -1), then resets.

These complementary toggles create a combined output clk_div (OR of clk_div1 and clk_div2) with the desired divided frequency and near 50% duty cycle.

We will:
- Use two counters cnt1 and cnt2 counting independently on posedge and negedge clk.
- Toggle clk_div1 and clk_div2 only once per cycle, exactly when their counters reach the specified toggle count.
- Reset counters immediately after toggling.
- Initialize all registers on active low reset.
- Keep the compile-time check for odd NUM_DIV.
- The final clk_div is OR of clk_div1 and clk_div2, producing the divided clock free of glitches.

This approach aligns with the original specification and addresses the previous issues, ensuring correct odd frequency division and proper waveform generation.
