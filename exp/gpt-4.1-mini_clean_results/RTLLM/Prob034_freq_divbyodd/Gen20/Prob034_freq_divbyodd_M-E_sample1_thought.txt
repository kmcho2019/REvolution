To implement a frequency divider by an odd number without using dual counters or toggling on both clock edges, I will use a single up-counter and a flip-flop for the divided clock output. The key idea is:

- Count clock cycles from 0 to NUM_DIV-1.
- At the midpoint (half_count = floor(NUM_DIV/2)), toggle the output clock.
- Also toggle the output clock when the counter resets from NUM_DIV-1 back to 0.
- This method produces a clock output that has a period of NUM_DIV input clock cycles and nearly 50% duty cycle, despite NUM_DIV being odd.
- The output clock toggles twice per cycle, once at half_count and once at the wrap-around, providing balanced high/low time slices.
- Use only a positive-edge triggered counter and output flip-flop, reducing complexity.
- Reset asynchronously resets counter and output clock.
- Include a compile-time assertion to ensure NUM_DIV is odd.

This approach simplifies timing since all logic is synchronous on posedge clk, no negedge blocks, easier to implement in hardware, and suitable for odd divisors.
