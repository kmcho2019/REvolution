To create a novel architecture for the odd frequency divider, I will use two separate counters for rising and falling edges, each clocked on their respective edges of the input clock. Each counter counts from 0 to (NUM_DIV-1). The outputs clk_div1 and clk_div2 are generated by toggling their respective flip-flops when the counters reach their terminal counts (specifically, when the counters reach (NUM_DIV-1)/2). By toggling each half-period on opposite edges, the combined output clk_div = clk_div1 XOR clk_div2 will have a 50% duty cycle over the full NUM_DIV period. This approach ensures clean timing since each counter is updated strictly in its own clock domain (posedge or negedge), avoiding clock domain crossing complexity. Asynchronous active-low reset initializes counters and dividers to zero. Using XOR for combining clk_div1 and clk_div2 provides improved duty cycle and clearer edge alignment than OR. The counters are modulo NUM_DIV to keep the correct division ratio. Parameter checks ensure that NUM_DIV is odd. This architecture is simple, structurally balanced, and naturally glitch-free.
