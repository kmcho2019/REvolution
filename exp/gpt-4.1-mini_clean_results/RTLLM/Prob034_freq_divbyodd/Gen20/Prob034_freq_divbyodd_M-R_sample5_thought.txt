I will refactor the original design by replacing the separate clk_div1 and clk_div2 flip-flops with combinational assignments driven by counters and a small state machine. Instead of using two separate always blocks triggered by posedge and negedge clk, I will use a single counter that increments on the positive edge and derive the divided clock output through combinational logic that considers the counter value and a registered phase bit toggled on specific counts. This will simplify the architecture by eliminating the negative-edge triggered always block and the delayed counter, thus reducing flip-flops and potential glitch sources.

Key changes:
- Use one counter on posedge clk with synchronous reset.
- Use a state bit (phase) to represent the high/low phase of the divided clock, toggled when counter reaches HALF_DIV.
- The output clk_div is generated by combining the counter and phase to create an odd division with approximately 50% duty cycle by controlling when clk_div goes high/low.
- Use parameterized counter width, check for odd NUM_DIV.
- All logic happens on the positive edge, no negative edge triggers.
- Output clk_div is a registered signal updated synchronously, avoiding glitch-prone OR gates of asynchronous signals.

This refactoring improves timing by relying solely on positive edge triggering, reduces power by cutting toggling on the negative edge, and reduces area by removing duplicated registers.
