I will create a superior frequency divider by fusing the best ideas from both examples as follows:

- Use a single counter operating on the positive edge of clk to reduce area and complexity.
- Generate clk_div1 on the positive edge by toggling it when the count reaches halfway and zero, similar to Example 1, to keep the toggling logic simple and balanced.
- For clk_div2 (negative edge toggling), rather than a delayed full counter register, I will use a small register to track a 1-bit pulse indicating when to toggle clk_div2, reducing complexity and improving timing over the full delayed counter approach.
- Specifically, on the negedge clk, toggle clk_div2 when the toggling condition (count == 0 or count == HALF_DIV) was true in the previous posedge cycle—tracked via a pulse register updated on posedge clk. This eliminates the need for a wide delayed counter register and reduces toggling glitches.
- Combine clk_div1 and clk_div2 with an OR gate to form the output clk_div.
- Keep the asynchronous active-low reset to initialize all registers.
- Include a compile-time odd divisor check with $error.
- Use minimal width counters sized by $clog2(NUM_DIV).
- This fusion retains the clean toggling logic and dual-edge triggering for near 50% duty cycle, reduces register count and complexity on negedge domain, and aims for improved timing and lower power consumption with reduced toggling logic and smaller delayed signals.

This approach balances performance, power, and area effectively while maintaining functional correctness and a good duty cycle for the odd-frequency divider.
