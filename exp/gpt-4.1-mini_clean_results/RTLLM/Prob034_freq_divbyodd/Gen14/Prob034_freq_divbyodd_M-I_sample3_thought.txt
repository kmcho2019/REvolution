The original approach using two counters toggling clk_div1 and clk_div2 at half the divisor count results in incorrect frequency and duty cycle because toggling occurs only once per count cycle, causing waveform mismatch. To correct this, I will use a single counter running on the positive edge of clk, counting from 0 to NUM_DIV-1. The divided clock output toggles exactly once per NUM_DIV input clock cycles, at a counter value of (NUM_DIV - 1)/2, ensuring a 50% duty cycle for odd NUM_DIV values.

This approach avoids multiple counters and edge complexities, making the design simpler and more robust. The divided clock is generated by toggling clk_div when the counter hits the midpoint, and resetting the counter to zero after reaching NUM_DIV-1. The active low reset initializes the counter and clk_div to zero. This method guarantees a clean divided clock output at input clock frequency divided by an odd number with balanced duty cycle.
