The existing design is functionally correct but uses dual registers clk_div1 and clk_div2 toggled on posedge and negedge clk with a delayed counter register to handle clk_div2. This adds extra registers and toggling activity which impacts power and area. Also, checking equality to 0 and HALF_DIV every clock edge adds combinational complexity.

Improvement strategy:
- Use a single counter and generate the divided clock by toggling clk_div at specific counts, rather than setting/clearing two separate clk_div signals.
- Since the division is by an odd number, the output clock cannot have a 50% duty cycle but can approximate it by toggling clk_div at count=HALF_DIV and count=NUM_DIV-1.
- Implement all logic on posedge clk only, eliminating clk_div2 and negedge clk logic, reducing complexity and toggling.
- Reset initializes counter and clk_div to zero.
- Increment counter from 0 to NUM_DIV-1, wrapping around.
- Toggle clk_div when counter == HALF_DIV or counter == NUM_DIV-1.
- This toggling on two counts per cycle simulates a divide-by-odd clock with minimal registers and no negedge logic.
- This reduces area, power and improves timing by removing negedge logic and delayed signals.
- Still keep the compile-time oddness check.

This approach simplifies the design and minimizes toggling and registers, optimizing power, area, and performance while maintaining correct odd division.

Note: The resulting duty cycle will not be exactly 50%, but this is inherent to odd division. The output toggling at these points produces a well-formed odd-divided clock.
