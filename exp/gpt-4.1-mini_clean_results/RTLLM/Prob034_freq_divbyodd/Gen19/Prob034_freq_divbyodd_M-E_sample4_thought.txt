To implement an odd-frequency divider, I will use a single up-counter that counts from 0 to NUM_DIV-1 and generates the divided clock output by toggling the output at specific counts to achieve a duty cycle close to 50% despite the odd divisor.

Architecture:
- Parameter NUM_DIV (odd) defines the division factor.
- A counter increments on each rising clock edge, resetting to zero at NUM_DIV-1.
- The divided clock output clk_div is toggled at counts 0 and at HALF_DIV (the midpoint).
- Since NUM_DIV is odd, toggling at two counts spaced approximately half of NUM_DIV apart yields a frequency divided by NUM_DIV with a nearly 50% duty cycle.
- Reset asynchronously sets counter and clk_div to 0.
- This approach uses one counter and one output register, minimizing area and power.
- The design operates solely on the rising clock edge to keep timing simple and avoid negative-edge complications.
- The clk_div output toggles twice per cycle of the input clock, so its frequency is input clock / NUM_DIV.
- Compile-time check ensures NUM_DIV is odd.
