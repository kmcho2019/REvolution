The original solution is structurally correct and meets the problem requirements, but it uses a fully structural approach with instantiations of mux2X1 for every bit in each stage, resulting in 24 mux instances total (8 per stage * 3 stages). While this is clear and modular, it can be optimized for better PPA by replacing these many small muxes with Verilog's bitwise multiplexing operators. Using the ternary conditional operator at the vector level can synthesize into efficient mux trees or multiplexers with fewer logic resources and reduced net delays. This keeps the design clean, reduces instantiations, and often improves timing and area.

The improved design will:
- Keep the mux2X1 module for conceptual clarity (but will not instantiate it repeatedly).
- Implement each stage's multiplexing as a vector-level conditional assignment.
- Use bit rotations by concatenation for shift amounts of 4, 2, and 1.
- Chain the three stages accordingly.
- This approach meets the original functionality, is concise, and improves PPA.

This also avoids multiple instantiations of mux2X1, so simulation environments with an existing mux2X1 won't conflict.

Overall, the improvement replaces structural mux instances with vectorized conditional operators for better synthesis results.
