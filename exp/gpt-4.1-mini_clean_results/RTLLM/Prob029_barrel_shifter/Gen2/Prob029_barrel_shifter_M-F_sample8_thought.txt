The original design implements left rotation, but the feedback suggests that the testbench expects right rotation instead. To fix this, I will change the rotation direction from left to right.

This means:
- The rotation by 4 positions will be `{in[3:0], in[7:4]}` for left rotate, so for right rotate, it should be `{in[3:0], in[7:4]}` reversed, i.e., `{in[3:0], in[7:4]}` flipped around is actually left rotate by 4. For right rotate by 4, it should be `{in[3:4], in[7:4]}`? No, let's carefully find the correct slices.

Rotation right by 4 bits means the bits are shifted right by 4 positions with wrap-around:
`out[i] = in[(i + 4) % 8]`

So for right rotation by 4:
`out = {in[3:0], in[7:4]}` (same as left rotate by 4 bits)
Actually, rotating right by 4 on 8 bits equals rotating left by 4 bits because 8 is even and rotation by half the width reverses halves.

Therefore, rotating right by 4 bits is the same as rotating left by 4 bits, so no change needed there.

For rotation by 2 bits right:
`out[i] = in[(i + 2) % 8]`

Left rotation by 2 bits:
`out = {in[5:0], in[7:6]}`

Right rotation by 2 bits:
`out = {in[1:0], in[7:2]}`

Similarly for rotation by 1 bit right:
`out[i] = in[(i + 1) % 8]`

Left rotation by 1 bit:
`out = {in[6:0], in[7]}`

Right rotation by 1 bit:
`out = {in[0], in[7:1]}`

Therefore, to fix the design:

- Stage 1 (shift by 4): no change because right rotate by 4 = left rotate by 4

- Stage 2 (shift by 2): change to right rotate by 2: `{stage1[1:0], stage1[7:2]}`

- Stage 3 (shift by 1): change to right rotate by 1: `{stage2[0], stage2[7:1]}`

This should align with the testbench expectations.

The parameterized mux remains for clean code.

The rest of the design remains combinational and modular.

This fix ensures all rotations are right rotations, consistent with likely testbench requirements.
