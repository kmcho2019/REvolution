To design an 8-bit barrel shifter that performs rotation using a hierarchical mux2X1 structure, we can build it by chaining three layers of mux arrays corresponding to the shifts by 4, 2, and 1 positions, controlled by bits ctrl[2], ctrl[1], and ctrl[0] respectively.

Each layer consists of 8 instances of 2-to-1 multiplexers (`mux2X1`), each selecting between the current bit and the bit shifted by the corresponding amount, thus implementing conditional rotation. We rotate left by selecting bits rotated left; the muxes pick either the unshifted or shifted bit.

This approach:
- Implements rotation as a cascade of mux layers, each controlled by one bit of `ctrl`.
- Clearly uses the `mux2X1` submodule as requested, capturing the physical mux implementation.
- Each mux selects between no shift and the appropriate rotated bit for its position.
- Provides fully combinational logic with direct signal assignments.
- Uses wire connections for intermediate stages between mux layers.

Thus, this design matches the problem’s mux-based architectural description and produces an 8-bit rotated output according to the 3-bit control signal.
