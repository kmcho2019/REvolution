Refactor the multiplier design by implementing a simple finite state machine (FSM) with two states: IDLE and BUSY. The FSM triggers multiplication on `start`, performs the shift-and-add over 16 cycles, and signals `done` when complete. Instead of shifting the multiplicand and multiplier in separate registers each cycle, maintain the multiplier in a shift register and perform addition only if its LSB is 1. Use a product accumulator register that shifts right each cycle, incorporating the addition result in the upper half. This approach stores the running partial product and reduces the complexity of shifting both multiplicand and multiplier separately. Use synchronous reset and non-blocking assignments. The `done` signal is asserted only in the BUSY state's last cycle. This FSM approach simplifies control, cleanly separates input loading and computation phases, and allows clear timing and power management with an enable signal gating the arithmetic operations.
