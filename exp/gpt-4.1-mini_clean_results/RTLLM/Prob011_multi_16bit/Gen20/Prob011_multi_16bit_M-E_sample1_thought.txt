Implement a straightforward iterative shift-and-add unsigned 16-bit multiplier with separate registers for multiplicand, multiplier, and partial product. On start, load multiplicand and multiplier into respective registers and clear the product register. Use a 5-bit counter to track steps from 0 to 16.

At each clock cycle during multiplication (when count > 0 and <=16), check the least significant bit of the multiplier register. If it is 1, add the multiplicand shifted by the current count-1 to the product register. Then shift the multiplier right by 1 bit.

When the count reaches 16, assert done signal and output the product.

The design keeps multiplicand and multiplier stable while shifting multiplier and accumulating product, making control logic simple and explicit. This approach directly maps the mathematical definition of multiplication using shifts and adds without packing multiplier bits into the product register.

Non-blocking assignments with synchronous reset are used. The done signal is asserted only on the clock cycle after completing the 16th iteration, then cleared on reset or new start.

This architecture isolates multiplier shifting and partial product accumulation clearly, improving traceability and easing debugging, at the cost of a straightforward 17-cycle latency.
