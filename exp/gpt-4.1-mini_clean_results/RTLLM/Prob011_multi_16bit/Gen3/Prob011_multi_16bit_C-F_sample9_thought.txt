Fuse the strengths of both examples by keeping the explicit control and clarity of the FSM from Example 2, while adopting the efficient serial bit-by-bit shifting and accumulation from Example 1 to minimize large dynamic shifts and simplify addition timing.

Key fusion points:
- Use an FSM with IDLE, CALC, DONE states for clear control flow and proper sequencing.
- Implement a 5-bit counter `i` for bit tracking inside the CALC state, incrementing each cycle.
- Load the multiplicand into `areg` and extend the multiplier into a 32-bit register `breg` at start.
- In CALC, check the LSB of `areg`; if set, add `breg` to the product accumulator.
- Shift `areg` right and `breg` left by one bit each cycle to align the partial products.
- Assert `done` when all 16 bits have been processed.
- Assign the final product at the DONE state.
- Reset all registers and flags properly on active low reset.
- This approach reduces large dynamic shifts (no wide shift by `i-1`) and structures control, improving timing, clarity, and potentially lowering power.
