Fuse the strengths of both examples by combining the explicit FSM from Example 1 (which cleanly separates idle and busy states, enabling clear control of start/done signals) with the clear and simple shift-count and done flag control logic from Example 2.

Use a two-state FSM (IDLE and BUSY) to gate the multiplication process and control the done flag precisely when the multiplication completes (count == 16). This also prevents overlap between consecutive multiplications.

Load multiplicand and multiplier at start (in IDLE state) and maintain a 32-bit product register where the upper 16 bits accumulate partial sums and lower 16 bits hold multiplier bits. On each BUSY cycle, perform shift-right on product and conditionally add the multiplicand shifted accordingly.

To reduce switching and improve power, perform addition only when the LSB of product is 1. The addition and shift are combined such that the product is right-shifted and then the multiplicand is conditionally added. This is a well-known shift-add multiplier approach.

Generate the done flag when the multiplication is complete (count == 16). After done is asserted for one cycle, the FSM returns to IDLE.

Reset logic is synchronous active-low.

This fusion gives clear control signals, minimal and intuitive control logic, and efficient shift-and-accumulate operation.

Non-blocking assignments ensure correct sequential operation. The done signal timing is precise and cleanly generated by FSM states and count.

This solution is easy to understand, synthesize, and optimize, with good timing and power characteristics.
