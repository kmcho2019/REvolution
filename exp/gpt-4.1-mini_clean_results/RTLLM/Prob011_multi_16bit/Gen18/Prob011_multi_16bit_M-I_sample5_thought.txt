The original design packs the multiplier and partial product into a single 32-bit register and shifts it right every cycle, performing a conditional addition each cycle. While functionally correct, shifting the whole product register and updating 32 bits every clock cycle leads to high switching activity and a potentially long critical path due to the large-width adder.

To optimize power and timing, I propose to:
1. Separate the multiplicand and multiplier registers from the product register.
2. Use a right-shift register for the multiplier only (16 bits), a 32-bit product register accumulating the partial sums, and keep the multiplicand unchanged.
3. On each cycle, if the LSB of the multiplier is 1, add the multiplicand shifted by the current count to the product.
4. Then shift the multiplier right by one bit.
5. This eliminates shifting the large product register; only the 16-bit multiplier register shifts, greatly reducing switching on the 32-bit product.
6. Use an explicit 5-bit shift count to control operation.
7. Control done flag as requested.
8. Keep the data registers synchronized with clock and active-low reset.
9. Keep start/idle logic and done flag as per spec.

This approach reduces switching activity on the product register (only additions, no shifts) and limits shifting to the 16-bit multiplier register, potentially improving power and timing. This approach is a classic shift-accumulate multiplier structure widely used in hardware.

The done signal will be asserted after 16 cycles, the count resets on new start.

This design meets the requirements exactly while improving PPA metrics.
