Implement the 16-bit multiplier using a classic shift-and-accumulate approach, but instead of merging multiplier and product in one register and shifting right, separate the multiplier and partial product registers and shift the multiplier right each cycle. On each cycle, if the LSB of the multiplier is 1, add the multiplicand shifted by the current bit index to the product accumulator.

Use a 5-bit counter 'i' to count from 0 to 16 representing the bit position. At i=0, load multiplicand and multiplier. For i=1 to 16, test multiplier LSB, conditionally accumulate multiplicand shifted left by (i-1) to the product, then shift multiplier right by 1. When i reaches 16, multiplication is done.

This avoids shifting the whole 32-bit product register each cycle, reducing switching. The multiplicand remains fixed, and only the multiplier and product registers change. The done flag is generated by comparing the counter to 16, following the requirement to reset done at i=17.

Control signals are handled by the counter and done register. The design has clear separation of states via the counter. Reset is asynchronous active low, and start initializes registers.

This approach is straightforward, easy to verify, and can be optimized for timing because additions are straightforward partial accumulations, and shifting is done only on the multiplier register (16 bits).
