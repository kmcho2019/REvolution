Refactor the design to use a single always block with a single 7-bit counter that counts from 0 to 99 (for a full 100-cycle period), thereby generating all output clocks in one centralized counter-driven state machine. The 50MHz clock toggles every 1 cycle (count[0] bit), the 10MHz clock toggles every 5 cycles, and the 1MHz clock toggles every 50 cycles using specific counter compare values.

This approach reduces the number of counters from three to one, simplifying the logic and potentially making timing easier to manage by having all outputs derive from a common counter. The outputs are generated by comparing the counter to toggle points and toggling the clocks accordingly.

Resets asynchronously clear the counter and clock outputs. Non-blocking assignments keep synchronous semantics.

This centralized counting and toggling method maintains the correct frequencies but reorganizes the logic to be more compact and easier to extend to other dividers by modifying compare values or counter widths.
