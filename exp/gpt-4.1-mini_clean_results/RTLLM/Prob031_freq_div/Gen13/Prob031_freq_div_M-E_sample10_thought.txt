Instead of using three separate counters, I will implement a single unified 7-bit counter that counts from 0 up to 99 (for the slowest clock), and then resets. I will derive all three output clocks from this single counter by toggling the output clocks at appropriate counts:

- CLK_50 toggles every clock cycle (divide by 2): generated by a simple flip-flop toggling every cycle.

- CLK_10 toggles when the counter reaches 4 and 9 (generating a 10-cycle period): to achieve a 10 MHz clock, toggle CLK_10 at counts 4 and 9, producing a 50% duty cycle.

- CLK_1 toggles when the counter reaches 49 and 99 (generating a 100-cycle period): to get 1 MHz, toggle CLK_1 at counts 49 and 99, producing a 50% duty cycle.

This approach reduces hardware by using a single counter and combinational logic to generate all toggle signals. This simplifies control and may improve area and power due to fewer counters and toggle flip-flops, while preserving synchronous reset and clean clock domain crossing. The output clocks have balanced duty cycles and precise frequency division.
