To implement a 32-bit carry-lookahead adder with a novel approach, I will design a 4-stage hierarchical CLA architecture. The 32-bit adder is composed of four 8-bit CLA blocks. Each 8-bit CLA block computes its own sum and generates a group propagate and generate signal. These group signals are used by a top-level 4-bit block carry lookahead generator to quickly compute the carry-in for each 8-bit block. This breaks the long carry chain into smaller parallelizable blocks, significantly reducing carry propagation delay compared to a flat ripple or simple 16-bit CLA cascading approach.

This hierarchical CLA approach:
- Uses small 8-bit CLA blocks, which have internal fast carry calculation via carry-lookahead logic.
- Uses a 4-bit carry-lookahead block that aggregates group generate/propagate signals from the 8-bit blocks to generate block carry-in signals in parallel.
- Provides better timing performance by limiting carry chain length and maximizing parallelism.
- Maintains modularity and reusability by clearly separating 8-bit CLA blocks and a 4-bit CLA carry generator.
- Correctly maps input and output bits according to the [32:1] indexing requirement.

This approach improves timing and scalability for 32-bit addition.
