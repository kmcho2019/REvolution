To improve the performance (reduce carry propagation delay) of the original design, I will implement a full 32-bit hierarchical carry-lookahead adder architecture.

Specifically, I will keep the two 16-bit CLA blocks but add a carry-lookahead unit at the top level to quickly compute the carry-in to the upper 16-bit block without waiting for the ripple from the lower block.

This approach reduces the critical path significantly compared to the original ripple carry between the two 16-bit blocks.

Inside each 16-bit block, I will keep a CLA structure that computes group generate (G) and propagate (P) signals and outputs sum bits and block carry-out.

The top-level CLA logic will use the two 16-bit G and P signals from the blocks and the initial carry-in (Cin=0) to compute the carry-in to the upper 16-bit block (C16) and the final carry-out (C32).

This hierarchical carry lookahead reduces carry latency between blocks, improving max clock frequency while preserving modularity.

Bit indexing [32:1] is preserved per problem statement for consistency.

This design balances complexity and performance with a scalable CLA hierarchy and clean interfaces.

Power and area slightly increase due to extra logic for group carry-lookahead but justified by performance gain.

No use of sequential logic; fully combinational.
