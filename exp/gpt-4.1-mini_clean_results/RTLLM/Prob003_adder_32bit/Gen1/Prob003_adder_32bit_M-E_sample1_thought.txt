To design a 32-bit carry-lookahead adder using two 16-bit CLA blocks with a novel approach, I'll explicitly compute the group propagate and generate signals for each 16-bit block and then use a higher-level carry lookahead logic to compute the carry-in for the upper block.

Instead of recursively computing group generate inside the 16-bit block, I'll implement the 16-bit CLA block to output sum, carry-out, and also individual propagate and generate vectors for each bit. Then, at the top 32-bit module level, I will manually compute the carry-in for the upper 16 bits using these group signals and the carry-in (fixed 0 here) in a clean, well-structured formula:

Carry_in_upper = G0 | (P0 & Cin)

where G0 and P0 are the group generate and propagate of lower 16 bits.

The 16-bit CLA module will implement carry lookahead internally as usual (using generate/propagate bits), but will also output group propagate and generate signals as:

- Group Propagate = AND of all bit propagates

- Group Generate = G[15] | (P[15]&G[14]) | ... | (P[15]&P[14]&...P[0]&G[0])

I'll use zero-based indexing internally for clarity.

Sum bits will be computed as S[i] = P[i] ^ C[i], where C[0] = Cin.

This separation and explicit top-level carry calculation should improve clarity and correctness.

Finally, I'll keep input/output vectors as [31:0] for clarity and common usage, mapping user input [32:1] ports to [31:0] internally.

This architecture clearly separates 16-bit CLA blocks and top-level carry-lookahead logic, which is scalable and modular.
