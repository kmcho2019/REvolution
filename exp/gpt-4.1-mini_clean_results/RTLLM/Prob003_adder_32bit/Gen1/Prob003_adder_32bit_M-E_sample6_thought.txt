We implement a 32-bit carry-lookahead adder using a hierarchical CLA architecture with a two-level approach.

First, design a 4-bit CLA module that computes sum bits and outputs a group propagate (P) and generate (G) signal. This 4-bit CLA is a classic building block that efficiently computes carries and sum within 4 bits.

Next, build a 16-bit CLA module from four 4-bit CLA blocks, along with a carry-lookahead generator (CLG) that computes carry inputs for each 4-bit block using the 4-bit group propagate and generate signals. This hierarchical approach reduces gate fan-in and critical path, improving speed and area compared to flat carry calculations.

Finally, build the 32-bit adder by chaining two 16-bit CLA blocks in the same hierarchical manner: two 16-bit blocks, each consisting of four 4-bit blocks.

This multi-level hierarchy (4-bit blocks in 16-bit blocks, then two 16-bit blocks in 32-bit adder) balances speed and complexity well.

The design inputs and outputs are bit indices [32:1] as requested.

Key points:
- 4-bit CLA: computes sum and carries inside 4 bits, outputs P and G
- 16-bit CLA: instantiates four 4-bit CLAs, uses a 4-block carry-lookahead unit to generate inter-block carries
- 32-bit adder: two 16-bit CLAs chained by carry

This approach reduces large fan-in gates and improves timing and area compared to a flat 16-bit CLA implementation.
