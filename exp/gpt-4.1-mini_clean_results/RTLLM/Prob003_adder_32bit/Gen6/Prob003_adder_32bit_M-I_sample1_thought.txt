To optimize the 32-bit carry-lookahead adder for better timing and power, I will redesign the 16-bit CLA block with hierarchical carry-lookahead logic instead of a simple ripple carry chain.

Instead of linearly computing each carry bit as C[i+1] = G[i] + P[i]*C[i], I'll implement group generate and propagate signals for 4-bit subgroups, so the 16-bit CLA block is composed of four 4-bit blocks whose carries are computed hierarchically. This approach significantly reduces carry propagation delay and switching activity.

Specifically:
- Divide the 16 bits into four 4-bit groups.
- Compute group generate (GG) and group propagate (GP) for each 4-bit group.
- Use these group signals to compute carries at the group boundary levels in parallel.
- Within each 4-bit group, compute carries using the usual CLA method.
- This hierarchical approach reduces the longest carry chain from 16 steps to 4 + 4 steps.

I will also use generate-for loops to reduce code redundancy and improve maintainability.

Finally, in the top-level 32-bit adder, chain two optimized 16-bit CLA blocks as before.

This implementation provides better timing by reducing the carry chain length and better power due to fewer transitions in carry lines.
