To improve the existing right shifter design for better Power-Performance-Area (PPA), I will optimize the logic to minimize switching activity and simplify the assignment. Instead of building a shifted wire each cycle, I will directly perform the right shift and insert the new bit in a single assignment in the always block, reducing wire fanout and extra logic. Since the initial solution uses a combinational wire for next state, removing it avoids unnecessary routing and gates.

Further, I will use non-blocking assignments carefully to update q in one statement: q <= {d, q[7:1]}; This is a common idiom for shift registers, and synthesis tools typically optimize it well.

This approach slightly reduces combinational load and maintains clean synchronous logic, improving timing and possibly power consumption without adding complexity or area.

Initialization remains with an initial block for simulation consistency.

Overall, this is a minimal and efficient solution well-suited for small shift registers.
