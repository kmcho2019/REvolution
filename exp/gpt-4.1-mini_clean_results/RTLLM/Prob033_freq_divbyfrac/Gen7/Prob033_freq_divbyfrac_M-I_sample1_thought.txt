To achieve a reliable fractional divide-by-3.5 clock with balanced duty cycle and minimal glitches, the design must address timing alignment and synchronization issues.

Improvement strategy:
1. Generate clk_intA and clk_intB as combinational signals directly from a synchronous counter incremented at posedge clk. This ensures signals change synchronously with clk.
2. Produce clk_intB_phase_shift by registering clk_intB on the negedge clk. This provides a clean half-clock delayed version without glitches.
3. Since clk_intA and clk_intB_phase_shift are stable and derived from a single counter, their OR forms a smooth fractional clock with half-integer division.
4. Ensure the counter resets asynchronously and cleanly.
5. This method avoids complicated multi-flop synchronization or clock domain crossing issues by utilizing both edges of the same clock for phase shifting.
6. Because the combinational signals are derived from the counter value, no additional delay or pulse width mismatch occurs.
7. This approach produces a 50% duty cycle output at the fractional divide-by-3.5 rate.

This is a clean, minimal, and robust approach suitable for FPGA or ASIC implementations that require fractional clock division using double-edge clocking and phase shifting.
