The key problem arises from sampling a combinational signal (`clk_intB`), which changes at posedge clk, directly at negedge clk to create a half-clock phase shift. This causes timing uncertainty and glitches since `clk_intB` transitions close to posedge clk and is asynchronous with negedge clk sampling.

To fix this:

1. Implement two synchronous counters clocked at posedge clk and negedge clk respectively. Each counter cycles from 0 to 6 (for division by 3.5, i.e., 7 half-cycles).
   
2. Generate two intermediate clocks:
   - `clk_intA` from the posedge clock domain counter (counts number of half cycles elapsed).
   - `clk_intB` from the negedge clock domain counter (phase-shifted by half a clk period relative to `clk_intA`).
   
3. Both counters reset synchronously with the asynchronous reset.
   
4. Generate `clk_intA` and `clk_intB` as registers driven in their respective clock domains, asserted for 4 and 3 half-cycles respectively (which together average to 3.5 input clock cycles per full output clock cycle).
   
5. The final output `clk_div` is the OR of `clk_intA` and `clk_intB`, resulting in a stable, duty-cycle-corrected fractional clock output with 3.5 division.

This approach:
- Removes unsafe negedge sampling of a posedge synchronous combinational signal.
- Uses symmetrical counters at both edges to create phase shifted clocks naturally.
- Ensures signals are stable and synchronous in their respective clock domains.
- Produces a glitch-free fractional divided clock with uniform period and balanced duty cycle.

Hence, the design uses two counters, each clocked at one edge of the input clock, each generating half-cycle pulses, combined to form the fractional frequency divider output.

The only complexity is handling reset properly and ensuring counters wrap correctly, but this is straightforward.

This approach is a standard way to create fractional dividers using double-edge clocking.
