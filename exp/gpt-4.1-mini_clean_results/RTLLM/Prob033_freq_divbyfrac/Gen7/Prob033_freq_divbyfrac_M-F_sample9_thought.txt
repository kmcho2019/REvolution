To fix the fractional clock divider, the key is to eliminate independent counters on posedge and negedge clocks because they become misaligned, causing glitches and incorrect output.

Instead, use a single counter clocked on the rising edge of the input clock to track the position in the 7-cycle period. From this single counter, generate two pulse trains: one aligned to the rising edge (clk_pos) and another phase-shifted by half a clock period (clk_neg). 

To achieve the half-cycle phase shift without relying on negedge counters, generate clk_neg by sampling clk_pos and delayed versions of clk or by using internal logic that uses the counter to create a delayed version of clk_pos effectively shifted by half a clock.

One practical approach is:

1. Use a single 3-bit counter (0 to 6) that increments on every rising edge of clk.
2. Generate clk_pos as high for count < 4 (4 cycles high, 3 cycles low) per 7-cycle period.
3. Generate clk_neg by delaying clk_pos by half a clk period using a flip-flop clocked on the falling edge of clk. This flip-flop delays clk_pos by half a cycle, creating a phase-shifted version.
4. OR clk_pos and clk_neg to form clk_div.

By generating clk_neg as a delayed version of clk_pos clocked on negedge clk (not as a separate counter), the phase shift is ensured and alignment maintained, avoiding glitches and misalignment.

This design guarantees:
- A single counting domain.
- Proper phase-shift using clock edge-triggered flip-flop.
- No asynchronous resets; use synchronous reset for counters and registers.
- Balanced 3.5 division output with a stable duty cycle.

The counter and clk_pos are reset synchronously with rst_n. clk_neg is generated by a negedge clk flip-flop sampling clk_pos. Then clk_div = clk_pos | clk_neg.

This fixes the earlier flaws, aligning the fractional divider output with simulation expectations.
