The key improvement is to generate the two intermediate clocks (clk_pos and clk_neg) synchronously from a single 3-bit counter (count) running on the rising edge of clk, eliminating asynchronous or independent counters on falling edges that cause synchronization issues.

To achieve the fractional division by 3.5 with double-edge clocking:

- The counter counts from 0 to 6 (7 cycles total).

- clk_pos and clk_neg are generated as combinational functions of the current count, each having a specific duty cycle:

  - clk_pos is high for 4 counts (e.g., counts 0 to 3) and low for 3 counts (4 to 6).

  - clk_neg is phase-shifted by half a clock cycle relative to clk_pos; effectively, it is high for 3 counts and low for 4 counts, but shifted by 3 or 4 counts.

- By combining these two signals with an OR gate, the final clk_div has a uniform period and duty cycle close to 50%, achieving the fractional division by 3.5.

- Both clk_pos and clk_neg are generated synchronously, avoiding asynchronous toggling and metastability risks.

- This approach uses pure synchronous logic, no asynchronous counters or toggling on negedge clk.

- The design produces a stable fractional frequency divider by 3.5 with good duty cycle and is fully synthesizable.

This method fixes the original problem of unsynchronized counters and ensures correct phase shift by defining clk_neg as a shifted version of clk_pos based on the counter value.
