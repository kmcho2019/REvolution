The given approach tries to implement fractional division by toggling two intermediate clocks (clk_pos on rising edges and clk_neg on falling edges) based on a 7-count cycle, then OR-ing them to produce the final fractional divided clock.

The main problem lies in referencing the same counter value `cnt` (updated on rising edge) inside the falling edge always block. This causes timing and synchronization issues because `cnt` is not synchronous to the falling edge of clk, resulting in glitches or latch inference.

To fix this, I will:

1. Use a single counter running on the rising edge only.
2. Implement both clk_pos and clk_neg toggling decisions based on this counter and the clock edge, but avoid sampling `cnt` in the negedge always block directly.
3. Instead of directly toggling clk_neg on the falling edge with a `cnt` reference, generate a falling edge enable signal by extending the logic into two separate clock domains simulated by using delayed signals or derived enables. However, this is complex in pure synchronous logic without extra timing assumptions.

A cleaner and robust approach is:

- Use a 1X clock for posedge updates.
- Derive a half-clock delayed signal by delaying clk by half period to simulate the falling edge toggling effect synchronously.
- Alternatively, generate clk_neg toggling logic also on posedge clk but delayed by half cycles, using the 7-count cycle to determine when to toggle each of clk_pos and clk_neg.

Practically, since it's not possible to sample `cnt` on falling edge correctly in pure synchronous logic, I will implement the entire logic on the rising edge with the help of a phase-shifted enable signal.

Implementation plan:

- Counter increments 0..6 at every rising edge.
- clk_pos toggles on counts 0 and 4 (two toggle points per 7 cycles).
- clk_neg toggles on counts 1 and 5, but on a "half-cycle delayed" clock.
- Since we can't clock on falling edge, we simulate the falling edge effect by toggling clk_neg half cycle later than clk_pos by using a clock enable signal that shifts the toggle timing.

This is done by introducing a 1-clock-cycle delayed version of the counter (`cnt_dly`), which effectively shifts clk_neg toggling relative to clk_pos toggling.

Finally, OR clk_pos and clk_neg outputs for the fractional clock output.

This removes direct negedge blocks and keeps all toggling synchronous, preventing metastability and glitches, and making the design synthesis-friendly.

The output clock clk_div will have a 3.5 division ratio with balanced duty cycle and stable phase-shifted edges.

I will also initialize clk_pos and clk_neg to 0 on reset to avoid unknown states.
