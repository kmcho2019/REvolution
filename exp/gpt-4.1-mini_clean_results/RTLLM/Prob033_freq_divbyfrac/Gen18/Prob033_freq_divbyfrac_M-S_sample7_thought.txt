To achieve a fractional frequency division by 3.5 using double-edge clocking, I will simplify the design by creating a free-running 3-bit counter that cycles from 0 to 6 (7 counts total). The output clock will toggle at two distinct counts on the rising edge, and two distinct counts on the falling edge, effectively producing two uneven half-periods (4 and 3 input clocks).

This approach leverages symmetrical toggling points without needing extra delayed counters or complex logic. The output clock is generated by OR'ing the two intermediate clocks toggled on rising and falling edges.

Key simplifications:
- Use a single 3-bit counter updated on rising edge.
- Generate clk_pos toggled on counts 0 and 4 at posedge clk.
- Generate clk_neg toggled on counts 1 and 5 at negedge clk (using a sampled counter synchronized to clk negedge).
- Initialize both clk_pos and clk_neg to zero at reset for deterministic start.
- OR clk_pos and clk_neg to produce the final fractional divided clock.

This results in a smooth fractional division with correct duty cycle and phase alignment, while greatly simplifying the code and control logic.
