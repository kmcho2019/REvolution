To refactor the fractional frequency divider for a division of 3.5 (7/2), I will maintain the core concept: generating two intermediate clocks toggled unevenly over 7 input clock cycles (4 cycles high, 3 cycles low) and combining these phase-shifted clocks to produce the final fractional output.

However, instead of using separate counters and toggles on positive and negative edges of `clk`, I will:

1. Use a single counter driven on the positive edge of `clk` to count from 0 to 6 (7 cycles total).
2. Generate the primary divided clock (`clk_div_even`) as a combinational signal based on the counter value, high for the first 4 counts and low for the next 3 counts, producing a 4/3 duty cycle.
3. To create the half-clock-period phase shift, I will generate `clk_div_odd` by sampling a delayed version of `clk_div_even` on the opposite clock edge. Since pure RTL cannot create exact half-cycle delays, I will use a flip-flop clocked on the negative edge of `clk` to delay `clk_div_even` by half a clock.
4. The final output `clk_div` is the OR of `clk_div_even` and `clk_div_odd`.

This eliminates dual counters and reduces complexity, providing a cleaner implementation while preserving the double-edge clocking concept.

Reset is synchronous and initializes all registers.

This approach should ensure synchronized, glitch-free generation of the fractional clock with the intended duty cycle and phase relation.
