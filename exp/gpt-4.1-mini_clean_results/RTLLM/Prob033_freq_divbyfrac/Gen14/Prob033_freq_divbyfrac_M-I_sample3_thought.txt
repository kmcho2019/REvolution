The previous solution attempts to implement a fractional divider by 3.5 using a double-edge counting technique, but it splits the counting and clk_div toggling logic across two always blocks triggered on opposite clock edges. This leads to multiple drivers for the clk_div register and potential race conditions, violating good synchronous design principles.

Improvement strategy:
- Use a single 3-bit counter that counts edges on both posedge and negedge of clk, effectively counting every half-clock cycle.
- Increment the counter modulo 7 on every clk edge (pos and neg).
- Maintain a single always block triggered on both edges for counter update and toggling clk_div.
- Use a derived signal or synchronized logic to decide when to toggle clk_div based on the counter value and edge type.
- To differentiate posedge and negedge within one always block, use the clock input as a sampled variable or edge detectors, or simply define separate toggling points on posedge and negedge in a single always block by checking the clock level.
- This ensures clk_div is driven from one always block, avoiding race conditions.
- The toggling points remain the same: toggle clk_div on cnt == 0 or 4 when clk is rising edge; toggle clk_div on cnt == 2 or 6 when clk is falling edge.
- Since we count every edge (pos and neg) modulo 7, the counter rolls over cleanly.
- Reset clk_div and counter synchronously on reset.
- This approach keeps fractional division frequency accurate and achieves good duty cycle by evenly spacing toggles on posedge and negedge clock edges.
- The code is cleaner, synthesizable, and free from multiple drivers on clk_div.

This method follows the original conceptual approach but fixes race conditions and integrates counting and output generation into a single process, resulting in robust and correct fractional frequency division by 3.5.
