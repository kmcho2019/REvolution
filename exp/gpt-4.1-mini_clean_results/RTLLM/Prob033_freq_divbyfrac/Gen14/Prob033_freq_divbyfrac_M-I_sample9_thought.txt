To fix the previous design's issue with generating a clean half-clock phase shift for clk_neg, we replace the simple negedge sampling with an explicit clock enable (clk_en) toggling at negedge, synchronized to the same counter cycle. Instead of just sampling clk_pos on negedge clk, we implement clk_neg as a separate flip-flop toggled at negedge clk, controlled by a slightly adjusted count logic to achieve the proper 3.5 division with balanced duty cycle and phase alignment.

Specifically:

- Use the same 3-bit counter running on posedge clk counting 0 to 6.
- Generate clk_pos as high for counts 0..3 and low for 4..6 (4 cycles high, 3 low).
- Generate clk_neg toggled on negedge clk, but toggle only at specific counter states so that clk_neg is phase-shifted by half a clock period relative to clk_pos, with the complementary high/low cycle lengths (3 cycles high, 4 cycles low), effectively generating the delayed phase.
- The toggle points for clk_neg are chosen to split the 7 cycle period into 3 and 4 cycles (inverse of clk_pos), shifted by half clock.
- Finally, OR clk_pos and clk_neg to get clk_div with near 50% duty cycle fractional division by 3.5.
- Use synchronous reset for all registers aligned on respective clock edges for stable start-up.

This approach eliminates the timing ambiguity of directly sampling clk_pos on negedge clk and ensures the two clocks are complementary and phase-shifted by a half cycle, achieving a clean fractional frequency divider output.
