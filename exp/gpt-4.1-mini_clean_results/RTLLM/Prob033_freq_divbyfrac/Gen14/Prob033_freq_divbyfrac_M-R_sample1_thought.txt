I will keep the core idea of using a 7-cycle counter and double-edge clocking for 3.5x division but refactor the implementation for clearer phase alignment and smoother duty cycle control.

Key changes:
- Use combinational logic to generate the toggling conditions from the counter values, avoiding separate toggle request registers.
- Generate two intermediate clock signals directly: one toggling on posedge clk at counts 0 and 4; another toggling on negedge clk at counts 3 and 6, creating a half-cycle phase shift.
- This symmetrical choice of toggle points (posedge toggles at 0 and 4; negedge toggles at 3 and 6) splits the 7-cycle period into roughly equal segments (4 cycles and 3 cycles).
- Initialize both intermediate clocks to zero on reset.
- The output clock is the OR of the two intermediate clocks, resulting in a fractional frequency division by 3.5 with a more balanced duty cycle and improved waveform smoothness.
- This avoids registering the toggle requests and ensures immediate toggling on edges when the counter matches the designated values.
- The approach stays fully synchronous with the counter only updating on posedge clk, while the negedge clock toggling uses combinational counter values sampled at the previous posedge.

This structure clarifies the relationship between counter values and toggle points, improves phase alignment of toggles relative to clock edges, and thus improves output waveform correctness.
