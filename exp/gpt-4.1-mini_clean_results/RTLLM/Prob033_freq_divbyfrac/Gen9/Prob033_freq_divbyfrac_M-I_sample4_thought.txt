To accurately implement a divide-by-3.5 fractional frequency divider using double-edge clocking and generate a smooth output clock with approximately 50% duty cycle, we need two intermediate clocks phase-shifted by half a clock period.

The approach:
- Use a modulo-7 counter running on the positive edge of the input clock.
- Generate one intermediate clock (`clk_intA`) toggling every 4 and 3 input clock cycles to realize uneven high and low periods.
- Generate another intermediate clock (`clk_intB`) similarly, but triggered on the negative edge of the input clock, effectively phase-shifting it by half a clock period.
- Combine the two clocks with a logical OR operation to produce the final fractional output clock (`clk_div`).

This method exploits both edges of the input clock to get the half-cycle phase shift without complex phase-shifting elements or delays. The uneven periods 4 and 3 cycles alternate, producing the fractional divide-by-3.5.

All flip-flops are synchronous (except the negative edge counter for phase B, which is required for phase shift), and asynchronous reset is applied.

This design strictly meets the requirement, producing a smooth fractional divided clock with reduced duty cycle distortion and a clean 3.5 division ratio.
