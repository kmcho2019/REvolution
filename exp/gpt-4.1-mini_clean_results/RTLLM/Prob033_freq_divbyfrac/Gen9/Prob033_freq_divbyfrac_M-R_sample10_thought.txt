To avoid asynchronous issues caused by toggling `clk_odd` on the negedge of `clk` and reading the counter asynchronously, we refactor the design to operate entirely within the rising edge clock domain, eliminating clock domain crossing.

We keep a 3-bit counter that counts from 0 to 6, representing a 7-step cycle.

Instead of toggling on negedge clk, we generate both `clk_even` and `clk_odd` signals combinationally from the counter and a flip-flop state updated on the rising edge of `clk`.

- Use two toggle flip-flops updated on posedge clk based on count values, avoiding negedge clock domain logic.
- For example, generate toggle enables for clk_even at counts 0 and 4, and clk_odd at counts 1 and 5.
- Update both toggles synchronously on the rising edge.
- Use a 1-bit signal `clk_even` toggled when `cnt` equals 0 or 4.
- Use a 1-bit signal `clk_odd` toggled when `cnt` equals 1 or 5.
- The counter resets to zero synchronously on reset and rolls over at 6.
- Generate `clk_div` as OR of `clk_even` and `clk_odd`.

This method eliminates asynchronous signals and domain crossing problems and still achieves fractional division by 3.5 using staggered toggling points to create uneven clock periods, then OR-ing the outputs to generate a clock with improved duty cycle.

The key difference is that all state updates occur on the rising edge of `clk` with synchronous reset, and no toggling on negedge clk.

This ensures glitch-free output, simplifies the timing, and maintains the functional intent of the original design.
