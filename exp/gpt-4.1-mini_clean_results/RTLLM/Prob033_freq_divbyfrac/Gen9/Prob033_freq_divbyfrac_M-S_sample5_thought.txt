To simplify the fractional divide-by-3.5 clock divider using double-edge clocking:

- Use a modulo-7 counter running on the rising edge of clk.

- Generate clk_intA as high for counts 0 to 3 (4 cycles).

- Generate clk_intB as high for counts 0 to 2 (3 cycles).

- Instead of registering clk_intB on both edges, directly generate clk_intB_pos on posedge clk and clk_intB_neg on negedge clk using the counter's value sampled appropriately.

- Implement a simple dual-edge triggered process:

  - One always block on posedge clk updates the counter and clk_intA, clk_intB_pos.

  - One always block on negedge clk updates clk_intB_neg from the counter sampled at posedge clk (stored in a register).

- Finally, output clk_div as OR of clk_intA and clk_intB_neg.

This method ensures clk_intB_neg is sampled on negedge clk in sync with the stored counter value to avoid glitches.

It eliminates asynchronous resets on negedge clk to simplify reset logic and minimize metastability.

The fractional divided clock will have alternating high periods of 4 and 3 clk cycles, with the second clock phase shifted by half a cycle via sampling clk_intB on negedge clk, producing a smooth 3.5 division.

This approach reduces complexity and improves reliability while maintaining the fractional division function.
