To achieve a fractional frequency division by 3.5 (i.e., output frequency = input frequency / 3.5), we need to generate an output clock that toggles every 3.5 input clock cycles on average.

A novel approach is to generate a clock enable signal that is asserted in a pattern of 4 and 3 input clock cycles alternately, creating an average toggle interval of 3.5 cycles.

**Key idea:**

- Use a 1-bit output clock that toggles on an enable pulse.
- Generate a "toggle enable" pulse every 3 or 4 input clock cycles alternately.
- We can implement this by a small state machine with a modulo-7 counter:
  - The counter counts from 0 to 6.
  - When counter == 3 or counter == 6, toggle the output clock.
- The toggling at count 3 creates a high period lasting 4 cycles (from count 0 to 3).
- The toggling at count 6 creates a high period lasting 3 cycles (from 3 to 6).
- This yields an average period of 7 input clocks for two full output clock cycles, so output frequency = clk / 3.5.

To improve the duty cycle and smoothness:

- Use double-edge clocking by creating two toggling signals:
  - One toggles on the posedge clk at count 3 and 6 (call it clk_a).
  - One toggles on the negedge clk at count 0 and 3 (call it clk_b), effectively shifted by half cycle.
- OR the two toggling signals to get final clk_div.
- This interleaving creates a fractional division clock with a better duty cycle and reduced jitter.

All counters and toggling registers are synchronous to clk or negedge clk with proper reset.
This approach avoids complex delays or phase shift primitives and relies purely on counters and toggles.

Advantages:
- Single simple modulo-7 counter.
- Double-edge toggling to mimic half-cycle phase shifts.
- Balanced toggling points creating uneven high/low pulse lengths for fractional division.
