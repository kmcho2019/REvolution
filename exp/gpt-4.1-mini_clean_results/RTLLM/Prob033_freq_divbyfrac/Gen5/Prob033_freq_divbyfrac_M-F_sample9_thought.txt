To properly implement a fractional frequency divider by 3.5x with good duty cycle and phase uniformity, we use double-edge clocking and generate two intermediate clocks (clk_int_a and clk_int_b) that represent uneven clock periods of 4 and 3 input clock cycles respectively, shifted by half a clock cycle relative to each other.

We run a 3-bit counter that increments on the positive edge of clk, counting from 0 to 6 (7 counts total). On this main clock, clk_int_a toggles to generate a divided clock with 4 cycles high and 3 cycles low (uneven period).

Separately, on the negative edge of clk, the same counter is sampled (delayed) and used to toggle clk_int_b with the complementary 3 high and 4 low cycles, effectively phase-shifting clk_int_a by half a clock period.

Finally, clk_div is generated by OR-ing clk_int_a and clk_int_b, producing a fractional divided clock with uniform period and duty cycle balanced by combining these two uneven but phase-shifted clocks.

The reset is synchronous to posedge clk, ensuring synthesis-friendly design.

This approach cleanly separates counting and toggling for the two internal clocks, uses posedge and negedge clk separately to create phase-shifted signals, and combines them logically to achieve the fractional division of 3.5x.

In summary:
- Counter increments on posedge clk (0 to 6)
- clk_int_a toggles at counts 0 and 4 (4 high half-cycles, 3 low)
- clk_int_b toggles at counts 2 and 6 on negedge clk (phase-shifted)
- clk_div = clk_int_a OR clk_int_b, producing a 3.5 division with good duty cycle

This approach implements the phase shifting logic explicitly and separates clocks for clarity and correctness.
