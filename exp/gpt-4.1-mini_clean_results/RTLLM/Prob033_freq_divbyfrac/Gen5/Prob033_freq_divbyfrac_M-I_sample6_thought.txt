To improve the reliability and correctness of the fractional frequency divider by 3.5, we must generate a stable half-cycle phase shift without ambiguous asynchronous sampling. Instead of sampling `clk_pos` on the falling edge of `clk`, we generate two clocks (`clk_pos` and `clk_neg`) directly from the counter and the main clock edges:

1. Use a modulo-7 counter counting from 0 to 6 on the rising edge of `clk`.
2. Generate `clk_pos` high for 4 cycles (cnt 0 to 3), low for 3 cycles (4 to 6).
3. Generate `clk_neg` by sampling the counter on the falling edge of `clk` and generating a complementary pattern shifted by half a clock:
   - On `negedge clk`, increment a falling-edge counter phase.
   - Generate `clk_neg` high for 3 cycles and low for 4 cycles, shifted relative to `clk_pos`.
4. Combine `clk_pos` and `clk_neg` by OR-ing to produce `clk_div` with a period of 7 cycles, giving an exact 3.5 division and balanced duty cycle over the full period.
5. Reset synchronously and asynchronously to avoid glitches.

This approach uses fully synchronous counters on both edges, avoids metastability from asynchronous sampling, and creates stable complementary clocks that generate a smooth fractional divided clock with correct duty cycle and phase alignment.

This method ensures:
- Precise timing control,
- Avoids any asynchronous sampling pitfalls,
- Generates a robust fractional frequency divider suitable for FPGA or ASIC implementation.

Note: The design assumes `clk` frequency is within synthesizable range and tools support dual-edge triggered logic.
