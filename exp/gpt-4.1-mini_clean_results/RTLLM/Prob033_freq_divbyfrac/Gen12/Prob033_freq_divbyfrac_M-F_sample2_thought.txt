To correctly implement a fractional frequency divider by 3.5 using double-edge clocking, we must leverage both the rising and falling edges of the input clock to create the required half-cycle phase shift. This cannot be done by simply toggling signals on the rising edge and logically OR-ing them, as that keeps the clocks aligned in phase.

The correct approach is:

- Use a 3-bit counter counting from 0 to 6 (7 states), updated on the rising edge of clk.
- Generate clk_intA on the rising edge of clk by setting it high for counts 0-3 (4 cycles) and low for counts 4-6 (3 cycles).
- Generate clk_intB on the falling edge of clk by setting it high for counts 3-5 (3 cycles) and low otherwise, but delayed by half cycle relative to clk_intA (since it is updated on the falling edge).
- Because clk_intB is updated on the falling edge, it inherently has a half-cycle phase shift relative to clk_intA.
- By OR-ing clk_intA and clk_intB, the output clk_div achieves a uniform period clock with frequency input clock divided by 3.5.
- Both clk_intA and clk_intB are registers updated synchronously on opposite clock edges, which avoids metastability and timing issues.
- Reset logic synchronously initializes all registers.

This approach correctly uses double-edge clocking to create the half-cycle phase shift needed for fractional division and solves the problem of the previous implementation where both internal clocks were synchronous and thus not phase shifted.

Finally, the output clock has a consistent 50% duty cycle averaged over the full 7 clock cycles (3.5 input clock periods), implementing the fractional division accurately.
