The original approach tries to create a half-clock-cycle phase shift by offsetting a modulo counter, which cannot truly represent half-cycle delays in synchronous logic operating only on posedge clocks. Also, using XOR instead of OR distorts the intended clock waveform.

To fix this and generate an accurate fractional divide-by-3.5 clock using the double-edge technique:

- Use two counters/toggles triggered on opposite clock edges (posedge and negedge) separately, each implementing uneven division cycles (4 and 3 cycles, respectively). This naturally creates a half-cycle phase difference between the two divided clocks.
- On posedge clk, a counter counts 4 cycles toggling clk_pos every 4 cycles.
- On negedge clk, a second counter counts 3 cycles toggling clk_neg every 3 cycles.
- By OR'ing clk_pos and clk_neg, the output clk_div effectively runs at a frequency clk / 3.5 with a 50% duty cycle averaged over the long term.
- Use synchronous reset and simple counters.
- This direct double-edge approach properly implements half-integer division and duty-cycle correction without needing complicated phase-shift approximations.

This technique is standard for fractional frequency division via double-edge sampling, ensuring correct timing alignment and waveform quality. It’s also synthesizable and robust.

Hence, the improved design uses two counters toggling clocks on posedge and negedge clk, generating the two clock phases that OR to produce the fractional divided clock.
