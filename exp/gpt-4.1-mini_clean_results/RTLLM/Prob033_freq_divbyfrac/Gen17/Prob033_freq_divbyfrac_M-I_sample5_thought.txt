The original code toggled the output clock only at two fixed counts of a modulo-7 counter, creating uneven high and low phases but did not implement the essential double-edge clocking and phase-shifted signals necessary for smooth fractional division with a better duty cycle.

To implement the fractional divider by 3.5 using double-edge clocking and phase-shifted intermediate clocks:

1. Use a modulo-7 counter that counts from 0 to 6 on the rising edge of clk.

2. Generate two intermediate clocks (clk_div_p and clk_div_n) that toggle at different counts to form uneven cycles of 4 and 3 clk periods respectively.

3. To realize the half-cycle phase shift between the two intermediate clocks, clock clk_div_n on the falling edge of clk (creating a phase shift of half a clk period relative to clk_div_p, which is clocked on the rising edge).

4. clk_div_p toggles at counts 0 and 4 (counting 4 and 3 cycles alternatively).

5. clk_div_n toggles at counts 2 and 6 on the falling edge.

6. Finally, the output clk_div is the OR of clk_div_p and clk_div_n, generating a smoother fractional frequency divided clock with approximately 50% duty cycle.

This method achieves the division by 3.5 (7 input clocks per 2 output clock cycles), effectively doubling toggling events by employing both clock edges, and balances the duty cycle by combining phase-shifted signals.

On reset, all signals are initialized.

This is synthesizable and uses standard clock edges without resorting to PLL or DLL primitives.
