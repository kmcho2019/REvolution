To improve timing alignment and synchronization, the counters and output signals can be combined into a single synchronous process with explicit state encoding rather than two independent counters running on opposite clock edges.

We implement a modulo-7 counter driven by the positive edge of the clock. The counter runs from 0 to 6, representing half clock cycles.

Two pulse signals are generated from the counter:

- clk_div_pos is high for counts 0 to 3 (4 cycles) corresponding to "high" half-cycles on the positive-edge timing.
- clk_div_neg is derived by a 1-count delayed version of clk_div_pos, effectively creating a half-cycle phase shift.

This delayed version simulates the negedge phase shift but is implemented synchronously on the posedge clock by using a 1-count delayed register.

Finally, the output clk_div is the OR of these two half-cycle shifted signals.

This approach removes the asynchronous reset on negedge, uses a single clock domain for counting and logic, and generates the phase shift internally with registers, avoiding glitches and misalignment from dual edge-triggered counters and asynchronous resets.

This refactor guarantees deterministic and glitch-free fractional division by 3.5, achieving the double-edge clocking effect internally without explicitly triggering logic on negedge clk.
