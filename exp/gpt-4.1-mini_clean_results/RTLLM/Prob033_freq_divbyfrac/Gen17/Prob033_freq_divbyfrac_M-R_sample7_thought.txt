To fix the original design’s issues with double-edge counting and phase-shifted toggling, I will:
- Use a single posedge clock domain.
- Double the internal clock frequency by generating a clock enable signal at both edges, effectively creating a "half-clock" tick.
- Implement a half-cycle counter incremented on every clk edge by using a signal toggled on every clk edge and sampled on posedge clk.
- Use this higher resolution counter to toggle `clk_pos` and `clk_neg` at appropriate counts corresponding to half cycles.
- Generate `clk_pos` toggling at counts 0 and 8 (4 full cycles).
- Generate `clk_neg` toggling at counts 4 and 11 (3.5 cycles offset by half clock cycle).
- Finally, OR `clk_pos` and `clk_neg` to produce the fractional division output.
  
This avoids multiple always blocks updating the same register asynchronously, and the entire logic is synchronous to posedge clk, improving timing and synthesis friendliness. The half-cycle counter effectively counts half periods by triggering increments on an internal toggle signal that changes on every clk edge.

This structure preserves the double-edge counting idea but implements it with a synchronous posedge clk-only counter, making the design synthesizable and correct.
