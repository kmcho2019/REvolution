Then, the delayed (phase-shifted) clock (clk_div_neg) is generated by sampling the same modulo counter value, but shifted logically by one count, and toggled on the negative edge clock domain with synchronous logic.

This approach uses one counter synchronized to the positive edge and a combinational decoding of the count to produce clk_div_pos (high for counts 0-3) and clk_div_neg (high for counts 1-3 and 0), effectively shifting clk_div_neg by half a clock period relative to clk_div_pos without relying on asynchronous resets or independent counters.

Both clk_div_pos and clk_div_neg signals are registered and synchronized on respective clock edges to avoid glitches and asynchronous combinational glitches. The final output is the OR of these two signals.

This design guarantees stable phase-shifted clock signals by having one source counter and carefully synchronized clock-domain logic, reducing timing uncertainties. The asynchronous reset is replaced by synchronous reset for better alignment.

Overall, this preserves the core technique of double-edge fractional division but ensures the internal signals are phase aligned and avoids glitches by careful synchronization and a single counting source.

This structure provides deterministic phase shift, better timing, and correct fractional division.
