To address the synchronization and phase alignment issues in the original design, I refactor the fractional divider to use a single synchronous process based on the positive edge of the input clock for all counting and toggling logic, avoiding asynchronous counters on negedge clk that cause phase drift.

I implement the double-edge technique by generating the two clock phases via internal registers updated on posedge clk with an explicit half-cycle delay achieved by a simple shift register and combinational logic.

The approach is:
- Use a 3-bit counter counting modulo 7 to represent the 7 half-cycles of the output clock period.
- Generate a toggle signal that changes state alternately after 4 and 3 counts to produce the half-integer division by 3.5.
- Use a delayed version of this toggle shifted by one clk cycle to represent the second phase shifted by half a clock period.
- Combine these two phases via OR to form the final output clock.

This synchronous method ensures stable phase alignment, eliminates asynchronous domain crossing, and preserves the required uneven pulse widths (4 and 3 cycles) for the 3.5 divisor with balanced duty cycle.

Using registered signals and carefully timed toggles results in glitch-free and stable fractional clock output. The logic is simpler and easier to time, enhancing PPA while keeping the original design intent.
