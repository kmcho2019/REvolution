Instead of using two independent counters on posedge and negedge clk, this design uses a single 3-bit counter cycling from 0 to 6 (7 states) synchronized on the posedge clk. The divided clock toggles twice per full count cycle to generate the fractional division by 3.5.

The idea is:
- The counter increments on each posedge clk, modulo 7.
- The divided clock toggles at two specific counts per cycle to produce alternating high and low durations of 4 and 3 clock cycles respectively.
- To implement the double-edge clocking effect and achieve a half-cycle phase shift without relying on negedge clk logic, we use a delay line internally within the same clock domain.
- Specifically, we generate a delayed version of the divided clock by one half clk period using a clever technique:
  - Generate two clocks internally with complementary duty cycles and phase shift by using a shift register.
- The final output clk_div is formed by OR'ing the main divided clock and its half-cycle delayed version to achieve an output frequency of input clk divided by 3.5, with a balanced duty cycle.

This method ensures everything runs in one clock domain (posedge clk), greatly improving timing and synchronization, and avoids asynchronous toggling or counters on negedge clk, thus preventing phase drift.

The output clk_div frequency = clk / 3.5.

The toggle points for the divided clock are at counts 3 and 6 (4 clocks high, 3 clocks low alternately).

We create a "half-cycle delayed" clock by sampling the divided clock output at the midpoint of the input clock using a simple 1-bit register clocked at posedge clk, but updated with the inverted divided clock, producing an effective half cycle shift when OR'ed with the original.

This implementation is cleaner, robust, and hardware-efficient.
