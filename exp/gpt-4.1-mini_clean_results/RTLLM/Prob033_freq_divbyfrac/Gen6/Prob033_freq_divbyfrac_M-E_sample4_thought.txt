We implement this by a modulo-7 counter that increments every input clock cycle, counting from 0 to 6. We generate the output clock with a toggle at counts 3 and 6, effectively producing an output clock period alternating between 4 and 3 cycles.

2. Use the main clock's negative edge to generate falling edges at counts 0 and 3 (phase shifted by half a clock cycle), creating an output clock that toggles on both edges of the input clock.
3. Use two separate flip-flops for rising and falling edges, synchronized to posedge and negedge respectively.

Finally, combine these toggles to produce the final clock output, which toggles on both edges, yielding a clock at 1/3.5 the frequency of the input clock, with an improved 50% duty cycle and less jitter.
