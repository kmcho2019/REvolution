The original design concept is valid, but using a negative-edge triggered flip-flop for phase shifting `clk_div_even` introduces timing uncertainty and potential glitches, especially during reset and initial cycles.

To improve:

1. **Use a double counter approach with clock enables**:  
   Instead of sampling `clk_div_even` on the negative edge, we split the 7-cycle period into two phases (4 and 3 counts) and generate two pulse trains on the positive edge only, but phase shifted by half a clock cycle using clock enable signals derived from the counter.

2. **Implement half-cycle phase shift via clock enables and simple toggling**:  
   We'll create two intermediate clock signals toggled on edges determined by the counter. One toggles after 4 cycles, another toggles after 3 cycles, but offset in time by counting properly.

3. **Use synchronous reset and carefully sequence the toggles**:  
   This avoids asynchronous glitches and aligns outputs on positive edges only.

4. **Generate two clocks (`clk_div_odd` and `clk_div_even`) with toggle events spaced unevenly**:  
   The `clk_div_even` toggles every 4 cycles (high period), and the `clk_div_odd` toggles every 3 cycles, but delayed by half a clock, effectively generating a 3.5 division when OR-ed.

5. **OR the two clocks to get the fractional divided output with uniform period and balanced duty cycle**.

This method removes negative-edge flip-flops, reduces glitch chances, ensures synchronization and initialization, and better matches expected testbench behavior.

In summary, the approach is:

- One counter modulo 7 cycles (0..6).
- On count 0 and 4, toggle `clk_div_even`.
- On count 3 and 6, toggle `clk_div_odd`.
- Initialize toggles on reset.
- The OR of these two signals produces the fractional clock.

This method fully uses positive edge clocking, ensures synchronous reset, and achieves the fractional division 3.5 with duty cycle and phase correction.
