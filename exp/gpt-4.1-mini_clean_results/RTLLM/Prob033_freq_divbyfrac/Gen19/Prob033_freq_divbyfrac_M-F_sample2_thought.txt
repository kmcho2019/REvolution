To address the issues and produce a stable fractional divide-by-3.5 clock output with near 50% duty cycle using double-edge clocking, the design will be revised as follows:

1. **Use a single modulo-7 counter driven on the positive edge:**  
   This counter will cycle from 0 to 6, representing 7 input clock cycles for one complete output clock period.

2. **Generate two pulse trains (`clk_div_even` and `clk_div_odd`) with carefully timed toggles:**  
   - `clk_div_even` toggles on the positive edge at counts 0 and 4, producing a high period of 4 clock cycles and low period of 3 clock cycles.  
   - `clk_div_odd` toggles on the negative edge at counts 3 and 0, producing a high period of 3 cycles and low period of 4 cycles, but shifted by half a clock cycle compared to `clk_div_even`.

3. **Align `clk_div_odd` toggles with negative edge counts via a delayed version of the counter:**  
   Create a half-cycle delayed count (register the counter on negative edge) to know when to toggle `clk_div_odd` at negedge.

4. **Generate `clk_div_odd` from a separate toggle controlled at negedge clk:**  
   This ensures phase shift of half input clock period relative to `clk_div_even`.

5. **Final output `clk_div` is the OR of the two intermediate clocks:**  
   This merges the two pulse trains to form a fractional frequency clock with smooth edges, approximating 3.5 division.

6. **Use asynchronous active-low reset for deterministic startup.**

This approach explicitly controls toggling points on posedge and negedge clock edges based on modulo-7 counting to generate uneven periods that are phase-shifted by half clock cycle. The toggling positions ensure that the combined output has a uniform period with nearly 50% duty cycle, resolving the timing misalignments and glitches from the previous design.

The design does not rely on sampling signals between clock edges but on well-defined toggling events, improving stability and functional correctness.
