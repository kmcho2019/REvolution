The original design attempted a fractional divider using a phase accumulator triggered on both clock edges in a single always block, which is not supported by most synthesis tools and deviates from the problem's requirement to use uneven clock cycles and phase-shifted clocks.

Improvement Strategy:
- Implement a conventional and synthesis-friendly design by counting source clock cycles modulo 7 (since division by 3.5 = 7/2).
- Generate two clock enable signals corresponding to 4 and 3 cycle intervals, which produce two clocks with uneven periods.
- Use two flip-flops clocked on opposite clock edges (posedge and negedge) to generate phase-shifted clocks based on these enable signals.
- OR these two intermediate clocks to get the final fractional divided clock output.
- This double-edge technique mimics half-clock-period phase shifts, producing an output clock with 3.5 division ratio, smooth duty cycle, and uniform period.
- Provide asynchronous active-low reset.
- This approach explicitly follows the problem description, uses standard synchronous logic on posedge and negedge of the clock, avoids unconventional always blocks, and ensures proper timing and glitch-free outputs.

The implementation uses:
- A 3-bit counter modulo 7 to track cycles.
- A state machine or combinational logic to generate clock enables with 4 and 3 cycle lengths.
