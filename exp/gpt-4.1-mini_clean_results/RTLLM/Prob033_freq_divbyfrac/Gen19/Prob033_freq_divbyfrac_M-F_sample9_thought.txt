The main issue is the failure to generate a true half-clock period phase shift between clk_pos and clk_neg. Delaying the counter by one full clock cycle does not create a half-period phase offset; it only shifts toggling by a whole clock cycle, which breaks the timing and makes clk_div behave incorrectly.

To fix this, the ideal solution is to toggle clk_pos and clk_neg on opposite edges of clk:

- clk_pos toggles on the rising edge of clk at counts 0 and 4.
- clk_neg toggles on the falling edge of clk at counts 1 and 5.

This effectively implements the double-edge clocking technique: clk_neg changes state halfway between clk_pos toggles, creating two clocks shifted by half a period.

Since `cnt` is updated on the rising edge, but we need its value on falling edge for clk_neg toggling, we must register `cnt` on the falling edge as well.

To safely do this:

1. Maintain `cnt` counter running on rising edge clk (0 to 6).
2. Create a sampled version of `cnt` on falling edge (`cnt_fall`).
3. On posedge clk, update `cnt`.
4. On negedge clk, read `cnt` and update `cnt_fall`.
5. Toggle clk_pos on posedge clk when `cnt` == 0 or 4.
6. Toggle clk_neg on negedge clk when `cnt_fall` == 1 or 5.

Reset both clk_pos and clk_neg to 0 on reset.

Finally, OR clk_pos and clk_neg to produce the fractional divided clock `clk_div`.

This approach aligns with the classical double-edge clocking method, avoids sampling asynchronous signals across clock edges (since the clk negedge always block is synchronous to the negedge of clk), and creates a stable half-cycle phase-shifted output.

We carefully handle reset asynchronously, and the counter cycles correctly from 0 to 6.
