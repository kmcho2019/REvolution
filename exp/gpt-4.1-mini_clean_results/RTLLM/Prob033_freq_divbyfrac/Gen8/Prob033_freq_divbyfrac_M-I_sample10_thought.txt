The original design uses a counter counting 0 to 6 to realize division by 3.5, splitting the period into two parts: 4 cycles and 3 cycles. It generates two clock phases: one based on the counter at posedge and the other sampled at negedge, then ORs these signals.

To improve correctness and clarity, and to better balance duty cycle:

- Use a single 3-bit counter counting from 0 to 6 on posedge.
- Generate clk_div_pos high for counts 0..3 (4 cycles) on posedge domain.
- Generate clk_div_neg high for counts 4..6 (3 cycles) on negedge domain.
- Instead of sampling the posedge counter asynchronously on negedge (which may cause timing issues), implement a separate negedge counter synchronized to clk (by counting on negedge), or better yet, generate clk_div_neg with a delayed and inverted version of the counter signal.
- Since the module uses double-edge clocking, define two enable signals (clk_div_pos and clk_div_neg) each asserted for their respective half-periods.
- Finally, OR these two signals for clk_div output.

This improves timing clarity and ensures that clk_div has a 50% duty cycle on average with a fractional frequency divide-by-3.5.

Reset is synchronous active low to initialize counters.

The design remains simple, free of metastability issues, and suitable for synthesis.
