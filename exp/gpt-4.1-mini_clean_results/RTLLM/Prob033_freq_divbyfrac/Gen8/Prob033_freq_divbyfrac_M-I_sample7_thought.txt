The original design attempts to achieve a fractional divide-by-3.5 by cycling a modulo-7 counter and generating two intermediate clocks (one high for 4 cycles and the other for 3 cycles) combined with a half-cycle phase shift via gating clk_intB with inverted clk. This gating-based phase shift is unreliable because it depends on combinational logic and the raw clock inversion, which can cause glitches and timing issues.

To correct and robustly implement the fractional divide-by-3.5 using the double-edge clocking technique:

1. Use a modulo-7 counter (0-6) driven by posedge clk.

2. Generate clk_intA: high for counts 0 to 3 (4 cycles).

3. Generate clk_intB: high for counts 0 to 2 (3 cycles).

4. Use two flip-flops clocked on opposing edges of clk to register clk_intB:
   - clk_intB_pos registers clk_intB on posedge clk.
   - clk_intB_neg registers clk_intB on negedge clk.

   These two registered versions of clk_intB produce two phases shifted by half clock period.

5. Finally, OR clk_intA and clk_intB_neg to produce clk_div.

This approach uses actual double-edge clocking to achieve the half-cycle phase shift, ensuring glitch-free, stable timing without relying on combinational gating.

While dual-edge clocking might increase design complexity and place constraints on synthesis and timing, it best matches the requirement for a fractional clock with half-cycle phase shift.

The clk_div output will have a period of 7 clk cycles, with alternating high periods of 4 and 3 clk cycles (due to clk_intA and shifted clk_intB), achieving an average frequency divide-by-3.5.

This fully synchronous, double-edge approach avoids metastability and timing hazards, producing a stable fractional divided clock.
