A novel approach to fractional division by 3.5 is to create a 7-step cycle that toggles an output clock after specific counts to approximate the 3.5 division ratio. We use a 3-bit counter (0 to 6) incrementing on the rising edge of the input clock. The output clock toggles twice per 7 input clock cycles, generating two output pulses: one lasting 4 input clocks, another lasting 3 input clocks.

To produce a smooth fractional clock with an improved duty cycle, use double-edge clocking by generating two clock signals:
- clk_even toggles on the rising edge of clk, switching at count 0 and count 4
- clk_odd toggles on the falling edge of clk, switching at count 1 and count 5 (shifted by half clock period)

We implement this by:
1. A synchronous counter incrementing on posedge clk to track phase.
2. A toggle flip-flop `clk_even` toggling at count == 0 and 4 on posedge clk.
3. A toggle flip-flop `clk_odd` toggling at count == 1 and 5 on negedge clk.
4. Output clock `clk_div` is generated by OR-ing clk_even and clk_odd.

This method produces output pulses that are staggered by half clock cycles, effectively combining to yield a fractional frequency output of clk/3.5 with a more balanced duty cycle and fewer glitches.

Additionally:
- Both toggles reset to 0 synchronously.
- Counter resets on active low reset.
- All state updates synchronized properly to avoid glitches.

This scheme achieves fractional division by mixing edges and toggling outputs at designed counts to create the 3.5 division with phase interleaving.
