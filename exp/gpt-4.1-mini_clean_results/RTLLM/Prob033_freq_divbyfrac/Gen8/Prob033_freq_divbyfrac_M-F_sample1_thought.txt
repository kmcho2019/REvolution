To correctly implement a fractional frequency divider by 3.5 using double-edge clocking, we must generate two complementary clock signals each toggling on opposite edges of the input clock, effectively producing a half-cycle phase shift between them.

Key points for the fix:
- Use a 3-bit counter that increments on every *posedge clk* to track cycles modulo 7.
- Generate clk_pos toggling on the posedge of clk: it should remain high for 4 cycles and low for 3 cycles, producing an uneven period (4 high + 3 low = 7 cycles).
- Generate clk_neg toggling on the negedge of clk, driven by the *next* counter value (count + 1 mod 7). clk_neg remains high for 3 cycles and low for 4 cycles, complementing clk_pos.
- By driving clk_pos on posedge and clk_neg on negedge, clk_neg is effectively phase shifted by half a clock period relative to clk_pos.
- Both clk_pos and clk_neg are registered outputs, avoiding glitches.
- Finally, clk_div is the OR of clk_pos and clk_neg, producing a clock with a period of 7 input clock cycles, achieving division by 3.5 with balanced duty cycle.
- The counter is updated only on posedge clk to maintain synchronization and avoid metastability.
  
This approach fixes the feedback issues by:
- Creating an actual half-cycle phase shift via clk edges.
- Registering intermediate clocks.
- Preventing overlapping high states on the same counter value by separating the timing domains (posedge vs negedge).
- Maintaining a stable and synthesizable fractional divider.
