The key to achieving a clean fractional frequency division by 3.5 (7/2) with balanced duty cycle using double-edge clocking is to create two complementary clocks that together form the divided clock with uniform period.

The previous approach sampled the positive-edge divided clock (`clk_div_pos`) at the negative edge of `clk` to generate a half-cycle delayed version (`clk_div_neg`). This simple sampling does not produce a consistent half-cycle phase shift required for smooth fractional division, causing glitches and mismatches.

To fix this, we need two complementary divided clocks generated from the same counter but triggered on opposite edges of `clk`:

- Use a 3-bit counter `cnt` counting 0 to 6 at positive edges of `clk`.

- Generate a divided clock `clk_div_pos` on posedge `clk` that is high for 4 cycles (cnt 0-3) and low for 3 cycles (cnt 4-6).

- Generate a second divided clock `clk_div_neg` on negedge `clk` based on the same count but delayed by half a cycle inherently by being updated on negedge. To align counting on negedge, latch the counter value at negedge into `cnt_neg`.

- `clk_div_neg` is high if `cnt_neg` is in the other half of the cycle (e.g., high for 3 cycles and low for 4 cycles), complementing `clk_div_pos` phase-wise.

- Finally, `clk_div` is the OR of `clk_div_pos` and `clk_div_neg`.

This approach creates two staggered pulses that interleave their high periods to form a uniform clock at half the frequency of the original divided clock, effectively dividing input frequency by 3.5 with a better duty cycle and clean edges.

By having two counters (or latches) triggered on opposite clock edges holding the count and generating each phase accordingly, the 0.5-cycle phase shift is naturally embedded. This removes the problem of naive sampling and ensures stable, glitch-free fractional division.

The reset synchronously clears the counters and outputs.

This fix fully realizes the double-edge clocking technique for fractional division by 3.5 and provides the expected waveform.
