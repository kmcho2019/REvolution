To improve the fractional divider design, I will remove toggling logic across posedge and negedge clk edges that cause synchronization issues. Instead, I will use a single synchronous posedge clk domain with a 3-bit counter cycling from 0 to 6.

The divided clock will be generated by comparing the counter value against thresholds that define the high and low periods of the output clock, which alternate between 4 and 3 input clock cycles, achieving the 3.5 division.

To create the effective double-edge clocking effect (phase shifts) and improve duty cycle, I will generate two intermediate clocks by defining two pulse signals that are offset in time:

- `clk_div_main` high when counter < 4 (high for 4 cycles, low for 3 cycles)
- `clk_div_shifted` high when (counter + 3) mod 7 < 4, which is phase shifted by half a clock period in terms of count, simulating a half cycle delay

Finally, I OR these two signals to generate the fractional output with a smoother duty cycle close to 50%.

This eliminates asynchronous toggling and cross-edge dependencies, making the design purely synchronous and glitch-free.

Reset initializes the counter to zero.

This approach preserves the original functionality (division by 3.5) using a different and more robust method based on combinational decoding of the counter instead of toggling flip-flops on both clock edges.

This leads to a clean and deterministic fractional clock with balanced duty cycle and proper phase distribution.
