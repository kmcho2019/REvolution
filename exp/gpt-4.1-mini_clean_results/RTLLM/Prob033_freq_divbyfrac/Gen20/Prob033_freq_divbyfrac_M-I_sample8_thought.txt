To improve the fractional frequency divider for 3.5x division using double-edge clocking, we focus on generating two intermediate clocks each toggling at uneven counts (4 and 3 cycles respectively) but phase-shifted by half a clock period to achieve a smooth fractional output. Key improvements:

- Use synchronous resets for both posedge and negedge logic to avoid metastability and ensure alignment.
- Generate one intermediate clock toggling on a pulse at posedge with 4 cycles period.
- Generate the other intermediate clock toggling on a pulse at negedge with 3 cycles period.
- Crucially, phase shift the negedge-derived clock by half clock cycle relative to the posedge clock (intrinsic by clocking on negedge).
- Both intermediate clocks toggle only on their respective rollover pulses (edge aligned).
- Finally, OR the two intermediate clocks to obtain the output fractional clock.
- This method ensures the output clock has an average period of 7 input clock cycles (3.5x division) and nearly 50% duty cycle.
- Avoid asynchronous resets; instead, synchronize resets on each clock domain.
- This approach leverages inherent phase difference of posedge and negedge clocks as the half-cycle phase shift.
- Toggling intermediate clocks on rollover pulses creates stable, glitch-free outputs.
- Clear separation of posedge and negedge logic ensures timing correctness and ease of implementation.
