To implement a fractional frequency divider by 3.5 (i.e., 7/2), I will use a toggle flip-flop approach combined with a mod-7 counter counting input clock edges. The key idea is to toggle the output clock every time the counter reaches a certain count, such that the output period covers 7 input clock cycles, but toggling occurs every half period, resulting in the 3.5 division factor.

Specifically, I will:

- Use a 3-bit counter that counts from 0 to 6 (mod-7).
- Toggle an output clock (`clk_div`) twice per 7 input clocks, so the full cycle of `clk_div` is 7 input clocks.
- To get 3.5 division, the output clock toggles at counter values 3 and 6 (halfway and full cycle), meaning the output clock's period = 7 input clocks and frequency = clk / 7 * 2 = clk / 3.5.
- To get a better duty cycle, toggle output clock at two uneven intervals (after 3 clocks and then after 4 clocks), resulting in a nearly 50% duty cycle by alternating the output high and low periods of lengths 3 and 4 input clocks.
- All logic synchronous on posedge clk and asynchronously reset by rst_n.

This approach uses a single counter and a simple toggle flip-flop, no negedge or double-edge clocking required. The uneven count intervals create the fractional division effect and a balanced duty cycle.

The output `clk_div` toggles at counts 3 and 6, and resets the counter at 6, generating a fractional frequency of clk/3.5 cleanly and simply.
