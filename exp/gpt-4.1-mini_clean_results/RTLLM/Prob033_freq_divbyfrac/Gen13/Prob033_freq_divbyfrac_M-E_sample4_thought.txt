To achieve fractional frequency division by 3.5, I will use a time-division approach based on a single synchronous counter running on the input clock's positive edge, cycling through 7 states (0 to 6) representing the multiplied division period (7 input clock cycles = 3.5 output clock cycles).

The output clock will toggle at specific counter values to produce the required uneven half-periods:

- The output clock period is 7 input clock cycles, so half periods are either 3 or 4 input clock cycles.
- To approximate a 50% duty cycle on average, the output clock will be high for 4 input clocks and low for 3 input clocks.
  
Implementation details:
- Use a 3-bit counter counting 0 to 6.
- Toggle the output clock at counts 0 and 4.
- Output clock is low at count 0, goes high at count 0 (toggle), remains high until count 4, then toggles low.
- This results in a clock that is high for 4 cycles and low for 3 cycles, with a period of 7 input clocks.
  
To improve duty cycle and smoothness, employ double-edge clocking by creating two clocks:
- One toggles at counts 0 and 4 on posedge clk.
- Another toggles similarly but shifted by half a clock period by toggling on negedge clk at the shifted counts.
  
Finally, OR these two clocks to generate the fractional clock with an effective half-cycle phase shift, producing a 3.5 division with reduced duty cycle distortion and smoother edges.

This method avoids asynchronous resets, uses a single synchronous counter on posedge clk, and derives the phase-shifted clock using negedge clk toggling logic with enable signals driven by the posedge counter state, guaranteeing phase alignment and avoiding counters running independently on different clock edges.

The design is fully synchronous and phase-aligned, ensuring stable and glitch-free fractional division output.
