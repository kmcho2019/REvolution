The original design uses a simple double-edge sampling approach to generate the half-cycle phase shift, but sampling div_clk_a on the falling edge of clk can cause glitches and timing misalignment because div_clk_a changes synchronously on clk rising edge.

To improve, the strategy is:

- Use a single counter cycling through 7 counts (0 to 6) synchronized on clk rising edge.
- Generate div_clk_a as high for 4 counts and low for 3 counts.
- Generate div_clk_b as a delayed version of div_clk_a but delayed by exactly half a clock period.
- Since pure half-cycle delay is hard to achieve in pure RTL without phase-shifted clocks, we emulate this by clocking div_clk_b on the falling edge of clk but **register the counter value itself** on clk falling edge, then generate div_clk_b based on this delayed count.
- This avoids sampling div_clk_a directly on falling edge and instead derives div_clk_b from a half-cycle delayed count, providing more accurate timing alignment.
- The final clk_div is OR of div_clk_a and div_clk_b, producing a 3.5x division with balanced duty cycle.
- Adding synchronous reset and clear signals ensures glitch-free startup.

This approach reduces metastability risk, ensures correct phase alignment by using delayed count instead of delayed signal, and guarantees smooth fractional frequency division with even duty cycle and no glitches.
