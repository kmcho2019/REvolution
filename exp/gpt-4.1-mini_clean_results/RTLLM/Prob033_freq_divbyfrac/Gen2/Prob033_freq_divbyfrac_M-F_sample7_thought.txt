To implement a fractional frequency divider by 3.5 using double-edge clocking with phase-shifted intermediate clocks, the design must:

- Use a single counter cycling from 0 to 6 (7 clock cycles total).
- Generate two intermediate clocks (clk_int_a and clk_int_b) that toggle unevenly: one clock stays high for 4 cycles and low for 3 cycles; the other is phase shifted by half input clock cycle, staying high for 3 cycles and low for 4 cycles.
- Achieve the half clock period phase shift by triggering clk_int_b updates on the negative edge of the input clock, while clk_int_a updates on the positive edge.
- Both clk_int_a and clk_int_b have non-overlapping active high periods shifted by half clock cycle, which when OR'ed, create a fractional clock with approximately 50% duty cycle.
- The final output clk_div is the logical OR of clk_int_a and clk_int_b.
- Reset initializes the counters and intermediate clocks to known states.
  
This approach captures the double-edge clocking and phase shifting needed to accurately generate the 3.5 division with balanced duty cycle. The counter values determine the high/low intervals of both intermediate clocks asynchronously (pos and neg edges), and their OR gives the smooth fractional clock output.
