To fix the fractional frequency divider by 3.5 using double-edge clocking, the key is to generate two intermediate clocks (`clk_pos` and `clk_neg`) that each have complementary uneven duty cycles (4 high/3 low and 3 high/4 low) and are phase-shifted by exactly half a clock cycle.

The main issues in the previous design were:

1. `clk_neg` toggling was driven by sampling the posedge counter asynchronously on negedge clock, causing timing uncertainty and improper phase alignment.
2. No dedicated negedge-domain counter or logic to generate the desired high/low durations for `clk_neg`.
3. The toggling scheme was oversimplified and didn't guarantee the required timing or synchronization.

**Proposed fix:**

- Maintain a single 3-bit counter that counts from 0 to 6 on the posedge `clk`.
- Generate `clk_pos` from this counter: high for counts 0-3, low for counts 4-6.
- Generate `clk_neg` using the negedge of `clk`. Because negedge occurs exactly halfway between posedges, we create a second 3-bit counter that increments on `negedge clk`. This counter also counts 0 to 6 cyclically, but is phase-shifted by half a clock cycle relative to the posedge counter.
- Use the negedge counter to generate `clk_neg`: high for counts 0-2 (3 cycles), low for counts 3-6 (4 cycles). This complements `clk_pos` timing and is phase-shifted by half clock.
- Synchronizing counters to their respective clock edges removes metastability and timing issues.
- Finally, OR `clk_pos` and `clk_neg` to produce the output `clk_div` which has a frequency divided by 3.5 with near 50% duty cycle.

This approach ensures:

- Both clocks have the correct uneven high/low cycles.
- `clk_neg` is phase-shifted by half clock due to negedge domain counter.
- Proper synchronization and clean timing.
- Clean fractional frequency division with balanced duty cycle.

We implement two counters: one on posedge clk for `clk_pos`, one on negedge clk for `clk_neg`. The output is the OR of these signals.

This design directly addresses feedback points by removing asynchronous sampling and simplifying the control logic.
