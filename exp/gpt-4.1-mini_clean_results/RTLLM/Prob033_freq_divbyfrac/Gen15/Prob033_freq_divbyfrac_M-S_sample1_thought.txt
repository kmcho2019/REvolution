To simplify the fractional frequency divider by 3.5, I will keep a single 3-bit counter cycling from 0 to 6 (7 half-cycles). Instead of toggling intermediate clocks on both edges with complicated synchronization, I will generate the final output clock purely on the positive clock edge by directly defining its level based on the counter.

The output clock will be '1' for 4 cycles and '0' for 3 cycles, forming one full period of 7 input clock cycles (which corresponds to 3.5 times frequency division). This inherently forms the uneven duty cycle needed.

To achieve a more balanced duty cycle and smooth waveform, I will generate the output as the OR of two shifted clocks: one starting at count 0, another delayed by half clock cycle simulated by a delayed counter. Instead of asynchronous toggling, the delayed version is generated by sampling the counter with a half clock cycle delay through a small pipeline (using a shift register or latch).

However, since we cannot generate true half-cycle delays inside a pure synchronous logic without phase-shifted clocks, the simplest approach is to use the double-edge toggling but fully controlled by posedge clk:

- Maintain the counter from 0 to 6.
- Generate clk_div with level '1' for counts 0 to 3 and '0' for 4 to 6, making the period 7 input clocks.
- Then, create a delayed version of clk_div by one half clock cycle by toggling a secondary clock signal on negedge clk using a flip-flop clocked by negedge clk, syncing it with the counter values delayed appropriately.

The output is the OR of clk_div and its half-cycle delayed version, which will create a fractional division with improved duty cycle without complex toggling or asynchronous logic.

This approach ensures all logic is synchronous and the phase shift is introduced by a simple register clocked by negedge clk, simplifying the previous design.

Reset initializes all registers to known states.

The design will be clear, fully synchronous, and maintain 3.5x frequency division with a better balanced output waveform.
