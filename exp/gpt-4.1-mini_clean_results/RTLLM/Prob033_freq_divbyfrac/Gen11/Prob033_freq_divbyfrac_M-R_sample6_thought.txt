The original design uses separate always blocks triggered on posedge and negedge clocks with independent counters and states to generate two phase-shifted clocks that are then XOR'ed to form a fractional divide-by-3.5 clock. However, this can be complex and prone to timing misalignments and off-by-one errors.

To refactor:

- Use a single counter running on the posedge of clk that counts from 0 to 6 (7 counts total).
- Generate two clock waveforms (`clk_pos` and `clk_neg`), representing toggling events at posedge and negedge of the input clock, by decoding the counter value.
- Since the half-clock phase shift corresponds to a negedge offset, use the counter plus an offset to produce a phase-shifted signal without relying on negedge-triggered processes.
- Implement both `clk_pos` and `clk_neg` as combinational outputs derived from the counter (and its offset), thus avoiding asynchronous logic and making timing explicit and stable.
- Finally, produce `clk_div` as the XOR of `clk_pos` and `clk_neg`.
- Use synchronous reset and counters only on posedge clock for simplicity and better synthesis results.
- This approach eliminates dual always blocks, simplifies state machines, and improves timing alignment by keeping all logic synchronous and combinational.
  
This restructured code improves reliability and clarity while maintaining the original fractional division behavior.
