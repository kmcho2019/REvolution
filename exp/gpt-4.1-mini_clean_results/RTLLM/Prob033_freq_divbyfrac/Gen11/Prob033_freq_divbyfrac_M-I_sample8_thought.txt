The prior approach using toggling at fixed counter values for clk_even and clk_odd is a good start but lacks precise control over pulse widths and phase relationships necessary for a stable and correct 3.5x fractional clock.

To improve:

1. Generate a 7-step counter (0 to 6) representing the 7 input clock cycles per output clock period.

2. Define two clocks `clk_even` and `clk_odd` based on the counter that produce pulses of uneven length (4 and 3 cycles) but phase-shifted by half a clock cycle relative to each other, emulating a double-edge clocking scheme.

3. Instead of toggling at specific counts, explicitly assign `clk_even` and `clk_odd` high or low based on the counter values:

   - `clk_even` is high for counts 0-3 (4 cycles) and low for counts 4-6 (3 cycles).

   - `clk_odd` is high for counts 1-4 (4 cycles, shifted by one count) and low for counts 5-0 (3 cycles).

4. Both are synchronous with the main clock, avoiding glitches and asynchronous toggling.

5. OR the two clocks to generate the final output clock `clk_div`.

This explicit pulse-width assignment and phase offset eliminate the toggling ambiguity, produce clean edges, and yield a nearly 50% duty cycle output with a fractional division of 3.5.

This method also simplifies the logic, improves stability, and ensures a deterministic initial state after reset.

Note: Because we cannot delay clocks by half cycles in pure RTL, shifting the pulse by one count approximates the half-cycle phase shift, and the OR combination produces the fractional divided clock with better duty cycle uniformity.
