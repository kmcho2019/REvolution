To safely implement fractional division by 3.5 with double-edge clocking and avoid metastability issues, the key is to update the counter and all related signals synchronously at the positive clock edge only.

We should generate the two intermediate clocks (clk_intA and clk_intB) as registers that toggle on the positive clock edge according to the count. Instead of sampling combinational signals at the negedge clock (which causes timing and metastability problems), we'll generate two intermediate clocks with different duty cycles by toggling them at specific counts, thereby inherently creating a half-cycle phase difference.

Concretely:
- Use a 3-bit counter counting from 0 to 6 (total 7 counts).
- Create two internal clocks, clk_intA and clk_intB, updated at posedge clk:
  - clk_intA is high for 4 counts and low for 3 counts.
  - clk_intB is high for 3 counts and low for 4 counts, but shifted by one count to generate a half-cycle phase difference.
- By OR-ing clk_intA and clk_intB, we generate the output clock clk_div which has a uniform period corresponding to 3.5 times the input clock period.
  
All registers update synchronously at posedge clk with reset handling. This avoids asynchronous sampling and ensures glitch-free operation, solving the previous problem of stuck or constant output.

This approach generates half-clock-phase shifted intermediate clocks internally, eliminating the need for sampling at negedge and thus removing metastability and timing issues.

Finally, the output clock has a 50% duty cycle averaged over the full fractional period, suitable for the fractional division by 3.5.
