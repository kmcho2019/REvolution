The core idea remains generating a 3.5x frequency division by counting modulo-7 cycles and splitting into 4-high and 3-low input clock cycles. However, to better manage the half-cycle phase shift and avoid clock domain crossing issues with inverted clock edges, I will:

- Implement the counter and clk_intA generation as combinational logic driven by the counter synchronous to clk.
- Generate clk_intB by using a one-bit delay line synchronized to clk (instead of clocking on clk_bar) but introduce the half-cycle shift by toggling clk_intB on clk falling edge based on clk_intA state.
- Use both edges of the input clock (posedge and negedge) to update clk_intB, producing a half-cycle shift inherently.
- Synchronize all logic in one clock domain by using both edges of clk, avoiding separate clocks.
- Produce clk_div as an OR of clk_intA and clk_intB, which now correctly have 4 and 3 cycles high split staggered by half clock.
- Reset synchronously and ensure initialization to avoid glitches.

This structure uses edge-triggered always blocks carefully to implement the half-cycle phase shift, maintains a single clock domain for ease of synthesis, and improves waveform correctness and timing.

The result is a cleaner and more robust fractional divider that divides input clock by 3.5 by combining uneven clocks and a half-cycle shifted version for better duty cycle.
