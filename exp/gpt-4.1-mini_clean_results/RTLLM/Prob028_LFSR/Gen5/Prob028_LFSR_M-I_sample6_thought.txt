The original design correctly implements a 4-bit LFSR with the specified feedback and synchronous reset behavior. However, initializing the LFSR to zero on reset is not ideal because an all-zero state in LFSRs is typically a lock-up state—once zero, it will remain zero forever, producing no pseudo-random sequence.

To improve PPA and functionality:

- Initialize the register to a non-zero seed value on reset (e.g., 4'b0001) to ensure the LFSR cycles through the maximal-length sequence.
- Replace inversion of the XOR for feedback with just XOR (or clarify the tap and inversion) to align with standard maximal LFSR polynomials. However, since the specification requires inversion, maintain that but seed to non-zero.
- Use an explicit reset value instead of zero to avoid lock-up.
- Keep synchronous reset for ease of timing and synthesis.
- The testbench remains valid, but add comments and a slightly improved reset sequence for clarity.
- Overall, these changes improve reliability and ensure the LFSR generates proper pseudo-random sequences with full period.

This small change avoids the trivial zero-lock problem and is standard practice for practical LFSR designs, improving functionality without impacting area or power significantly.
