To optimize the 4-bit LFSR for better power, performance, and area (PPA) while maintaining correctness and clarity, I will:

- Replace the combinational always block used for feedback calculation with a continuous assignment. This is more concise and typical for simple combinational signals, potentially improving synthesis optimizations.
- Use asynchronous active-high reset instead of synchronous reset to allow immediate reset response, reducing reset latency.
- Initialize the register to a non-zero seed on reset (e.g., 4'b0001) to avoid lock-up at all zeros (a common LFSR design practice to keep the sequence cycling).
- Use non-blocking assignments only in the sequential always block to prevent simulation-synthesis mismatches.
- Keep feedback as a wire since it is purely combinational.
- The LFSR still shifts left and inserts the feedback at LSB.
- By eliminating the extra always block and making the reset asynchronous, the design may have better timing and simpler logic, thus improved PPA.

This approach retains functionality but refines reset behavior and coding style, improving clarity and practical usability.
