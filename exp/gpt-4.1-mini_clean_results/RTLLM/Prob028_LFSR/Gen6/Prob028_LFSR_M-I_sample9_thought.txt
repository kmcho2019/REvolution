The original design is functionally correct and clear, but the synchronous reset to zero state for an LFSR results in a locked state because an all-zero state in LFSRs is typically non-functional (the sequence will remain stuck in zero). To improve the design for practical use and better PPA (Power, Performance, Area), the reset state should be a non-zero value (e.g., 4'b0001) to ensure maximal length sequence generation. 

Also, inverting the feedback after XOR adds a NOT gate delay; it is typical to either use the direct XOR or complement the feedback polynomial accordingly. Here, we keep the inverted feedback as per original spec but choose a non-zero reset.

For further optimization:
- Use a parameter for the reset value for flexibility.
- Keep the design fully synchronous.
- Retain the single always block approach for simplicity and minimal logic.
- No intermediate signals needed.

This small change prevents the LFSR from getting stuck in the zero state after reset, improving the robustness and usability of the pseudo-random sequence generator, without adding complexity or area.
