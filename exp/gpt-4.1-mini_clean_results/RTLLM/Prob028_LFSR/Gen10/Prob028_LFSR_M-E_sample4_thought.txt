Implement the 4-bit LFSR using a shift register initialized to a non-zero value on reset to avoid the lock-up state at zero. Use a synchronous always block triggered on the rising clock edge. Instead of calculating feedback from the current output, maintain the LFSR state internally in a separate register and only assign this state to the output port, separating internal state and output. The feedback bit is calculated as the XOR of the highest and second highest bits, without inversion, and shifted into the LSB. This approach avoids feedback inversion but still produces a maximal length sequence for a 4-bit LFSR with the chosen taps. Initializing to a non-zero seed on reset prevents the all-zero deadlock state common in LFSRs.
