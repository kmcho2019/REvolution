Instead of the classic left-shifting LFSR with feedback applied at the LSB, we can implement a 4-bit Fibonacci LFSR that shifts right with the feedback applied at the MSB. This approach changes the feedback calculation to XOR bits 0 and 1 (the two least significant bits), feeding back into the MSB (bit 3) on each clock cycle.

Key points of this new design:
1. Feedback = XOR of the two least significant bits (out[0] ^ out[1]).
2. Shift register shifts right on each clock cycle.
3. On synchronous active-high reset, initialize the register to a non-zero seed (e.g., 4'b1000) to avoid lock-up at zero.
4. Output always reflects the current state of the LFSR.
5. This right-shifted Fibonacci LFSR architecture is functionally equivalent but offers a different structural view, sometimes preferred in hardware.
6. Provide a concise testbench verifying the behavior by observing the output sequence over multiple clock cycles and performing reset sequences.

This new architecture still produces a maximal length sequence for a 4-bit LFSR but via a different feedback and shifting scheme.
