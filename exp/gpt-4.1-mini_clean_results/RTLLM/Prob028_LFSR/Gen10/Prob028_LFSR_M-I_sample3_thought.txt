The original solution is functionally correct, but initializing the LFSR to all zeros causes it to lock up and never produce a changing pseudo-random sequence. A 4-bit LFSR typically should not start with all zeros because it is a terminal state. To fix this, initialize the LFSR to a nonzero seed value (e.g., 4'b0001) on reset. This ensures the LFSR cycles through a maximal-length pseudo-random sequence. The rest of the logic remains the same, keeping the inverted XOR feedback and synchronous reset. This improves functional correctness and usability while maintaining simplicity and minimal area.
