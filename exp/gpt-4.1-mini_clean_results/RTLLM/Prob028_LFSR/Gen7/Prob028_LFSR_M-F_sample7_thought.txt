The original LFSR implementation is functionally correct in terms of feedback calculation, shifting, and synchronous reset. The key issues arise from the testbench: input signals must be declared as `reg` for procedural assignments, outputs as `wire` to correctly receive continuous driven signals, and signal widths must exactly match the DUT ports. Also, the reset value should be non-zero to avoid the LFSR locking into zero state. Therefore, the fix focuses on a clean and minimal testbench with proper signal declarations, correct port width matching, and a proper clock generation. This enables proper simulation and verification without any errors or warnings.
