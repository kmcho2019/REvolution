1. **Avoid inverted feedback signal assignment in combinational logic:**  
   Instead of inverting the XOR of bits to get feedback, it's often better to choose taps and feedback as per a known maximal-length polynomial. The inversion can complicate synthesis slightly. For a 4-bit LFSR, a common maximal-length tap is XOR of bit 3 and bit 2 without inversion. However, if the problem requires inversion, I will keep it.

2. **Use non-blocking assignments for registers (already correct).**

3. **Avoid initializing LFSR to zero on reset:**  
   Initializing an LFSR to all zeros makes it lock up forever (all zeros state is invalid for an LFSR). Typically, LFSRs are initialized to a non-zero value. So I will initialize the LFSR to 4'b0001 on reset.

   This makes the module reusable.
