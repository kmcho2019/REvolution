The key issue was that the LFSR was initialized to zero on reset, which causes it to lock in the zero state and never generate a sequence. To fix this, initialize the LFSR output to a non-zero seed value on reset, for example 4'b0001. The feedback calculation remains the inverted XOR of the two highest bits (out[3] and out[2]). On each clock rising edge, if not in reset, the register shifts left by one, and the new feedback bit is inserted at the least significant bit (LSB). This prevents lock-up at zero and enables the LFSR to produce a proper pseudo-random sequence as intended.
