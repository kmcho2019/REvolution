To optimize the 4-bit LFSR for better power, performance, and area (PPA), I will:

1. Use an asynchronous active-high reset to allow immediate clearing of the register, reducing latency on reset and simplifying state initialization.
2. Use a simple always_ff block (SystemVerilog style) to improve synthesis readability, if allowed.
3. Remove the inversion on the feedback signal, as it typically is not necessary for maximum-length sequences and can simplify logic and reduce power. However, since the problem states the feedback is inverted, I'll keep it as is for correctness.
4. Use a localparam for the bit indices to clarify which bits are used for feedback, enhancing readability.
5. Make the register "out" a logic type (if SystemVerilog is allowed) or keep it reg in Verilog.
6. Synthesize feedback combinationally outside the clocked block, which is optimal.
7. Keep the feedback logic as simple as possible for lower gate count.
8. Use synchronous reset as in the original problem if asynchronous reset is not allowed.
9. Provide a known non-zero seed to avoid lock-up at zero.
10. Add synthesis directives to infer flip-flops cleanly and help optimization.

This approach balances clarity, correct functionality, and typical LFSR efficiency with low area and power.

If strictly pure Verilog without SystemVerilog, I'll keep reg and always @(posedge clk).
