The original solution correctly implements the 4-bit LFSR with left shifting and feedback insertion at the LSB based on the inverted XOR of bits 3 and 2. However, it uses a non-zero seed (4'b1001) on reset to prevent lock-up in an all-zero state, which is good.

To improve the design, I will:

1. Use a synchronous reset to avoid asynchronous glitches.
2. Initialize the register on reset to the seed as before.
3. Make the feedback calculation fully combinational outside the always block.
4. Add a parameterized seed value to allow flexibility in initialization.
5. Ensure the design is fully synthesizable and clear.
6. Add comments for clarity.

These steps enhance readability, reusability, and maintainability without affecting performance or area significantly. The original design is already efficient and minimal for a 4-bit LFSR, so no further PPA optimization is needed.

The key is to keep the interface simple and consistent, which helps in successful simulation and implementation.
