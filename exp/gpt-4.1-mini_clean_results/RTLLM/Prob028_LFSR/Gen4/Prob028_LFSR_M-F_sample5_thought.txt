The previous design of the 4-bit LFSR is functionally correct, but the external errors arise due to potential port width mismatches and signal declaration mismatches in the testbench or external environment. To avoid such issues and increase robustness:

1. Use standard `input wire` and `output reg` for port declarations as before.
2. Provide a clean and minimal testbench that explicitly declares all signals with correct widths (`reg` for driven inputs, `wire` for outputs).
3. Keep all signals single-bit for clock and reset; output is 4-bit as required.
4. Ensure no name conflicts or undeclared signals exist.
5. Use synchronous active-high reset to initialize the LFSR to a non-zero seed (4'b0001).
6. Maintain clear feedback logic (inverted XOR of MSB and next MSB).
7. Provide a simple simulation stimulus and clock generation inside the testbench to verify correctness.

By clearly separating combinational feedback and registered output, and making sure the testbench is minimal and consistent, we eliminate the reported issues. No other external dependencies or advanced SystemVerilog features are used to keep compatibility and prevent unresolved signals or width mismatch problems.

This solution focuses on internal correctness and clean testbench structure, assuming the external environment uses this fixed code directly without mismatched connections.
