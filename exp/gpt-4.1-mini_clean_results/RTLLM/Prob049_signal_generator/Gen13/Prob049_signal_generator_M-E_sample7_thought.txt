Use a signed 6-bit register to hold the waveform value allowing a simple addition or subtraction of 1 every cycle according to a direction bit. The output wave is taken as the lower 5 bits of this signed register, ensuring values stay within 0 to 31. On each clock, the signed register increments or decrements by 1 depending on the direction state. When the signed value reaches the boundaries (0 or 31), the direction flips. This approach avoids separate increment/decrement logic or explicit case statements, instead leveraging simple arithmetic and boundary checks on a single signed register. It reduces complexity and uses arithmetic comparison which can be efficiently synthesized.
