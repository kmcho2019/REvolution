To optimize the design for better power and possibly timing, reduce unnecessary toggling of the state and wave registers at the boundary conditions by eliminating the "hold" behavior in next_wave. Instead of holding wave constant when it reaches 0 or 31, directly switch the state and update wave accordingly on the same clock edge, thus removing a cycle of no change. This allows wave to always update by either incrementing or decrementing, avoiding redundant cycles where wave holds steady but state toggles. Additionally, use synchronous reset instead of asynchronous reset to simplify timing closure in typical FPGA or ASIC flows. This can improve timing and area slightly by removing asynchronous reset logic. Finally, minimize combinational logic by combining the state and wave update logic into one clocked always block and removing the extra combinational next_wave register, making the design more compact and possibly faster.
