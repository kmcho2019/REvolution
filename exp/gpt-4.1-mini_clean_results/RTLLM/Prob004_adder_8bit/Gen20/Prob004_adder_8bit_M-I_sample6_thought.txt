The original design uses a hierarchical 4-bit carry lookahead adder (CLA) structure, which is efficient but can be further optimized for area and power by using simple full adders in a ripple carry configuration for only 8 bits. 

Considering the small operand width (8 bits), the overhead of the CLA logic (generate and propagate signals, plus multiple gates for carry lookahead) can be more than the area and power cost of ripple carry using full adders. Ripple carry full adders are simpler, have fewer gates, and usually smaller area and power on small bit-widths. 

The ripple carry approach also has a straightforward combinational logic path, which for 8 bits is generally acceptable in latency and timing, especially when optimized by synthesis tools.

Hence, the improvement strategy is:
- Implement the 8-bit adder using 8 full adder (FA) instances connected in a ripple carry chain.
- Each FA takes a bit of 'a' and 'b' plus a carry-in, produces sum bit and carry-out.
- The carry-out of one FA connects to the carry-in of the next.
- This modular approach reduces gate count, likely reduces area and power.
- Timing is still combinational and should be acceptable for an 8-bit adder.

This design is simple, modular, easy to maintain, and expected to show better PPA for the small adder case than a CLA-based approach.
