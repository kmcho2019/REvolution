Instead of the classic ripple carry, I'll implement the 8-bit adder using a hierarchical approach that splits the 8-bit inputs into two 4-bit halves and uses two 4-bit ripple-carry adders. The carry-out from the lower 4 bits will feed the carry-in of the upper 4 bits. This breaks the adder into smaller blocks, improving modularity and potentially allowing easier pipelining or reuse of the 4-bit adder module in other designs. The 4-bit adder itself will be built with four full adders connected serially. This hierarchical decomposition is novel relative to the flat 8-bit ripple carry, while still using only combinational logic and ripple carry internally.

Advantages:
- Modular and reusable 4-bit adder blocks.
- Simpler hierarchical design aids clarity.
- Slightly better manageability and possible timing benefits from smaller carry chains.

The code defines a single-bit full adder, a 4-bit ripple carry adder from four full adders, and the top-level 8-bit adder composed of two 4-bit adders chained by carry signals.
