The original design is clean and modular, using a ripple carry adder composed of full adders with explicit gate-level assignments. To improve PPA (power, performance, and area) without changing the fundamental ripple carry structure:

- Optimize the carry chain wiring by using a dedicated carry bus of 7 bits instead of 8. The LSB adder outputs carry[0], which feeds the next bit's cin, and so forth until carry[6] feeds the MSB adder. The final carry-out comes directly from the MSB adder, reducing wire width and slight complexity.

- Instantiate the LSB full adder separately outside the generate loop, which now only handles bits 1 to 7. This avoids conditionals inside the generate loop, improving readability and potentially enabling better tool optimization.

- For the LSB, since it is the first bit with cin input, keep the full adder, but if cin is always zero in certain usage scenarios, a half adder could be used (left out for generality).

- Use a parameter WIDTH=8 to allow easy scalability in the future.

- Keep gate-level logic for sum and carry for minimal logic gate count.

- Rename carry wires and organize code for clarity and synthesis friendliness.

This results in slightly improved area (due to simpler generate loops and less wiring), better readability, and possibly a tiny performance gain due to simpler carry wire handling. Power impact is minimal but may improve due to smaller fanout on the carry lines.

The trade-off is minimal and keeps the design clean, modular, and professional.
