To optimize power and area further while maintaining clear logic, the design can reduce unnecessary timer register toggling by enabling the timer decrement only when needed (timer > 0 and no shortening condition active). This gating reduces dynamic power. Also, explicitly registering outputs reduces glitches and simplifies timing closure. Encoding states as parameters with meaningful names improves readability and maintainability. Since the maximum timer value is 60, an 8-bit timer remains adequate. The pedestrian request logic clamps the timer once per green cycle to 10 only if the remaining time is larger than 10, avoiding redundant writes. Outputs are registered synchronously with state updates to minimize glitches. This balanced approach maintains the clean state machine and interface while improving power through clock gating on timer and ensuring stable outputs.
