I will implement the multiplying accumulator (MAC) using a serial accumulation approach where the multiplication is done in one cycle and accumulated in a 32-bit register with saturation arithmetic to prevent overflow. The key points of this approach are:

1. Use a 64-bit internal register to hold the full precision product before accumulation, but only accumulate the lower 32 bits to the accumulator register with saturation.

2. Instead of accumulating the full 64-bit product result, truncate to the lower 32 bits for addition but saturate on overflow, which prevents wrap-around issues on the 32-bit accumulator output.

3. This reduces internal register width and complexity compared to a full 64-bit accumulator, trading off some precision for simpler and smaller hardware.

4. The accumulator register will be 32 bits wide and will saturate at 32'hFFFFFFFF on overflow.

5. On reset, accumulator is cleared to zero.

6. The multiplication is combinational with a full 64-bit output; only the lower 32 bits are accumulated with saturation logic.

7. Synchronous reset on the positive clock edge.

This design is simpler in terms of register size and ensures the output does not wrap around due to overflow by saturating at maximum 32-bit unsigned integer value, making it suitable where overflow wraparound is undesirable.
