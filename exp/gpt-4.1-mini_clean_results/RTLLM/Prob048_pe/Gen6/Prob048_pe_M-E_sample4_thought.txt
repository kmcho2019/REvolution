To create a different architectural approach, I will implement the multiplying accumulator using a hierarchical accumulation technique to reduce the size of intermediate registers and potentially simplify timing.

Instead of a full 64-bit accumulator, I will maintain two 32-bit registers:
- A low 32-bit register accumulating the least significant part of the product sums.
- A carry register tracking the overflow from the low 32-bit accumulation.

In each cycle:
1. Multiply the inputs to get a full 64-bit product.
2. Add the low 32 bits of the product to the low accumulator.
3. If this addition causes a carry out, increment the carry register.
4. Add the high 32 bits of the product plus any carry from the low part to the carry register.

This two-register scheme simulates a 64-bit accumulator but manages the addition in two parts with 32-bit registers only, which can simplify arithmetic units in some FPGA or ASIC architectures.

Finally, I will output only the low 32 bits of the accumulation register, as specified.

This approach differs from using a direct 64-bit register and adder in the original example and may help modularize the arithmetic while maintaining full 64-bit accumulation accuracy internally.
