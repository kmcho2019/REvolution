The original design uses a 64-bit accumulator to prevent overflow during multiplication and accumulation and outputs the lower 32 bits. This approach is functionally correct but has some potential issues for PPA:

1. **Performance**: Multiplying two 32-bit numbers to get 64-bit results in a large combinational multiplier. The 64-bit adder to update the accumulator adds to the critical path delay. 
2. **Area**: A full 64-bit accumulator and multiplier consumes significant area.
3. **Power**: Large wide datapaths consume more dynamic power.

**Improvement Strategy**:
- Split the accumulation into two 32-bit registers: a higher and a lower part, implementing a 64-bit accumulation using two 32-bit additions with carry logic. This can reduce the width of the adder and possibly optimize critical paths.
- Register the multiplier result to pipeline multiplication, separating multiplication and addition stages. This reduces combinational delay per clock and improves max frequency.
- Add an internal pipeline register to store the multiplier output (64-bit), then add it to accumulator in the next cycle. This balances timing and reduces critical path at the cost of one clock cycle latency.
- Continue to output only the lower 32 bits of the accumulator as required.
- The pipeline stage also enables the multiplier and accumulator to be clock-gated or power-optimized more easily.

This approach reduces critical path length (multiplier pipelined), minimizes wide adder delay per cycle, and keeps functionality intact. It trades latency for better clock frequency and power.

Note: To keep reset logic synchronous, the reset is handled only on clk posedge, which is typical and preferable in FPGA/ASIC flows.
