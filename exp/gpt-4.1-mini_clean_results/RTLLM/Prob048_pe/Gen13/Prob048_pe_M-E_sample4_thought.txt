To offer a novel architectural idea different from the conventional single accumulator register approach, I will implement a dual-register ping-pong accumulating architecture:

1. Use two 32-bit accumulators toggled every cycle, alternating accumulation between them.

2. In one cycle, accumulate the product into accumulator A; in the next cycle, accumulate into accumulator B, and so forth.

3. Output "c" as the sum of both accumulators at every cycle.

4. This ping-pong accumulation reduces critical path load on a single accumulator register since each register is updated every other cycle, potentially improving timing.

5. The sum output gives the full accumulation result distributed over two registers, avoiding a wide 64-bit adder in the critical path each clock cycle.

6. Use synchronous reset to clear both accumulators.

7. A cycle counter toggles which accumulator is active.

This approach trades area for timing improvement and uses two 32-bit accumulators instead of one 64-bit, outputting their sum as the 32-bit result (which can overflow, consistent with output width). This novel method can help with timing closure in high-frequency designs while keeping the design simple and synthesizable.
