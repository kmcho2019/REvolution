Fuse the two examples by combining clear separation of combinational and sequential logic (from Example 1) with simple synchronous reset and saturation logic (from Example 2). The fusion strategy is:

- Use a registered accumulator `acc` as a 32-bit register.
- Compute the full 64-bit product combinationally.
- Accumulate only the lower 32 bits of the product (since outputs and accumulator are 32-bit).
- Use a separate combinational block to calculate the next accumulator value with saturation logic on overflow (like Example 1).
- Implement synchronous reset on the rising clock edge only (avoid async reset for better timing closure).
- Drive the output `c` directly from the accumulator register `acc` to avoid unnecessary combinational logic on output.
- Use non-blocking assignments in sequential logic.
- This approach keeps the data path clean, modular, and synthesizable with clear timing, saturation protection, and minimal output logic.
- The design targets unsigned 32-bit operands and saturates on addition overflow, preventing wrap-around.
- This fusion improves clarity, maintainability, and synthesis friendliness over both examples.

- Combinational multiplier and saturation logic separated from sequential accumulator register.
