Implement a fully 64-bit accumulator to avoid overflow during accumulation of the 32x32 multiplication result. Use a 64-bit register to store the intermediate sum, since the product of two 32-bit numbers can be up to 64 bits. On each clock, accumulate the 64-bit product into the 64-bit accumulator. The output port c will deliver the lower 32 bits of the accumulated sum, truncating the upper bits. This approach ensures exact accumulation without saturation or wrap-around on the lower 32 bits and simplifies overflow handling by extending the accumulator width.

The reset is synchronous and clears the 64-bit accumulator register to zero. Multiplication is performed combinationally each cycle, feeding into the accumulator update. Output c is assigned directly from the lower 32 bits of the accumulator register.

This design prioritizes accuracy and simplicity in overflow behavior, capturing full 64-bit accumulation but outputting a 32-bit truncated result. It's appropriate when full precision accumulation is necessary internally and the final output can tolerate truncation or external logic handles the upper bits.

By handling the accumulator in 64 bits, the design avoids complexity in saturation or overflow detection logic, simplifying timing and verification.
