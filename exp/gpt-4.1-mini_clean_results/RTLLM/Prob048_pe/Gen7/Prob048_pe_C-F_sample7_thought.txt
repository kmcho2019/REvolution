Fuse the strengths of both examples by:
1. Using a 64-bit accumulator register to safely hold the full product accumulation and avoid overflow.
2. Computing the 64-bit product combinationally each cycle for simplicity.
3. Using a synchronous reset inside the always block triggered only on the clock's positive edge to align with typical FPGA/ASIC reset styles.
4. Keeping the combinational product computation outside the sequential logic to reduce critical path inside the clocked always block.
5. Outputting the lower 32 bits of the accumulator as the 32-bit result, meeting the output spec.

This fusion ensures accuracy, clear separation of combinational and sequential logic, a straightforward reset scheme, and easy timing analysis. It also supports potential future optimizations like pipelining or clock gating without complicating the base logic.

No asynchronous resets or mixing resets in sensitivity lists avoids synthesis and timing pitfalls. The design is clean, synthesizable, and easy to verify.
