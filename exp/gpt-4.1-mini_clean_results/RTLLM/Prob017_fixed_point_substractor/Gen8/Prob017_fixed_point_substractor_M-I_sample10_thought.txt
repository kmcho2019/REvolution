The original approach uses explicit sign-magnitude arithmetic and custom absolute value and sign reconstructions, causing complexity, potential bit-width mismatches, and synthesis issues. 

A much more synthesis-friendly and clean approach is to leverage the inherent properties of two's complement arithmetic for fixed-point subtraction directly. Two's complement subtraction inherently handles sign and magnitude correctly, including zero result sign bits.

To address the problem statement precisely (especially the explicit zero-sign bit), the subtraction can be done using standard two's complement subtraction, and then the sign bit can be forced to zero when the result is exactly zero.

This simplifies the logic, reduces combinational logic depth, avoids tricky sign-magnitude conversions, and easily synthesizes on hardware.

Key improvements:

- Perform direct two's complement subtraction: `res = a - b`.
- Detect zero result and force sign bit zero explicitly.
- Parameterized precision is implicitly supported by two's complement arithmetic.
- No intermediate sign-magnitude conversions or magnitude additions/subtractions.
- This approach guarantees correct handling of all cases including same-sign and different-sign inputs inherently.
- Internal register `res` implemented as a wire for combinational logic, but can be a register if sequential logic is desired.

This simplification improves synthesis success, reduces area and power, and enhances clarity and maintainability.
