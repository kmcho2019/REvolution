The original design correctly performs fixed-point subtraction and clears the sign bit when the result is zero by using masking logic. However, the zero detection and masking approach introduces additional combinational logic that can lead to synthesis difficulties and longer critical paths.

To improve synthesis friendliness, I will replace the masking approach with a simple conditional operator that explicitly checks if the subtraction result is zero and then sets the output accordingly. This eliminates the need for building a sign_mask and reduces wide equality comparators from combinational paths.

I will:
- Use a signed wire for the subtraction result.
- Assign output `c` combinationally using a ternary operator: if `res` is zero, output zero with sign bit cleared explicitly, else output `res`.
- This approach is clean, synthesis-friendly, and efficient.
- The internal register `res` is replaced by a wire as the computation is combinational.
- The code is fully parameterized and maintains precision by relying on signed arithmetic.
- I will keep the same testbench for validation.

This improves performance (less combinational logic), area (fewer gates for zero detection), and power (simpler switching logic), and ensures robust synthesis across tools.
