A simpler and synthesis-friendly approach is to rely on direct two's complement subtraction for fixed-point numbers, as fixed-point values in two's complement format can be subtracted directly without separating sign and magnitude.

To meet the requirement of sign handling and zero sign bit explicitly set to zero:

1. Perform direct subtraction `res = a - b` as two's complement arithmetic.
2. Check if the result is zero; if yes, clear the sign bit.
3. Otherwise, output the result as is.

This approach automatically handles same and different sign subtraction accurately and keeps the fixed-point precision intact.

No complex sign/magnitude conversions or magnitude comparisons are necessary.

This minimizes combinational logic, guarantees synthesizability, and respects fixed-point arithmetic semantics.

Internally, the subtractor is a pure combinational block with a register to hold the result. The output is assigned from the register with the zero sign bit correction.

This approach is clean, efficient, and fully synthesizable for any parameterized N and Q.
