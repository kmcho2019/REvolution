To improve synthesis compatibility and optimize for performance, power, and area, the design will:

1. Declare inputs and outputs as signed vectors to simplify sign handling and enable native signed arithmetic in synthesis tools.

2. Perform subtraction directly on signed N-bit inputs, avoiding extra sign extension logic and wider intermediate signals, thus reducing area and complexity.

3. Use a combinational always block with blocking assignments for clarity and synthesis friendliness.

4. Handle zero result by checking if the subtraction yields zero and then explicitly setting the sign bit to zero, preventing negative zero representation.

5. Maintain parameters Q (fractional bits) and N (total bits) to keep fixed-point precision consistent.

6. Keep internal result stored in a register `res` for clarity, assigned directly from the subtraction result.

7. Provide a testbench with the same parameterization for thorough verification, using signed ports for direct arithmetic and eliminating unnecessary sign extension.

This approach leverages signed data types, simplifying the design and improving synthesis results while preserving the fixed-point subtraction semantics, sign handling, and explicit zero representation as required.
