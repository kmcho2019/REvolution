To improve synthesis compatibility and maintain the same functional behavior, I will refactor the design by explicitly declaring the subtraction result as a combinational signed wire assigned using an `assign` statement rather than initializing a wire with an expression.

I will also replace the concatenation and masking of the sign bit with a simple bitwise operation applied directly on the full bus, which simplifies synthesis. Specifically, I will clear the sign bit when the entire subtraction result is zero by ANDing the result with a mask that forces the sign bit to zero only in that case. This avoids a bitwise concatenation in the assignment.

Additionally, I will keep the internal register `res` as a wire rather than register because the operation is purely combinational. The outputs will be assigned in combinational style.

The testbench remains unchanged for verification.

This refactoring retains the same arithmetic and zero-sign clearing logic but uses a straightforward and synthesis-friendly style:
- `assign` statements for combinational logic
- direct bitwise masking instead of sign-bit concatenation
- no wire initialization on declaration
- fully parameterized signed arithmetic

This should ensure synthesis tools handle the subtractor smoothly while preserving the design intent and arithmetic accuracy.
