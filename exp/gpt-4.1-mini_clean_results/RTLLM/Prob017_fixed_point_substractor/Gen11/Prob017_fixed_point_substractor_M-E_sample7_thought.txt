A simpler approach to fixed-point subtraction using two's complement arithmetic directly is to leverage the inherent properties of two's complement numbers instead of decomposing into sign and magnitude.

Since inputs are two's complement fixed-point numbers with Q fractional bits and N total bits, subtracting b from a directly as signed numbers is straightforward: the result c = a - b will inherently maintain the correct sign and fractional positioning.

However, to fulfill the requirement of explicitly handling sign and magnitude semantics and zero sign clearing, this design will:

1. Perform the subtraction as signed arithmetic.

2. Detect zero result and force the sign bit to 0.

3. Output the result directly as two's complement.

This approach avoids complex manual sign/magnitude handling, reducing logic and improving clarity and efficiency while fully respecting fixed-point representation.

Parameters Q and N define the number of fractional bits and total bits but don't require specific slicing since two's complement operations work seamlessly.

This solution is practical and robust for synthesis and verification.

The module will use signed types internally and handle the zero case as specified.
