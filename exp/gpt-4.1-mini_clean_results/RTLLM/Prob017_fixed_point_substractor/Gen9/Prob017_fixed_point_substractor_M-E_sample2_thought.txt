To implement a fixed-point subtractor that handles signs and zero detection explicitly as requested, I will take a different architectural approach:

1. Extract sign bits and absolute magnitudes of inputs `a` and `b` explicitly.
2. Based on signs of `a` and `b`:
   - If same sign, perform magnitude subtraction and keep the sign.
   - If different signs, perform magnitude addition and determine sign based on which magnitude is larger.
3. Use magnitude comparison to determine the sign for the result.
4. After obtaining the signed magnitude result, detect zero explicitly and force the output to zero with sign bit zero.
5. Internally, magnitude operations will be unsigned to simplify addition/subtraction of absolute values.
6. The final result is reassembled with the computed sign and magnitude to form the fixed-point signed output.
7. All operations are combinational, stored in intermediate regs to clarify steps.
8. This design emphasizes manual sign and magnitude handling rather than relying on Verilog signed arithmetic to demonstrate the concept clearly.

This approach explicitly implements the logic described in the problem statement, showing clear sign handling and zero sign resetting, avoiding implicit signed arithmetic operators.

This also allows easy parameterization for the fixed-point word size (N) and fractional bits (Q).

No clock or sequential logic is used to keep the design purely combinational.

The final output `c` is assembled from sign + magnitude bits, and zero detection is done explicitly on the magnitude to clear sign bit if zero.

This design serves as a more educational and manual handling of fixed-point signed subtraction that aligns strictly with the specification.
