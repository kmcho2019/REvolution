The original design is functionally correct and simple, but the explicit zero sign bit correction in a purely combinational assignment could cause synthesis challenges or slightly increase area and delay due to the extra mux.

To improve synthesis friendliness and reduce complexity without changing functionality, I propose the following:

- Remove the explicit zero sign bit fix in the combinational assignment since in two's complement zero is already unique and has a sign bit zero.
- Keep inputs, outputs, and intermediate wires as signed for natural arithmetic.
- Remove the zero-detection mux to simplify the data path and eliminate extra logic.
- Document the parameter Q for scaling but keep it unused since arithmetic works the same.
- Maintain the module purely combinational without registers.
- Keep the testbench as is for functional verification (not synthesized).

This will yield a minimal subtractor that is synthesizable, area- and power-efficient, and maintains correct fixed-point subtraction semantics.

If a user requires explicit zero sign bit correction, it can be done externally or with a registered output with a dedicated zero-detection and sign fix, but that adds complexity and latency.

Therefore, the improved module will perform straightforward signed subtraction without the zero-fix mux.

This approach optimizes PPA by reducing gates and muxes, improving timing, lowering power, and simplifying synthesis.

The testbench remains unchanged since it verifies the module correctness.

Summary of improvement:
- Remove explicit zero sign bit fix conditional.
- Keep signed input/output/wire.
- Pure combinational subtraction.
