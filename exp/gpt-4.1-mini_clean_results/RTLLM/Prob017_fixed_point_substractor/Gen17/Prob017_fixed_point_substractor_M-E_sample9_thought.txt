To precisely implement fixed-point subtraction with explicit sign and magnitude handling, I will:

- Treat inputs `a` and `b` as signed fixed-point numbers in two's complement format.
- Extract sign bits (`sign_a` and `sign_b`), and absolute magnitudes (`mag_a`, `mag_b`) by taking two's complement of negative inputs.
- Implement logic for subtraction based on the signs:
  - If signs are the same, perform magnitude subtraction (`mag_a - mag_b`), and assign result sign as the common sign.
  - If signs differ, perform magnitude addition (`mag_a + mag_b`), and assign result sign according to which magnitude is greater.
- Handle zero result explicitly by clearing the sign bit.
- Assemble the signed magnitude result back into two's complement format.
- Use parameters `N` and `Q` to represent total bits and fractional bits.
- This architecture is more transparent, explicitly distinguishes sign and magnitude, and follows the problem statement strictly.
- Implement all logic combinationally inside a single always block to ensure synthesizability.
- The output is driven by a register `res`, which is then assigned to output `c`.

This design provides clear control over sign and magnitude arithmetic, aligns with fixed-point representation, and matches the required behavior.
