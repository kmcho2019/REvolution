Instead of directly relying on the signed subtractor, this design will explicitly separate the integer and fractional parts of inputs `a` and `b` based on the fixed-point parameter `Q`, and handle subtraction with explicit sign and magnitude processing. This approach mimics manual fixed-point subtraction accounting for sign bits, thus adhering closely to the problem specification about different sign subtraction and same sign subtraction.

Key architectural ideas:
1. Extract sign, integer, and fractional parts separately from inputs.
2. Convert inputs to magnitude (absolute values) by stripping the sign bit.
3. Based on input signs:
   - If signs are the same, subtract magnitudes; the sign of the result is the input sign.
   - If signs differ, add magnitudes; the sign of the result depends on which operand has the larger magnitude.
4. Implement magnitude comparison to decide the result's sign for different sign cases.
5. After calculation, re-apply the correct sign to the magnitude to form the final fixed-point signed result.
6. If result magnitude is zero, explicitly force the sign bit to zero as required.
7. Entire logic will be implemented combinationally using always @* blocks and internal regs.
8. Parameters N (total bits) and Q (fractional bits) guide the bit slicing.
9. This approach is explicit about fixed-point arithmetic parts, suitable for fixed-point verification or custom arithmetic pipelines.

The advantage:
- Explicit fixed-point decomposition and sign-magnitude processing can be more transparent for understanding fixed-point behavior.
- Matches the problem requirement of handling same-sign and different-sign subtraction distinctly.
- The zero sign-bit fix is naturally integrated into the final output assignment.

This design is more verbose than direct subtraction but educational and verifiable for fixed-point arithmetic purposes.
