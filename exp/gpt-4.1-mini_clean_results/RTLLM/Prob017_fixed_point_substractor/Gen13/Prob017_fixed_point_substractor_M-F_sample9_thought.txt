To address synthesis concerns while preserving correct fixed-point subtraction and sign handling, simplify the design by leveraging two's complement arithmetic directly for subtraction without decomposing into explicit sign and magnitude. Since inputs are in two's complement fixed-point form, the direct subtraction `a - b` inherently handles sign and magnitude correctly, preserving fractional precision.

To implement the problem's sign and zero handling requirements:

- Perform `res = a - b` as two's complement subtraction.
- Detect zero result and explicitly set the sign bit to zero.
- Output `c = res`.

This approach:

- Eliminates complex magnitude extraction and recomposition logic.
- Ensures straightforward combinational logic, more synthesis friendly.
- Maintains full precision and correctness.
- Avoids ambiguous or duplicated inversion logic.
- Simplifies output assignment to a wire.

Hence, a simple combinational subtractor with zero detection suffices.

This directly meets the requirements with a minimal, synthesis-robust implementation.
