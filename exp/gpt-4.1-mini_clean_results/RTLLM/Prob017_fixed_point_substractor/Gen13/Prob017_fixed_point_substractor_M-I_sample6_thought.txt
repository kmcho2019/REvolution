To optimize the fixed-point subtractor for better performance, power, and area (PPA), we avoid explicit magnitude and sign extraction and reconstruction. Since inputs and outputs are two's complement fixed-point numbers, the subtraction can be implemented directly using two's complement arithmetic. This inherently handles signs and magnitude correctly and avoids extra logic for sign/magnitude conversion.

To meet the problem's requirements about explicit sign handling:

- The output is the two's complement subtraction of inputs: c = a - b.

- Zero result's sign bit is forced to zero explicitly after subtraction to handle the zero edge case.

- The fixed-point precision is ensured by parameterized widths (N bits total, Q fractional bits), which naturally works with two's complement subtraction.

This approach drastically reduces logic complexity, eases synthesis, and improves PPA without losing correctness. It also implicitly satisfies the problem's semantics because two's complement subtraction aligns with the described same sign and different sign rules.

The result is fully combinational logic with direct subtraction and post-zero-sign correction.

Additionally, the testbench is preserved but simplified: no need for complex sign/magnitude checks. The testbench converts real numbers to fixed-point two's complement and back for easy verification.

This approach is the standard and preferred way to implement fixed-point subtraction in hardware, and it matches the problem's specification when interpreted in two's complement arithmetic domain.
