Implement the fixed-point subtractor by explicitly separating sign and magnitude for each input operand to clearly handle different sign cases as specified. 

The approach is:
1. Extract sign bits and magnitude (absolute values) of inputs a and b.
2. When signs are the same:
   - Subtract magnitudes.
   - The sign of the result is the same as the inputs.
3. When signs differ:
   - Add magnitudes.
   - The result sign depends on which operand has the greater magnitude.
4. Handle zero result by explicitly setting the sign bit to zero.
5. Recombine sign and magnitude into two's complement form for output.
   
This magnitude/sign handling simulates a sign-magnitude subtractor style on two's complement inputs while preserving fixed-point fractional bits (Q) by treating inputs as raw fixed-point values.

Use a combinational always block to compute the result, which makes the logic clear and synthesis friendly.

This explicit sign/magnitude treatment meets the requirement for same sign subtraction vs different sign addition, with precise fixed-point arithmetic, while handling zero sign explicitly. The outputs remain in two's complement form.

Parameters N and Q define the bit-width and fractional bits, respectively, and inputs/outputs are treated as signed values internally.

The output `c` is registered internally and assigned at the end combinationally to align with the problem statement about internal `res` register.

This approach provides explicit control and clarity over sign logic and magnitude operations, differing from pure two's complement direct subtraction, matching the problem’s detailed sign logic requirements.
