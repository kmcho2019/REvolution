The initial design is functionally correct and cleanly implements fixed-point subtraction with signed operands and handles the zero-result sign bit explicitly. To optimize for better Power, Performance, and Area (PPA), I will eliminate the conditional assignment with bit slicing that may hinder synthesis and instead use an arithmetic subtractor directly (which synthesis tools handle efficiently). 

Then, to clear the sign bit when the result is zero without introducing muxes or complex bit slicing, I propose a simple combinational logic to detect zero and force the sign bit to zero by masking the output when zero. This can be done with a straightforward bitwise AND of the sign bit with a zero-detection signal complemented, thus avoiding conditional operators or concatenation.

This approach reduces extra muxes and simplifies the logic for synthesis while still meeting the design spec: the sign bit is cleared when the result is zero, and the subtraction is signed and precise.

I will keep inputs and outputs signed so the subtraction respects fixed-point signed arithmetic inherently. Additionally, I will remove the internal register `res` since it is not required and is combinational.

The testbench remains the same for verification.

This optimization reduces logic complexity and improves the likelihood of synthesis success across tools, and reduces power by removing unnecessary muxing.

Summary of improvements:
- Use direct signed subtraction without conditional concatenation.
- Clear sign bit on zero result by a simple AND mask on the sign bit.
- Keep code clean, parameterized, and fully combinational.
