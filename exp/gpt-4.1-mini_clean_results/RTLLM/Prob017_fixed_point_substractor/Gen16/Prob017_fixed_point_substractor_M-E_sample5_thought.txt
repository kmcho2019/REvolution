I will implement the fixed-point subtractor by explicitly decomposing inputs `a` and `b` into sign, integer, and fractional parts to clearly handle the sign and magnitude as per the problem statement.

The plan is:
- Extract the sign bits of `a` and `b`.
- Compute absolute values of `a` and `b` by conditionally inverting and adding one (two's complement).
- If signs are equal:
  - Subtract the magnitudes to get magnitude difference.
  - The sign of result is same as inputs sign.
- If signs differ:
  - Add the magnitudes.
  - The sign of the result is the sign of the operand with the larger magnitude (since effectively we are doing `a - b`).
- Handle zero result explicitly by forcing sign bit to zero.
- Recombine the sign and magnitude to form final result `c`.
- Use parameters `N` and `Q` to split integer and fractional bits properly, but the bit-width arithmetic is always on full `N` bits.
- Implement the whole logic combinationally using always @(*) block for clarity.
- Use internal registers to store intermediate magnitude and sign values, then combine them into output.

This approach ensures precise control over sign and magnitude logic per problem requirements, explicitly addresses same and different sign cases separately, and explicitly zeroes sign bit on zero result. The solution is modular, understandable, and suitable for fixed-point arithmetic with any bit-width and fractional precision.

This design will be fully synthesizable, and the parameterized width allows scalability.
