The original solution is functionally correct and clean, using direct signed subtraction and an explicit zero sign fix. However, synthesis tools sometimes have issues with output ports declared as reg assigned inside combinational always blocks, especially for combinational logic. To improve synthesis compatibility and potentially reduce inference complexity, the following improvements are applied:

1. Change the output port `c` from `reg` to `wire` and drive it via a continuous assignment. This approach is preferred for pure combinational logic, making intent clearer to synthesis tools.

2. Perform the subtraction in a signed wire `res` rather than a reg inside an always block.

3. Implement the zero sign fix using a conditional operator in the continuous assignment itself, eliminating the always block altogether.

4. Preserve the signed declarations for inputs and outputs and internal signals.

5. Keep parameters N and Q as before to maintain fixed-point precision context.

This approach reduces complexity by removing procedural blocks, simplifying logic for synthesis tools, and making the module purely combinational and more clearly described by dataflow.

The testbench is left unchanged since it doesn't affect synthesis and thoroughly tests the design.

Overall, this improves synthesis friendliness and potentially area/power by reducing inferred registers and muxes.
