Instead of directly shifting right and inserting the inverted LSB, this design maintains an explicit state machine step counter that tracks the current pattern index in the Johnson sequence, thus generating the output pattern deterministically by calculating the bit pattern on-the-fly. This removes the need for a shift register operation each cycle and instead uses combinational logic to generate the 64-bit output based on the step count. 

The step counter increments modulo 128 (twice the width), reflecting the 2N states of a Johnson counter. The output Q is generated by concatenating two halves: the upper half filled with ones up to the current step, and the rest zeros, effectively reconstructing the Johnson pattern without shifts. This approach may simplify timing by replacing a 64-bit shift register with a smaller counter and combinational logic, trading off some combinational complexity for potentially better PPA.

Reset initializes the step counter to zero. On each rising clock edge, step increments modulo 128, cycling through all Johnson states. The output Q is formed such that the pattern matches a 64-bit Johnson counter sequence.

This novel approach leverages the mathematical definition of Johnson counters and can be beneficial in some synthesis contexts, especially where wide shift registers are costly or timing-critical.
