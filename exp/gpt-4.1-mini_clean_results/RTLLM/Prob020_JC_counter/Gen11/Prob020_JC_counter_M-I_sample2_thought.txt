The original design is already minimal and efficient for a Johnson counter: a single 64-bit shift register with MSB assigned as the inverted LSB, and asynchronous active-low reset. To improve PPA (timing, power, and area) further, we can consider the following refinements:

1. Convert asynchronous reset to synchronous reset:
   - Asynchronous reset adds complexity to timing closure and may increase area due to reset trees.
   - Synchronous reset simplifies timing analysis and can allow higher frequency operation.
   - This change may increase reset latency by one clock cycle, but it improves timing and potentially reduces area.

2. Use non-blocking assignments consistently (already done).

3. To reduce switching activity:
   - We can gate the counter update such that if the counter is not enabled or not used, the register does not toggle unnecessarily.
   - Since no enable input is provided, skipping this step.

4. Since the logic is simple, no further structural optimization is needed.

Thus, the main improvement is switching the reset from asynchronous active-low to synchronous active-low reset. This should yield better timing and area without changing functional behavior.
