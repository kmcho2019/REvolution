Instead of a conventional shift-right with MSB = ~LSB approach, I will implement the 64-bit Johnson counter as two 32-bit shift registers chained together for pipelining and potential timing improvement.

The idea is:
- Split the 64-bit counter Q into two 32-bit registers: Q_hi and Q_lo.
- On each clock cycle, shift Q_lo right by one bit, feeding its LSB complement to Q_hi's MSB.
- Similarly, shift Q_hi right by one bit, feeding its LSB complement back to Q_lo's MSB.
- This creates a torsional effect where two halves of the register toggle each other, mimicking the Johnson pattern.

For reset, both Q_hi and Q_lo start at zero.

This approach enables potentially faster routing and less critical path since each half-register operates separately and only interacts via single bits.

Though functionally equivalent to the original, this architectural partitioning might help timing by dividing the 64-bit shift into two 32-bit shifts with minimal combinational logic between them.

The final output Q concatenates Q_hi and Q_lo.

This technique showcases a novel architectural variation maintaining the Johnson counter's behavior with the prescribed reset and update semantics.
