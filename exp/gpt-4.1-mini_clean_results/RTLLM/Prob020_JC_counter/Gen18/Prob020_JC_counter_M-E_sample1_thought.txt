A novel approach to implement the 64-bit Johnson counter is to use two 32-bit shift registers operating in parallel, each implementing half of the 64-bit Johnson count, and combine their outputs. Specifically, we can maintain two 32-bit registers, high and low halves, and on each clock cycle shift both right by one bit. The MSB of the high half is loaded with the inverted LSB of the low half, and the MSB of the low half is loaded with the inverted LSB of the high half. This cross-feedback architecture ensures that the 64-bit Johnson sequence is generated correctly, but decomposes the logic into two smaller registers, possibly allowing better timing or resource sharing in some implementations. The asynchronous reset sets both halves to zero.

This approach differs from the traditional single-shift-register implementation and may facilitate targeted synthesis optimizations or enable partial updates in hardware. The overall Johnson sequence is the concatenation {high, low}.

This alternative architecture preserves correct Johnson counting behavior, satisfies the same reset and clocking specifications, and can improve modularity and clarity in very large-width Johnson counters.
