Implement the 64-bit Johnson counter by dividing the 64-bit register into eight 8-bit segments chained together, each updated sequentially within a synchronous clock process. On reset, the entire 64-bit Q is cleared. On each clock cycle, the next state for each 8-bit segment is computed from its previous value and a bit derived from either the previous segment or, for the first segment, the inverted LSB of Q. This modular approach mimics a chain of smaller Johnson counters, which may facilitate hierarchical design and ease of debugging. Instead of a direct wide concatenation shift, this segmented processing reduces the large combinational fan-in and allows potential for pipelining or partial updates in certain implementations. The concatenation of updated segments forms the next Q. This style increases clarity on the internal structure, promotes modularity, and can assist synthesis tools to optimize partial logic paths, possibly improving timing and maintainability.
