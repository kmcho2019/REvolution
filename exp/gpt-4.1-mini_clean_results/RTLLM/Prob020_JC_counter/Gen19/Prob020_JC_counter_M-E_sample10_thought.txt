Instead of a simple shift with conditional MSB insertion based on the current LSB, I will implement the Johnson counter using a circular shift-left operation combined with bit inversion on the input bit. Specifically, on each clock, the new MSB is the inversion of the previous MSB (or equivalently the feedback bit is the inverted output bit that "wraps around"). For a Johnson counter, this corresponds to shifting left and inserting the inverted previous MSB at LSB position, but since the problem's approach is right shift, I'll invert the logic by implementing a right shift with MSB updated based on the inversion of LSB. This approach uses a combinational feedback signal and keeps the design fully synchronous. This also clearly represents the Johnson pattern as a torsional ring where the feedback bit is the inverse of the bit being "exited". This differs from the original solution by explicitly computing the feedback bit as the inverse of Q[0], then shifting right and appending that bit at MSB, making the logic very transparent.
