Instead of implementing the 64-bit Johnson counter as a simple serial shift register with MSB loaded by inverted LSB each cycle, we can architect it using a 2-stage pipeline of two 32-bit Johnson counters that operate in tandem. The first 32-bit Johnson counter updates on the clock, and its LSB drives the input of the second 32-bit Johnson counter.

Specifically:
- Partition the 64-bit Johnson counter Q into two 32-bit registers: Q_hi[63:32] and Q_lo[31:0].
- On every clock edge, update Q_lo using standard Johnson logic with its own LSB.
- Simultaneously update Q_hi using Johnson logic driven by Q_lo's LSB instead of its own, effectively cascading the two counters.
- On reset, both Q_hi and Q_lo are cleared to zero.
- This approach breaks down the wide shift register into two smaller ones, potentially improving timing by reducing the fanout and critical path of the large concatenation.
- It also demonstrates a modular hierarchical design that can be more scalable and may improve tool optimizations.
- The final 64-bit output Q is the concatenation of Q_hi and Q_lo.

This architectural approach changes the input feed for the high part to come from the low part's LSB, creating a torsional effect that maintains Johnson counter properties across the entire 64-bit output.
