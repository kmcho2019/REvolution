To implement the 64-bit Johnson counter without using a shift loop or bit-by-bit assignments, we exploit vector concatenation directly with a conditional operator to efficiently update the counter. Instead of always shifting right and complementing the LSB, we interpret the counter update in terms of Q[0]: if Q[0] == 0, we shift right and insert 1 at the MSB; if Q[0] == 1, we shift right and insert 0 at the MSB. This logic can be expressed as:

Q <= {Q[0] ? 1'b0 : 1'b1, Q[63:1]};

This compact expression not only optimizes for performance by minimizing critical path and logic depth but also yields an elegant and easily synthesizable design.

Additionally, reset is implemented asynchronously and active-low to set Q to zero at any time, ensuring known initial conditions.

This approach provides minimal logic, fast clocking capability, and reduces both area and power consumption by avoiding iterative constructs and intermediate registers.
