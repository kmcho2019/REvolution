Implement the 64-bit Johnson counter by separating the logic into two registers: one 63-bit shift register and one single bit register holding the LSB. On each clock edge, the LSB register updates according to its current value (flipping the MSB input to the shift register), and the shift register shifts in the LSB from the previous cycle. This pipeline-like split allows the right shift and insertion operations to be cleanly partitioned, making timing and debugging easier. The full 64-bit output is reconstructed by concatenating the shift register (MSB side) with the single LSB bit. This approach maintains the Johnson counter functionality but uses two smaller registers instead of one wide register.
