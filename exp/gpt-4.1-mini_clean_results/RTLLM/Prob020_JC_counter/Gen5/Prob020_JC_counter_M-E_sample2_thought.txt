Instead of using a single shift and inverted LSB insertion, I will implement the 64-bit Johnson counter as two 32-bit shift registers that work in tandem. The upper 32 bits shift right and insert the inverted LSB of the lower 32 bits, and the lower 32 bits shift right and insert the inverted LSB of the upper 32 bits. This split approach models the 64-bit Johnson counter as two interconnected halves, which can improve modularity and potentially support parallel implementation or pipelining. The reset initializes Q to zero. On each clock, both halves update simultaneously, effectively generating the correct Johnson sequence over the 64-bit vector.
