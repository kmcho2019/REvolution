Instead of directly shifting the bits every clock cycle, the Johnson counter can be implemented by using a 64-bit shift register combined with a single flip-flop to hold the inverted feedback bit. On each clock, the stored feedback bit is shifted into the MSB of the register, and the new feedback bit is the current LSB inverted. This isolates the inversion operation to a single flip-flop, simplifying the combinational logic and potentially improving timing by reducing fanout and combinational depth. The register content is updated synchronously with the clock and asynchronously cleared on reset.
