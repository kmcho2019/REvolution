A different approach to implement the 64-bit Johnson counter is to use a conditional concatenation-based shift update focusing explicitly on the value of Q[0] at each clock cycle, without relying on looping constructs or bitwise iteration.

Specifically:
- Maintain Q as a 64-bit register.
- On reset, initialize Q to zero.
- On each rising clock edge (or reset), check Q[0]:
  - If Q[0] == 0, shift right by one bit and insert '1' at the MSB.
  - Otherwise (Q[0] == 1), shift right by one bit and insert '0' at the MSB.

This method captures the Johnson counter behavior compactly with a single concatenation, making the code succinct and potentially enabling better synthesis optimization by avoiding the loop and explicit per-bit updates.

Since the logic depends only on the current Q[0], the critical path will be through Q[0] to the MSB generation, which may yield better timing than a serial chain of flip-flops shifting bit by bit.

Overall, this solution offers clarity, minimal combinational logic, and a direct expression of the Johnson counting principle via concatenation and conditional MSB insertion.
