Instead of shifting right and appending a bit directly each cycle, I will split the 64-bit Johnson counter into two 32-bit shift registers that operate in a pipelined manner. On each clock, the LSB of the entire 64-bit value is checked; if it is 0, the combined 64-bit value shifts right by one bit with a 1 inserted at MSB; otherwise, it shifts right with 0 inserted at MSB.

To achieve this, I'll keep two 32-bit registers: upper (bits 63:32) and lower (bits 31:0). The shift operation is implemented by shifting both registers right by one bit, and the MSB input is determined by the condition (~Q[0]). The tricky part is propagating the bit crossing the boundary between lower and upper registers.

This split approach explicitly shows boundary crossing and can improve synthesis tools' mapping by using smaller registers and explicit logic. It is a novel structural decomposition compared to a monolithic 64-bit shift.

On reset, both registers are cleared to zero.

Finally, the 64-bit output Q is formed by concatenating the upper and lower registers.
