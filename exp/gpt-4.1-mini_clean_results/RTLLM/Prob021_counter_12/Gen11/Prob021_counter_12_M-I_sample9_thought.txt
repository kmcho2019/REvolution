The previous design used a gated clock driven by the AND of clk and valid_count, which can cause timing issues and clock skew. A more robust and industry-standard approach is to use the original clock (clk) without gating and implement an enable signal inside the always block. This approach improves timing closure and reduces potential glitches.

The reset is active-low asynchronous, which is common. We keep that for quick reset responsiveness.

Inside the always block triggered on posedge clk or negedge rst_n:
- On reset, set count to 0.
- Else if valid_count is high, increment count.
- Wrap around to 0 when reaching 11.
- If valid_count is 0, hold the current count.

This approach maintains functionality, improves timing, and preserves power efficiency by preventing unnecessary toggling of out when valid_count is 0.

Additionally, we keep the design concise, with a single 4-bit register and simple comparator, ensuring minimal area.

This modification is a well-known best practice for counters with enable signals.
