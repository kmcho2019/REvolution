To achieve a superior design, I will fuse the best ideas from both examples by keeping the clock clean (no gating), which improves timing closure and eliminates potential glitches, and incorporating the `valid_count` signal as an enable inside the always block to control counting. This internal enable avoids the pitfalls of RTL clock gating while still reducing switching activity when counting is paused, improving power efficiency.

Also, I will use an asynchronous active-low reset (`rst_n`) as per the requirement, resetting the counter to 0 immediately when asserted. The counting logic increments on each rising clock edge only if `valid_count` is asserted, wrapping from 11 back to 0. When `valid_count` is low, the counter holds its value.

This approach balances performance, power, and area:
- Performance: clean clock edge with enable improves timing and removes glitches.
- Power: no toggling in counter register when counting paused due to enable gating register update.
- Area: minimal logic with simple comparator and incrementer.

Hence, the design is robust, synthesis-friendly, and efficient for the given specification.
