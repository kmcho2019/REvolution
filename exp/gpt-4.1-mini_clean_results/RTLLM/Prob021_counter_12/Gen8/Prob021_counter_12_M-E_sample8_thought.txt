To implement the counter with a novel architectural idea, I will separate the reset and counting logic more explicitly and use a simple synchronous reset and enable approach but with a registered enable signal. This registered enable, sampled on the clock, will ensure glitch-free enable for counting, reducing potential combinational toggling on the counter output. Additionally, I will implement the counter as a one-hot encoding internally for the count values from 0 to 11, then convert the one-hot to binary output. This approach could potentially simplify timing for certain FPGA architectures that are optimized for one-hot state machines and also helps in debugging by allowing a direct visual representation of count states. The one-hot register is 12 bits wide. When valid_count is low, the one-hot register holds its state, effectively pausing counting. On reset, it initializes to state 0 (one-hot at bit 0). The output is generated by priority-encoding the one-hot state to a 4-bit binary count value. This design highlights an alternative to binary counters, useful in some scenarios.
