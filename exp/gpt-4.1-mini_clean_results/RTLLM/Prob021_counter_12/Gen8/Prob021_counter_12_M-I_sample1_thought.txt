To improve the existing design's timing and power characteristics, we avoid gating the clock directly at the RTL level because clock gating at RTL can cause glitches and timing issues. Instead, we keep a clean clock and use an enable signal (`valid_count`) inside the sequential logic. This approach improves timing closure and reduces clock skew risks.

The logic remains the same: the counter resets when `rst_n` is low, increments when `valid_count` is high, and wraps from 11 back to 0. When `valid_count` is low, the count holds its value.

This will improve the worst negative slack (WNS) and total negative slack (TNS), maintain low power (by preventing unnecessary toggling inside the registers), and keeps area minimal due to simple logic.

Hence, the improvement strategy:
1. Use a synchronous enable (`valid_count`) instead of gating the clock.
2. Use non-blocking assignments for proper RTL semantics.
3. Keep asynchronous active-low reset, as specified, for quick reset response.

This will yield better PPA by ensuring a clean clock path and reducing glitching caused by gated clocks.
