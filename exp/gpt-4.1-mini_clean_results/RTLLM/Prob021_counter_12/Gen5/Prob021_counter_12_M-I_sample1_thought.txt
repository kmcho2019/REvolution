To optimize for better PPA and resolve potential timing and power issues from gated clock usage, I will replace the gated clock with a clean clock and use the `valid_count` signal as a synchronous enable inside the always block. This avoids glitches and clock skew problems related to clock gating at the RTL level.

The counter will:
- Reset synchronously with `rst_n` (active low).
- Increment only when `valid_count` is high.
- Wrap from 11 back to 0.
- Hold the count value when `valid_count` is low.

This approach keeps the clock path clean, simplifies timing closure, and reduces the risk of hold violations or glitches. It also keeps the counter logic minimal and power-efficient by avoiding unnecessary toggling of registers when counting is disabled.

Using synchronous reset instead of asynchronous reset is better for timing predictability. The reset condition uses a positive edge triggered clock and checks `!rst_n` inside the always block.

Overall, this design improves timing (less negative slack), maintains minimal area, and reduces dynamic power caused by glitches in clock gating.

The Verilog code implements a standard enabled counter with synchronous reset and count enable.
