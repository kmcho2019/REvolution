Instead of using a shift register for the last two samples of data_in, we can implement a finite state machine (FSM) with defined states representing each step in detecting the pulse "0->1->0".

The FSM states could be:

- IDLE: Waiting for a 0 on data_in to start detection.
- WAIT_HIGH: After detecting 0, waiting for data_in to go high (1).
- WAIT_LOW: After detecting 0 then 1, waiting for data_in to go low (0) again to complete the pulse.

On the rising clock edge, the FSM transitions according to input data_in. When in WAIT_LOW and data_in goes 0, the pulse is detected, so data_out is asserted for one cycle.

This approach explicitly models the detection process, making the logic clearer for complex extensions or modifications, and does not rely on shift registers.

Reset initializes the FSM to IDLE and data_out to 0.

This approach uses one state register and combinational next-state logic, making it different structurally from the previous shift-register approach.
