The original design is clean and meets the specification using a simple three-state FSM with a Mealy output. To further optimize for power and timing, we can reduce switching activity by avoiding unnecessary state transitions when the input remains at 0 during IDLE or at 1 during HIGH. We can also refine state encoding to one-hot, which can simplify combinational logic and potentially improve timing by reducing logic depth. Since there are only three states, one-hot encoding requires three flip-flops but can speed up next-state logic and reduce glitches. The output remains a Mealy signal asserted exactly at the falling edge of the pulse (transition from 1 to 0). This approach may slightly increase area (3 flip-flops vs 2) but typically yields better timing and potentially lower dynamic power due to simplified combinational logic. We also ensure all outputs and state transitions use nonblocking assignments and proper reset synchronization. This trade-off suits designs targeting higher frequency or lower glitching outputs.
