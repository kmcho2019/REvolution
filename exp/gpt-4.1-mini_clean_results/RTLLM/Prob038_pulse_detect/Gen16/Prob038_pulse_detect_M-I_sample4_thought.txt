The original FSM approach is correct and clean, detecting the 0->1->0 pulse pattern and asserting data_out for one clock cycle at the pulse end. For optimization, we can reduce area and power by:

1. Using a single register 'state' rather than separate current and next state variables; update state and data_out synchronously in one always block, reducing register count.
2. Simplify the state encoding by using a 2-bit reg but avoid typedef for synthesis simplicity.
3. Remove combinational always block and combine state transition and output logic into one synchronous block to reduce combinational logic delay and glitches.
4. Use non-blocking assignments consistently in sequential block.
5. Keep output asserted only for one cycle at the end of the pulse, ensuring minimal switching on data_out.

These changes slightly reduce area and power by simplifying logic and registers, improve timing by eliminating combinational next-state logic, and maintain correctness.
