Use a simple two-bit shift register to track the last two values of data_in synchronously. At every clock cycle, shift in the current data_in bit. A pulse is detected if the previous two bits were "1" and "0" (data_in pattern "0->1->0" means the sequence on three cycles is 0, then 1, then 0). When the shift register contains "10" and the current data_in is 0, we know a pulse just ended. Output data_out high at that cycle. This approach eliminates explicit state machines and relies on pattern matching over shift register stages. It is simple, resource-efficient, and inherently synchronized.
