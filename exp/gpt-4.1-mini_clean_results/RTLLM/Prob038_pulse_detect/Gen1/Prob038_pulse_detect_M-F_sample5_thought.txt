The original design correctly detects the pulse pattern 0->1->0 and asserts data_out for one clock cycle at the end of the pulse. However, the data_out signal is assigned both combinationally and sequentially, which is redundant and could cause confusion or synthesis issues.

To fix this, data_out should be driven purely by sequential logic to register the output pulse precisely at the clock edge. The combinational block will only determine next_state, and data_out will be asserted in the sequential block when the pulse end condition is met (transition from WAIT_LOW state and data_in==0). This clean separation improves clarity and eliminates the redundant combinational assignment to data_out.

Also, the states can be renamed slightly for clarity, but keeping the three-state FSM is sufficient and minimal. The output pulse will be generated with exactly one cycle width on the clock edge following the detection, which meets the specification.

This fix respects the feedback and ensures robust, clear, and efficient pulse detection FSM logic.
