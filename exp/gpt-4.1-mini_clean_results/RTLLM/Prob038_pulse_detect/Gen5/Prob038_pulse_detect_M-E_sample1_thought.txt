A novel approach is to implement the pulse detection by directly detecting the pattern "0 -> 1 -> 0" in the input signal using a small shift register and combinational logic, instead of a traditional FSM. By storing the last two samples of data_in and analyzing the current sample, we can check if the pattern appears.

Specifically:
- Maintain a 2-bit shift register holding the previous two samples of data_in.
- On each clock cycle, shift in the new data_in.
- At every clock, check if the three samples (previous two + current) form the pattern `0,1,0`.
- If so, assert data_out = 1 for exactly one cycle.
- The reset clears the shift register and deasserts data_out.

This approach simplifies the design, avoids explicit FSM states, and naturally produces a one-cycle pulse on the output aligned with the last 0 of the pulse pattern. It's easy to understand and efficient, requiring only a couple of flip-flops for the shift register and a simple comparator.

This design aligns exactly with the specification:
- The detected pulse is "0->1->0" over three cycles.
- The output is '1' only at the end cycle of the pulse (the last '0').
- The output is '0' otherwise.

This new architectural idea is clean and elegant.
