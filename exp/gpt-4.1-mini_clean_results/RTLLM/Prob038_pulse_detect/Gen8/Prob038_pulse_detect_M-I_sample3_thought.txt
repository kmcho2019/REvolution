The original design uses a 3-bit shift register and compares it every cycle to detect the "010" pulse pattern. Although simple and effective, it updates shift_reg and data_out every clock cycle, causing unnecessary switching and power consumption even when data_in is stable.

To optimize for power and performance, I propose using a finite state machine (FSM) that explicitly tracks the pulse detection stages (waiting for rising edge and falling edge). The FSM only transitions when relevant edges on data_in occur, reducing unnecessary toggling. This approach reduces switching activity in data_out and internal states, beneficial for power. It also simplifies logic by focusing on edges rather than continuous pattern matching.

The FSM states:

- IDLE: Waiting for data_in to go high (0->1)
- WAIT_FALL: After detecting rising edge, wait for data_in to go low (1->0)
- PULSE_DETECTED: Output pulse detected signal (data_out=1 for one cycle), then return to IDLE.

This implementation sets data_out to 1 only at the cycle when the falling edge completes the pulse, else 0. It is more power efficient while keeping minimal latency and area.

This state machine also handles glitches or longer pulses gracefully by strictly defining transitions, preventing false triggers on noisy inputs.
