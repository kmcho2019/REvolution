Refactor the pulse detection FSM by explicitly tracking the previous input value (`data_in`) to detect the 0->1->0 pattern spanning exactly three cycles. Instead of a multi-state FSM, use a small state counter that tracks which part of the pulse sequence is being detected. The states represent waiting for initial 0, detecting 1, and detecting the final 0.

At every clock edge, compare the current and previous data_in to detect edges. The output `data_out` is asserted only for one cycle at the falling edge of the pulse (when the sequence completes). After outputting the pulse, reset to wait for the next pulse pattern.

This structure reduces FSM complexity but maintains clear sequence detection, avoids combinational loops by using registers, and separates input sampling and output logic cleanly.
