Leverage the shift register idea from Example 1 to track recent inputs efficiently but combine it with FSM-like output generation as in Example 2 to have a clean, synchronous output pulse. The key insight is that the pulse occurs when a 0->1->0 pattern is detected over three cycles. Tracking two previous samples (a 2-bit shift register) is sufficient and logic for output can be derived combinationally.

To improve timing and glitch robustness, register data_out synchronously (not purely combinational), so data_out asserts 1 only at the end of the pulse cycle and returns to 0 immediately after.

Reset asynchronously clears shift register and output.

This fusion keeps the minimal register usage and short critical path of Example 1, adds the FSM clarity and registered output from Example 2 for better timing stability, and reduces power by avoiding unnecessary complex state machines or large registers.

Result: A clean, efficient pulse detector asserting data_out exactly once per detected pulse on the falling edge of data_in after a rising edge, matching the 0->1->0 pattern.
