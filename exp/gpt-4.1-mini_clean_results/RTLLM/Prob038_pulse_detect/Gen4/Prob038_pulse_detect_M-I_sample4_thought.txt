The original solution uses a one-hot state encoding, which is simple but not area efficient. To optimize for area and power (PPA), I will use a 2-bit binary encoded state machine. This reduces the number of flip-flops from 3 to 2 and simplifies combinational logic. The states will represent IDLE(0), HIGH(1), LOW(2) reflecting the 0->1->0 pulse pattern.

To minimize glitch and power, I will keep data_out registered and assert it only at the end of the pulse (on transition from HIGH to LOW). The output logic is placed in sequential always block.

This approach balances area and power while maintaining clear state transitions and output behavior. The reset remains asynchronous for quick initialization. Nonblocking assignments are used to avoid simulation mismatches.

Thus, the design is functionally identical but uses fewer flip-flops and simpler logic, improving area and potentially power without sacrificing timing.
