To optimize the original FSM design for better Performance, Power, and Area (PPA):

1. Performance: Use one-hot encoding for states to speed up the next-state logic, especially beneficial for FPGA implementations where flip-flops are abundant and decoding logic is simpler.
2. Power: Limit switching activity by carefully controlling output toggling only in the pulse detection state and ensure no glitches by using registered outputs.
3. Area: Although one-hot increases flip-flop usage, the simplified combinational logic and faster timing can allow for better optimization overall.

Additional improvements:
- Make output registered (clocked) to avoid glitches and synchronize data_out with clk.
- Use non-blocking assignments consistently.
- Combine state transition and output generation in a single sequential always block to simplify control and reduce combinational logic.

This approach improves timing and robustness with minor area cost tradeoff, optimal for typical FPGA or ASIC flows.

The behavior remains exactly as specified: data_out is asserted only for one cycle at the end of the detected pulse pattern (0->1->0).
