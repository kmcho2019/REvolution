From Example 2, we adopt separating state update and output logic by using registered data_in and combinational output generation for clarity. The fusion approach keeps a 2-bit shift register to detect the "0→1→0" pattern and registers data_in to ensure synchronous sampling. The output is asserted combinationally based on the shift register and current data_in, producing a single-cycle pulse on data_out exactly when the "pulse" ends. This design keeps minimal state, clear timing, and low power usage by only updating registers every cycle without complex FSM states, and simplifies output logic. It also ensures safe, non-blocking sequential assignments and synchronous resets, improving timing and clarity.
