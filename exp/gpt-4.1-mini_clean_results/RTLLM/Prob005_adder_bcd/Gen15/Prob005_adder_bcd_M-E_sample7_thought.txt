Implement the 4-bit BCD adder by first performing a binary addition of inputs A, B, and Cin into a 5-bit raw sum. Instead of a direct numeric comparator, detect BCD overflow with logic that identifies when the raw sum is greater than 9, which can be simplified as: overflow = (raw_sum[4] == 1) or (raw_sum[3] & (raw_sum[2] | raw_sum[1])). This method avoids a full comparator, using bitwise logic for efficiency.

Once overflow is detected, add the fixed BCD correction value 6 (0110) to the lower 4 bits of the raw sum, producing a corrected 5-bit sum. The carry-out is then the 5th bit of the corrected sum, and the 4-bit Sum output is the lower nibble of the corrected sum.

This approach simplifies overflow detection, potentially reducing critical path delay and hardware complexity by replacing a numeric comparator with minimal combinational logic. It also keeps the correction addition straightforward. The overall design remains purely combinational and clear.
