Perform the binary addition of A, B, and Cin to get a 5-bit raw sum. Then, instead of first detecting the need for correction with a boolean expression and adding 6 conditionally, this design calculates both the uncorrected and corrected sums in parallel.

The corrected sum is computed by adding 6 to the raw sum's lower 4 bits (with no initial carry-in). We then select between the raw sum and the corrected sum based on whether the raw sum is invalid as a BCD digit (i.e., greater than 9).

To detect invalid BCD, we use a direct magnitude comparator (raw_sum[4:0] > 9), implemented by checking if the 5-bit sum is greater than 9.

Since both sums are computed in parallel, the multiplexer selects the correct final sum and carry without sequential dependency, enabling faster timing at the cost of slightly more hardware.

This architectural idea trades off some area and power for timing: precomputing both sums removes the carry chain dependency through correction logic, potentially improving the maximum clock frequency.

The Cout is the carry-out from the corrected sum path, as the corrected sum properly represents the BCD result with carry out if overflowed.
