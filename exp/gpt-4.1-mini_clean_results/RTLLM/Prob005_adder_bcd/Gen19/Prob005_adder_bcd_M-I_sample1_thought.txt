To optimize the original design for better performance and area, reduce the complexity of the comparator (raw_sum > 9) by implementing it as simple combinational logic rather than a numeric comparison operator. Since raw_sum is 5 bits (sum of two 4-bit BCD digits plus carry), the condition raw_sum > 9 can be expressed as: raw_sum[4] == 1 or (raw_sum[3] and (raw_sum[2] or raw_sum[1])). This simple logic detects whether raw_sum exceeds 9 without using '>'. This reduces logic levels and improves timing.

Additionally, we keep the correction addition straightforward by adding 6 when needed. The corrected sum addition is a 5-bit addition, but since the correction is fixed, it can be quickly synthesized and optimized.

This approach maintains correctness and clean structure but lowers critical path delays and gate usage compared to the original operator-based comparator. It also reduces potential power due to simplified logic gates.

The carry-out is still driven by the correction_needed signal as before.

This yields a minimal performance and area gain with negligible increase in code complexity.
