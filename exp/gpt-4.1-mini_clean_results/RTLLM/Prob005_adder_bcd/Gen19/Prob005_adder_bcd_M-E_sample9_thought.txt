Implement the 4-bit BCD adder by first performing a straightforward 4-bit binary addition of inputs A, B, and Cin, producing a 5-bit raw sum. Instead of adding 6 conditionally using bitwise addition, detect if correction is needed by checking if the raw sum is greater than 9. If correction is needed, generate the corrected sum by subtracting 10 (binary 1010) from the raw sum instead of adding 6. This subtraction effectively aligns with BCD correction since adding 6 or subtracting 10 from the raw sum both yield the correct BCD digit and carry behavior. The carry-out will be the MSB of the raw sum or set when correction is applied.

This subtraction-based correction reduces the combinational logic overhead because subtracting a fixed number is analogous to adding its two's complement, which can be implemented efficiently by wiring. The corrected sum is always within 0–9, and Cout signals overflow to the next decimal digit. This approach uses the intuitive arithmetic relationship for BCD correction (sum > 9 → subtract 10 and set carry), yielding a clean, simple, and optimized module without explicit multi-bit adders for correction. The design is fully combinational, suitable for synthesis, and reduces the critical path by replacing the addition of 6 with subtraction of 10.
