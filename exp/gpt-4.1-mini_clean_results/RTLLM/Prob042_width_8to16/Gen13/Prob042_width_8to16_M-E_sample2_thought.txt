Use a small FSM with two states to track whether we are waiting for the first byte or the second byte. Store the first byte in a register when received. When the second byte arrives, form the 16-bit output by concatenating the stored first byte (upper 8 bits) and the new byte (lower 8 bits). Instead of outputting immediately, register the concatenated data and valid_out in the next clock cycle, providing a clean one-cycle delay after receiving the second input. If no valid_in arrives, keep the FSM in the current state and hold registers stable. On reset, clear all registers and return to the initial waiting state. This design avoids glitching outputs, cleanly separates data and control paths, and guarantees the output is valid one clock cycle after receiving two valid inputs.
