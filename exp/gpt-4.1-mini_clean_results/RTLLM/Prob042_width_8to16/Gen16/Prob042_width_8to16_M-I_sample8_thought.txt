To correctly meet the requirement that valid_out and data_out are generated in the clock cycle *after* receiving the second valid input (i.e., one cycle delay after concatenation), we introduce a small pipeline stage. On receiving the second valid input, we capture the concatenated 16-bit data into an intermediate register and assert a one-cycle delayed valid_out.

This decouples data capture from output staging, avoiding simultaneous updates of valid_out and data_out that caused synthesis or timing issues. It also ensures proper timing semantics: first store the initial byte, wait for the next valid input; upon second valid input, prepare concatenated output in an intermediate register, then output it in the next clock cycle with valid_out asserted.

Additionally, resetting all registers, defaulting valid_out low each cycle, and making all registers synchronous except reset improves timing and synthesis robustness. This method adds a slight pipeline stage, improving timing closure and reducing glitch chances while slightly increasing latency, which matches the requirements.

This approach optimizes performance and synthesis friendliness without increasing area or power noticeably.
