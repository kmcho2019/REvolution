Refactor the design by separating data capture, output register, and valid signal generation with clear pipeline stages to strictly meet the requirement: valid_out and data_out are asserted exactly one cycle after two valid inputs have been received.

Use a two-stage pipeline approach:
1. Capture the first 8-bit data when valid_in arrives and store it in data_lock.
2. Upon arrival of the second valid data, form the 16-bit concatenation and store it in a pipeline register (output_reg).
3. On the next clock cycle after forming output_reg, assert valid_out and output data_out from output_reg.

Avoid complex FSM signals by using a simple flag register (`have_first`) to indicate if the first data is stored.
- When have_first=0 and valid_in=1, latch data_in into data_lock and set have_first=1.
- When have_first=1 and valid_in=1, latch {data_lock, data_in} into output_reg, clear have_first=0, and assert output_valid delayed by one clock.
- Introduce a registered signal (output_valid_reg) to assert valid_out exactly one clock cycle after output_reg is loaded.

This method is straightforward, fully synchronous with async reset, uses non-blocking assignments correctly, and ensures output valid and data appear one cycle after the second input, meeting the problem spec.

The use of separate registers for stages clarifies timing and makes synthesis easier. No combinational assignments for output valid or data_out; all are registered.

This design is clean, easy to understand, and meets all requirements without the complexity or glitches of a state machine.
