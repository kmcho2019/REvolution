To strictly meet the requirement that valid_out and data_out are generated exactly in the cycle *after* receiving the second byte, we separate the output stage from input handling clearly.

We will:

- Use a small state machine with two states: WAIT_FIRST and WAIT_SECOND.

- On reset, clear all registers and go to WAIT_FIRST.

- When a valid_in arrives in WAIT_FIRST, store the data in data_lock and move to WAIT_SECOND, no output yet.

- When a valid_in arrives in WAIT_SECOND, form the 16-bit concatenation and store it in an output register; set a flag output_valid_next.

- The output registers (valid_out, data_out) get updated on the next clock cycle from these "next" registers.

- To cleanly separate stages, introduce output_valid_d and output_data_d registers that drive valid_out and data_out.

- Maintain a clear pipeline: input handling updates the "next" output registers, which are registered into valid_out and data_out on the next clock.

- Reset logic clears everything.

This separation ensures valid_out and data_out are asserted exactly one cycle after the second byte arrives, and valid_out is asserted only for one cycle.

This design cleanly satisfies the timing and behavior requirements.
