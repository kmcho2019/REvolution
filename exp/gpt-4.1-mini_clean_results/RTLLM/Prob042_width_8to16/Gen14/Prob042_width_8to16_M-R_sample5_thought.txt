Refactor the design by using a two-stage approach with registers and a simpler FSM-like control to ensure timing correctness. Store the first incoming 8-bit data in a register when valid_in is asserted. When the second valid_in arrives, latch it into another register. Then, in the following cycle, output the concatenated 16-bit data (first stored byte as high bits, second byte as low bits) and assert valid_out for one cycle. This ensures valid_out and data_out are generated exactly one clock cycle after receiving the second input. Use a simple flag to indicate waiting for the second input and another flag to trigger output. Avoid an explicit multi-state FSM; instead, rely on sequential logic with clear flags and pipelining of input data. This clarifies timing and avoids premature clearing of output valid signals.
