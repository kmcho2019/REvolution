To create a different architectural approach for the 64-bit subtractor with overflow detection, I will split the operands into two 32-bit halves and perform subtraction in two stages: first subtract the lower 32 bits, then subtract the upper 32 bits along with the borrow from the lower subtraction. This simulates a ripple borrow subtractor structure, which is a classic approach for multi-word arithmetic. 

For overflow detection, instead of directly checking the sign bits of inputs and result, I'll compute the signed result using signed types and then detect overflow by comparing the sign of the result with expected sign based on the operands, similar to the two's complement overflow rule, but explicitly using Verilog signed arithmetic. This highlights usage of Verilog signed operators and staged arithmetic. 

The overall idea demonstrates multi-cycle borrow propagation with explicit handling and signed arithmetic, distinguishing this implementation from the direct single-step subtraction approach.
