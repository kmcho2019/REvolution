Implement the 64-bit subtractor by first extending the sign bits to a 65-bit width and performing subtraction as unsigned 65-bit values, thereby allowing explicit overflow detection by checking the upper bits.

Specifically:
- Concatenate the sign bit of A and B to form 65-bit signed operands.
- Perform 65-bit subtraction to get a 65-bit result.
- The 64-bit result is the lower 64 bits of the 65-bit result.
- Overflow occurs if the 64-bit sign bit differs from the 65th bit (MSB) of the result. This check leverages the idea that for correct signed subtraction result, the sign extension bit should match the 64-bit sign bit. If they differ, overflow occurred.

This approach simplifies overflow detection by turning it into a single bit comparison after subtraction, without explicitly checking combinations of input signs.
