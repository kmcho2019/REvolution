I will implement the 64-bit subtractor by dividing the 64-bit inputs into four 16-bit chunks and performing subtraction chunk-by-chunk using 16-bit ripple borrow subtraction. Each chunk subtracts B's chunk and a borrow-in from the previous chunk. This modular chunk-based approach simplifies the logic and helps conceptualize borrow propagation without large carry-lookahead logic.

For overflow detection:
- Overflow depends only on the top sign bit and the overall result sign.
- I will compute the final result by concatenating all chunks' results.
- The borrow signal at the most significant chunk is used implicitly but overflow is only about sign bits of A, B, and result, so borrow chain won't directly affect overflow logic.
- The borrow-out of the highest chunk is not overflow but underflow indication, so overflow detection logic remains checking sign bits of A, B, and result as specified.

This chunked ripple borrow subtractor is easier to understand and can be modified or pipelined easily. It also allows reuse of smaller 16-bit subtractor units, which might be beneficial for custom datapaths or partial reconfiguration.

The module is purely combinational. Each 16-bit subtractor outputs a 16-bit result and borrow-out for next chunk borrow-in.

Overall:
- Break inputs A and B into 16-bit slices.
- Instantiate 4 subtractor units chained by borrow signals.
- Compose the 64-bit result from 4 results.
- Detect overflow by comparing sign bits.

This modular design is a novel alternative to direct 64-bit subtract or carry-lookahead.
