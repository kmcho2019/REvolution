Fuse the modular borrow-chained approach of example 1 with the efficient two's complement subtraction of example 2 to build a high-performance 64-bit subtractor.

Strategy:
- Use two 32-bit carry lookahead adders (CLAs) for the lower and upper 32-bit parts, chaining the carry-out from the lower CLA as carry-in to the upper CLA. This modular decomposition reduces carry propagation length from 64 to 32 bits per block, improving timing over a flat 64-bit CLA.
- Perform subtraction by adding A to the two's complement of B, i.e., A + (~B + 1). The initial carry-in to the lower 32-bit adder will be 1, representing the +1 for two's complement.
- This merges the fast carry lookahead arithmetic from example 2 with the borrow/ carry chain splitting of example 1.
- Overflow detection remains the same: overflow occurs if sign of A differs from B and the result sign differs from A.
- This structure balances performance (due to shorter carry chains and lookahead adders), modularity, and clear subtraction semantics, reducing critical path delay compared to ripple borrow subtractors and the flat CLA.
- Additionally, it minimizes area and power by avoiding full 64-bit flat carry chain and ripple borrow chains.
- The 32-bit CLA blocks implement fast carry propagation internally for better performance.
- The design is fully combinational, uses structural hierarchy for clarity, and aligns with the original overflow detection requirement.

This approach combines modular decomposition for timing, fast carry logic for performance, and efficient two's complement subtraction for correctness.
