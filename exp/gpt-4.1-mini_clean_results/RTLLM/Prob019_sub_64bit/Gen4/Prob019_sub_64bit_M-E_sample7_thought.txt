I will implement the 64-bit subtractor using a hierarchical block approach by dividing the operands into four 16-bit segments and performing subtraction segment-wise with borrow chaining between them. This "4-stage 16-bit subtractor cascade" approach enables modular design and potential reusability of smaller subtractor blocks.

Each 16-bit subtractor block:
- Performs subtraction with borrow-in and outputs borrow-out.
- Is implemented using simple combinational logic using built-in subtraction operator for clarity and efficiency at 16-bit granularity.

The top-level module:
- Chains the borrow-out from each lower 16-bit block to the borrow-in of the next higher block.
- Combines the 16-bit results into the final 64-bit result.

Overflow detection:
- Done at the final 64-bit result level using the sign bits of A, B, and result.
- The condition remains: overflow occurs if sign_A != sign_B and sign_result != sign_A.

This approach allows clearer segmentation of the subtractor, easier timing management, and a potential tradeoff between ripple borrow delay and modularity. It also uses simple built-in subtraction operations for each segment, leveraging synthesis optimizations.

This design is fundamentally different from the prior carry-lookahead bitwise adder approach and may help in pipelining or partial reuse of the 16-bit subtractor blocks in other designs.
