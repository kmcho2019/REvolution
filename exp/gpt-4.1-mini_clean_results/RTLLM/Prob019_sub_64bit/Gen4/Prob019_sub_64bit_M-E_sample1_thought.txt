Implement the 64-bit subtractor by explicitly performing two's complement addition: compute B's two's complement (~B + 1) and then add it to A. This approach can clarify the internal operations and make it modular. Overflow detection is done by checking the sign bits of A, B, and the result per the standard overflow definition in subtraction: when subtracting a number with different signs, overflow occurs if the sign of the result differs from the sign of A.

By separating the negation and addition, it also exposes an opportunity to reuse an adder module or pipeline stages later. This explicit approach can be helpful for educational or verification purposes.

Additionally, carry out the two's complement negation in a separate step and then add to A. The overflow logic is implemented as a combinational expression based on the sign bits.
