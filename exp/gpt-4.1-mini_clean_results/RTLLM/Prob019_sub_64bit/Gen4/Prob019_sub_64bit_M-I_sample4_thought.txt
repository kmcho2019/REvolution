The original solution correctly implements subtraction via a manual carry-lookahead adder and overflow detection, but it is somewhat verbose and likely less efficient in area and power due to the explicit bitwise carry chains and multiple intermediate nets.

To optimize for better PPA while preserving correctness:
- Use the built-in subtraction operator (`assign result = A - B;`), which synthesis tools optimize efficiently, producing fast, low-area hardware.
- Keep overflow detection logic explicit and simple by checking the sign bits as required.
- Remove explicit carry signals and propagate/generate arrays to reduce combinational logic complexity and switching activity.
- This reduces gate count, wiring complexity, and switching, improving power and area, and typically yields equal or better timing.
- The design remains purely combinational and self-contained.

Thus, this is a classic and industry-standard approach for a 64-bit subtractor with overflow detection in Verilog.

This approach trades manual pedagogical adder implementation for synthesis-friendly concise code with better PPA.
