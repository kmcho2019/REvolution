- Use a hierarchical design dividing the 64-bit operation into four 16-bit blocks for modularity and manageable carry complexity.
- Within each 16-bit block, implement a single optimized 16-bit CLA subtractor (instead of chaining two 8-bit blocks) with proper group propagate and generate signals for faster carry lookahead locally.
- Employ a higher-level 4-block carry-lookahead stage to chain the four 16-bit subtractors, replacing the ripple borrow chain with a small-level CLA for the block carries. This reduces carry propagation delay compared to ripple chaining while keeping area manageable.
- Share complemented B operand at the top-level to avoid multiple inversions of B inside sub-blocks, reducing power.
- Keep the overflow detection logic simple and accurate by using the sign bits of A, B, and the result.
- This design balances speed (by using hierarchical CLA both internally within blocks and across blocks), power (less redundant inversion of B), area (by using 16-bit CLA blocks instead of multiple 8-bit blocks), and modularity (clear block structure).
  
Thus, the module performs:  
- Compute ~B once at the top.  
- Implement four 16-bit CLA subtractors: each adds A_seg + (~B_seg) + carry_in.  
- Implement a 4-block carry lookahead generator to compute carry_outs of these 16-bit blocks rapidly.  
- Concatenate the results into the final 64-bit output.  
- Detect overflow using sign bits.

This fusion will yield improved timing (shorter carry paths than pure ripple across blocks), reduced power by shared inversion, and better area by reducing hierarchy depth.
