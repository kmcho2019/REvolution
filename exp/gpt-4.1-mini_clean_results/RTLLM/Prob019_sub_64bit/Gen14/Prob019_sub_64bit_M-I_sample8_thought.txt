To improve the original 64-bit subtractor design in terms of performance and power, I will eliminate the ripple borrow chain by performing the subtraction directly as a 64-bit two’s complement operation using Verilog's built-in subtraction operator. This reduces critical path delays caused by borrow propagation and cuts down intermediate signals switching, improving timing and power consumption.

For overflow detection, the logic remains simple and direct, relying on the sign bits of A, B, and result. By performing a single 64-bit subtraction, the code becomes more synthesis-friendly, likely enabling the tool to infer an optimized carry chain (carry-lookahead or fast adder) automatically.

This approach:
- Removes modular segmentation and borrow chaining.
- Uses direct 64-bit subtraction which synthesis tools optimize well.
- Maintains the same overflow detection logic for signed subtraction.
- Minimizes netlist complexity and interconnects, reducing area and switching power.

This design achieves better performance and power at likely minimal or no area increase and keeps functional correctness intact.
