The original design uses eight 8-bit CLA blocks to build a 64-bit subtractor, which balances modularity and performance well. However, the carry chain traversing all eight blocks serially can limit maximum clock frequency (worst negative slack in timing). 

To improve timing (reduce carry propagation delay), a hierarchical carry-lookahead architecture can be applied:

- Implement 16 4-bit CLA sub-blocks (smaller blocks have faster internal carry calculation).
- Introduce a higher-level 16-bit carry-lookahead generator that quickly computes carries between 4-bit blocks.
- This approach reduces the longest carry path from 8 to 4 blocks, potentially speeding up the design.
  
For overflow detection, the logic remains the same.

Also, replacing the explicit ~B plus 1 logic with direct subtraction operator in Verilog can allow synthesis tools to optimize better (but may be less instructive). Since user requested a clear explicit design with overflow, we keep explicit logic.

This redesign trades a slight area increase for better timing and potentially slightly reduced power due to shorter carry chains and smaller ripple within blocks.

Thus, the improved design:

- Uses 4-bit CLA sub-blocks as building blocks.
- Includes a hierarchical carry-lookahead for inter-block carry signals (carry lookahead over 16 blocks).
- Performs subtraction as A + ~B + 1, propagated through the hierarchy.
- Overflow detection is implemented as before.

This approach maintains clarity, improves timing by limiting carry path length, and optimizes carry logic granularity for better PPA metrics.
