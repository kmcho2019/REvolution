Implement a 64-bit subtractor using a 64-bit ripple borrow subtractor approach rather than an adder-based method. The subtraction A - B can be performed bitwise from LSB to MSB by propagating borrow bits instead of carry bits. This involves implementing a chain of full subtractor stages, where each stage subtracts bits A[i], B[i], and a borrow-in from the previous stage to produce result[i] and borrow-out to the next stage.

For overflow detection, use the sign bits (MSB) of A, B, and result. The overflow rules remain the same:
- Positive overflow occurs when subtracting a negative number (B) from a positive number (A) produces a negative result.
- Negative overflow occurs when subtracting a positive number (B) from a negative number (A) produces a positive result.

The advantage here is a conceptual demonstration of direct binary subtraction with borrow, providing educational clarity on the borrow chain's effect and how overflow arises in subtraction, distinct from the adder-based two's complement approach. This method may be less optimal for performance but gives a different architectural perspective.

Implement a 1-bit full subtractor module and instantiate it 64 times in a generate block to create the 64-bit subtractor.

Signals:
- Each subtractor outputs a borrow_out to the next stage.
- The initial borrow_in = 0.
- The overflow is calculated from sign bits as specified.

This design avoids using addition and complement internally and clearly illustrates subtraction with borrow chains.
