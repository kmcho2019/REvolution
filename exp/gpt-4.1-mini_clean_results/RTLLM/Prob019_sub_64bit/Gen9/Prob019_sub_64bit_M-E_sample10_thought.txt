Implement the 64-bit subtractor as a 64-bit ripple borrow subtractor constructed from 64 1-bit subtractor stages, each calculating the difference and borrow out. This modular structural approach clearly reveals the subtraction process and naturally produces borrow chains.

Each 1-bit subtractor calculates:
    difference = A_bit ^ B_bit ^ borrow_in
    borrow_out = (~A_bit & B_bit) | ((~A_bit | B_bit) & borrow_in)

At the top level, chain these 64 stages, connecting borrow out of stage i to borrow in of stage i+1, starting with borrow_in = 0.

For overflow detection (signed 64-bit integers):
- Overflow happens if the sign bit overflowed according to rules:
    Positive overflow: A sign=0, B sign=1, result sign=1
    Negative overflow: A sign=1, B sign=0, result sign=0
So overflow = (A[63] != B[63]) && (result[63] != A[63])

This approach avoids complex carry-lookahead logic and is very intuitive for debug and verification. It trades off latency for simplicity and small logic per bit, also making it easy to reuse the 1-bit subtractor module in other designs.

The top-level module instantiates 64 1-bit subtractors wired in a chain to produce the final result and overflow flag.
