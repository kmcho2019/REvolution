Refactor the 64-bit subtractor using a single continuous assignment with the Verilog subtraction operator and then separately implement the overflow detection based on sign bits. This leverages the synthesis tool's efficient native subtractor implementation, reducing code complexity and potentially improving performance and area by avoiding explicit ripple borrow chains.

Overflow detection remains the same logic: overflow occurs when A and B have different signs and the result's sign differs from A's sign.

The design becomes more concise and readable, relying on the synthesis tool to infer the optimal hardware for subtraction, rather than explicitly building it from 1-bit subtractors.
