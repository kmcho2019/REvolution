Fuse the area-efficient ripple-carry adder subtraction approach from Example 1 with the modular 16-bit block partitioning and faster carry propagation style of Example 2 to achieve a balanced high-performance, area-efficient, and modular 64-bit subtractor with overflow detection.

Fusion strategy:

- Partition the 64-bit operands into four 16-bit segments (like Example 2) for better modularity, maintainability, and localized carry chains.
- Implement each 16-bit subtractor as a simple ripple-carry subtractor using full adders in a chain (like Example 1's ripple carry) for area and power efficiency, avoiding the overhead of carry-lookahead inside blocks.
- Chain the four 16-bit ripple subtractors via carry/borrow signals, so overall carry propagation involves at most four ripple chains cascaded, better than a full 64-bit ripple and simpler than a full CLA.
- Use full adder modules within the 16-bit blocks to implement A + (~B) + borrow_in subtraction.
- Keep overflow detection at the top level based on the sign bits of A, B, and the result per problem description.
- This fusion gains:
  - Reduced area and power by using ripple carry inside smaller 16-bit blocks rather than 64-bit ripple or full CLA.
  - Improved performance vs a single 64-bit ripple by shortening ripple chains and adding parallelism via block partitioning.
  - Simple and clear RTL easily synthesizable and debuggable.
  - Overflow detection remains straightforward and accurate.

This solution balances performance and area well, leveraging modular 16-bit ripple-carry subtractors that are simple and power efficient while improving speed by limiting ripple length to 16 bits per block.
