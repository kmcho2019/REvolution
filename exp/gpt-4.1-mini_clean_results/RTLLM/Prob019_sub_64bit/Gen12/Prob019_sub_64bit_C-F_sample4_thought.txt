Fuse the hierarchical modularity and segmented design of Example 1 with the more uniform 8-bit CLA block approach of Example 2 to form a hybrid 64-bit subtractor architecture that balances speed, modularity, and simplicity.

Fusion strategy:
- Divide 64-bit operands into four 16-bit segments (as Example 1) for manageable borrow propagation stages.
- Implement each 16-bit segment with two cascaded 8-bit CLA subtractors internally (from Example 1 and Example 2) to keep fast local carry lookahead inside each 16-bit block.
- Chain the four 16-bit blocks with borrow ripple to reduce long carry chain delay compared to an 8-block approach, minimizing carry propagation stages.
- Use a single-level 8-bit CLA subtractor module as in Example 2 for clarity and fast carry lookahead inside 8-bit blocks.
- Share the complemented B signals inside each 16-bit block only once for power efficiency.
- Maintain clear overflow detection by comparing the sign bits of inputs and result per standard two’s complement subtraction overflow conditions.

This fusion:
- Reduces the number of carry propagation stages from eight to four.
- Retains fast 8-bit CLA local blocks for speed within each segment.
- Keeps the design modular and clear with reusable 8-bit CLA subtractors.
- Saves power by localizing the complemented B and minimizing repeated inversions.
- Provides clean, easily synthesizable logic with explicit carry lookahead and ripple borrow between 16-bit segments.
