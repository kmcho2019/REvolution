Fuse the modularity and explicit borrow tracking of Example 1 with the fast carry-lookahead structure within smaller blocks of Example 2 for an optimized 64-bit subtractor with overflow detection.

Fusion strategy:
- Divide the 64-bit operands into two 32-bit halves, improving borrow chain simplicity and reducing long ripple chains.
- Implement each 32-bit subtractor using two cascaded 16-bit CLA subtractors with internal 8-bit CLA subtractors, as in Example 2, to speed up carry/borrow propagation.
- This reduces the borrow propagation stages to just two 16-bit CLA blocks per 32-bit subtractor, and two such 32-bit subtractors in total, lowering latency and improving timing versus a pure ripple borrow of 32 bits.
- Use explicit borrow chaining between the two 32-bit subtractors, enhancing modularity and clarity.
- Reuse the hierarchical CLA subtractor design (8-bit CLA subtractors inside 16-bit subtractors) from Example 2 to balance speed and area.
- Keep the overflow detection logic identical to both examples: check sign bits of A, B, and result to detect overflow.

Benefits of this fusion:
- Modular and hierarchical design facilitates synthesis and debugging.
- Shorter borrow chains improve performance.
- 8-bit CLA blocks balance complexity and speed efficiently.
- Explicit borrow signals increase clarity and testability.
- Overflow logic is simple and reliable.

This design combines performance, area efficiency, and clean architecture from both examples to deliver a superior 64-bit subtractor.
