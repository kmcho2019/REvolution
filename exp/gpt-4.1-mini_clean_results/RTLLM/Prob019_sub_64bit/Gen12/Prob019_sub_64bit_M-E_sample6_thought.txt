I will implement the 64-bit subtractor using a 4-stage hierarchical approach, splitting the 64-bit subtraction into four 16-bit subtractors chained together with borrow propagation.

Each 16-bit stage computes the difference of its segment of A and B with a borrow-in from the previous stage, producing a 16-bit result segment and a borrow-out. This approach makes the design modular and easier to pipeline or optimize at block granularity.

The borrow logic:
- Subtraction with borrow_in: result = A_segment - B_segment - borrow_in.
- borrow_out is set when the subtraction borrows.

The final 64-bit result is the concatenation of all four 16-bit results.

Overflow detection uses the sign bits of the full 64-bit operands and result as specified:
- Positive overflow if A positive and B negative but result negative.
- Negative overflow if A negative and B positive but result positive.

This approach differs from the previous carry-lookahead adder style by explicitly handling borrow chaining in smaller chunks, allowing easier hierarchical reuse and potentially better timing by localizing borrow propagation to small blocks.

All logic is combinational. The design uses simple 16-bit subtractors with borrow in/out and combines their outputs. Overflow detection remains at top-level with the full 64-bit sign bits.
