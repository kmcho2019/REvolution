I will implement the 64-bit subtractor as a single wide subtraction using Verilog's built-in operators for clarity and efficiency. Instead of segmenting the operation, the subtraction is done in one step, leveraging synthesis tools' optimization to generate a fast carry/borrrow chain.

Overflow detection will be done by applying the standard rule for signed subtraction overflow:
- Overflow happens when subtracting a negative number (B) from a positive number (A) produces a negative result.
- Or when subtracting a positive number (B) from a negative number (A) produces a positive result.

This is detected by comparing the sign bits (MSB) of A, B, and result.

This approach is simpler, likely more efficient in area and power since synthesis tools can optimize a single wide subtractor better than a manually segmented one. The design is fully combinational and concise while meeting the functionality and overflow detection requirements.
