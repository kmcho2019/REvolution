The goal is to design a 64-bit subtractor with overflow detection by fusing the strengths of both example solutions:

- From Example 1, we keep the clear and simple ripple carry adder structure with explicit full adders, which provides full control over the bitwise addition and is very understandable for debugging and customizing.
- From Example 2, we take the carry-lookahead concept but implement it in a hybrid way to reduce the critical path and keep area moderate by grouping bits into small blocks to compute block-level carry signals and then ripple carry between blocks. This "block ripple carry adder" approach balances timing and area better than a full ripple carry or a full carry-lookahead for 64 bits.
- We use 16 blocks of 4 bits each. Each 4-bit block has a simple carry-lookahead logic internally and generates propagate and generate signals for the block.
- Then block carries ripple between the 16 blocks, reducing long carry chains.
- This hybrid approach provides a better performance than a full ripple carry while keeping the area lower than a full carry-lookahead.
- Overflow detection uses the same sign-bit logic as before: overflow occurs if A and B sign differ and result sign differs from A.
- This approach improves PPA by reducing long carry chains (better performance), limiting gate count (moderate area), and using generate/propagate logic efficiently (less switching/power).
- The design remains purely combinational and is synthesizable.
- The code includes helper functions for 4-bit carry-lookahead blocks to improve readability and modularity.

This fusion maximizes performance gains from carry-lookahead at small granularities while avoiding the high area and complexity of a full 64-bit carry-lookahead, and retains clarity and correctness of the ripple carry approach for overflow detection.
