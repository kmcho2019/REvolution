The original solution modularizes the 64-bit subtractor as four chained 16-bit subtractors with borrow propagation. While this is structurally clear and reusable, the ripple-borrow chain adds delay and may limit performance.

To improve performance (timing) and possibly power without significantly increasing area:
- Replace the ripple-borrow structure with a single 64-bit subtractor using built-in Verilog subtraction operator to leverage synthesis tool optimizations (carry-lookahead, carry-select, or fast subtractor implementations).
- Implement the entire subtraction in one combinational operation, which usually results in better timing and lower power.
- Overflow detection remains the same and is kept explicitly for clarity.

This approach simplifies the design by removing the borrow chain and uses the synthesizer’s optimized subtractor generation. This generally leads to improved timing (lower critical path), slightly reduced power from less net toggling and simpler wiring, and similar or slightly reduced area due to elimination of four discrete modules and interconnect overhead.

The overflow logic is straightforward and correctly implemented according to two's complement rules for overflow in subtraction.

Hence, the optimized solution is a single 64-bit subtractor implemented in combinational logic, with explicit overflow detection based on the sign bits of inputs and output.
