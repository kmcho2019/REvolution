To improve timing and power efficiency, implement a deeper pipeline that splits the addition of partial products into multiple stages. Instead of adding all eight partial products at once, accumulate them progressively over several clock cycles using registers for intermediate sums. This reduces the critical path by dividing large combinational adders into smaller ones and allows higher clock frequency.

We will:

- Keep input sampling and enable propagation in stage 1.
- Generate partial products combinationally.
- Split partial products addition into three pipeline stages:
    - Stage 1: Add partial_products[0] + partial_products[1], partial_products[2] + partial_products[3], partial_products[4] + partial_products[5], partial_products[6] + partial_products[7].
    - Stage 2: Add stage 1 sums into two sums.
    - Stage 3: Final addition of two sums producing product.

The enable pipeline shifts accordingly (4 stages total). Only update registers when the enable is active to reduce switching.

This staged addition pipeline reduces the maximum combinational delay per cycle, improving max frequency and lowering power from glitch reduction. Area impact is minimal since registers replace some large combinational adders.

Outputs are valid one cycle after final stage register update; enable is properly synchronized through pipeline stages.

Asynchronous reset is retained per spec.
