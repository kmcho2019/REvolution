Fuse the best ideas from both examples to build a balanced 4-stage pipelined 8-bit multiplier that reduces combinational path length and improves timing while preserving correctness and pipelined operation:

1. Use a 4-bit shift register pipeline for the enable signal to track the multiplication operation through four pipeline stages, enabling clean flow control.

2. Register inputs (mul_a and mul_b) on mul_en_in asserted.

3. Generate partial products combinationally from registered inputs.

4. Instead of summing all eight partial products in a single step, split the addition into two pipeline stages to reduce critical path:

   - Stage 1: Pairwise sum of partial products (4 sums)

   - Stage 2: Sum the intermediate results in two adders, then register the final sum in stage 3.

5. Register each partial sum at every pipeline stage to break long combinational paths.

6. The final product is registered in the last stage and output when the enable pipeline MSB is high.

7. The output product is gated by the output enable signal, and zero otherwise.

This fusion combines the fine-grained pipelining and multi-stage summation of partial products from Example 1 with the simple control and clean pipelining methodology from Example 2 to optimize timing and power while maintaining area efficiency.
