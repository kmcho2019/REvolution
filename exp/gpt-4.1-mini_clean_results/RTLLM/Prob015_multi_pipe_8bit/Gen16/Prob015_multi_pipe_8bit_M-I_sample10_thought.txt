To optimize the pipelined unsigned 8-bit multiplier for better performance, power, and area (PPA), I will restructure the partial product summation into a balanced 3-stage adder tree pipeline. This divides the wide combinational addition into smaller 16-bit additions spread over multiple clock cycles, reducing the critical path and improving timing (WNS). 

Specifically, pipeline stage 1 generates partial products and sums pairs of them into 4 intermediate sums. Pipeline stage 2 sums those 4 sums into 2 intermediate sums. Pipeline stage 3 adds the last 2 sums to get the final product.

This deeper pipelining improves max clock frequency by reducing combinational depth per cycle. It also reduces glitching and switching in large adders, thus lowering power. Registering intermediate sums suppresses glitches.

The enable signal is propagated through a 4-bit shift register to align with the 4-stage pipeline latency.

Overall, this pipeline uses more registers but a smaller and faster combinational addition per stage, enhancing timing without significantly increasing area or power.

Input registers latch operands only when input enable is asserted. Output is zeroed if output enable is low.
