Fuse the two examples by implementing a pipelined 8-bit unsigned multiplier with three pipeline stages to balance timing, power, and area:

1. Stage 1 (Input capture): Register mul_a, mul_b, and mul_en_in, updating only when mul_en_in is high.
2. Stage 2 (Partial products generation and partial sums): Compute partial products as shifted multiplicand masked by multiplier bits (registered inputs). Then, instead of summing all partial products combinationally at once, use a balanced two-stage adder tree pipeline to sum partial products in two pipeline registers, breaking the big addition into smaller steps and improving critical path.
   - First partial sum stage: sum pairs of partial products into four partial sums (sum stage 1).
   - Second partial sum stage: sum pairs of the four partial sums into two partial sums (sum stage 2).
3. Stage 3 (Final sum and output register): sum the two partial sums into the final product and register it.

Use a 3-bit shift register for mul_en_in to keep enable signals synchronized with pipeline data. Generate mul_en_out from the MSB of this pipeline.

This approach balances pipeline stages, reduces combinational critical path compared to summing all partial products at once, and retains the simple, clean design of registered inputs and outputs. It also gates input sampling on mul_en_in and provides clean output enable control. This fusion maximizes clock frequency and lowers power by pipelining addition, and limits area by spreading adders across stages.
