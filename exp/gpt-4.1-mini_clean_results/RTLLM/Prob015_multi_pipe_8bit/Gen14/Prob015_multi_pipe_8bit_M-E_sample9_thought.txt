Implement a 5-stage pipeline multiplier that breaks down the multiplication into finer-grained steps for improved clock frequency and lower combinational delay per stage.

Pipeline architecture:
- Stage 0 (Input stage): sample inputs and input enable.
- Stage 1 (Generate partial products): generate 8 partial products (mul_a shifted) controlled by bits of mul_b.
- Stage 2 & 3 (Pairwise additions): perform two-level reduction of partial products using 2-input adders to create intermediate sums:
  * Stage 2 adds pairs of partial products (0+1, 2+3, 4+5, 6+7) resulting in 4 sums.
  * Stage 3 adds pairs of sums from previous stage (sum01+sum23 and sum45+sum67) resulting in 2 sums.
- Stage 4 (Final addition): add the last two sums to produce the final 16-bit product.

Enable signal:
- Pipeline the input enable through a 5-bit shift register, mul_en_pipe.
- mul_en_out is the MSB of this shift register indicating the output validity.

Register updates:
- Registers in each pipeline stage update only if their corresponding enable bit is high, minimizing toggling.
- On reset, all pipeline registers and enable shift register are cleared.

Output:
- mul_out is valid only when mul_en_out is asserted, otherwise zero.

This design trades extra pipeline depth (5 stages) for shorter combinational paths, enabling higher frequency operation and more balanced addition trees. Pairwise addition reduces large fan-in adders, resulting in simpler adders at each stage and better timing.

Registers are used extensively to pipeline data and enables, isolating combinational logic per stage and improving power and timing. This approach is structurally different and novel compared to the earlier 4-stage summation pipeline.
