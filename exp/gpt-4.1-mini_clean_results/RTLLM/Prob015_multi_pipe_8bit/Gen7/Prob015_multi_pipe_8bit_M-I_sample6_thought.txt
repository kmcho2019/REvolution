To improve the original design for better PPA, the main bottleneck is the large combinational adder that sums all 8 partial products in a single clock cycle, limiting maximum frequency and increasing power due to wide fan-in addition.

To optimize:
- Introduce a multi-stage pipelined adder tree to reduce the combinational delay at each stage, thereby improving maximum clock frequency (performance).
- Stage 1: Generate and register the partial products.
- Stage 2: Sum partial products pairwise in 4 additions, registered.
- Stage 3: Sum the 4 results into 2 sums, registered.
- Stage 4: Final addition of 2 sums, registered output product.
- Propagate the enable signal through pipeline registers to maintain valid data tracking.
- Keep the input registers capturing only when mul_en_in is asserted.
- Use non-blocking assignments properly and synchronous active-low reset.
- This deeper pipelining reduces the critical path width per stage, improving timing, and also reduces switching activity in any single cycle, benefiting power.
- Area increases slightly due to added registers but will improve max frequency and power-delay product overall.

This staged adder tree approach is a common pipelined multiplier technique that balances throughput and clock frequency while ensuring correctness and proper flow control.
