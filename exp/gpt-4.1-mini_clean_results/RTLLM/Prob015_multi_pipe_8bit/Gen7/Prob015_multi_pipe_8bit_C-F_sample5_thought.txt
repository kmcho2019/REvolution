Fuse the strengths of both examples by creating a well-balanced 3-stage pipeline that improves timing and power efficiency:

1. Input Stage:
   - Sample inputs and input enable signal into registers.
   - Use a 4-bit enable pipeline for precise tracking through pipeline stages, reducing glitch propagation.
   
2. Partial Product Generation Stage:
   - Generate 8 partial products by bitwise AND and shift of multiplicand with multiplier bits.
   - Store partial products in registers to break long combinational paths and reduce switching.
   
3. Partial Sum Calculation Stage:
   - Instead of summing all 8 partial products in a single large combinational block, pipeline the addition in two steps:
     a) Sum partial_products[0..3] into sum_lower
     b) Sum partial_products[4..7] into sum_upper
   - Then sum sum_lower and sum_upper in the final pipeline register.
   - This approach creates a balanced adder tree split across two pipeline registers, improving max frequency.
   
4. Output Stage:
   - Output the final sum registered in the last pipeline stage.
   - The output enable is derived from the MSB of the enable pipeline.
   - Output product is gated by the output enable, else zero.

This design reduces the critical path of the addition by splitting it over pipeline stages, lowers switching by registering partial products and sums, and maintains proper enable signal tracking through a 4-stage enable pipeline. The pipeline depth (4 cycles latency) strikes a good balance between throughput, latency, and timing.
