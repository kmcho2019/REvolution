Fuse the two examples to implement a three-stage pipelined unsigned 8-bit multiplier that balances timing and complexity. 
Stage 1: Register the inputs and input enable when mul_en_in is active.
Stage 2: Generate partial products combinationally using the registered inputs and compute a balanced adder tree to reduce combinational delay, then register the intermediate sums.
Stage 3: Accumulate final sums in a register and propagate the enable signal through a three-stage shift register to track valid data through the pipeline.
Output the product only when the output enable signal (MSB of enable pipeline) is high.

This fusion incorporates:
- The multi-cycle enable pipeline shift register from Example 2 for clean enable propagation.
- Input sampling gated by mul_en_in to avoid unnecessary toggling.
- Partial product generation as a vector of shifted multiplicand conditions from multiplier bits.
- Breaking the summation into a two-level adder tree (e.g., sum pairs first, then final sum) to reduce critical path, instead of a single large combinational adder.
- Registering intermediate sums to pipeline the addition and improve timing.
- The output product is gated by the output enable and reset properly.

This approach leverages the minimal pipeline stages and clean enable tracking of Example 2 but improves timing by pipelining the addition of partial products in two stages like a tree adder, avoiding the long critical path of summing eight terms at once.

The design is scalable, synthesizable, and power-aware since registers enable clock gating opportunities. It also improves maximum clock frequency while maintaining low latency (3 cycles) and straightforward control.
