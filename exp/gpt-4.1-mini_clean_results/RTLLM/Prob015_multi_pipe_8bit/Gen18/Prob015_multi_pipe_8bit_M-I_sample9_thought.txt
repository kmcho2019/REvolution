To optimize the design for better PPA (performance, power, and area), the addition of all eight partial products should be pipelined rather than computed in a single combinational stage, which causes a long critical path and reduces max clock frequency. We will split the addition into multiple pipeline stages by using a balanced adder tree structure over 3 pipeline stages after input registers. This reduces the combinational logic per cycle and improves timing. Each pipeline stage will register partial sums, thereby reducing glitching and dynamic power.

We will also pipeline the enable signal through a corresponding 5-stage shift register to match the total pipeline latency (1 input stage + 3 addition stages + 1 output register stage).

The pipeline stages will be:

- Stage 1: Input registers (mul_a_reg, mul_b_reg) and enable register
- Stage 2: Generate 8 partial products and sum pairs (4 sums)
- Stage 3: Sum the 4 partial sums to 2 sums
- Stage 4: Sum the final 2 partial sums to get the final product
- Stage 5: Register the output product and enable

This approach breaks down the large addition tree into multiple registered stages, improving max frequency, reducing power by registering intermediate sums, and potentially reducing area by balancing adder sizes each cycle.

All registers will reset on active-low reset. The output enable will be the MSB of the enable pipeline register.

Finally, the output product will be valid only when mul_en_out is high; otherwise zero.

This approach improves timing (shorter critical path per cycle), reduces dynamic power (registered intermediate sums reduce glitching), and may reduce area by distributing adders over multiple cycles.
