Design an 8-bit unsigned multiplier using a 5-stage pipeline focusing on a bit-serial partial product accumulation approach to reduce combinational complexity per stage.  
Key ideas:  
- Pipeline the enable signal through 5 stages to track data validity, mul_en_out derived from the final stage.  
- Inputs (mul_a, mul_b) are registered only when mul_en_in is asserted.  
- Instead of generating all partial products at once, generate one partial product per cycle (bit-serial multiplication of multiplier bits).  
- Each pipeline stage accumulates partial product for a single multiplier bit shifted accordingly and adds it to the sum passed along the pipeline.  
- The sum register is carried forward across stages, accumulating the product incrementally over 8 cycles total (with first 3 cycles for pipeline filling and last 2 for output validity alignment).  
- Pipeline stages:  
  1) Input register stage  
  2-5) Partial product generation and accumulation stages, each adding 2 multiplier bits’ contributions.  
- The multiplier bits are processed two bits per stage (except the last stage with fewer bits), reducing pipeline stages from 8 to 5 and balancing latency and throughput.  
- On each stage, the partial sum is registered and passed to the next pipeline stage for further accumulation.  
- Output product is the final partial sum once all multiplier bits have been processed.  
- This approach minimizes the combinational complexity per stage and eases addition logic, while still providing pipelined operation and throughput of one multiplication per cycle after latency.  
- Active-low asynchronous reset clears all pipeline registers.  
- Output zero is provided when mul_en_out is low, ensuring no invalid data is presented.  
- mul_en_out is delayed accordingly to reflect data validity after the full pipeline.

Overall, this bit-serial partial-product accumulation pipeline balances resource usage, timing, and clarity with a simple and scalable structure.
