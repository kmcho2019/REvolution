Implement an 8-bit unsigned multiplier using a balanced pipelined adder tree architecture. The design splits the partial product addition into multiple pipeline stages, each stage adding pairs of partial products or sums to reduce the critical path and balance the pipeline depth. This approach achieves high throughput by producing a new product every clock cycle after initial latency, while keeping the combinational delay low per stage. The enable signal is pipelined in parallel to maintain proper flow control. Input registers latch inputs only when enable is asserted. The pipeline consists of four stages:

1. Input registers and partial products generation.
2. First adder stage: sum pairs of partial products to reduce 8 inputs to 4 sums.
3. Second adder stage: sum pairs of the 4 sums to 2 sums.
4. Third adder stage: sum the final two sums to produce the 16-bit product.

At each clock cycle, partial sums are registered, and the enable signal is shifted along the pipeline stages to generate mul_en_out aligned with mul_out. When output enable is not asserted, mul_out outputs zero.

This method improves timing by limiting the size of combinational adders per stage and reducing critical path delay, and increases throughput using pipelining, producing results every cycle after pipeline fill.
