Combine the benefits of indexed bit selection from Example 1 and the rotation-based data preservation from Example 2 for power and area efficiency. Instead of maintaining a shift register that rotates or shifts every clock cycle (causing toggling), keep the input data stable and use the counter as an index to select the output bit directly from the input data `d`, which reduces toggling on the register outputs and avoids extra flip-flops for shifted data. This eliminates the need for a data register altogether, reducing area and power.

To meet the requirement of asserting `valid_out` only when outputting the MSB (start of each 4-bit serialization), assert `valid_out` when the counter is zero. The counter counts from 0 to 3 sequentially, cycling every 4 clock cycles. Reset synchronously for easier timing closure.

This design combines:
- Area & power reduction by avoiding shift register toggling and flip-flop data storage,
- Valid signal generation tied precisely to the first bit output,
- Simple and minimal control logic with a small counter and combinational bit selection,
- Synchronous reset for better timing predictability.

The output bit `dout` is always `d[3 - cnt]` to serialize from MSB to LSB, and `valid_out` is asserted only when `cnt == 0`.
