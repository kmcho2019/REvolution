I will implement the sequence detector using a binary-encoded Moore FSM with 3 bits to represent the states, which reduces area compared to one-hot encoding. The states will correspond to how many bits of the sequence "1001" have been matched so far.

The FSM states:
- IDLE (0): no bits matched yet.
- S1 (1): matched the first bit '1'.
- S2 (2): matched "10".
- S3 (3): matched "100".
- S4 (4): matched "1001" (final matched state).

Transitions will be based on the current state and data_in, and the output `sequence_detected` will be high only in the S4 state. Since it's a Moore FSM, the output is registered and depends solely on the state, avoiding combinational glitches on the output.

The reset is synchronous active low (reset_n), which simplifies timing.

Also, the FSM will be designed to support overlapping sequences, so after detecting the sequence it can immediately detect another starting bit if present.

This binary encoding reduces flip-flops from 5 to 3, reducing area and power, and the Moore output logic improves output stability.
