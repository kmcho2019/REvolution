To simplify the sequence detector, I will use a binary-encoded FSM with 3 bits instead of one-hot encoding to reduce the state register size from 5 bits to 3 bits. This reduces area and logic complexity.

The states represent the progress of matching bits of the sequence "1001":
- IDLE (0): no match yet
- S1 (1): matched '1'
- S2 (2): matched "10"
- S3 (3): matched "100"

Transitions are determined by input bit, and overlapping sequences are handled by transitioning to appropriate states.

Output `sequence_detected` is asserted synchronously when the sequence is fully matched (on entering S4 equivalent, represented by output condition).

The reset is synchronous and active low, clearing the state to IDLE.

This binary FSM design is straightforward, with fewer registers and simpler next-state logic, while fully supporting sequence detection functionality.
