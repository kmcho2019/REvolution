To optimize the sequence detector FSM for better power, performance, and area (PPA), I will change the state encoding from one-hot (5 flip-flops) to binary encoding (3 flip-flops) since we have only five states. This reduces the number of flip-flops and area significantly. 

Using binary encoding simplifies the state register and reduces switching activity, improving power consumption. I will also convert the asynchronous reset on output register to a synchronous reset to reduce critical path complexity, as asynchronous resets can add delay and routing congestion.

The next state logic and output logic will be expressed using binary-coded states with a case statement to maintain clarity. The sequence_detected output will be asserted high for one clock cycle immediately after the fourth bit '1' is detected. 

This approach balances clarity and efficiency, achieving a more compact and power-friendly design while preserving the correct detection behavior.
