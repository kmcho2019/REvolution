To detect the 4-bit sequence "1001", we can implement a Moore finite state machine (FSM) with five states, each representing the progress through the sequence. Unlike the Mealy FSM that outputs based on transitions, the Moore FSM asserts the output only in the final matched state. This approach provides a stable output signal synchronized to the clock.

The states are:
- IDLE: No bits matched yet.
- S1: Matched '1'
- S2: Matched '10'
- S3: Matched '100'
- S4: Matched full '1001' sequence, output asserted.

Transitions are made on the rising edge of the clock, and the output `sequence_detected` is asserted when in S4. To support overlapping sequences, after detecting the full sequence, the FSM transitions to an appropriate state according to the last input bit.

We use synchronous active-low reset to initialize the FSM to IDLE.

This design uses parameter-based state encoding and is completely synchronous, which is good for synthesis and timing.
