To create a superior instruction register module, I will fuse the strengths of both examples:

1. Maintain two separate 8-bit registers (`ins_p1` and `ins_p2`) as per the original problem statement. This keeps clarity and simplicity in separating instructions from different fetch sources, avoiding complex multiplexing in outputs.

2. Use an internal 1-bit flag `last_fetched_p2` to remember if the last updated instruction was from fetch=2'b10 (RAM/ROM source). This flag is smaller and simpler than a 2-bit selector, reducing area and power.

3. Use clock enable (`ce`) to update registers only when fetch is valid (01 or 10), reducing switching activity and improving power efficiency.

4. On reset (active low), clear both instruction registers and the selector flag.

5. Outputs `ins` and `ad1` are always derived from `ins_p1` (the instruction from fetch=01). Output `ad2` is either `ins_p2` when `last_fetched_p2` is set or zero otherwise, matching the requirement that `ad2` shows full 8-bit data from the second source.

This design has two dedicated instruction registers for each source, a minimal selector flag for source tracking, controlled updates with enable for power saving, and straightforward output assignments. It balances clarity, area, power, and timing effectively.
