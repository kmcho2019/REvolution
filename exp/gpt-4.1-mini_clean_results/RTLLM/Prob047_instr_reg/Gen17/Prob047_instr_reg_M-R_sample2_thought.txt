Refactoring this design, I will separate the instruction storage into two distinct registers, ins_p1 and ins_p2, as originally described, each holding instructions from the two fetch sources (01 and 10). 

On reset, both registers clear to zero. On each clock's rising edge, when fetch indicates source 01, ins_p1 captures the input data; when fetch is 10, ins_p2 captures data. Otherwise, they hold their previous values.

Outputs ins and ad1 come from ins_p1 (opcode and address fields), and ad2 is directly from ins_p2 (the full 8-bit data). This explicit separation aligns with the original problem, improves clarity, and simplifies output assignments without extra flags.

This also avoids additional state bits like `ins_p2_sel`, and leverages straightforward combinational assignments for outputs, resulting in easy-to-understand and maintainable code.

No clock enables are used here; instead, both registers update conditionally inside the clocked always block, which should be efficient for typical synthesis tools.
