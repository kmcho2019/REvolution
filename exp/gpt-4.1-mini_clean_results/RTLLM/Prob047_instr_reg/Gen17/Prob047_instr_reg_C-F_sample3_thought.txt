Fuse the strengths of both examples by implementing two separate 8-bit registers, ins_p1 and ins_p2, to explicitly store instructions from the two fetch sources, as originally specified. Use a 2-bit fetch input to determine which register to load on each clock cycle, with synchronous active-low reset initializing both to zero. This adheres strictly to the problem description, improving clarity, modularity, and potentially allowing synthesis tools to optimize paths independently, reducing critical path delay and power. Outputs ins and ad1 directly come from ins_p1, representing the instruction from source 1, while ad2 outputs the full 8 bits from ins_p2, representing the instruction from source 2. When no valid fetch is active, registers hold their values, and outputs derive accordingly. This design avoids the overhead of selector registers and multiplexing output logic by cleanly separating instruction storage and output decoding, blending clarity, simplicity, and efficient hardware use.
