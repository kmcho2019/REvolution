Implement a novel combinational 16-bit by 8-bit divider using a sequential-style iterative method fully unrolled in combinational logic without intermediate arrays. 

Instead of storing multiple remainder stages, keep a single remainder register updated at each iteration. At each step, shift the remainder left by 1 and bring in the next dividend bit. Then, compare this updated remainder with the divisor and conditionally subtract it if possible, setting the corresponding quotient bit.

To achieve this without multiple intermediate arrays, implement the division iteration explicitly in a for-loop where each iteration updates local variables representing the remainder and quotient bits. This reduces complexity by avoiding large arrays and makes logic clearer.

Two combinational always blocks are maintained as requested:
- The first block latches inputs.
- The second block implements the combinational division logic with a single remainder register updated iteratively inside the loop and constructing the quotient bits accordingly.

This approach reduces logic complexity, avoids large remainder arrays, and keeps the design easy to understand and synthesize, while meeting all requirements.

The remainder output extends the final remainder to 16 bits by zero padding the upper bits.
