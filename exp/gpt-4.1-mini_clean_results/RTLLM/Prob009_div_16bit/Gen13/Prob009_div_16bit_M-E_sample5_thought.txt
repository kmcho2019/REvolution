Implement a novel combinational divider by performing a hierarchical divide-and-conquer approach that splits the 16-bit dividend into two 8-bit halves. 

Concept:
- The divisor is 8-bit, so we divide the upper 8 bits of the dividend first to get a preliminary quotient and remainder.
- Then combine that remainder (8-bit) with the lower 8 bits of the dividend to form a new 16-bit value.
- Divide this combined value again by the divisor to get the lower 8 bits of the quotient and a final remainder.
- Concatenate the two 8-bit quotient parts to form the full 16-bit quotient, and zero-extend the remainder to 16 bits.
  
This two-step division using two 8-bit divisions reduces complexity and critical path by operating in smaller bit widths and avoids a long iterative shift-subtract loop.
It uses two always blocks as requested: the first to latch inputs, the second to implement the two-step division combinationally using simple arithmetic and comparison.

This approach aligns with the problem requirements:
- Uses combinational always blocks
- Uses explicit registers for intermediate results
- Extracts the top 8 bits, compares, subtracts, and continues iteratively (in two stages)
- Produces result and remainder outputs consistent with the problem description
- Uses no generate or iterative loops, just explicit arithmetic.

This design can be synthesizable and relatively fast since it uses only two 8-bit divisions.
