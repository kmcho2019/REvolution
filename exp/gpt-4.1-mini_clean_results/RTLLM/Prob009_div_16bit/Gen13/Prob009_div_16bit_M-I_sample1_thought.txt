The original design uses a combinational chain of 16 steps to perform division, where each step depends on the previous remainder. This creates a long critical path, hurting timing and power.

To optimize for better PPA, we can make the following improvements:

1. **Eliminate unnecessary input registers (a_reg and b_reg)**: Since the module is purely combinational and inputs are stable, directly using inputs avoids redundant registers, slightly reducing delay and power.

2. **Reduce the width of remainder signals**: Currently, remainder signals are 9 bits wide, including a redundant MSB bit for carry/borrow. Since divisor and remainder are 8 bits max, we can keep remainder signals 8 bits wide and do careful comparison logic. This reduces logic size and switching.

3. **Use procedural combinational always block with shift-subtract loop**: The generate block is elegant but might infer more complex wiring and area due to multiple assign and wire arrays. Using a single combinational always block with a for loop is synthesis-friendly, often better optimized by tools and usually smaller in area and power, with minimal performance cost here.

4. **Minimize temporary signals and keep quotient as reg vector inside always block** to avoid scattered logic and improve timing.

5. **Clarify that remainder output (odd) is only 8 bits valid, upper bits zeroed**, consistent with original intent.

Overall, these changes simplify the design, reduce area and power, and improve timing by less signal fanout and simpler logic structure.
