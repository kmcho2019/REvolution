To optimize the original combinational divider for better PPA (Power, Performance, Area), the main focus is to reduce combinational logic depth and resource usage:

1. Remove the first always block that just copies inputs into registers combinationally, as it does not help timing or structure; directly use inputs A and B in the division block.

2. Replace the array of remainder stages with a single remainder register updated in each iteration within the combinational always block. This avoids multiple 9-bit registers and reduces area and complexity.

3. Use a for-loop to iterate through dividend bits MSB-first, shift remainder, append current dividend bit, compare and subtract divisor if possible, and update quotient bit.

4. By removing the intermediate arrays and combining operations, synthesis can better optimize subtraction and muxes, potentially sharing logic and reducing area and power.

5. Keep the entire division combinational as required but keep code simpler and more synthesis-friendly.

This simplification leads to a smaller combinational logic cone, less redundant storage, and potentially faster critical paths due to fewer intermediate signals and less complex combinational interconnect.

The quotient is built from MSB down, remainder kept as an 8-bit value after all bits processed and zero-extended to 16 bits for output.

This approach meets the problem specification but is cleaner, more compact, and more optimized for synthesis.
