To implement the 16-bit by 8-bit division combinationally per the problem description, we will mimic the manual long division process bit-by-bit, starting with the highest bits of the dividend.

The key idea:

- Use two always blocks: the first captures inputs into registers for stable reference.
- The second performs the division by repeatedly extracting the top 8 bits of the current remainder combined with the next dividend bit, compare with divisor, set the quotient bit, and update the remainder.
- Initialize the remainder with the top 8 bits of the dividend (A[15:8]).
- For each of the 8 iterations (since divisor is 8 bits), do:
  - Extract the remainder (8 bits) and bring down the next bit of dividend (from lower 8 bits of A) to form a 9-bit value.
  - If this 9-bit value ≥ divisor, subtract divisor, set quotient bit to 1.
  - Otherwise, quotient bit is 0.
- After 8 iterations, quotient will be 16 bits (with upper 8 bits from comparison steps and lower 8 bits from the bits shifted in).
- The remainder is the final 8-bit value after all subtractions, extended to 16-bit output 'odd' by placing it in the lower 8 bits and zero-padding upper bits.
- Quotient bits are filled from MSB to LSB as the process iterates.

This approach accurately models the bit extraction, comparison, and concatenation described in the problem, all combinationally in two always blocks.
