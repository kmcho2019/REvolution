Implement a combinational 16-bit divider with an 8-bit divisor by performing a partial-restoration style division approach that processes 8 iterations instead of 16. 

Key ideas:
- Since the divisor is 8-bit, and the dividend is 16-bit, we can consider dividing the 16-bit dividend by the 8-bit divisor in 8 steps, each producing two bits of the quotient.
- In each step, extract the top (8-bit) bits from the remainder concatenated with the next two bits of the dividend.
- Compare this 8-bit segment with the divisor, set the two quotient bits accordingly via a small lookup of possible multiples (0,1,2,3).
- Use two always blocks combinationally: 
  - The first always block stores inputs into registers (a_reg, b_reg).
  - The second always block performs the division via unrolled loop of 8 iterations, each extracting 2 bits from the dividend and updating remainder and quotient bits accordingly.
- This architectural choice reduces iterations, balances complexity, and uses 2 quotient bits per iteration, keeping the division combinational but more efficient than bitwise 16-step long division.
- The remainder is always less than the divisor, stored in 8 bits, placed into the output 'odd' (remaining bits zero-extended).
- Quotient bits are assigned progressively with proper positioning.
