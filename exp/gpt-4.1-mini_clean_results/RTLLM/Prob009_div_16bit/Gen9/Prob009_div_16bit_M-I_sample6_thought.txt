The original solution implements a fully combinational restoring divider using two always blocks: the first latches the inputs, and the second performs a 16-step division loop. While functionally correct, the large combinational loop induces a long critical path and relatively high area/power due to repetitive subtraction and comparison.

Improvement Strategy:

1. Reduce combinational critical path by reorganizing the division steps into a single combinational always block without input latching registers (since no clock domain is specified). This eliminates the extra registers and allows synthesis to optimize better.

2. Use a non-restoring division style concept to reduce subtraction operations by conditionally adding or subtracting divisor depending on the remainder sign; however, given the original problem constraints, we stay with restoring but try to minimize logic duplication.

3. To reduce combinational logic complexity, perform the division starting from the MSB to LSB as before but keep the remainder to exactly 8 bits (since the divisor is 8 bits), eliminating the unnecessary 9th bit extension.

4. Replace the 9-bit remainder register with 8-bit remainder plus a carry bit (borrow bit) implicitly handled by the comparator and subtraction, thereby reducing bit width by 1.

5. Merge the two always blocks into one combinational block triggered by inputs, removing redundant registers.

6. Use intermediate signals (for quotient and remainder) declared as reg but assign outputs at the end, improving code clarity.

7. Use blocking assignments carefully to preserve combinational logic semantics.

8. Keep divisor unsigned and remainder unsigned; comparator logic remains intact.

9. Zero extend remainder to 16 bits at the output as before.

By combining input capture and division logic into a single always @(*) block and reducing remainder bit width to 8 bits, we reduce area and power with a shorter critical path while preserving functionality.

This approach respects the requirement for two always blocks by splitting:

- One always @(*) block that just captures inputs into local regs (for stable inputs in second block).

- One always @(*) block that performs division on latched inputs, as originally requested.

We optimize the remainder to 8 bits and remove unnecessary 9-bit extensions, and clean code style.

This reduces area and dynamic power by minimizing bit-width and logic, and may marginally reduce critical path.
