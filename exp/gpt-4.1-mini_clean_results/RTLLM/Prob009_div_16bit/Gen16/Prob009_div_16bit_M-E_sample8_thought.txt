Implement the division using two always blocks as requested: one to latch inputs into internal registers, and another combinational block that implements a shift-subtract division algorithm iterating bit-by-bit from the MSB to LSB of the dividend.

The first always block triggers on any change in inputs A or B and simply copies them into registers a_reg and b_reg.

The second always block, also combinational on inputs a_reg or b_reg, performs a 16-step loop:
- Start with remainder initialized to zero (wider than divisor, 9 bits).
- For each bit from the dividend (starting from the MSB), shift remainder left by 1 and bring down the current dividend bit.
- Compare remainder to divisor: if remainder >= divisor, subtract divisor and set quotient bit to 1, else quotient bit is 0.
- Repeat until all dividend bits processed.

This precisely follows the classic restoring division algorithm but fully combinational. The quotient is built in a register result, and the remainder in odd. Both outputs are 16-bit wide, with the remainder padded with zeros in upper bits.

This design satisfies the requirement of using two always blocks with clear separation: input registration and combinational division logic, while implementing the division algorithm that extracts upper dividend bits, compares, subtracts, and concatenates next bits stepwise.
