Implement a combinational 16-bit by 8-bit divider using a shift-subtract division approach, simulating the manual long division in combinational logic:

- Load inputs A (16-bit dividend) and B (8-bit divisor) into internal registers.
- Use a 24-bit register to hold the partial remainder and dividend combined:
  - Initialize it by placing the 16-bit dividend in the lower bits, and zero in upper bits (since divisor is 8-bit, partial remainder fits in 16 bits).
- Perform 16 iterations (one per bit of the dividend), each iteration:
  1. Shift the combined register left by 1 bit to bring down the next dividend bit into the remainder area.
  2. Compare the upper bits (remainder portion) with divisor:
     - If remainder >= divisor, subtract divisor and set current quotient bit to 1.
     - Else quotient bit is 0.
- After all iterations, the quotient register contains the full 16-bit quotient.
- The remainder register contains the final remainder, which fits in 8 bits.
- Use two always blocks: 
  - One to latch the inputs into internal registers (for clearer coding).
  - One combinational block to implement the division steps.
- Output the 16-bit quotient and zero-extended 16-bit remainder.
- This approach explicitly simulates the shift-subtract division algorithm with clear indexing, no out-of-bound accesses, and produces full 16-bit quotient and 8-bit remainder.
