Implement the division as a combinational iterative process that simulates long division bit-by-bit over the full 16-bit dividend and 8-bit divisor. 

Idea:
1. Use the first always block to latch input values A and B into stable internal registers a_reg and b_reg.

2. In the second always block, perform a combinational long division:

   - Initialize a 24-bit internal variable 'remainder_shift' with the 16-bit dividend in the most significant bits (shifted left by 8 bits). This extra space (8 bits) allows shifting in bits during division steps.
   
   - Initialize a 16-bit quotient register 'quotient_tmp' to zero.
   
   - The division loops 16 times (from i=15 down to 0), in each step:
     - Shift remainder_shift left by 1 bit.
     - Extract the upper 8 bits of remainder_shift as the current remainder segment.
     - Compare this remainder segment with the divisor b_reg.
       - If remainder >= b_reg, subtract b_reg from remainder segment.
       - Set the corresponding quotient bit i to 1 if subtraction occurs; else 0.
     - Update remainder_shift with the new remainder segment concatenated with the lower bits.
   
3. After all iterations, remainder_shift's upper 8 bits contain the remainder (odd), quotient_tmp contains the quotient (result).

4. Assign these to outputs.

This method fully implements the required division behavior:
- Extracts and compares the higher bits of the dividend segment matching the divisor width.
- Sets quotient bits accordingly.
- Updates remainder by concatenating with the next dividend bit (via shift).
- Repeats until all dividend bits are processed.

It also respects the problem's requirement of two combinational always blocks: one to latch inputs, one to perform division.

This approach is efficient and general for an 8-bit divisor dividing a 16-bit dividend combinationally.
