Simplify the combinational 16-bit division by performing a straightforward shift-and-compare process:

- Directly use inputs A and B in the division logic without registering them.

- Use a 16-bit remainder initialized to zero.

- For each of the 16 bits (from MSB to LSB) of A:

  1. Shift remainder left by 1 and bring down the current bit of A.

  2. Compare the entire remainder with divisor B.

  3. If remainder >= B, subtract B from remainder and set the current quotient bit to 1; else set it to 0.

- This removes the complexity of trying to extract upper bits or shift divisor.

- The quotient accumulates bit-by-bit, remainder is updated at each step.

- The division handles divisor zero by setting quotient and remainder to zero (avoid undefined behavior).

- Use two always blocks: one combinational block to latch inputs into internal regs (optional but per problem), and second combinational block to perform division logic.

This approach is simpler, cleaner, and aligns with the requested iterative long division method.
