Implement a combinational divider using a non-restoring division approach with a shift-subtract cycle unrolled for all 16 bits. The idea is:

- Use a 17-bit remainder register to accommodate shifts and subtraction results.
- Initialize remainder to zero.
- For each bit from the highest to the lowest in the dividend:
  - Shift the remainder left by 1, bringing down the current dividend bit.
  - Perform conditional subtraction: if the remainder's upper bits indicate the remainder is larger or equal to the divisor, subtract divisor and set quotient bit to 1; otherwise, quotient bit is 0.
- Unroll this logic using a generate or a loop inside an always_comb block by mimicking the sequential process combinationally.
- Store quotient bits as they are computed.
- After 16 iterations, output the quotient and remainder.
- This method differs by using a non-restoring style and a wider remainder to reduce logic complexity.
- Keep two always blocks: one to latch inputs combinationally (as requested) and one to do the division combinationally on latched inputs.
