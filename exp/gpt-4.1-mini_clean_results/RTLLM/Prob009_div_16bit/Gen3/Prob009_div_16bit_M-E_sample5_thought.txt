Implement a combinational 16-bit by 8-bit divider using a non-restoring division concept inspired by the problem statement:

- Instead of the classic long division, extract the top 8 bits of the dividend as the initial "partial dividend".
- For each step (total 9 steps), do:
  1. Compare the current partial dividend with the divisor:
     - If partial dividend >= divisor, subtract divisor, quotient bit = 1.
     - Else quotient bit = 0.
  2. Concatenate the subtraction result (or the original partial dividend if no subtraction) with the next highest remaining bit of the dividend, forming the new partial dividend.
- Since the divisor is 8 bits, and dividend is 16 bits, we can do 9 such iterations (initial 8 bits + 8 remaining bits, concatenated progressively).
- The quotient bits are formed from each comparison (one bit per iteration), and the remainder is the last partial dividend after all iterations.
- Store inputs into internal registers in one combinational always block.
- Perform the described division process in a second combinational always block, which calculates the quotient and remainder accordingly.
- This design matches the described method of extracting high bits, comparing, setting quotient, updating remainder, concatenating next dividend bit, and repeating.
- Zero-extend the final 8-bit remainder to 16-bit `odd` output.
- Quotient is 9 bits (for 9 steps), pad to 16 bits with zeros on LSB side for output consistency.
