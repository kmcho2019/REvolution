Refactor the pipeline multiplier by explicitly defining two pipeline stages:

- Stage 1: Register all partial products individually after combinational partial product generation.
- Stage 2: Register the sum of all partial products to get the final product.

The addition of partial products is done combinationally by summing all registered partial products together in stage 2 input logic, then the sum is registered in stage 2 registers.

This approach maintains the two-level register pipeline:

1. Registers at the outputs of partial product generation (stage 1 registers).
2. Registers at the sum output (stage 2 registers).

The output `mul_out` is directly registered from the stage 2 sum register.

This removes the extra addition stage from the original design (which had a three-stage pipeline) and replaces it with a two-stage pipeline with intermediate register sets.

Use non-blocking assignments in always blocks with asynchronous reset.

Use wires and assign statements for partial product generation, and only registers for pipelining.

This will improve timing, reduce latency to two clock cycles, and keep the design structurally simple and scalable.

Also, separate the pipeline registers clearly:

- stage1_pp: pipeline registers for partial products (array of registers).
- stage2_sum: pipeline register for sum of all partial products.

This matches the requirement of two pipeline levels and aligns with the original multiplication and extension requirements.

Finally, keep input zero-extension to `2*size` bits and perform shifts on the extended multiplicand.

This structure will help the user to clearly understand pipeline stages, timings, and facilitate easier verification.
