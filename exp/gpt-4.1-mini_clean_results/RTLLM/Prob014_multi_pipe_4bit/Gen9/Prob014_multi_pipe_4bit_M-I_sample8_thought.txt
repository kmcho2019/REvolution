The original design correctly implements a 2-stage pipeline multiplier with extended inputs and partial products generated from registered inputs. However, synthesis issues stem from the complexity of summing all partial products combinationally via a loop and handling large width left shifts inside generate blocks, which can create inefficient hardware or synthesis difficulties.

To improve:

1. **Reduce combinational complexity:**  
   Instead of summing all partial products in one combinational always block, perform the summation in a pipelined manner over the two pipeline stages:
   - Stage 1: Register extended multiplicand and multiplier.
   - Stage 2: Generate partial products for bits 0 and 1, sum them and register the partial sum.
   - Stage 3: Generate partial products for bits 2 and 3, sum them and add to the stage 2 sum, then register the final product.

   This breaks down the summation into smaller chunks, reducing combinational logic per stage and improving synthesizability.

2. **Pipeline depth:**  
   Though the problem requests two levels of registers, a small third stage register can be included to cleanly finish the multiplication pipeline with minimal logic per stage, or can alternatively be merged into two stages by carefully organizing the addition.

3. **Avoid wide left shift on registers:**  
   Instead of left-shifting the entire extended multiplicand, pre-shift the multiplicand extension during partial product generation and assign zero when the multiplier bit is zero. This can be done with wires and simple combinational logic.

4. **Explicit pipeline registers and latency:**  
   The output `mul_out` updates after 2 clock cycles latency, reflecting the two-stage pipeline.

5. **Avoid using arrays of wires and loops inside always blocks:**  
   Replace generate loops with individual explicit assignments since size=4 is fixed. This enhances clarity and synthesis friendliness.

6. **Parameterize properly:**  
   Keep parameter `size=4` but use explicit fixed-size wires and registers.

This leads to a fully pipelined multiplier with two pipeline register stages (stage1 and stage2), dividing the summation to smaller chunks to ease synthesis. The output `mul_out` is valid after two clock cycles latency.
