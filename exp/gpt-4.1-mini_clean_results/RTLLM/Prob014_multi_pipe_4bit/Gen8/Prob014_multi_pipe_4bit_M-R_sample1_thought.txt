The main issue with the original code lies in the summation of partial products in stage 2: using a for-loop with non-blocking assignments inside a clocked always block prevents correct accumulation within the same cycle. To fix this, I will refactor the design to:

- Generate all partial products combinationally in a generate block based on mul_b bits and shifted mul_a.
- Register these partial products in the first pipeline stage registers on clk.
- Sum all partial products combinationally using a parallel adder tree (or simple reduction) and register the final sum in the second pipeline stage registers on clk.
- Output the final registered sum as mul_out.

This approach cleanly separates combinational generation and addition from registered storage, ensuring correct pipelined operation and avoiding accumulation issues in sequential logic.

Key points of refactoring:
- Partial products generated combinationally as an array of wires.
- Stage 1 pipeline registers store partial products.
- Stage 2 pipeline register stores sum of stage 1 registers combinationally added.
- Inputs are zero-extended by concatenation of zeros at MSB to 2*size bits to align width.
- On reset (active low), all registers clear to zero.

This satisfies the requirement of two levels of registers, correct pipeline behavior, and stable timing. It also avoids non-synthesizable constructs and improper use of non-blocking assignments in loops for accumulation.

The pipeline latency is 2 cycles: one to register partial products, one to register sum and output product.

This approach improves synthesis results, clarity, and correctness.
