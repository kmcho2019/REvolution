I will implement a 4-bit unsigned multiplier using a 2-stage pipeline with explicit partial product generation and sequential accumulation, but with a novel approach:

1. Stage 1: Generate all partial products combinationally as usual by shifting the multiplicand conditioned on each multiplier bit.

2. Instead of summing partial products all at once, use a single accumulator register and an index register that counts through each bit of the multiplier over several clock cycles (one partial product added per cycle).

3. This means the multiplication takes `size` clock cycles but is deeply pipelined in terms of datapath simplicity, reducing area and complexity.

4. Stage 2: After all partial products are accumulated, output the final product register value.

5. The module will have two pipeline registers: 
   - one for holding the multiplicand extended,
   - one accumulator register for the sum of partial products.

6. Use a finite state machine controlled by a counter to synchronize partial product accumulation over multiple clock cycles.

7. All registers update synchronously on positive clock edge with active-low reset.

8. This architecture trades latency (multi-cycle multiply) for a simple, low-area, low-power pipeline design that still meets the requirement of two-level registers and sequential control.

9. Output `mul_out` updates after all bits are processed, held stable until next start of multiplication.

10. This design is novel compared to pure combinational or pairwise summation pipelines; it pipelines the multiply over multiple cycles while storing intermediate result in accumulator register.

This approach fulfills the problem requirements in a novel way and provides a clean, synthesize-friendly design.
