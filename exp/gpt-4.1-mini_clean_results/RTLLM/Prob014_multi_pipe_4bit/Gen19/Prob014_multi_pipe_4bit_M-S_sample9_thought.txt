To simplify the 4-bit pipelined multiplier with two levels of registers storing intermediate values, the design will:

1. Zero-extend the multiplicand (mul_a) to 2*size bits by padding higher bits with zeros.
2. Generate all partial products combinationally using bitwise shift and condition on mul_b bits.
3. In the first pipeline stage (first register level), register each partial product.
4. In the second pipeline stage (second register level), sum all registered partial products and register the intermediate sum.
5. In the third clock cycle (output register), register the final product sum to output.

This creates a simple 3-stage pipeline with two levels of intermediate registers:
- Level 1: Registers for each partial product.
- Level 2: Single register for sum of partial products.
- Level 3: Register output product.

The pipeline registers help meet the requirement for two levels of storing intermediate values and cleanly separate partial product generation, accumulation, and output.

Reset (active low) asynchronously clears all registers to zero.

This approach reduces complexity by using straightforward partial product storage and then a single sum register, instead of multiple adders or complex adder trees, while still pipelining the multiplication.

Latency is 3 cycles.

This solution maintains the required pipeline register levels, uses non-blocking assignments on posedge clk or negedge rst_n, and complies with problem specs.
