To improve the original pipeline multiplier design for better performance, power, and area (PPA), we adopt these strategies:

1. **Scalability & Code Conciseness:**
   - Replace explicit partial product wires and registers with a generate loop to scale easily with `size`.
   - Use an array of partial products and pipeline registers instead of individual wires.

2. **Adder Optimization (Balanced Addition Tree):**
   - Instead of summing partial products linearly, implement a balanced binary tree of adders:
     - First pipeline stage: register partial products.
     - Second pipeline stage: add pairs of partial products to reduce the number of operands.
     - Third pipeline stage: add final sums to get the product.
   - This reduces the critical path of additions per pipeline stage, improving maximum frequency.

3. **Pipeline Stage Count:**
   - To keep two register levels per spec, we do:
     - Stage 1: register partial products.
     - Stage 2: register sums of partial products pairs.
     - Stage 3 (combination + register): compute and register final product sum.
   - Since the spec requests two pipeline levels, we combine stage 2 and 3 additions in the final register stage.

4. **Power Optimization:**
   - Use clock enables for pipeline registers (optional, depends on external control).
   - Generate partial products only from registered multiplier bits, reducing toggling.

5. **Reset Handling:**
   - Use asynchronous active-low reset for all pipeline registers as requested.

The final design:
- Uses generate loops for partial products.
- Registers partial products at stage 1.
- Adds them pairwise combinationally.
- Registers sums at stage 2.
- Finally adds remaining sums combinationally and registers output product.
- Keeps two pipeline register stages, balancing complexity and latency.

This approach improves timing due to balanced additions, reduces combinational logic per stage, and scales easily for different sizes.

For `size=4`, the partial products are four 8-bit values:
- Stage 1: registers partial products (4 registers)
- Stage 2: registers sums of partial product pairs (2 registers)
- Final product output computed combinationally and registered at stage 2 output.

Thus 2 levels of registers as required.

Note: For larger `size`, extending this to more pipeline stages with carry-save addition would be better, but here 2 levels suffice.

We also explicitly declare parameters and signals for clarity and maintain asynchronous reset.
