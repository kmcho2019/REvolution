Refactor the pipeline multiplier by separating the generation of partial products and the summation into two distinct pipeline register stages explicitly, instead of summing all partial products combinationally first.

First stage registers each partial product separately; on the clock edge, partial products are captured into registers.

Second stage adds these registered partial products together and stores the sum into the output register.

This restructures the design to use an explicit pipeline of partial product registers followed by an addition stage register, emphasizing the pipeline nature.

Use `always` blocks for registering partial products and their summation. The partial products are generated combinationally and captured on clk edge; the sum of these registered partial products is computed combinationally and registered in the second stage.

The input multiplicand is still extended by `size` zeros at MSBs, and multiplier bits control whether to generate each shifted partial product or zero.

This approach maintains the two-level pipeline: stage 1 holds partial products, stage 2 holds their sum.

Advantages:
- Partial products are registered separately, clarifying pipeline stages.
- The adder at stage 2 works on registered inputs, reducing combinational delay after stage 1.
- Clear separation of multiplication into partial product generation/registering and summation/registering.

This implementation clarifies the pipeline stages and could ease timing and debug.
