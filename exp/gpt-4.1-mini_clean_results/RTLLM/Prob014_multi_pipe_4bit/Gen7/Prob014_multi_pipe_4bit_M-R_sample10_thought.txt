Refactor the 4-bit pipelined multiplier into a clear two-stage pipeline that accumulates partial products incrementally over cycles:

1. **Extend inputs**: Zero-extend both multiplicand and multiplier by adding `size` zero bits at MSB, forming 2*size bits width.

2. **Generate partial products combinationally**: For each bit `i` of the multiplier, the partial product is `(mul_b[i]) ? (ext_mul_a << i) : 0`. This stays combinational.

3. **Two-level pipeline registers**:
   - **Stage 1 registers** capture sums of partial products for bits [0:1] and [2:3] separately, from the *current* input cycle.
   - **Stage 2 registers** add the stage 1 sums from the *previous* cycle to produce the final sum output.
   
4. **Pipeline delay**: This approach yields a two-cycle latency: partial products combined in stage 1, sums combined in stage 2, and finally output.

5. **Separate pipeline registers from combinational logic**: Partial products computed combinationally; intermediate sums registered synchronously on clk.

6. **Use synchronous active-low reset**: Registers cleared on `rst_n == 0`.

7. **Output registered final product**: `mul_out` updated with final sum at stage 2 register.

This structure cleanly separates combinational partial product generation from pipelined accumulation, follows the problem's requirement for extending inputs, generating partial products, using two levels of registers to pipeline the multiplication, and registering the final output. The latency is explicit, and the code avoids combinational loops or timing hazards.
