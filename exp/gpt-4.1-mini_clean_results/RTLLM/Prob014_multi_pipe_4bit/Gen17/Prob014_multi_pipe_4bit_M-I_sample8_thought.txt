To improve the original 4-bit pipelined multiplier, we focus on better handling of the partial product summation stage to reduce combinational delay and improve PPA (performance, power, area):

1. **Two-level pipelining for summation:**  
   Instead of summing all partial products at once (which creates a large wide adder and long combinational path), we split the summation into two pipeline stages:
   - Stage 1: Sum pairs of partial products into intermediate registers.
   - Stage 2: Sum these intermediate results and output the final product.

2. **Parameterized summation via generate block:**  
   Use generate loops to sum partial products in pairs to handle scalable 'size' parameter cleanly.

3. **Efficient reset and synchronous logic:**  
   Keep all registers updated on posedge clk and async active-low reset as specified.

4. **Cleaner and scalable code:**  
   Avoid hardcoding summations beyond `size`. Support any `size` up to 8 (common range for 4-bit design).

5. **Maintaining zero extension and partial product generation as before:**  
   Multiplicand extended by `size` zeros at MSB to 2*size bits, partial products shifted accordingly.

This approach cuts the critical combinational path roughly in half by adding a pipeline stage, improving maximum clock frequency. It also keeps power reasonable by minimizing large combinational adders and maintains area efficiency.

The two pipeline register levels correspond to the problem statement's requirement: two levels of registers to store intermediate values and control multiplication.

Overall, this balances design complexity and PPA improvements while meeting all problem specifications.
