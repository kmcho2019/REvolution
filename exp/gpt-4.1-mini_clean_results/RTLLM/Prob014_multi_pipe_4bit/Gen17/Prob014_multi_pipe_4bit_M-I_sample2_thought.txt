The original design is functionally correct and meets the requirements of a 4-bit pipelined unsigned multiplier with two pipeline stages. However, the combinational summation of all partial products in one step can create a long critical path, potentially limiting maximum clock frequency and power efficiency.

Improvement strategy:

1. Break down the summation of partial products into two pipeline stages explicitly to reduce the combinational delay before the first pipeline register:
   - **Stage 1:** Register partial products.
   - **Stage 2:** Sum partial products using two adders, register intermediate sums.
   - **Stage 3:** Register final sum as output.

   But the problem states two levels of registers only, so we should keep to that and optimize within two registers.

2. Since we have two registers, we can sum partial products in two groups and then add them in the second stage:
   - Sum partial products [0,1] → reg1_part1
   - Sum partial products [2,3] → reg1_part2
   - Register reg1_part1 and reg1_part2 in first pipeline stage (two registers)
   - In second pipeline stage, add these two registered sums and register the result as final output.

3. This approach reduces the single large adder into two smaller adders plus one adder in the next stage, thus balancing combinational delay better.

4. Fully parameterized for size = 4.

5. Add comments and clean up the logic for clarity and easy modification.

6. Maintain asynchronous active-low reset.

This design improves maximum clock frequency by splitting summations and reducing the longest combinational path before registers while keeping only two pipeline register stages as required.

Note: For size=4, this two-step summation splitting is straightforward. For larger sizes, a more scalable reduction tree can be considered, but it is out of scope here.
