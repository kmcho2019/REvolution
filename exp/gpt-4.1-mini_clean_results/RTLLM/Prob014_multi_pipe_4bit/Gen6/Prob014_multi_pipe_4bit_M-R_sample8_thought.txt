Refactoring the 4-bit pipelined multiplier with two register stages for intermediate sums, while keeping the original concept of partial product generation via a generate block, but improving:

1. **Proper zero-extension**: Extend multiplicand `mul_a` by adding zeros at the MSB side (pad to 2*size bits) without shifting it. This allows correct partial product alignment when shifting later by bit index.

2. **Partial product generation**: Use generate block to create partial products as `(mul_b[i]) ? (ext_mul_a << i) : 0`, no extension or shifting of `mul_a` before that.

3. **Two-level pipeline registers**:
   - Stage 1 registers accumulate the sums of partial products for half of the multiplier bits (e.g., bits [0:1]).
   - Stage 2 registers accumulate sums for the other half (bits [2:3]) and add them to the Stage 1 sums from the previous cycle.
   
4. **Pipeline data synchronization**: Introduce separate registers for partial products and ensure sums are registered in subsequent clock cycles to avoid combinational loops and timing mismatches.

5. **Synchronous reset and clocking**: All registers update on the positive edge of clk or reset asynchronously.

6. **Use combinational assignments outside always blocks for partial product generation and summation, and use sequential always blocks for pipeline registers only**.

This approach strictly pipelines the partial sums, ensuring the output is valid after two clock cycles of latency, matching the two-level register pipeline concept, avoiding output capturing sums computed in the same cycle (which causes timing issues).

No redundant signal declarations. The pipeline latency is explicit, so output corresponds to inputs delayed by two cycles.

The input extension is zero-padded at MSB side, correctly matching the size to 2*size without shifting multiplicand prematurely.

This structure improves clarity, timing correctness, and functional correctness while following the original problem constraints.
