The original design attempts a pipelined 4-bit multiplier using two levels of registers but accumulates partial products sequentially over multiple clock cycles with an internal counter, leading to output timing mismatch and functional failures in a typical synchronous environment where new inputs arrive every clock cycle.

Improvement strategy:
1. Fully pipeline the multiplication in exactly two pipeline stages as requested:
   - Stage 1: Register the partial products.
   - Stage 2: Sum all partial products simultaneously and register the final output.

2. Remove the sequential accumulation approach (no internal counter needed), since the problem states only two register levels and pipeline multiplier.

3. Keep the input zero extension for both mul_a and mul_b for clarity, but use mul_b bits directly to generate partial products.

4. Use a generate block to produce each partial product combinationally, then register all partial products at stage 1.

5. In stage 2, combinationally add all partial products and register the sum to mul_out.

6. On reset, all registers including mul_out are zeroed.

7. This two-stage pipeline yields throughput of one multiplication per clock cycle with a latency of two cycles, which matches typical pipelined multiplier expectations.

8. Declare all loop variables properly, use nonblocking assignments inside always blocks.

This approach solves timing and synchronization issues, meets the specification exactly, and provides clean and efficient pipelined multiplier code.
