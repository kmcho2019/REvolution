The previous solution had pipeline stage synchronization issues and combinational logic dependencies within the same clock cycle, causing incorrect partial product accumulation.

To fix this, we will clearly define two pipeline stages for a 4-bit unsigned multiplier:

- **Stage 0 (input registration):** Register the extended multiplicand and multiplier inputs.

- **Stage 1 (partial product generation and storage):** Generate partial products from registered inputs, store them in pipeline registers.

- **Stage 2 (partial product accumulation):** Sum partial products in pairs using registers to produce intermediate sums.

- **Stage 3 (final output):** Sum intermediate sums and register the final product output.

This way, each stage's output is stable and registered before being used in the next stage, respecting pipeline principles and avoiding combinational loops or race conditions.

Pipeline stages summary:
- Clock cycle 1: Register inputs.
- Clock cycle 2: Generate and register partial products.
- Clock cycle 3: Sum partial products pairwise and register sums.
- Clock cycle 4: Sum final partial sums and register output.

This 4-stage pipeline ensures stable data flow and proper synchronization.

Implementation details:
- Extend multiplicand by adding zeros at MSB side.
- Use generate blocks to create partial products array at stage 1.
- Use registers for pipeline storage at each stage.
- Use synchronous reset.
- The output is registered at the final stage.
- The product latency is 4 clock cycles.

This approach meets the original requirement of two levels of registers for intermediate values and control, expanded slightly to ensure correctness and proper pipelining.
