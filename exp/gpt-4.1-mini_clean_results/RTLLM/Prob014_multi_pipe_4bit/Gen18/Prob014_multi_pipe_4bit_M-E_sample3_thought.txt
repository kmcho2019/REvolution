New architectural idea:

Implement a 4-bit unsigned multiplier with two pipeline stages by splitting the multiplication into partial sums of pairs of partial products, thereby balancing the addition tree into two pipeline stages.

Pipeline stage 1:
- Generate all partial products combinationally from mul_a and mul_b bits.
- Sum the partial products in pairs: (pp[0] + pp[1]) and (pp[2] + pp[3]) to get two intermediate sums.
- Register these two intermediate sums at stage 1 registers.

Pipeline stage 2:
- On next clock, add the two registered intermediate sums to get the final product.
- Register the final product to output.

This approach balances the addition in two pipeline stages, reducing the critical path in each stage compared to adding all four partial products at once.

Additional details:
- Use parameterized size=4.
- Extend multiplicand mul_a by padding zeros at MSB side to 2*size bits.
- Partial products generated by shifting extended multiplicand by i positions if mul_b[i] is 1, else zero.
- Registers reset asynchronously active-low by rst_n.
- Use non-blocking assignments in sequential logic.
- Output mul_out is registered after stage 2 addition.
- The two levels of registers:
  - Level 1: Registers for two partial sums (pp0_1_sum and pp2_3_sum).
  - Level 2: Register for final product (mul_out).
- Latency is 3 clock cycles from input to output (partial products → partial sums → final sum → output).

This design differs from example 1 by splitting the partial product summation into two halves and pipelining their addition separately.
