Improvement Strategy:

The existing design implements a 4-bit unsigned multiplier with two pipeline stages: the first pipeline stage registers all partial products; the second pipeline stage sums them combinationally and registers the final sum with a one-cycle output delay, totaling three-cycle latency.

While functionally correct and pipelined, the main bottleneck is the combinational addition of all four 16-bit partial products in a single cycle, which can increase critical path and power consumption.

To optimize for better performance, power, and area (PPA):

1. **Balanced adder tree in second pipeline stage:**  
   Instead of summing all four partial products directly, perform the addition as two pairs first, then sum their results, distributing the addition workload over multiple adders with balanced fan-in. This reduces the combinational delay.

2. **Two pipeline levels with registers:**
   - Stage 1: Register partial products.
   - Stage 2: Register sums of pairs of partial products.
   - Stage 3: Register final sum.
   
   This pipeline introduces a 3-stage pipeline with two intermediate registers for sums, which matches the idea of "two levels of registers to store intermediate values" and improves throughput by better balancing addition delays.

3. **Active-low asynchronous reset (`rst_n`) retained.**

4. **Parameterization maintained for flexibility.**

5. **Code clarity improved by explicitly naming pipeline registers for partial products and intermediate sums.**

Result:  
- Latency increases from 3 to 4 cycles (if counting from input), but clock frequency can be higher due to reduced combinational delays per cycle.  
- Power and area optimized by smaller adders and better register distribution.  
- The design strictly follows the requirement of two levels of registers for intermediate values controlling multiplication.

This approach implements a fully pipelined multiplier suitable for high-frequency applications.
