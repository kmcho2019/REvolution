The original code correctly implements a 4-bit pipelined multiplier with two pipeline stages: partial product generation and summation in stage 1 registers, then final summation in stage 2 registers.

However, to improve PPA and synthesis friendliness:
1. Avoid synthesis-unfriendly `always @*` loops summing arrays. Instead, unroll and use generate to create balanced adder tree summations combinationally, then register the final sums. This reduces combinational delay and improves timing.
2. Use only 2 pipeline register stages total: stage 1 for partial product pair sums, stage 2 for summing those pairs into the final product.
3. Implement balanced adder trees for summation to minimize critical path delay.
4. Use only Verilog-2001 constructs compatible with most synthesis tools — keep register arrays but avoid implicit large loops in combinational blocks.
5. Keep the interface and extension of inputs as before.

The improved implementation:
- Stage 0: partial product generation (no registers).
- Stage 1: pairwise summation of partial products, registered.
- Stage 2: final summation of these pairs via balanced tree (e.g. two levels of addition combinationally) registered on clk.
- Output register at stage 2.

For 4-bit size, partial products = 4:
- Stage 1: (pp0+pp1), (pp2+pp3) stored in registers (2 registers).
- Stage 2: sum of stage1_regs[0] + stage1_regs[1], stored in output register.

This reduces addition complexity at stage 2 and matches the problem's "two levels of registers" requirement.

This design should be synthesis-friendly, low latency, and optimized for timing and area.

Note: The design is fully parameterizable for any size, but here size=4 by default.
