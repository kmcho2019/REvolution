Refactor the design by explicitly pipeline-registering each partial product first, then summing the registered partial products in a second pipeline stage. Instead of summing partial products combinationally first, register each partial product independently at stage 1, allowing better timing and clearer data flow.

At the first clock stage, register each partial product derived from the multiplicand and corresponding multiplier bit. At the second clock stage, sum the registered partial products in pairs with registers. In the third clock stage, sum these partial sums and register the final product output.

This approach splits the pipeline into three stages with registers holding partial products, partial sums, and the final product. The combinational logic (multiplications and additions) is separated from the registers explicitly, and no combinational summation happens across multiple partial products in one cycle, which can improve timing.

Active-low asynchronous reset clears all pipeline registers and output.

The input extension and partial product generation remains combinational as continuous assignments.

This provides a clearer and more fine-grained pipeline structure, suitable for high-frequency operation.
