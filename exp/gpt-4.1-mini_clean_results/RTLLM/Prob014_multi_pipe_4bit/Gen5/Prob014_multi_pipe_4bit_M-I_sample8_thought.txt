To strictly follow the problem's requirement of two levels of registers and produce a 4-bit unsigned multiplier with pipelined operation:

- First, extend inputs by concatenating size zeros to MSB side of mul_a.

- Level 1 registers: Register extended multiplicand and multiplier inputs (stage0 registers).

- Level 2 registers: Register partial products generated from the registered inputs (stage1 registers).

- After this, on the next clock cycle, sum the partial products combinationally, and register the final sum as the output (mul_out).

This matches the problem statement:

1. Extend inputs (mul_a extended with zeros on MSB).

2. Use generate block to generate partial products from multiplier bits and shifted multiplicand.

3. Register these partial products (first level of registers).

4. On the next clock edge, sum the registered partial products and store the sum in a second-level register.

5. Output register stores the final product from that sum.

Thus, two levels of registers are used: one for partial products, one for the final product output.

Reset is handled asynchronously active-low, setting registers to 0.

This approach matches the problem statement, is simpler than the example solution with multiple pipeline stages, and avoids extra latency or complexity.

It is scalable, easy to understand, and meets the problem requirements.

The product latency will be two clock cycles:

- cycle 1: register extended inputs.

- cycle 2: register partial products.

- cycle 3: sum partial products and register product output.

(If desired, partial product summation and final product can be combined in one clock cycle to keep strictly two levels of registers for intermediate values.)

Here, to keep two levels of registers, we consider partial products as level 1 and the final product as level 2 output register.

This avoids combinational loops and ensures the multiplication process is pipelined.

No need for multiple sums and intermediate pairing since the partial products are directly summed in combinational logic between the two pipeline stages.
