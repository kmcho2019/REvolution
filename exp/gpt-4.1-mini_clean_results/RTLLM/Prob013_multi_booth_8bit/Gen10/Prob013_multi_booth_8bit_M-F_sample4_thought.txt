The original code correctly implements a radix-4 Booth multiplier but the variable left shift by (2*cycle_cnt) in the critical path results in a large barrel shifter that affects timing and area. To fix this, I will restructure the design to eliminate the wide variable shift.

Instead of shifting the partial product combinationally by a variable amount each cycle, I will use an accumulator register (`product`) and keep the partial products aligned by adding them to `product` directly, then shifting the product right (or left) each cycle accordingly.

Since the Booth algorithm processes 2 bits per cycle (radix-4), there are 4 cycles for 8-bit operands. We can implement a shift-add architecture where the product register shifts right 2 bits each cycle, and we add the partial product aligned to the least significant bits.

Specifically:

- Keep `product` as a 34-bit signed register to hold intermediate results (to avoid overflow).
- The multiplicand is sign-extended to 16 bits.
- At each cycle, decode the current 3 Booth bits, generate partial product (multiplicand * factor).
- Add partial product to the lower bits of `product` without any shifting.
- Shift `product` right by 2 bits for the next cycle.
- After 4 cycles (for 8-bit inputs), the product register holds the final result in its upper bits.
- Output the lower 16 bits of `product` as the final product.

This approach removes the costly variable shifter. The only shifting is a fixed 2-bit right shift of the product register per cycle, which is cheap in hardware.

The reset logic and ready signal remain as before, with proper initialization and flags.

This design improves timing and area while maintaining functionality and correctness.
