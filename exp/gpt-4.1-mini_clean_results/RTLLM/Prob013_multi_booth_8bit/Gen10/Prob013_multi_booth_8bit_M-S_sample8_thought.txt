To simplify the radix-4 Booth multiplier for 8-bit inputs while maintaining functionality, we will avoid complex FSM and signed registers inside always blocks. Instead, use synchronous reset only, sign-extend inputs once at reset, and perform iterative multiplication over 8 cycles since radix-4 processes 2 bits per cycle. 

At each cycle:
- Examine 3 bits of the multiplier (including a previous bit) to decide the operation (multiply by -2, -1, 0, +1, or +2 times multiplicand).
- Add the partial product accordingly (multiplicand shifted by 1 for *2).
- Shift the multiplier right by 2 bits (arithmetic shift to preserve sign).
- Accumulate result in a 17-bit product register to handle sign extension.
- After 4 cycles (since 8 bits / 2), multiplication completes and ready is asserted.

This approach reduces cycle count from 16 to 4, simplifies control logic, uses basic case decoding for booth recoding, and avoids large shifts of multiplicand each cycle. It remains synchronous and straightforward for synthesis.

The output is the lower 16 bits of the accumulated product. The design uses signed arithmetic and handles sign extension correctly.

This simplification maintains radix-4 Booth functionality with less complexity and faster completion.
