I will fuse the best ideas from both examples to create a clean, efficient, and well-structured 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier.

Fusion strategy:
- Use a simple FSM with three states (IDLE, RUN, DONE) from Example 1 for clear control and ready signaling.
- Use signed 9-bit inputs (with sign extension) for multiplicand and multiplier, and extend multiplier with one extra LSB bit for Booth recoding, as both examples do.
- Perform radix-4 Booth recoding using a combinational case statement for the current 3-bit window of multiplier (bits [2:0]) to generate a partial product.
- Accumulate the partial product into an 18-bit signed accumulator shifted by 2*step, just like Example 1 (which has a neater partial product handling).
- Shift multiplier arithmetic right by 2 bits each cycle.
- The step counter counts from 0 to 4 (five steps) since radix-4 multiplication of 8 bits requires 5 cycles.
- On reset, load inputs and initialize; on completion assert 'rdy' and output the lower 16 bits of the accumulator.
- Separate combinational Booth recoding logic from sequential logic to improve clarity and timing.
- Use non-blocking assignments inside the sequential block.
- Keep code compatible with Verilog-2001 (no SystemVerilog types).
- Maintain stable outputs and states after multiplication done until next reset.

This design benefits from:
- Clear separation of FSM states and combinational logic (good for synthesis and verification).
- Radix-4 Booth algorithm applied in 5 cycles (efficient).
- Sign extension handled properly for signed multiplication.
- Ready signaling indicating completion.
- Minimal state logic, efficient area and reasonable power.

Overall, the design merges the structured FSM control and clean combinational Booth partial product logic of Example 1 with the concise and cycle-reduced radix-4 handling from Example 2.
