To create a superior 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier, I will fuse the strengths of both example solutions:

1. From Example 2:
   - True Radix-4 Booth implementation processing 2 bits per cycle with a 3-bit window.
   - Only 5 cycles needed instead of 16, improving throughput and lowering switching activity.
   - Well-structured booth_op function for partial product generation.
   - Signed inputs and outputs with proper sign extension.

2. From Example 1:
   - Fully synchronous design with a clear FSM (IDLE and BUSY states) managing the operation and ready signaling.
   - Proper synchronous reset and clean state transitions.
   - Controlled register updates to prevent unnecessary toggling after completion.
   - Clean separation of states makes it easier to extend or integrate into larger systems.

Fusion Strategy:
- Implement the radix-4 Booth multiplier from Example 2 but inside a FSM with IDLE and BUSY states as in Example 1.
- On reset or idle, load inputs with sign extension, clear accumulator, counter, and ready.
- On BUSY, perform 5 steps of radix-4 Booth decoding:
  * Extract 3 bits from multiplier (including extra bit for Booth recoding).
  * Generate partial product using booth_op function.
  * Accumulate partial product shifted by 2*step.
  * Shift multiplier arithmetic right by 2 bits.
  * Increment step counter.
- When done (step == 5), set ready=1 and output the product.
- Hold ready until next reset or new start.
- Use synchronous reset only (no async reset).
- Avoid redundant assignments and toggling by updating registers only during BUSY.
- Ensure all registers are signed appropriately to avoid synthesis issues.
- Keep variable shifts minimal and fixed shifts by powers of two only.
- Maintain readability and synthesis friendliness.

This combined approach yields a radix-4 Booth multiplier that is:
- Faster (5 cycles instead of 16),
- Cleaner and easier to integrate,
- Lower power due to fewer cycles and gated operation,
- Easily testable with clear ready signal.
