Implement a true radix-4 Booth multiplier for 8-bit inputs, which encodes two multiplier bits per iteration to reduce the number of cycles from 8 (for two bits at a time) or 16 (if one bit at a time) down to 4 iterations. 

Key points:
- Use a 17-bit extended multiplier register by appending an extra zero LSB to enable 3-bit overlapping windows for radix-4 Booth encoding.
- At each cycle, consider 3 bits of the multiplier: bits [current_bit + 1 : current_bit - 1] to decide how to add/subtract multiples of the multiplicand.
- The multiple can be: 0, +1, -1, +2, or -2 times multiplicand.
- Multiplicand is sign-extended to 16 bits.
- Use a product register initialized to zero, which accumulates partial sums, shifted accordingly after each operation.
- The counter counts from 0 to 4 (since 8 bits / 2 bits per cycle = 4 iterations).
- The ready signal is set when all four iterations are completed.
- Shift the multiplicand left by 1 for the times 2 operation.
- The product register holds the intermediate partial product; at the end, its lower 16 bits are output.
- Use synchronous reset and clock.
- Keep all operations fully synchronous and use a state machine with states IDLE, RUNNING, DONE.

This approach performs true radix-4 Booth encoding, reduces cycle count to 4, and correctly handles signed multiplication with less overall latency and power than bit-serial methods.
