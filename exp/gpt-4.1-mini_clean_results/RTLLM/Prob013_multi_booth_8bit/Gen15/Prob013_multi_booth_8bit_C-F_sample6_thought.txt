I will create an 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier by combining the structured FSM control, clear combinational Booth partial product logic, and efficient sign extension from both examples. The design will:

- Use a 3-state FSM (IDLE, RUN, DONE) for clean control and ready signaling.
- On reset, load sign-extended multiplicand and multiplier (multiplier extended with a zero bit as Booth encoding LSB).
- Use a 5-step process (step counter from 0 to 4) to implement radix-4 Booth multiplication.
- In each RUN cycle:
  - Extract a 3-bit Booth window from the multiplier's least significant bits.
  - Compute the partial product combinationally according to Booth recoding (-2, -1, 0, +1, +2 times multiplicand).
  - Accumulate the partial product shifted left by 2 * step into an 18-bit accumulator.
  - Arithmetic right shift the multiplier by 2 bits for next step.
- On completion (step=4), output the lower 16 bits of accumulator and assert ready signal.
- Hold outputs and states stable after done until reset.
- Use non-blocking assignments, sign-extensions via concise replication, and keep combinational and sequential logic well separated.
- Avoid redundant or conflicting assignments and ensure clean coding style for clarity and synthesis friendliness.

This fusion merges the FSM-driven clarity and stable control from Example 2 with the concise Booth partial product generation and proper sign handling from Example 1.
