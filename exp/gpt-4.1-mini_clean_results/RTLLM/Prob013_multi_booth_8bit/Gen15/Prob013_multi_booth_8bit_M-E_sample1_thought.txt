Implement the Radix-4 Booth multiplier using a shift-add approach with a partial Booth encoding on the multiplier input. Instead of checking one bit at a time over 16 cycles, process two bits per cycle over 8 cycles, which reduces the number of cycles and exploits Radix-4 Booth encoding rules.

- Use a 5-bit counter (ctr) counting from 0 to 7 (total 8 cycles).
- On reset, sign-extend inputs to 17 bits (one extra bit for safe Radix-4 encoding).
- Create a concatenated multiplier input with one extra LSB bit zero to form groups of 3 bits for Radix-4 encoding.
- Each cycle, take 3 bits of the multiplier (2 bits processed + 1 overlapping bit for Booth encoding).
- Based on 3 bits, decide the partial product multiplication factor: -2, -1, 0, +1, +2 times multiplicand.
- Accumulate the shifted partial product accordingly into a 34-bit product register to handle max width.
- After 8 cycles, shift is done, extract the lower 16 bits as product output.
- The ready (rdy) signal is asserted when all cycles complete.
- Use synchronous reset and synchronous logic only.
- This approach reduces cycles to 8 (better throughput), uses fewer shifts, and handles Radix-4 Booth's signed digit encoding.
- The sign extension and partial product calculation are explicit.
- No FSM used; controlled directly with counter and reset logic.
- Product register wide enough to hold full result during accumulation and shifting.
