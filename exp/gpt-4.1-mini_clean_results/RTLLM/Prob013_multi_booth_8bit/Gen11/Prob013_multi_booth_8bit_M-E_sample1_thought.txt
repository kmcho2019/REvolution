To correctly implement an 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier with proper synchronous operation and ready signaling, I will design a sequential multiplier that processes two bits of the multiplier per clock cycle, thus completing multiplication in 4 cycles (since 8 bits / 2 bits per cycle = 4 steps).

Key design points:

- Inputs: a (multiplicand), b (multiplier), clk, reset.
- Outputs: p (16-bit product), rdy (ready signal).
- Registers: a 17-bit product register that holds the partial product and the multiplier bits (extra bit for Booth encoding).
- On reset:
  - Sign-extend inputs.
  - Initialize product register to {0, multiplier, 0} to form the initial Booth bits.
  - Initialize multiplicand and -multiplicand for addition/subtraction.
  - Set counter to zero, rdy to 0.
- On each clock cycle (if not done):
  - Extract 3 bits from product register for Booth encoding (current 2 bits plus one extra bit).
  - Depending on these 3 bits, add 0, +multiplicand, +2*multiplicand, -multiplicand, or -2*multiplicand shifted appropriately.
  - Arithmetic shift right the product register by 2 bits (to process next 2 multiplier bits).
  - Increment counter.
- After 4 cycles (processing all 8 bits in groups of 2), output product (lower 16 bits of product register).
- Assert rdy high to indicate multiplication done.

This approach follows the classical Radix-4 Booth algorithm with a 17-bit product register (16 bits product + 1 bit to assist with encoding), suitable for signed multiplication.

Using non-blocking assignments and synchronous reset with careful sign extension and arithmetic shifts ensures correct synthesis and stable operation.

The overall process will finish in 4 cycles after reset deassertion, toggling rdy to 1.

This design avoids inferred latches and unknown modules by fully defining all registers and signals at every clock edge.
