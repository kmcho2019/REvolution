To correctly implement an 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier according to the problem:

- Radix-4 Booth algorithm processes multiplier bits 2 at a time, plus an implicit previous bit (usually initialized to 0). For 8-bit multiplier, there are 4 cycles (8 bits / 2).
- On each cycle, form a 3-bit group: {multiplier[i+1], multiplier[i], previous_bit}, which determines the Booth encoding.
- The Booth encoding table translates the 3-bit group into an operation on the multiplicand shifted by appropriate multiples.
- The operations can be: 0, +M, +2M, -M, -2M, where M is multiplicand shifted appropriately.
- Implement the product as a 17-bit signed accumulator (to hold sign and shifted partial sums).
- Keep the multiplier register with an appended zero bit (previous bit) to form overlapping 3-bit groups.
- Use a 3-bit booth_code signal extracted from multiplier bits for each cycle.
- On each clock cycle, depending on booth_code:
    - Add, subtract, or do nothing with the multiplicand shifted accordingly.
    - Shift the product right by 2 bits to prepare for next group (since Radix-4 processes 2 bits at a time).
- Use a 3-bit counter (0 to 3) since 4 iterations suffice for 8 bits.
- On reset, sign-extend inputs, initialize product with multiplier and appended zero bit, clear counter and ready.
- When counter reaches 4, set ready signal.
- Use non-blocking assignments for all registers in clocked block to avoid latches.
- This design matches the typical Radix-4 Booth multiplier architecture.
