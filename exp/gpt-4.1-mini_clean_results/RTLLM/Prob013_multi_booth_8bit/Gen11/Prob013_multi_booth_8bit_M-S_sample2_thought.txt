To simplify the design while maintaining functionality, I will implement a straightforward radix-2 Booth multiplier instead of radix-4, following the originally requested process:

- Use two 16-bit registers: 
  - `multiplicand` initialized as sign-extended `a`
  - `multiplier` initialized as sign-extended `b`
- Use a 5-bit counter `ctr` from 0 to 15 to process each bit of the multiplier.
- On reset, initialize `multiplicand` and `multiplier` accordingly, reset product and counter.
- On each clock cycle (when reset is low and multiplication not done):
  - If the current multiplier bit `multiplier[ctr]` is 1, add multiplicand to product.
  - Left-shift multiplicand by 1 for next partial product.
  - Increment counter.
- After 16 cycles, multiplication is done and ready signal `rdy` is set.
- The output product `p` is the accumulator register holding the sum.

This design follows radix-2 Booth multiplication (simpler), avoids wide shifters, and matches the original problem statement. It is synchronous, uses sign-extended inputs, and produces a 16-bit product after 16 cycles.

This approach is simpler than radix-4, with clear control flow and minimal combinational logic, making it suitable for educational and practical use.
