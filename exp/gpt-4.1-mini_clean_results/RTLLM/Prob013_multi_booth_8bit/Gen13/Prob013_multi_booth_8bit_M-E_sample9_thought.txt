Implement a fully synchronous radix-4 Booth multiplier for 8-bit inputs that processes the multiplication in 4 cycles, each cycle handling 2 bits of the multiplier (radix-4 encoding). 

Key ideas:
- Use a 5-bit counter to track 4 iterations (0 to 3).
- Sign-extend inputs to 16 bits.
- Extract 3 bits (overlapping) from multiplier shifted appropriately: bits [2i+1 : 2i-1] for iteration i (with a zero bit appended at LSB for i=0).
- Decode the 3-bit pattern using radix-4 Booth encoding to determine the partial product multiplier (-2, -1, 0, 1, 2) times multiplicand.
- Multiply multiplicand by the decoded factor using shifts and negation (avoid complex multiplication).
- Accumulate the partial product shifted left by 2*i (since radix-4 processes 2 bits per iteration).
- After 4 cycles, output the final product and assert ready.
- Provide synchronous reset.
- All arithmetic done in signed 16-bit registers to handle sign properly.
- This approach greatly reduces cycles from 16 to 4 and directly implements radix-4 Booth encoding.

This design offers better performance with fewer cycles, explicit radix-4 logic, and clear synchronous FSM with a small state machine and ready signaling.
