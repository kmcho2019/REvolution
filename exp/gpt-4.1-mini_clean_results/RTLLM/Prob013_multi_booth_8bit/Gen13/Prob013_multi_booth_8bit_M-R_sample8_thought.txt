Refactor the original synchronous FSM-based multiplier into a two-process design separating control logic and datapath logic for clarity and modularity.

1. Use a state machine with three states: IDLE, RUN, and DONE:
   - IDLE: Wait for reset release to load inputs.
   - RUN: Perform multiplication cycles up to 16 iterations.
   - DONE: Set ready signal and hold the final product.

2. Control signals such as load, shift, add_enable, and ready are generated combinationally based on current state and counter.

3. The datapath registers (multiplier, multiplicand, product, counter) update in one always block triggered on clk, with synchronous reset.

4. By separating control logic (combinational) and datapath (sequential), the design improves readability and aligns with good RTL coding style.

5. Use signed registers explicitly and avoid any `$signed` casting in procedural code.

6. Output signals (product and ready) are registered and updated only at the end of multiplication.

This approach preserves original functionality with a clear FSM structure and explicit control signals, facilitating easier verification and possible future modifications.
