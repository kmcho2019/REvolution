The original solution implements a simple bit-serial multiplier processing one bit per cycle, although the problem requests an 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier.

To optimize and better match Radix-4 Booth multiplication:

- Use Radix-4 Booth encoding which examines two multiplier bits (plus one previous bit) at a time, halving iteration count from 16 to 8 cycles.
- Sign-extend inputs to 17 bits (to accommodate one extra bit for Booth encoding).
- Maintain a 17-bit register containing multiplier bits and an appended zero bit at LSB for Booth encoding.
- Each cycle extracts a 3-bit group from the multiplier for decoding to determine addition/subtraction/shift operations.
- Instead of shifting multiplicand each cycle, shift partial product (accumulator) accordingly.
- Use an explicit finite state machine: IDLE, RUNNING, DONE.
- On reset, load multiplicand and multiplier registers, clear product, set ready low.
- On start, iterate 8 times performing Radix-4 booth decoding and addition/subtraction.
- After 8 iterations, output the final product and set ready high.
- This reduces cycles by half, improving latency and power.
- Use synchronous reset, registered inputs.
- Carefully handle signed arithmetic by explicitly declaring registers as signed and sign-extending inputs.
- Avoid unnecessary shifting of multiplicand register every cycle.

This approach improves timing by reducing cycle count, lowers power due to fewer operations and toggling, and can reduce area slightly due to fewer iterations and smaller counter size.
