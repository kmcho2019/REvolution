I will implement an 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier using a sequential approach with an explicit 3-bit Booth recoding window that processes two multiplier bits per cycle (overlapping with an extra bit), effectively reducing the number of partial additions compared to bit-by-bit. 

Key ideas:
- Sign-extend inputs to 9 bits to simplify Booth recoding including the extra bit (b[-1]) initialized to 0.
- Use a 5-bit counter to track the number of recoding steps (maximum 5 steps for 8 bits in radix-4).
- In each cycle, extract 3 bits from multiplier (current and next bit plus the previous bit b[-1]) for Booth encoding.
- Calculate the partial product based on Booth code: multiples of the multiplicand (-2, -1, 0, 1, 2 times multiplicand).
- Shift partial product accordingly by 2*step index and accumulate into a signed 18-bit accumulator (to avoid overflow).
- Shift the multiplier right by 2 bits and update the previous bit for next step.
- After 5 cycles, output the lower 16 bits of the accumulator as the product and assert ready signal.
- Reset synchronously clears registers and readies module for next multiplication.

This approach is a proper radix-4 Booth multiplier, reduces the total number of cycles from 16 (bit by bit) to 5 (radix-4 steps), and properly handles signed multiplication with sign extension and accumulation.

The code uses only a synchronous process sensitive to clk and reset, no SystemVerilog features. It uses non-blocking assignments, explicit sign extension, and clean state control with a simple counter.

This will produce correct 16-bit signed multiplication of two 8-bit signed inputs in 5 clock cycles with ready signaling completion.
