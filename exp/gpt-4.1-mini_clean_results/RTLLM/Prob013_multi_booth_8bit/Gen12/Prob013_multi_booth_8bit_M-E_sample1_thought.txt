Implement a sequential 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier using a simple control and datapath approach without explicit FSM enums or SystemVerilog features. The design uses a 17-bit multiplicand and a 9-bit multiplier with an appended zero bit for radix-4 Booth encoding. 

The multiplication proceeds over 8 cycles, processing two bits of the multiplier per cycle. In each cycle, a 3-bit group (overlapping bits from multiplier register) determines the Booth encoding to select the partial multiple (0, ±1, ±2) of the multiplicand to add/subtract from an accumulator shifted accordingly.

The module keeps track of the cycle count via a 4-bit counter and updates the product accumulator at each clock. When the count reaches 8 (full multiplication), the ready signal is asserted, and the final 16-bit product is output.

To avoid complex indexing or negative index issues, the multiplier register is right-shifted by 2 bits each cycle, and the recoding bits are simply the least significant 3 bits of the multiplier register at that cycle. This greatly simplifies the hardware and indexing.

On reset, all registers are initialized including loading sign-extended multiplicand and multiplier inputs.

This approach meets the requirements, maintains synthesis-friendly Verilog (no SystemVerilog constructs), and uses only positive edge clocking with synchronous reset. The ready signal is asserted for one cycle when multiplication completes.

Inputs:
- a: multiplicand (8 bits)
- b: multiplier (8 bits)
Outputs:
- p: 16-bit signed product
- rdy: multiplication done signal

Internal registers:
- multiplicand: signed 17-bit
- multiplier: signed 9-bit (b plus appended 0 bit)
- product: signed 25-bit accumulator
- count: 4-bit cycle counter (counts from 0 to 7)

The main loop:
- Extract booth_bits = multiplier[2:0]
- Decode booth_bits to -2, -1, 0, +1, +2 factor
- Compute partial multiple = factor * multiplicand
- Accumulate partial multiple into product
- Right shift multiplier by 2 for next cycle
- Increment count
- After 8 cycles, output product[15:0], assert ready.

This design is clean, easy to simulate, and meets the spec.
