Implement a simplified sequential 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier that processes the multiplier in overlapping groups of 3 bits (including an implicit zero LSB), producing 2 bits of the product per cycle in 4 cycles total (since 8 bits → 4 Radix-4 groups). 

Key points:
- Use a signed 17-bit register for the partial product to accommodate sign extension and intermediate sums.
- Extract 3 bits (q_{i+1}, q_i, q_{i-1}) for each step and perform the Booth recoding to decide the multiplicand multiple to add/subtract.
- Shift the partial product arithmetic right by 2 bits each cycle to prepare for next group.
- Use a 3-bit counter to run 4 iterations.
- Reset initializes multiplicand and partial product, sign-extended from inputs.
- Ready signal asserted when all groups are processed, and product is assigned from the partial product register.
- Avoid complex FSM, just use a counter and simple state logic.
- Output product is the lower 16 bits of the partial product register after processing.
