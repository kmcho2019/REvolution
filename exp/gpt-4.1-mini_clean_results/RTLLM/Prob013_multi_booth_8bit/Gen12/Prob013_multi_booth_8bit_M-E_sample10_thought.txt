Implement a radix-4 Booth multiplier with a micro-coded iterative approach processing 2 bits of the multiplier per clock cycle to halve the number of cycles needed (from 16 to 8). 

Key points:
- Use radix-4 Booth encoding: look at 3 bits (current and previous bits) to decide the operation per iteration.
- Maintain a concatenated register holding the multiplier and one extra bit (initialized to 0) to form overlapping 3-bit groups.
- Sign-extend the multiplicand to 17 bits to simplify addition and subtraction (shifted left by 1).
- The partial product is stored in a 34-bit register, shifted right by 2 bits each cycle after addition/subtraction, effectively building the result from LSB upwards.
- On reset, initialize all registers and load inputs.
- Each cycle decodes 3 bits of the multiplier to generate multiplicand multiples: 0, +1x, +2x, -1x, -2x.
- Accumulate the partial sum accordingly.
- After 8 cycles, product is ready (upper 16 bits of partial product).
- Output ready signal when done.
- All synchronous logic triggered by clk, synchronous reset.
- Use a 4-state FSM: IDLE, RUNNING, DONE, and a wait state is optional.
- This architecture reduces cycles by half vs simple radix-2, and uses a partial product register shifted right to align partial sums properly.
