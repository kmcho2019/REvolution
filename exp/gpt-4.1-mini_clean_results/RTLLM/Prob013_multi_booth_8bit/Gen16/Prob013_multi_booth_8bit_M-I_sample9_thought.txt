To optimize the existing 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier for better power, performance, and area (PPA), while keeping the correctness and ready signaling intact, I will:

1. Simplify sign extension by using a consistent signed width (17 bits) for multiplicand and accumulator, avoiding multiple different-length sign-extensions in booth_op.

2. Replace the function booth_op with a combinational always_comb block using a small LUT style approach to reduce complex sign-extension and negations spread across multiple places.

3. Use non-blocking assignments consistently and local signals to reduce unnecessary register toggling.

4. Reduce the accumulator width from 18 to 17 bits, as maximum product is 16 bits and partial sums fit within 17 bits in Radix-4 approach (the highest product is within 16 bits, and accumulator holds partial sums with sign; so 17 bits signed is sufficient).

5. Avoid shifting the multiplier register every cycle by instead using a 3-bit sliding window index and reading the multiplier bits with adjusted indexing, reducing switching in the multiplier register.

6. Store the multiplier in a register (17 bits) with two appended zero bits at LSB for easy 3-bit windows extraction; no arithmetic shift of multiplier, only the window pointer moves.

7. Accumulate partial products accordingly by shifting partial product by 2*step.

8. Keep FSM with IDLE and BUSY states for clarity, load inputs only once at start, and hold output ready until reset.

This approach reduces switching activity in multiplier and accumulator registers, simplifies combinational logic in booth partial product generation, and reduces the width of data paths, likely improving timing and lowering area and power. It retains a 5-cycle multiplication with the radix-4 Booth algorithm.
