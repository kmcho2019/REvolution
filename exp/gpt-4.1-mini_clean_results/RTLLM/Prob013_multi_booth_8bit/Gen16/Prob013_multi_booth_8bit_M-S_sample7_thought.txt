Implement a simplified 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier that uses a single 16-bit product register combining multiplier and accumulator to reduce register width and complexity. Extend inputs to 9 bits with sign extension for Booth recoding. Perform multiplication over 8 cycles, each processing 2 bits of the multiplier (since 8 bits / 2 = 4 cycles), plus one initial cycle to load inputs and reset internal states.

For each cycle:
- Extract the current 3-bit Booth recoding window from the product (which holds multiplier bits plus one extra LSB bit).
- Determine the partial product using a simple combinational case statement for Booth encoding.
- Add or subtract the appropriately shifted multiplicand (sign extended to 16 bits) to the upper half of the product register.
- Arithmetic right shift the entire product register by 2 bits to process next bits.
- Increment a 4-bit counter tracking cycles.

After 8 cycles (or counter reaching 8), set ready (rdy) high and output the lower 16 bits of the product register as the final product.

This approach reduces register width from 18 to 16 bits, removes a separate accumulator, and eliminates complex signed 18-bit operations by carefully using 16-bit arithmetic and sign extension. The combinational Booth decode logic is simplified to minimal case statements without separate functions. The design uses synchronous reset and a simple counter. Ready signal is asserted when multiplication finishes. This simplification maintains correctness while lowering complexity and resource usage.
