I will refactor the original FSM-based Radix-4 Booth multiplier to use a simpler cycle-count controlled iterative approach without explicit FSM states, replacing the state machine with a single counter and control signals. The idea is to:

- Remove the explicit three-state FSM and replace it with a busy signal and a 5-step counter to control the operation.
- Use a synchronous process to update registers on clock edges, but have combinational logic for Booth partial product calculation outside of sequential blocks.
- Use a registered start signal triggered by the reset falling edge to initiate multiplication, making reset only initialize registers.
- Keep multiplicand and multiplier sign-extended similarly, but store them as signed variables consistently.
- Shift the multiplier arithmetically by 2 bits every cycle by concatenation rather than variable shifts.
- Accumulate partial products into a signed accumulator.
- Assert ready (rdy) when the counter reaches 5 (i.e., 5 Radix-4 Booth cycles for 8-bit inputs).
- Hold outputs stable after completion.
- Avoid variable shift amounts in partial products by pre-shifting multiplicand inside the booth_op function.

This structure reduces FSM complexity by using the counter and a busy flag, making the design easier to modify and potentially synthesize with simpler control logic. It also clarifies the ready and start control signals, making the interface cleaner.

The core Booth operation and sign extension remains the same to ensure correctness.
