Implement a radix-4 Booth multiplier by processing 2 multiplier bits per cycle over 4 cycles for 8-bit inputs. Use an FSM with states: IDLE (waiting for start), LOAD (loading sign-extended inputs and initializing registers), MULT (perform radix-4 Booth steps), and DONE (signal ready). 

In each cycle of MULT:
- Examine three bits from the multiplier register (current bits plus one extra bit as the Booth radix-4 encoding uses overlapping groups of 3 bits).
- Based on Booth's radix-4 recoding, determine the partial product multiplier (-2, -1, 0, 1, 2).
- Add or subtract shifted multiplicand multiples accordingly from an accumulator (product register).
- Shift the multiplier register right by 2 bits and increment a 2-bit cycle counter.

At the end of 4 iterations (processing 8 bits two at a time), the product register holds the final product. Use signed arithmetic and proper sign extension. Output product and raise ready when done.

This approach reduces cycles from 16 to 4, processing two bits per cycle as required by radix-4 Booth multiplication. The overlapping 3-bit group is formed by extending multiplier with an extra zero LSB bit (multiplier_shifted). Registers hold 17 bits for multiplier (to include the extra bit), 16 bits for product, and 16 bits for multiplicand. The design uses only add/sub and shifts per cycle, with a clean FSM controlling the operation and registered outputs.
