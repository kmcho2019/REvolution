Improve the original solution by fully implementing the Radix-4 Booth algorithm to halve the number of cycles (from 16 to 4) needed for an 8-bit multiplier. This reduces runtime and switching activity, enhancing performance and power efficiency.

Key improvements:
- Process 2 bits of the multiplier per cycle, extracting 3 bits for Booth encoding (current bit, previous bit, and next bit).
- Use a pre-decoded signed partial product (from -2*multiplicand, -1*multiplicand, 0, +1*multiplicand, +2*multiplicand) based on the Booth encoding.
- Accumulate the partial product shifted accordingly into a 34-bit accumulator to avoid overflow.
- Shift the multiplier right by 2 bits each cycle.
- Use a 3-bit counter counting 0 to 4 (for 8 bits processed 2 bits at a time).
- Provide a ready signal after 4 cycles.
- Use sign extension properly in multiplicand and multiplier loads.
- Output the final 16-bit product at the end.
- Use a clean FSM: IDLE (wait for reset release), LOAD (load inputs), RUN (4 cycles of multiply), DONE (ready signal asserted).

This approach significantly cuts the iterations and power consumption, and simplifies the critical path by handling partial products instead of shifting multiplicand every cycle. It also properly matches the radix-4 Booth algorithm as requested.

The product register is wider (34 bits) to accumulate shifted partial products safely before truncating to 16 bits output. The multiplier is also extended with one extra bit (multiplier_ext) to facilitate booth encoding.

This optimized design improves timing and power while preserving correctness.
