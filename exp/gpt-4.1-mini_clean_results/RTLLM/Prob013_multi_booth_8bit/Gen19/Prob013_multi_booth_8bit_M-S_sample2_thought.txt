I will simplify the Radix-4 Booth multiplier by implementing a straightforward iterative approach without an explicit FSM or step counting to keep it minimal and clear. The multiplier and multiplicand will be sign-extended once at reset. The process iterates through all Booth steps (8 iterations for 8-bit inputs in radix-4) inside the sequential always block, performing shifts and additions conditionally based on the current Booth encoding extracted from multiplier bits.

Key simplifications:
- Use a single always block triggered by clk or reset.
- Use 17-bit registers: multiplier extended by one bit for Booth recoding, multiplicand sign-extended.
- The product register p will accumulate partial sums.
- A 4-bit counter iterates from 0 to 8 (since radix-4 handles 2 bits per iteration, total 8 bits = 4 iterations).
- At reset, initialize registers and rdy=0.
- During operation, at each clock cycle, process one Booth radix-4 digit, update product, shift multiplier by 2 bits.
- When done, rdy is set to 1.
- This approach avoids FSM and extra 18-bit wide accumulator; uses a 16-bit product register and extended 17-bit operands.
- Booth recoding is implemented with combinational logic inside the sequential block for simplicity.

This keeps the code smaller and easier to understand while maintaining correct functionality and timing.
