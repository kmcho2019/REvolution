To simplify the 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier, I will avoid the FSM and use a straightforward shift-and-add approach driven by a counter. The multiplier and multiplicand will be sign-extended to 9 bits to handle sign properly. The multiplier register will be extended by one extra bit (initialized to 0) for Booth encoding. At each clock cycle (when not reset), the module will examine the lowest 3 bits of the multiplier register to decide the partial product to add or subtract from an accumulator. Then, the accumulator and multiplier register will be arithmetically right-shifted by 2 bits to process the next Booth group. The process continues until 4 steps are done (covering 8 bits since radix-4 processes 2 bits per step). After the last step, the ready signal is asserted and the lower 16 bits of the accumulator are output as the product.

This approach removes the FSM and multiple states, making the code more concise and easier to understand while still implementing radix-4 Booth multiplication correctly.

Key points:
- Use a 5-bit counter to track 4 steps (each step processes 2 bits).
- Sign-extend inputs for proper signed arithmetic.
- Use arithmetic right shift of multiplier register by 2 after each step.
- Compute partial product based on 3-bit Booth code combinationally.
- Accumulate partial products each cycle.
- Output product and assert ready after completion.
- Reset initializes registers and starts operation.
