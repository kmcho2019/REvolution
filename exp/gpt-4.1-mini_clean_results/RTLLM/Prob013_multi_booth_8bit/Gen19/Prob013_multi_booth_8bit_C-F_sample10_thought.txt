To implement a high-quality 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier, I will combine the advantages of the two examples:

1. Radix-4 Booth algorithm (processing 2 bits per cycle) drastically reduces the number of cycles from 16 to 5, improving throughput and lowering switching activity (from Example 2).

2. A clear FSM with three states (IDLE, LOAD, CALC) and fully synchronous reset for clean, robust control, easy integration, and maintainability (from Example 1).

3. Maintain separate registers for multiplier, multiplicand, product (accumulator), and counter with proper sign extension.

4. Implement the booth partial product generation as a function with careful sign extension and common sub-expressions minimized.

5. In the CALC state, perform:
   - Extract a 3-bit window from multiplier bits (including the extra appended bit).
   - Generate the partial product using the Booth function.
   - Shift the partial product by 2*step.
   - Accumulate into the product.
   - Arithmetic shift the multiplier right by 2 bits.
   - Increment step.

6. When the step counter reaches 5, output the product and set rdy.

7. Use only synchronous resets and nonblocking assignments in sequential blocks.

8. The ready signal asserted only after calculation is complete.

This fusion yields a design with:
- Improved speed due to radix-4 decoding (5 cycles).
- Clear and robust FSM control and handshake.
- Reduced switching activity and power consumption.
- Easier debugging and testability.
- Clean separation of combinational and sequential logic, improving synthesis results.
