To optimize the original FSM-based Radix-4 Booth multiplier design for better power, performance, and area (PPA), the following improvements are applied:

1. **Combine multiplier and product registers:** Instead of separate product and multiplier registers, maintain a single 17-bit accumulator register which holds both the partial product and multiplier bits. This approach is a classic Booth multiplier style which concatenates product and multiplier for shifting.

2. **Use 3-bit Booth recoding:** Radix-4 Booth encoding operates by examining groups of 3 bits (including one overlapping bit) at a time to decide whether to add, subtract or skip multiplicand multiples. This reduces iterations roughly by half compared to bit-by-bit.

3. **Reduce cycle count to 4 (for 8-bit inputs):** Radix-4 processes 2 multiplier bits per cycle, so with 8-bit multiplier we only need 4 cycles.

4. **Synchronous reset only:** Simplify to synchronous reset to ease timing and synthesis.

5. **Minimize combinational path in critical cycle:** Move addition/subtraction of multiplicand multiples inside sequential logic to avoid large combinational paths.

6. **Control when shifting and addition occurs:** Perform shifts only after addition/subtraction to reduce switching.

7. **Use signed arithmetic carefully:** Keep multiplicand sign-extended, use signed addition/subtraction.

8. **Update ready (rdy) only when done:** Reset rdy upon starting multiply, set after 4 cycles.

This approach reduces power by halving iterations, cutting the addition operations, and minimizing switching on registers. Also reduces critical path by moving arithmetic into registers. This design uses a minimal FSM with states IDLE, LOAD, CALC. It also makes the code cleaner and closer to classical Radix-4 Booth multiplier structure.
