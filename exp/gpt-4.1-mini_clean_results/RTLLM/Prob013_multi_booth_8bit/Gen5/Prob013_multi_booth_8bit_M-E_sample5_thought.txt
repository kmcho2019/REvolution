Implement the 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier by iteratively examining pairs of bits from the multiplier with an appended zero bit, decoding the required partial product addition/subtraction. Maintain three registers: a signed 17-bit accumulator initialized to zero to hold the intermediate sum, a 9-bit extended multiplier register (including appended zero) for scanning bits, and the 16-bit sign-extended multiplicand. At each clock cycle (up to 4 cycles), based on the current 3 bits of the multiplier, add or subtract multiplicand shifted by multiples of two bits to the accumulator, then arithmetic right shift the concatenated accumulator and multiplier registers by two bits to prepare for the next cycle. Use a 3-bit counter to track cycles. When complete, output the lower 16 bits of the accumulator as the product and assert ready. This approach keeps shifts limited to arithmetic right shifts on the large concatenated register and small constant left shifts on the multiplicand, minimizing variable shifts and intermediate large shift logic.
