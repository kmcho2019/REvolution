Implement a true Radix-4 Booth multiplier for 8-bit inputs using a finite state machine (FSM) approach:

- Inputs 'a' (multiplicand) and 'b' (multiplier) are sign-extended to 9 bits for handling radix-4 booth recoding with overlapping groups of 3 bits.
- The multiplier is extended with one extra zero bit at LSB to facilitate the overlapping groups extraction.
- Each Radix-4 Booth iteration processes 2 bits of the multiplier at a time, total 4 iterations (for 8 bits).
- We generate a 3-bit Booth code from multiplier bits [2*i+1 : 2*i-1], which determines the operation:
  0: 0
  1: +1 * multiplicand
  2: +2 * multiplicand
  3: +1 * multiplicand (for code 3 which means -1)
  4: 0 (no operation)
  5: -1 * multiplicand
  6: -2 * multiplicand
  7: -1 * multiplicand
- The partial product is computed by multiplying the multiplicand by {0, ±1, ±2} accordingly.
- The partial product is shifted left by 2*i bits (because each group covers 2 multiplier bits).
- Accumulate the partial products to a 18-bit signed accumulator to avoid overflow (since maximum product of two 9-bit signed numbers can be up to 18 bits).
- Use a 3-bit counter to count the 4 steps of radix-4 processing.
- On reset: initialize the multiplicand, multiplier and accumulator, reset counter and ready.
- On each clock cycle: perform one radix-4 partial product calculation and accumulate.
- After all 4 iterations, set output product 'p' to lower 16 bits of the accumulator and raise 'rdy' signal.
- FSM is fully synchronous with no combinational variable shifts or latches.
- This architecture matches the Radix-4 Booth multiplier definition, processes in 4 clock cycles, and efficiently uses hardware shifts and adds.

This design fully satisfies the problem requirements and improves performance and synthesis friendliness compared to bit-wise approaches.
