Implement a sequential Radix-4 Booth multiplier that processes 2 bits of the multiplier per clock cycle, performing a total of 4 cycles to cover 8 bits (since each cycle handles 2 multiplier bits). 

On reset, sign-extend inputs `a` and `b` to 16 bits and 9 bits respectively (`b` with an appended zero bit for Booth encoding). Initialize the accumulator to zero and the cycle counter to zero. 

At each clock cycle (when not reset), extract 3 bits from the multiplier register (the two least significant bits and a trailing zero bit to form the Booth encoding triplet), decode the Booth code to determine the factor to multiply with the multiplicand: -2, -1, 0, +1, +2. 

Add or subtract the multiplicand (shifted appropriately by 2*cycle) multiplied by the Booth factor to the accumulator. Shift the multiplier register right by 2 bits to process the next two bits in the next cycle. Increment cycle counter.

After 4 cycles, set ready to 1 and output the lower 16 bits of the accumulator as the product.

This design uses a 5-bit counter as required (though only lower 3 bits used), performs sign extension as specified, and handles Radix-4 Booth multiplication properly and sequentially over 4 cycles. The pipeline keeps the datapath simple, with multiplication by 2 handled by shift operations.

This approach fulfills the requirements cleanly and efficiently.
