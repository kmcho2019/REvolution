To improve the original iterative bit-serial multiplier, we will implement a true Radix-4 Booth multiplier for 8-bit inputs, which processes two bits of the multiplier per cycle instead of one. This reduces the number of cycles from 16 down to 4, which greatly improves performance by reducing the iteration count and associated power consumption. The key points of this approach are:

1. Radix-4 Booth encoding examines overlapping groups of 3 bits of the multiplier (including an appended zero bit at LSB), generating a multiplier digit between -2 and +2.

2. At each cycle, based on the Booth encoding, we add or subtract multiplicand or multiplicand×2 shifted accordingly to the partial product.

3. The multiplier is sign-extended and shifted by 2 bits every cycle.

4. The module works synchronously with a clock and synchronous reset only.

5. We keep signed 17-bit partial product register to accommodate potential overflows from double multiplicand additions.

6. Ready signal is asserted when multiplication completes after 4 cycles.

7. The multiplicand and multiplier are loaded and sign-extended at reset.

8. Using an FSM with states IDLE and BUSY controls operation cleanly.

This approach achieves better throughput and power efficiency by reducing cycle count and add operations, and shifting the multiplier 2 bits at a time reduces switching. It also implements radix-4 Booth algorithm as requested.

The complexity is slightly higher in the combinational Booth encoding, but it is efficient and suitable for synthesis.

The outputs are registered and updated synchronously.

This approach represents a clear improvement in PPA over the original 16-cycle, bit-by-bit approach while meeting all problem constraints.
