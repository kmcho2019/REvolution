To implement a superior 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier combining the best ideas from both examples:

- Use a synchronous reset only on clk posedge for synthesis friendliness and cleaner timing.
- Accurately implement Radix-4 Booth algorithm by processing two bits of the multiplier per cycle (except the initial appended bit), reducing the number of cycles from 16 to 8.
- Store the multiplier with an appended zero LSB to simplify Booth recoding (multiplier = {b, 0}).
- Use a signed 17-bit multiplier register (to hold 8 bits of multiplier plus an appended 0 bit plus sign extension) and signed 17-bit multiplicand and product registers to properly accommodate sign extension and shifting.
- Implement a 4-bit counter for 8 iterations of Radix-4 Booth encoding.
- Use Booth recoding logic on 3 bits per cycle: {multiplier[2*i+1], multiplier[2*i], multiplier[2*i-1]} (for i from 0 to 7; for i=0, multiplier[-1] = 0).
- Determine partial product multiple (0, +1, -1, +2, -2) times multiplicand based on recoding value.
- Accumulate the partial product shifted appropriately into the product register.
- Shift the multiplicand by multiples of 2 bits is not necessary since the algorithm uses multiples (-2, -1, 0, +1, +2) to generate partial products to add.
- After 8 cycles, the product register holds the final 16-bit signed product.
- Output registered product and assert ready signal for one cycle to indicate completion.
- Use non-blocking assignments throughout to ensure clean sequential behavior.
- Keep code clean and readable with appropriate comments and FSM states for clarity.
- This approach fuses correct radix-4 encoding (vs radix-2 bit-by-bit), cleaner synchronous reset and signed registers, output registration, and ready signal handshake.

This design improves performance by halving the iteration count, reduces switching on the multiplicand register by not shifting every cycle, and achieves proper radix-4 Booth multiplication accuracy with proper sign extension and output stability.
