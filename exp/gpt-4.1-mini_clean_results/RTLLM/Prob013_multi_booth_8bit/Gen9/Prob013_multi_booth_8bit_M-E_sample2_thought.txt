Implement a synchronous Radix-4 Booth multiplier using an iterative approach with a 5-bit counter running from 0 to 8 (processing two multiplier bits per cycle).  
Key architectural ideas:
- Use three main registers:  
  * A 17-bit accumulator/product register `product` which holds partial sums and multiplier bits (initialized by placing multiplier and one extra zero bit at LSB to handle radix-4 Booth encoding).  
  * An 8-bit multiplicand register `multiplicand` sign-extended to 17 bits.  
  * A 5-bit counter to count the 9 iterations (0 to 8) needed to process 16 bits in radix-4 (two bits per cycle plus an initial bit).  

- On reset:  
  * Initialize `multiplicand` to sign-extended input `a`.  
  * Initialize `product` to `{ {8{b[7]}}, b, 1'b0 }` (sign-extended multiplier shifted left by 1, LSB=0).  
  * Reset counter and ready signal.  

- On each clock cycle (when not reset and counter < 9):  
  * Examine three bits `product[2:0]` for current radix-4 Booth encoding.  
  * Depending on `product[2:0]` (which encodes -2, -1, 0, +1, +2 multiples), compute a partial value to add to `product[16:1]`.  
  * Shift `product` right arithmetically by 2 bits after addition to prepare for next iteration.  
  * Increment counter.  

- When counter reaches 9, multiplication is complete:  
  * Output product is the middle 16 bits `[16:1]` of `product` (signed result).  
  * Set ready signal high.  

- Keep combinational logic for Booth decoding separate.  
- Use non-blocking assignments in synchronous always block.  
- This design handles sign extension correctly and aligns the multiplier and multiplicand consistently.  

This approach processes two multiplier bits per cycle, finishing in 9 cycles including initial alignment, uses a single 17-bit product register to hold the entire partial product + multiplier + extra bit for encoding, and strictly follows Radix-4 Booth algorithm. Ready signal indicates completion.
