I will implement an 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier with a clean, cycle-based sequential design that performs the multiplication over multiple clock cycles.

My approach:
- Inputs a (multiplicand) and b (multiplier) are sign-extended to 16 bits.
- The multiplier is extended by one LSB bit (appended zero) to facilitate Radix-4 Booth recoding.
- The multiplication process will run over 4 iterations since Radix-4 processes 2 multiplier bits per iteration.
- At each iteration, 3 bits (overlapping 2 bits plus previous bit) of the extended multiplier are examined to determine the Booth operation.
- The partial product is computed using the multiplicand multiplied by +2, +1, 0, -1, or -2 depending on the Booth code.
- The partial product is shifted left by (2 * iteration count) bits and added to a signed 32-bit accumulator register.
- A 3-bit counter (0 to 3) controls iteration steps; after 4 iterations, the ready signal is asserted.
- The product output is the lower 16 bits of the accumulator.
- Reset synchronously initializes all internal registers and sets ready low.
- This architecture uses only small shifts and additions per cycle, making it hardware-friendly and synthesizable.
- All internal variables are declared outside always blocks to avoid syntax errors.
- No blocking assignment conflicts: calculations are done combinationally, registered results on clock edges.
- This approach directly aligns with a standard Radix-4 Booth multiplier implementation, clean and fully synthesizable.

Overall, this design efficiently implements the requested Radix-4 Booth multiplier with a clocked sequential process and clear, readable RTL.
