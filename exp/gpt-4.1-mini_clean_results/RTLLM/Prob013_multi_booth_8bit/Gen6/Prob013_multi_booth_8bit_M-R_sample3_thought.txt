Refactor the radix-4 Booth multiplier into a clean state-machine-based design with properly declared intermediate registers outside the procedural blocks. Use a synchronous process triggered on clk and reset. Maintain three main registers: a 17-bit signed accumulator, a 9-bit extended multiplier (multiplier plus appended zero), and a 16-bit multiplicand sign-extended from input `a`. Use a 3-bit cycle counter to track up to 4 cycles needed for radix-4 encoding of 8-bit inputs.

At each cycle:
- Decode the 3 LSBs of multiplier_ext to determine the Booth operation (-2, -1, 0, 1, 2).
- Calculate the partial product outside combinational logic by using pre-declared reg variables.
- Add the partial product to the accumulator.
- Arithmetic right shift the concatenated accumulator and multiplier registers by 2 bits.
- Increment cycle count.

When done (4 cycles), output the lower 16 bits of accumulator as the product and assert ready signal.

This approach avoids illegal variable declarations inside always blocks by predeclaring all regs, uses non-blocking assignments for sequential logic, and matches the cycle count with the radix-4 algorithm (processing 2 bits per cycle). The multiplier bits are scanned in radix-4 fashion, thus needing ceil(8/2)=4 cycles. This design cleanly separates combinational Booth decoding and sequential state update.
