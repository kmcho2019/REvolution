To implement an 8-bit radix-4 Booth multiplier properly, the algorithm should process two bits of the multiplier at a time (plus an additional previous bit, called Booth encoding bits). This reduces the number of partial products roughly by half (from 8 to 4 cycles) and supports signed multiplication naturally.

Key points of the design:
- Inputs: multiplicand (a), multiplier (b), both 8-bit signed.
- Sign-extend a and b to 9 bits, so we can consider the previous multiplier bit (initialized to 0).
- Use a 17-bit register that concatenates the partial product and the extended multiplier and previous bit (Booth bits) for easy shifting.
- Use a 5-bit counter that counts from 0 to 4 (because each iteration consumes 2 bits).
- On reset, load the partial product register with {16'b0, b, 1'b0} (multiplier extended with previous bit = 0).
- On each clock cycle (if counter < 4), examine the lowest 3 bits (bits 1:0 plus the previous bit) to decode the Booth multiplier.
- Depending on the Booth code (0 to 7), add or subtract multiplicand multiplied by 0,1,2 (i.e., 0, +a, +2a, -a, -2a) to the upper 16 bits of the partial product register.
- Shift the partial product register right by 2 bits (arithmetic shift) to prepare for next 2 bits.
- After 4 cycles, the upper 16 bits of the partial product register contain the final product.
- The ready signal (rdy) is asserted when the multiplication is complete.
- The product output (p) is updated at the end.
- Use non-blocking assignments and synchronous reset.

This design correctly implements radix-4 Booth multiplication in a pipelined fashion over 4 clock cycles, reduces clock cycles compared to naive approach, and handles signed inputs properly.
