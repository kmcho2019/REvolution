The original implementation was a simple bit-by-bit shift-and-add multiplier, not a Radix-4 Booth multiplier as required.

To implement a true Radix-4 Booth multiplier for 8-bit inputs, the multiplier is processed 2 bits at a time, with an extra appended zero bit at the LSB side, thus requiring (8/2) = 4 cycles.

Each cycle examines a 3-bit group (including the previous LSB), and based on Booth encoding rules, decides whether to add, subtract, or do nothing with the multiplicand multiplied by 0, 1, 2.

Key points:
- Inputs 'a' and 'b' are sign-extended to 16-bit signed registers to handle signed multiplication correctly.
- A 17-bit register 'multiplier_extended' is created by appending one zero bit at LSB to multiplier (b) to simplify group selection.
- A 32-bit 'product' register accumulates the result since the product of two 8-bit numbers can be up to 16 bits, but we keep 32 bits internally to handle intermediate arithmetic and shifts safely.
- At each clock cycle, extract 3 bits from multiplier_extended corresponding to 2 bits processed plus the previous bit (for overlapping groups).
- The multiplicand is shifted left by 2 bits times the current cycle index, reflecting the radix-4 approach (processing 2 bits per cycle).
- Perform signed addition or subtraction of multiplicand shifted by 2*cycle based on Booth encoding.
- After 4 cycles, the final product is available in lower 16 bits of 'product', and the 'rdy' signal is asserted.

This implementation aligns with Radix-4 Booth encoding, using a 5-bit counter only up to 4, processes 2 bits of multiplier per cycle, and properly handles signed inputs and outputs.

The ready signal 'rdy' is low during multiplication and goes high when the product is ready.

This fixed design matches the problem's intent and eliminates the earlier incorrect 16-step shift-add approach.
