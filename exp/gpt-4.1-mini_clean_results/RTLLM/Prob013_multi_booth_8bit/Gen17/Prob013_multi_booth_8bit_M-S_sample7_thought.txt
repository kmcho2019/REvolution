Implement a straightforward radix-4 Booth multiplier by grouping the multiplier bits in overlapping triplets, processing two bits per cycle. 

On reset, sign-extend inputs, initialize the product register and counter (counting down from 4 since 8 bits / 2 bits per cycle = 4 cycles).

At each clock cycle (when not reset), examine three bits from the multiplier (including an appended 0 bit at LSB for the first cycle) to decide the operation on the multiplicand: multiply by -2, -1, 0, 1, or 2, and accumulate accordingly.

Shift the product right by two bits per cycle (since two multiplier bits processed each time).

After 4 cycles, output the final product and assert ready.

This approach uses simple signed arithmetic with clearly separated steps, reduces cycles to 4 instead of 16, and uses non-blocking assignments for synchronous operation. It avoids a complex FSM by using a counter and a ready flag.
