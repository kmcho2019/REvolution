The existing design uses a 3-state FSM and 18-bit accumulator updated every clock cycle with a shifted partial product. While correct and straightforward, the accumulator is large (18 bits), and shifting the partial product by (2*step) each cycle introduces long combinational paths, affecting timing. The multiplier is sign-extended to 9 bits with one extra LSB for Booth recoding and shifted arithmetically every cycle.

To optimize for better PPA (performance, power, area):

1. **Reduce combinational complexity by avoiding variable large shifts inside the clocked path**: Instead of shifting the partial product by 2*step every cycle, shift the multiplier right by 2 bits every cycle and always add the unshifted partial product to the accumulator. This simplifies accumulator addition to a fixed addition per cycle without large shifts, moving the shifting responsibility fully to the multiplier.

2. **Reduce accumulator width from 18 to 16 bits**: The product of two signed 8-bit numbers fits in 16 bits. By carefully managing sign extensions and arithmetic right shifts of multiplier, we can keep accumulator 16 bits, saving area and power.

3. **Use signed 17-bit multiplier and arithmetic right shifts by 2 bits per cycle**: The multiplier is extended with two extra bits (one LSB zero and one MSB sign extension) to handle radix-4 Booth recoding, improving correct sign handling in shifts and recoding.

4. **Eliminate the FSM by using a simple counter and a ready signal**: On reset, load inputs and start multiplication; each clock, update accumulator and multiplier; after 4 cycles (since 8 bits / 2 = 4 radix-4 steps), assert ready. This reduces control logic area and simplifies timing.

5. **Precompute the partial product using 3 bits from multiplier (bits 1:0 plus bit -1), where bit -1 is stored in a separate reg (prev_lsb)**: This improves clarity of the Booth code extraction.

6. **Use smaller signed partial product width (16 bits) instead of 18** to match the accumulator, simplifying logic.

This approach simplifies critical path and reduces register widths, improving performance and area while maintaining functionality.

Summary:
- 16-bit signed accumulator
- Multiplier extended to 17 bits for radix-4 recoding
- Fixed shift of multiplier by 2 bits per cycle (arithmetic right shift)
- Partial product added without shifting to accumulator
- 4 cycles total (counter from 0 to 3) for 8-bit radix-4 multiplication
- Ready signal set when done

This balances simplicity, correctness, and PPA improvements.
