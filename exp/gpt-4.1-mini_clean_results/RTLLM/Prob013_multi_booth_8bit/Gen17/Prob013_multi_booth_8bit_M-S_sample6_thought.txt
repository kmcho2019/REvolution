I will simplify the design by removing the FSM and instead implement the multiplication in a straightforward iterative process controlled by a 5-bit counter. The algorithm will:

- On reset, initialize multiplicand and multiplier (sign-extended to 9 bits), zero the accumulator, reset counter and ready signal.
- On each clock cycle while counter < 5, perform one step of radix-4 Booth multiplication:
  - Extract the bottom 3 bits of multiplier.
  - Determine partial product using Booth encoding.
  - Accumulate partial product shifted by (2 * counter).
  - Arithmetic right shift multiplier by 2 bits.
  - Increment counter.
- When counter reaches 5, set ready and output lower 16 bits of accumulator.
  
This approach reduces complexity by removing state machine overhead and makes control logic straightforward while preserving functionality and performance. Sign extension is handled cleanly. The partial product calculation is still combinational and clear. The output stays stable after ready is asserted.

This design is fully synchronous, resettable, and meets the problem's interface requirements.
