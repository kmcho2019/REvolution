To implement a true Radix-4 Booth multiplier for 8-bit inputs, the algorithm processes two multiplier bits per cycle (overlapping one bit from the previous cycle). This reduces the number of partial products and cycles roughly by half compared to radix-2.

Key design points:
1. Inputs (a and b) are sign-extended to 9 bits for Radix-4 Booth recoding to simplify handling sign bits.
2. Multiplier is encoded in groups of three bits (the current two bits plus the previous bit) to determine the partial product action per iteration.
3. The multiplicand and its multiples (-2, -1, 0, +1, +2) are precomputed to avoid complex dynamic calculations during the operation.
4. A 5-bit counter tracks iterations; since 8 bits are processed two bits at a time, the process takes 4 cycles.
5. A 32-bit signed accumulator register stores the running product (to accommodate shifts and sums).
6. At each cycle:
   - Extract three bits of multiplier for current Booth encoding.
   - Lookup the partial product multiplier (0, ±a, ±2a).
   - Shift the partial product accordingly (by 2*iteration).
   - Add the partial product to the accumulator.
7. After all 4 cycles, the lower 16 bits of the accumulator form the final product.
8. The design is synchronous with a reset; upon reset, the process initializes and starts multiplication on the next clock.
9. The ready signal (rdy) signals completion and holds until reset.
10. This approach directly implements Radix-4 Booth's efficient partial product generation, improving speed by processing two bits per cycle.

This architecture efficiently implements Radix-4 booth multiplication for signed 8-bit inputs, completing in 4 cycles, and outputs a signed 16-bit product.
