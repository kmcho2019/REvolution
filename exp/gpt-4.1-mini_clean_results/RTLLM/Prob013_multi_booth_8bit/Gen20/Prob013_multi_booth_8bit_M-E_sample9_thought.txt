Implement a fully synchronous, iterative Radix-4 Booth multiplier processing two bits of the multiplier per cycle to halve the number of cycles compared to radix-2. 

The multiplier register will be 9 bits wide (8 bits + one extra bit for Booth encoding), initialized with the multiplier and a trailing zero. On each cycle, the lowest three bits of this register are examined as the Booth encoding window, determining the operation on the multiplicand to be performed on the partial product: -2 * multiplicand, -1 * multiplicand, 0, +1 * multiplicand, +2 * multiplicand.

The multiplicand is sign-extended to 17 bits to handle additions of ±2*multiplicand without overflow. The partial product is also kept 17 bits to accommodate intermediate sums and shifts.

The iterative process:

- On reset, load inputs with proper sign extension:
  - multiplicand_reg = {multiplicand sign bit, multiplicand, 8'b0} (shift left 8 for alignment)
  - multiplier_reg = {multiplier, 1'b0} (9 bits total)
  - partial_product = 0
  - count = 0

- On each clock cycle:
  - Extract Booth bits (multiplier_reg[2:0])
  - Compute partial product addend based on Booth encoding (±0, ±multiplicand, ±2*multiplicand)
  - Add to partial product
  - Arithmetic right shift partial_product and multiplier_reg combined by 2 bits (since radix-4)
  - Increment count by 2 (process 2 bits per cycle)

- When count reaches 8 (since multiplier is 8 bits and two bits are processed per cycle), multiplication is done

- Output is lower 16 bits of the partial product.

Ready signal (rdy) is asserted at the cycle after processing completes.

This architecture reduces the iteration count from 16 (radix-2) to 4 (radix-4), improving throughput while maintaining clean synchronous design. All registers update on clock edges, and reset asynchronously initializes the process.
