Fuse the clear, structured FSM and control-signal approach from Example 1 with the concise state encoding and synchronous reset handling from Example 2 to deliver a fully synchronous, easy-to-read design.

Fusion strategy:
- Use a 2-bit FSM with three states (IDLE, LOAD, CALC) for clarity and modularity.
- On reset, transition to IDLE and clear registers.
- In LOAD, sign-extend inputs and zero registers, then proceed to CALC.
- In CALC, perform multiplication over 16 cycles:
  - At each cycle, examine the multiplier bit at the counter index.
  - If the bit is 1, add the multiplicand to the product.
  - Shift multiplicand left by 1 each cycle (simulate Booth algorithm shifting).
  - Increment counter.
- When counter reaches 16, set ready signal and update output product.
- Use nonblocking assignments in sequential blocks, and blocking assignments in combinational blocks.
- Separate next-state logic and datapath control signals combinationally for readability and modularity.
- Maintain synchronous resets and output register updates on clock edges only.
- Keep outputs registered and ready asserted only at completion.
This fusion improves clarity, maintainability, and correctness, minimizing switching by gating register updates to calculation cycles and cleanly separating control and datapath.

This solution is synthesizable, maintains problem requirements, and balances clarity and efficiency.
