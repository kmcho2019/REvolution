I will refactor the Radix-4 booth multiplier by using a Moore state machine with three states: IDLE, CALC, and DONE. The multiplicand and multiplier will be registered on reset, and the multiplication will progress over a fixed number of steps, updating the accumulator with each Booth partial product. 

Instead of shifting the multiplier register arithmetically by 2 bits per cycle, I will keep the multiplier constant and use a separate 3-bit "booth_slice" extracted based on the current step (i.e., bits [2*step+1: 2*step-1]) to determine the Booth operation, avoiding variable shifts of the multiplier register itself. This keeps the multiplier stable and simplifies hardware.

The accumulator will hold the running sum of partial products, each appropriately shifted by 2*step bits using fixed shifts (left shifts of constant amounts), removing variable shift operations that are expensive to synthesize.

`multiplicand` will be sign-extended once to 9 bits. The Booth operation is implemented as a pure combinational function, returning an 18-bit signed partial product. The product output will be registered only after the calculation is done, and `rdy` asserted accordingly.

This approach simplifies the datapath by avoiding arithmetic shifting of the multiplier register, stabilizes the multiplier input, and uses fixed shifts on the partial products, potentially improving synthesis results and timing. Also, I will implement reset synchronously on `clk` (reset active high) to simplify timing analysis.

The state transitions are controlled by the step counter, which runs from 0 to 4 (5 iterations for 8-bit Radix-4 Booth). The ready signal goes high when done, and the module waits in DONE until reset asserted again.

This restructuring is functionally equivalent but uses a cleaner separation between data and control, improves timing and area by replacing variable shifts with fixed shifts, and clarifies the flow by extracting booth bits directly from multiplier input without shifting it.
