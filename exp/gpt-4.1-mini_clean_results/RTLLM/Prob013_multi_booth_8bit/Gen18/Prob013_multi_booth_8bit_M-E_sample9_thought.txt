Implement the 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier as a fully synchronous iterative design that leverages radix-4 encoding to halve the number of cycles compared to radix-2, thus improving performance.

Key ideas:
- Extend the multiplier by appending an extra bit at LSB (multiplier_ext = {b, 1'b0}) to form overlapping 3-bit groups for radix-4 Booth encoding.
- Each cycle processes one Booth digit (2 bits of multiplier), so we only need ceil(8/2) = 4 cycles to complete the multiplication.
- Use a 3-bit register to hold current Booth bits (overlapping sliding window), updated by shifting multiplier_ext right by 2 bits after each cycle.
- Use a signed partial product register (accumulated sum), updated each cycle by adding the appropriate multiple of multiplicand based on Booth encoding (-2x, -1x, 0, +1x, +2x).
- Maintain a counter for cycles (0 to 4).
- After 4 cycles, assert ready and output the product.
- Reset initializes internal registers and waits for new inputs.
- This approach reduces cycles, operations, and shifting complexity compared to bit-by-bit radix-2.
