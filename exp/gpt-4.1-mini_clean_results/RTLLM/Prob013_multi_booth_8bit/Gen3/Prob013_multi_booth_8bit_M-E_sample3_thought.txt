Implement a sequential Radix-4 Booth multiplier for 8-bit inputs using a shift-and-add approach driven by a 5-bit counter to process 16 bits of the multiplier one bit at a time, as requested in the problem statement.

Key points of this novel architecture:
1. Represent both multiplier and multiplicand as 16-bit sign-extended registers to support negative numbers.
2. Use a 16-bit product register initialized to zero.
3. Use a 16-bit multiplier register holding the extended multiplier.
4. On reset, load the multiplicand and multiplier registers with sign-extended inputs and clear product and counter.
5. On each clock cycle when not done, check the current multiplier bit (multiplier[ctr]):
   - If multiplier[ctr] == 1, add multiplicand to product.
   - If zero, no addition.
6. After checking the bit and possible addition, left-shift multiplicand by 1 (multiply by 2) to align for the next bit position.
7. Increment counter ctr from 0 to 15.
8. When ctr reaches 16, set ready (rdy) high and output product.
9. This design directly corresponds to the problem’s given algorithm but improves clarity by separating registers for product, multiplier, and multiplicand and proper synchronous reset logic.
10. The design is purely in Verilog (no SystemVerilog enums) for maximum tool compatibility.

This structure is simpler than typical radix-4 Booth implementations that do group bits, but it directly implements the problem statement with precise control on counter and shifting multiplicand per clock cycle. It is a standard sequential shift-add multiplier that accumulates partial products according to Booth's signed multiplicand and multiplier representation.

This approach keeps the multiplier fixed, shifts the multiplicand left at each step, and accumulates when the multiplier bit is 1, reflecting a partial and clear Radix-4 inspired method by processing bit-by-bit with shifts of multiplicand.

The ready signal flags multiplication completion after 16 cycles post reset.
