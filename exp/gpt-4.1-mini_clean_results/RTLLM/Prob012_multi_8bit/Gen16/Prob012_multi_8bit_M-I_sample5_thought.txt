To optimize the previous fully combinational multiplier for better area and power without sacrificing correctness, implement an iterative shift-and-add multiplier using a simple sequential approach with a finite state machine. This reduces the large combinational summation by spreading addition over multiple clock cycles, greatly reducing critical path delay and area.

The improved design:
- Uses registers to store intermediate product and multiplicand.
- Iteratively checks each bit of B from LSB to MSB.
- Adds the shifted A to the partial product only when the current bit of B is 1.
- Shifts the multiplicand A left each cycle.
- Shifts B right each cycle to move through bits.
- After 8 cycles, outputs the full 16-bit product.

This approach drastically reduces combinational logic by replacing large parallel adders with a single adder and a small FSM, improving timing and power metrics at the expense of latency. The module includes a clock and reset input for synchronous operation.
