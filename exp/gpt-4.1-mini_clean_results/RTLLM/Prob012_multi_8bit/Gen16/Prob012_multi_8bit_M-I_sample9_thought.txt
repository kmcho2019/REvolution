The original design uses a fully combinational balanced adder tree to sum all partial products, which leads to a potentially long critical path, high power, and large area due to multiple wide adders working in parallel. To improve PPA:

- Replace the balanced adder tree with an iterative shift-and-add multiplier that accumulates partial sums sequentially.
- Implement the multiplication as a sequential process using a clock and reset, which naturally reduces area and power by reusing a single adder and registers rather than parallel adders.
- The sequential approach reduces switching activity, uses fewer resources, and shortens the critical path, improving frequency potential.
- The design will have a valid output signal to indicate when the product is ready, as the multiplication takes 8 clock cycles.
- This approach is a trade-off: throughput reduces (one product every 8 cycles), but area and power are significantly improved.
- The design uses a simple FSM style process: On reset, product and multiplier registers are initialized; each cycle processes one bit of the multiplier B, conditionally adding shifted multiplicand A to the product.
- Output product is updated after all bits are processed.

This iterative shift-and-add multiplier implementation is a standard low-area, low-power design suitable when high throughput is not required.
