To optimize the original fully combinational balanced adder tree for better power and area efficiency while maintaining correctness, I will convert the design into an iterative sequential multiplier using a shift-and-add algorithm controlled by a clock and reset.

Key improvements:
- Replace combinational multi-operand addition with an iterative accumulation of partial products controlled by a simple FSM and a counter.
- This approach uses a single 16-bit accumulator and a shifted multiplicand register, minimizing hardware usage.
- Power is reduced since only one add operation per clock cycle occurs instead of eight parallel adders.
- The latency is 8 clock cycles, but area and power are significantly improved.
- Output is registered and valid after 8 cycles, which is practical for many systems.

Inputs and outputs remain the same, with an additional clock and reset inputs for synchronous operation.

This approach simulates the classical shift-and-add multiplier in hardware with a minimal data path.
