Implement the 8-bit multiplier using a sequential shift-and-add approach in a synchronous design. The design uses a register to accumulate the partial sum (product) and a register to hold the multiplier. On each clock cycle, if the least significant bit of the multiplier is 1, the multiplicand is added to the product. The multiplicand is shifted left each cycle to align with the corresponding bit position. The multiplier is shifted right each cycle to process the next bit. After 8 cycles, the multiplication is complete, and the product register holds the final result. This design trades latency for smaller combinational logic and area savings, suitable for hardware where clocked iterative computation is preferred.
