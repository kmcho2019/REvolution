Implement the 8-bit multiplier using an iterative shift-and-add method within a combinational always block. Instead of creating multiple partial product wires and summing them with a chain of adders, use a simple for loop to simulate the traditional multiplication process: initialize a 16-bit accumulator (product) to zero, then for each bit of multiplier B, if the bit is 1, add the current multiplicand (properly shifted) to the accumulator. Shift the multiplicand left by one at each iteration to align with the multiplier bit position. This approach closely models the manual shift-and-add algorithm and keeps the logic simple and clear, avoiding arrays of partial products and intermediate sums. The implementation is fully combinational and synthesizable.
