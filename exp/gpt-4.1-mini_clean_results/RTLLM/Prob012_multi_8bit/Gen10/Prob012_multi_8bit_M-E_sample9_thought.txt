Implement an 8-bit multiplier using an iterative shift-and-add approach within a purely combinational module by unrolling the iterative process explicitly in combinational logic.

Instead of generating all partial products and summing them simultaneously, simulate the classic manual multiplication method as a chain of add and shift steps:
- Initialize a 16-bit accumulator (product) to zero.
- For each bit of the multiplier B, from LSB to MSB:
  - If the current bit is 1, add the shifted multiplicand A to the accumulator.
  - Shift A left by one bit for the next iteration.
  
This approach performs the addition and shifting step-by-step, but unrolled combinationally (no loops or sequential logic), which leads to a cascade of conditional adders and shifts. It mimics the sequential shift-and-add hardware algorithm but fully combinational.

The benefits:
- Clear conceptual mapping to classic multiplication.
- Potentially smaller logic than adding all partial products at once because additions depend on previous sums.
- Shift operations become wiring.
- Single long combinational chain, so slower than balanced tree, but novel architecture distinct from example.

Implementation:
- Use a generate block with 8 stages.
- At each stage, based on B[i], add shifted A to the cumulative sum.
- Keep track of the shifted multiplicand each stage by shifting left by 1 from previous.
- Final product output is the accumulator after all 8 stages.
