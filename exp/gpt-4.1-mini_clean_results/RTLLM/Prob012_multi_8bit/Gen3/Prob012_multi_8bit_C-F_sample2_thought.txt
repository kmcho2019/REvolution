To combine the strengths of both examples, I will implement an 8-bit multiplier using a shift-and-add approach that:
- Generates partial products combinationally by shifting A according to each bit of B.
- Uses a balanced adder tree to sum partial products in stages, reducing the long critical path caused by adding all eight partial products in a single expression.
This approach maintains a fully combinational design for simplicity and clarity, while improving performance by reducing the addition depth and improving the maximum achievable frequency. It also limits power and area overhead by avoiding a single large multi-operand addition.

Specifically, the partial products will be generated as wires, then summed pairwise in three levels:
- Level 1: sum 4 pairs (2 inputs each) → 4 sums
- Level 2: sum 2 pairs (2 inputs each) → 2 sums
- Level 3: sum the final 2 sums → final product

This balanced tree reduces the addition depth from 7 (chaining 8 adds) to 3, substantially improving timing without changing the basic shift-and-add multiplication principle.
