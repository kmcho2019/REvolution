I will implement an iterative, clocked shift-and-add multiplier that processes one bit of the multiplier per clock cycle. This approach significantly reduces combinational complexity compared to a fully combinational adder tree. The multiplicand and partial product will be accumulated in a 16-bit register, and the multiplier will be shifted right each cycle to examine its least significant bit. When the LSB of the multiplier is 1, the multiplicand (shifted appropriately) will be added to the product register. After 8 cycles, the final product will be ready. This architecture trades latency (multi-cycle) for simpler hardware, reduced area, and improved timing. It is synchronous, controlled by a clock and reset, and suitable for hardware where cycle budget is available.
