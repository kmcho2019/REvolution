To optimize the original fully combinational multiplier for better power and area without changing correctness, I will convert the design into a sequential multiplier using the classic shift-and-add algorithm with an 8-cycle process:

- Use a clocked process with a control signal to step through each bit of B.
- At each clock cycle, examine the least significant bit of the multiplier register.
- If it's 1, add the multiplicand to the product accumulator.
- Shift the multiplicand left by one bit each cycle.
- Shift the multiplier right by one bit each cycle.
- After 8 cycles, the product register holds the final result.

This approach replaces the large combinational adder tree with a simple adder and registers, reducing area and switching power drastically at the expense of latency (8 clock cycles). The sequential logic also facilitates pipelining or integration into larger FSMs.

I will implement an enable and done signal for proper operation control, and synchronous reset.

The output product will be updated only when done is asserted.

This is a classic shift-and-add multiplier with minimal hardware and reduced power dissipation.
