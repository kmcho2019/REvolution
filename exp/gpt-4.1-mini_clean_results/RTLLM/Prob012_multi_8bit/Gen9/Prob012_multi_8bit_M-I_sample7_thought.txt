The original design uses a fully combinational balanced adder tree to sum all partial products in parallel, which optimizes latency but can cause relatively high power consumption and area due to parallelism and a large combinational path. 

To optimize for better power and area while retaining correct functionality:
- Use an iterative shift-and-add approach implemented with a simple for-loop inside an always_comb block. 
- This approach serially accumulates partial products by checking each bit of B and shifting A accordingly, but done combinationally.
- This reduces area by avoiding multiple parallel 16-bit adders and can help the synthesis tool optimize the additions more efficiently.
- Also, the design remains purely combinational, matching the problem requirements.
- Using a for loop for addition and shifting keeps the implementation concise and easy to understand, with improved power due to fewer simultaneous additions.

This approach is a classic shift-and-add multiplier optimized for area and power relative to the fully parallel balanced adder tree, trading off some latency (not a pipeline here, but pure combinational latency should be manageable).
