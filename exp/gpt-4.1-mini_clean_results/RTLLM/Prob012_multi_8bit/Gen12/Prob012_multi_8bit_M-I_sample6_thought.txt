To improve the existing combinational 8-bit multiplier for better power and area efficiency without losing correctness, I will implement the classic shift-and-add multiplication iteratively using a simple sequential approach inside an always block with a for loop. This design simulates the traditional manual multiplication process:

- Start with a 16-bit product register initialized to zero.
- Iterate through each bit of the multiplier B.
- For each set bit in B, add the appropriately shifted multiplicand A to the product.
- The addition and shifting are done stepwise, avoiding generation of all partial products simultaneously.
- This approach is sequential in code but fully combinational due to the use of always_comb (or always @*) block with a for loop unrolled by synthesis.
  
Benefits over the previous design:
- Avoids summing eight partial products in parallel, reducing the total adder hardware and switching activity.
- Reduces area by eliminating the large adder tree.
- Potentially lowers power by not generating all shifted partial products at once.
- Keeps the design combinational for simplicity, suitable for medium-speed applications or where smaller area/power is important.

This shift-and-add method closely models the "shift and add" multiplication algorithm requested, achieving the multiplication by selective addition of shifted multiplicand controlled by the multiplier bits.

The final product is assigned as an output wire derived from the combinational always block.
