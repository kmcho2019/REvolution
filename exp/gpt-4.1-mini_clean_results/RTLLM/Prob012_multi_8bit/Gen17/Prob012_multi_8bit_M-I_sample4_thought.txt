To optimize the 8-bit shift-and-add multiplier for better PPA, we target the critical path and switching activity:

1. Use a sequential multiplier design rather than fully combinational. 
   This means the multiplication happens across multiple clock cycles, greatly reducing combinational path length and thus increasing maximum frequency (improving WNS and TNS).

2. Reduce switching by only adding the multiplicand (properly shifted) to the product register when the current multiplier bit is '1'. This avoids generating all partial products upfront.

3. Use registers to hold intermediate product and multiplicand, performing shift operations and conditional addition sequentially.

4. Introduce an 'enable' and 'start' input signals and an internal counter to manage the 8 cycles of multiplication, making it reusable and controllable. The output 'product' is valid after the operation completes.

This sequential approach reduces area (less combinational logic needed at one time), lowers power (due to limited toggling), and improves timing (smaller critical path per clock cycle).
