To optimize the multiplier for better performance, power, and area, implement the shift-and-add multiplication algorithm iteratively inside a sequential always block. This approach processes one bit of B per clock cycle, adding A shifted accordingly when B's bit is 1, and shifting A left by one bit each iteration. It avoids the large combinational chain of adders, reducing critical path delay and dynamic power by sequential operation.

The module will include a clock and reset input to control the iterative process, exposing a start signal to begin multiplication and a done signal indicating completion. This approach uses a small FSM and registers to hold intermediate results, significantly improving timing and power compared to fully combinational logic.

If a purely combinational design is required, an alternative is to replace the long linear addition chain by a balanced adder tree and pre-extend the multiplicand once, but the sequential iterative approach is more scalable and power-efficient.
