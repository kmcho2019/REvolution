Fuse the explicit procedural clarity of the iterative shift-and-add loop (Example 2) with the structural clarity and parallel partial product generation of the generate-for approach (Example 1). Use generate-for to create partial products combinationally, but instead of summing all eight in a flat addition (which can create a long critical path), sum the partial products in a balanced binary tree fashion using intermediate wires. This reduces the logic depth and critical path delay compared to a linear chain of adders.

This fusion leverages:
- The efficient hardware mapping of partial product generation via bitwise masking and shifting (like Example 1).
- Improved timing by summing partial products pairwise (a tree-based summation), reducing the longest carry chain.
- Fully combinational logic ensuring no sequential elements or latency, suitable for applications needing one-cycle multiplication.
- Clear modular and scalable structure for easy adaptation to different bit-widths.

Overall, this approach aims for better performance (timing) and moderate area and power by balancing parallelism and hierarchical summation, improving on both example designs.
