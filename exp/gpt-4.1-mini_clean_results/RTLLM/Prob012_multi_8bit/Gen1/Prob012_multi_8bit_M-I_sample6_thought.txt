To optimize the initial combinational shift-and-add multiplier for better PPA (power, performance, and area), implement it as an iterative sequential multiplier using a clock and reset input. This approach breaks the multiplication into multiple clock cycles, each processing one bit of the multiplier and adding the shifted multiplicand only if the current multiplier bit is set. The multiplicand is shifted left each cycle, and the multiplier is shifted right to examine the next bit. This reduces combinational critical path length significantly, improving maximum clock frequency and lowering power consumption by spreading computation over cycles. Additionally, using registers reduces glitching and switching activity. This trade-off increases latency but is beneficial for large designs or higher-speed requirements. The output is registered and valid after 8 cycles signaled by a 'done' output. This design fits typical RTL coding styles for hardware multipliers with shift-and-add and provides better PPA than a fully combinational one.
