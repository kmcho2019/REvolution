Implement an 8-bit multiplier using an iterative shift-and-add approach within a sequential process.  
Use a combinational always block triggered on any input change to simulate the multiplication by iterating over the bits of B.  
At each iteration, if the current bit of B is set, add the shifted multiplicand to an accumulator representing the partial product.  
Shift the multiplicand left by one after each bit is processed to align with the corresponding bit position in B.  
This approach models the classical shift-and-add multiplication algorithm explicitly in RTL but within a purely combinational block to yield a single-cycle output.  
This solution differs architecturally by explicitly using a procedural loop for clarity and does not generate partial products in parallel.  
It also avoids multiple parallel adders, leading to smaller synthesized logic, potentially lower area and power at the cost of combinational delay due to iterative addition.
