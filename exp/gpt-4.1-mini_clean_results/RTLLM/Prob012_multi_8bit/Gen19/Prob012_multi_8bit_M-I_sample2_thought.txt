To optimize the 8-bit multiplier for better power, performance, and area (PPA), I will switch from the fully combinational balanced adder tree to an iterative shift-and-add multiplier implemented as a sequential circuit. This approach uses a single adder and a register to accumulate partial sums over 8 clock cycles, significantly reducing the combinational complexity and hardware resources, hence improving area and power. It also shortens the critical path to just one 16-bit adder and registers, improving timing. While latency increases due to sequential operation, the tradeoff favors better PPA in typical ASIC or FPGA designs.

The module will have a clock and reset input, and an input valid signal to start the multiplication. It will output the product and a done signal when the operation is finished. Internally, the multiplicand and multiplier are stored in registers; the multiplier bits are processed one-by-one: when a bit is set, the multiplicand shifted appropriately is added to the partial product. The multiplicand is shifted left each cycle, and the multiplier is shifted right. After 8 cycles, the product is available.

This state-machine driven multiplier optimizes area and power by reusing a single adder and registers, and improves timing by shortening the combinational path to a single addition per cycle.
