I will implement a purely combinational 8-bit multiplier using the classic iterative shift-and-add approach unrolled in parallel. Instead of generating all partial products at once and summing them via an adder tree, this design will simulate the sequential algorithm combinationally by accumulating the partial sum in a chain of adders and shifts.

Specifically:
- For each bit of multiplier B, starting from LSB to MSB, if the bit is set, add the multiplicand A shifted by that bit index to the accumulated sum.
- The accumulation is done by a series of intermediate sum wires, each representing the product after processing one bit of B.
- This method explicitly models the shift-and-add process structurally, producing a cleaner datapath resembling a multi-stage pipeline but purely combinational.
- It also uses smaller incremental additions at each stage instead of adding all partial products together at once, which spreads complexity evenly.

The output product is the final accumulated sum after processing all bits of B. The design remains fully combinational, with all additions and shifts instantiated explicitly.
