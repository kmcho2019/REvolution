The original fully combinational design uses a balanced adder tree to sum partial products, which provides good performance but suffers from potentially long combinational paths and high switching power. To improve PPA (power, performance, area), I propose adding a simple sequential implementation with a clock and reset inputs. This converts the design into an iterative multiplier using shift-and-add, greatly reducing combinational delay and area by reusing a single adder and register. Although latency increases, the cycle time can be reduced, improving maximum clock frequency and lowering power by spreading computations over multiple cycles with less switching per cycle.

Key improvements:
- Add clock and reset inputs for synchronous operation.
- Use registers for product and multiplicand shifted versions.
- Iterate over each bit of multiplier B, adding shifted A to product if corresponding B bit is 1.
- Output the final product after 8 cycles.
- This approach is area- and power-efficient compared to fully combinational.
- Useful for designs where latency is acceptable in exchange for better PPA.

This approach aligns with classic shift-and-add multiplier design principles and is synthesizable and cleanly structured.
