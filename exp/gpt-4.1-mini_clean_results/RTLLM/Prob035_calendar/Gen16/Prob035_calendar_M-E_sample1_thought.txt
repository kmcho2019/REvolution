Instead of one monolithic always block or multiple separate always blocks, I will implement the perpetual calendar using a hierarchical and event-driven approach with enable signals. Each time unit (seconds, minutes, hours) is updated in its own always block, but seconds drive the enable signal for minutes, and minutes drive the enable for hours. This modularizes the design and allows for better clarity and potential optimizations by the synthesis tool.

- The seconds counter increments every clock cycle.
- When seconds roll over (i.e., from 59 back to 0), it asserts a "sec_rollover" signal (internal wire).
- The minutes counter uses sec_rollover as an enable to increment itself.
- Similarly, when minutes roll over, a "min_rollover" signal is asserted.
- The hours counter uses min_rollover as its increment enable.
- Each always block is triggered on posedge CLK or posedge RST.
- All counters wrap naturally at their limits (Secs and Mins at 59, Hours at 23).
- Non-blocking assignments preserve sequential semantics.
- This design cleanly separates concerns, facilitates debugging, and can provide marginally improved timing due to less combinational logic between counters.

Even though this approach uses multiple always blocks, the enable signals explicitly define the data flow, avoiding glitches or unintended dependencies.

This architecture also closely mimics a hardware ripple counter with controlled enables, which is a classic and well-understood structure.
