The original design is functionally correct and clean, with separate always blocks for seconds, minutes, and hours to reduce logic depth.

To optimize for better PPA (timing, power, and area), I propose the following improvements:

1. Combine the minutes and hours counters into one always block. Minutes and hours only update on the same condition (when seconds = 59), so combining avoids redundant clock enables and removes one always block, reducing area and logic.

2. Use synchronous reset only (remove asynchronous reset on RST). Asynchronous reset can increase timing complexity and routing overhead. Synchronous reset suffices for most FPGA/ASIC flows and generally improves timing.

3. Use nonblocking assignments consistently.

4. Instead of testing "Secs == 59" multiple times in different always blocks, store a wire or register for the wrap condition to simplify logic.

5. The Hours output is 6-bit in the interface, but range is 0-23; using 5 bits is sufficient and saves area and toggling. Keep Hours as 5 bits internally, and assign to 6-bit output port.

6. Use a single clock edge (posedge CLK) sensitivity and synchronous reset to improve timing.

7. These changes reduce area by one always block, reduce toggle in hours/minutes registers, and improve timing by simplifying the logic paths.

This combined block approach will slightly reduce maximum delay and power due to fewer state elements and logic layers. Overall, better PPA with no functional change.
