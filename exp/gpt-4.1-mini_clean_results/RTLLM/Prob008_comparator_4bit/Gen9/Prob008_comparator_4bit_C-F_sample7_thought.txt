To combine the best aspects of both examples, I will use vectorized operations for borrow generate (g) and propagate (p) signals to maintain concise and clear code from Example 1, while explicitly computing intermediate borrow signals to clarify the borrow lookahead chain as in Example 2. 

This fusion keeps the readability and synthesis friendliness of explicit borrow stages while leveraging vector operations to simplify signal definitions and equality checking. The equality check uses a reduction AND on XNOR of A and B, ensuring all bits match.

The output encoding remains mutually exclusive by deriving A_less from the borrow_out (borrow_4), A_equal from equality, and A_greater from their complement.

This approach results in clear, efficient, and easily optimizable combinational logic suitable for synthesis, with explicit borrow chain signals aiding timing optimization and debugging, and vector logic reducing code verbosity.

No always blocks or sequential logic are introduced; the design is purely combinational.
