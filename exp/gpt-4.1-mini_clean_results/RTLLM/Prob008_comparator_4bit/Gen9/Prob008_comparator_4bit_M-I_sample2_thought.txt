To optimize the existing 4-bit comparator for better PPA (power, performance, area), I will implement a more direct combinational logic method that avoids the borrow lookahead chain, which involves multiple levels of AND-OR gates and intermediate signals.

Instead, I will use bitwise comparison starting from the most significant bit (MSB) down to the least significant bit (LSB), using a priority-based approach that detects the first bit position where A and B differ:

- If at the highest differing bit A[i] > B[i], then A_greater=1
- If at the highest differing bit A[i] < B[i], then A_less=1
- If no bits differ (all bits equal), then A_equal=1

This approach is efficient and maps well to hardware as it naturally mimics hierarchical comparison, avoids subtraction or borrow propagation, and results in shorter critical paths.

This priority encoding also ensures mutually exclusive outputs, as only one of the conditions can be true.

The logic is purely combinational, no registers, and straightforward to synthesize efficiently.

This approach reduces gate delays compared to borrow lookahead and can yield smaller area and lower power by reducing logic depth and complexity.
