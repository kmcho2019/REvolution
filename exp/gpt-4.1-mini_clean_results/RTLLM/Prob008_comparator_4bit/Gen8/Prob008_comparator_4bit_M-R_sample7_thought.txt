Refactor the 4-bit comparator by expressing the borrow chain explicitly as a ripple borrow subtraction rather than a lookahead logic. This approach uses simple bitwise comparisons and borrow chaining combinationally, which can be easier to understand and synthesize for some tools. Additionally, equality is checked by direct comparison of A and B using reduction XOR. Outputs remain mutually exclusive.

The borrow chain is computed as:
- borrow[0] = ~A[0] & B[0]
- borrow[i+1] = (borrow[i] & (~A[i+1] | B[i+1])) | ((~A[i+1]) & B[i+1])

Finally:
- A_less = borrow[4] (final borrow out)
- A_equal = (A == B)
- A_greater = ~(A_less | A_equal)

This ripple borrow structure offers a clean bitwise combinational form as an alternative to the borrow lookahead tree in the original design.
