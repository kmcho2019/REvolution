Instead of using borrow generate and propagate signals and borrow lookahead logic, I will directly perform a 4-bit subtraction (A - B) using the Verilog subtraction operator in a combinational always block or continuous assignment. Then, the result's sign and zero status will determine the outputs: if the result is zero, A_equal=1; if the result is positive (most significant bit of the result is 0 and result != 0), A_greater=1; if negative (sign bit of result is 1), A_less=1.

This approach reduces complexity by avoiding borrow chains and multiple gate-level calculations while maintaining mutually exclusive outputs and correct functional behavior.

Note that subtraction in Verilog will yield a 4-bit result, but to detect sign properly, the subtraction result should be widened to 5 bits or use signed arithmetic. I'll use a 5-bit signed subtraction to correctly detect the sign.

All outputs are mutually exclusive and combinational.
