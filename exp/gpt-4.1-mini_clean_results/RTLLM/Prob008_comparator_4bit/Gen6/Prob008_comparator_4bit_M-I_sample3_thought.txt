Improvement Strategy:

The original design is logically correct and clean but uses a serial borrow chain that may slightly limit timing performance. For a 4-bit comparator, the timing impact is minimal, but we can improve the design by implementing parallel borrow generation logic instead of the sequential chain. This can reduce the critical path to just combinational logic per bit without cascading dependencies.

Approach:
- Use full subtractor borrow logic but compute borrow bits in parallel using generate signals or direct logic expressions.
- Alternatively, derive borrow signals directly from A and B without chaining.
- Since only the final borrow_out matters for comparison, we can implement the subtraction borrow_out as a combinational expression.
- Keep the equality check with bitwise XNOR and AND reduction for its simplicity and area efficiency.
- Outputs remain mutually exclusive as before.

By computing borrow_out directly for the MSB, we break the serial chain and potentially improve timing slightly with no area or power penalty for 4 bits.

Given the small scale, this parallel approach clarifies logic and improves scalability.

Implementation:
- Use intermediate generate and propagate signals:
   g[i] = ~A[i] & B[i]
   p[i] = ~(A[i] ^ B[i])
- borrow[0] = g[0]
- borrow[i] = g[i] | (p[i] & borrow[i-1]) for i=1..3
- For borrow[3], unroll the chain:
  borrow[3] = g[3] | (p[3] & g[2]) | (p[3]&p[2]&g[1]) | (p[3]&p[2]&p[1]&g[0])

This avoids ripple logic and gives a parallel borrow_out calculation.

Finally, outputs are derived from borrow[3] and eq_bits as before.

This optimization improves worst-case delay (wns) by eliminating the ripple chain, while maintaining low area and power for a 4-bit comparator.
