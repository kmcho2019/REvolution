This reduces redundant logic and logic depth. Also, implement a ripple borrow subtractor chain combinationally to detect the borrow at the end, which directly indicates if A < B. Use the subtraction result to determine equality (zero result) and A > B (no borrow and result non-zero). This matches the problem's original subtraction and borrow-based approach and is likely more area and power efficient due to shared carry/borrow chains and fewer repeated comparisons.

Steps:
1. Compute bitwise equality signals (eq_bit[i]) for each bit.
2. Build a ripple borrow subtractor chain: subtract B from A bit by bit, producing diff bits and borrow out.
3. Determine zero of the subtraction result by AND of negated diff bits.
4. Borrow out = 1 means A < B.
5. Borrow out = 0 and diff != 0 means A > B.
6. Zero means equality.
This ensures mutually exclusive outputs and uses efficient combinational logic.
