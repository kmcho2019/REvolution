To create a superior 4-bit comparator, I will fuse the best ideas from both examples by:

1. Using the clear and synthesis-friendly bitwise XNOR operator (~^) for per-bit equality signals, improving clarity and potentially aiding synthesis.

2. Retaining the hierarchical, factored borrow_out expression from Example 2:
   borrow_out = g3 | (p3 & (g2 | (p2 & (g1 | (p1 & g0))))
   This reduces gate count, logic depth, and switching activity, improving power and area.

3. Computing borrow generate (g) and propagate (p) signals directly from input bits, where p[i] = eq[i], maintaining conceptual clarity and reusing eq bits.

4. Using a balanced AND tree for equality checking as in Example 1, which slightly improves logic depth and helps timing:
   eq_01 = eq0 & eq1;
   eq_23 = eq2 & eq3;
   equal_bits = eq_01 & eq_23;

5. Ensuring mutually exclusive outputs (A_greater, A_equal, A_less) with minimal logic:
   A_less = borrow_out;
   A_equal = equal_bits;
   A_greater = ~(borrow_out | equal_bits);

6. Keeping all logic purely combinational with continuous assignments for clarity.

This fusion combines the clarity and XNOR usage from Example 1 with the optimized, hierarchical borrow chain from Example 2 and a balanced equality AND tree, achieving a good trade-off among power, area, and performance.
