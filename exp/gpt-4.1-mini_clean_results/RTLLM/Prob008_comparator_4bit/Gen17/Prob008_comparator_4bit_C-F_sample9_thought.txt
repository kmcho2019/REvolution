Fuse the strengths of both examples by combining a simplified equality computation using a direct reduction AND of bitwise XNORs, which minimizes signals and logic depth, and the factored borrow logic from Example 2, which reduces gate count and potential glitches.

Specifically:

- Use bitwise XNOR for equality bits and combine them into a single equality signal using the reduction AND operator (&), reducing explicit wires and simplifying the equality detection logic.
- Define generate (g) and propagate (p) signals for borrow logic as in both examples: g = (~A) & B, p = eq bits.
- Use the factored borrow_out chain as in Example 2 to minimize logic gates:
  borrow_out = g3 | (p3 & (g2 | (p2 & (g1 | (p1 & g0))))
- Outputs are mutually exclusive by design:
  - A_less = borrow_out
  - A_equal = equal_bits (reduction AND of eq)
  - A_greater = complement of (A_less | A_equal)
  
This fusion achieves clearer logic with fewer intermediate signals, reducing area and power, and ensures a structurally simple but efficient borrow chain, balancing delay and gate count.

This design is clean, concise, and provides a good compromise for a 4-bit comparator implementation.
