Instead of using borrow lookahead logic from subtraction, this design uses direct bitwise comparison starting from the MSB down to LSB to determine the relation between A and B. The idea is:

- Compare each bit from the most significant bit (bit 3) to the least significant bit (bit 0).
- At each bit position, if A[i] > B[i], then A > B regardless of lower bits.
- If A[i] < B[i], then A < B.
- If A[i] == B[i], continue checking the next bit.
- If all bits are equal, then A == B.

This approach implements a priority encoder-like structure with a combinational logic tree to determine the first bit where A and B differ, deciding the output accordingly.

This avoids borrow and generate/propagate signals and is conceptually simpler, relying on bitwise greater-than and less-than checks cascaded with equality checks.

The outputs remain mutually exclusive, and the logic is purely combinational.

This method also matches intuitive binary magnitude comparison and may synthesize well with priority mux or chained comparators.
