The original design already uses an efficient borrow chain and balanced equality logic. To optimize for better PPA (power, performance, area), I propose the following improvements:

1. Remove the redundant vector assignment for the propagate signals `p` and directly use the equality signals `eq_bit` because the propagation depends on bit equality, so `p` is essentially `eq_bit`.

2. Flatten the balanced AND tree into a direct reduction AND (`&eq_bit`) to reduce logic area and simplify synthesis, as for 4 bits the difference in glitches and delay is minimal.

3. Replace the explicit borrow chain with a ripple borrow subtraction logic using bitwise operations only, keeping the same logic but with less intermediate wires.

4. Use continuous assignments without generate blocks for per-bit equality since it's only 4 bits, improving tool optimization opportunities.

5. Combine the borrow logic into a small function for clarity and better synthesis, reducing redundant wires.

These changes reduce signal count and simplify the logic without sacrificing clarity or correctness. The borrow chain remains explicit but simplified, and equality checking uses a direct AND reduction. This should reduce area slightly and simplify timing paths with minimal impact on delay.

Outputs remain mutually exclusive: 
- A_less from final borrow,
- A_equal from equality,
- A_greater as complement of (less or equal).

Overall, this approach yields slightly smaller area and potential power savings while maintaining performance and clarity.
