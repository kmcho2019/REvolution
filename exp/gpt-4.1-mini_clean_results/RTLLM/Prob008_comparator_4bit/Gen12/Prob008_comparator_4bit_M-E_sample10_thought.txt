Instead of using borrow lookahead logic or direct subtraction, I will implement the 4-bit comparator by cascading four 1-bit comparators that compare each bit starting from the most significant bit (MSB) to the least significant bit (LSB). 

Each 1-bit comparator outputs signals indicating whether A is greater, equal, or less than B for that bit, assuming the higher bits are equal so far. By cascading these, the final outputs are determined combinationally based on the first bit position where A and B differ.

This approach models the comparison as a priority encoder of bit comparisons from MSB to LSB, which aligns well with hardware priority logic and is easy to understand and synthesize.

Advantages:
- Clear bitwise hierarchical decision making.
- Naturally mutual exclusive outputs, as only the first differing bit determines result.
- Uses simple bitwise logic and multiplexing without subtraction or borrow logic.

This design is fully combinational, with no borrow chain or subtraction logic, providing a structurally different approach from borrow lookahead or full subtraction.
