I will implement the 4-bit comparator by performing bitwise comparisons starting from the most significant bit (MSB) down to the least significant bit (LSB), using a chain of bit-level comparators that directly produce "greater," "equal," and "less" signals per bit. This approach avoids explicit subtraction and borrow calculations.

At each bit position, we determine if A[i] > B[i], A[i] == B[i], or A[i] < B[i]. The final outputs are resolved by prioritizing the MSB result: if MSB bits are unequal, their comparison decides the overall result; if equal, proceed to the next lower bit, and so on until LSB.

This approach is a classic cascade comparator that propagates equality down the chain and can be implemented purely combinationally with simple logic. It inherently produces mutually exclusive outputs, as only one condition can hold at the highest bit where A and B differ.

The advantages are:
- No explicit subtraction or borrow logic needed.
- Logic is simple, and often synthesized efficiently.
- Directly expresses the comparator concept in bitwise priority.
