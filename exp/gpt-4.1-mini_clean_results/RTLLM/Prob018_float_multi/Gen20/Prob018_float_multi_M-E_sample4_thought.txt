Implement a sequential 32-bit IEEE-754 floating-point multiplier with a microcoded FSM controlling each step over multiple cycles. Unlike fully pipelined designs, this FSM-based approach uses a shared datapath to reduce area by reusing the multiplier and logic across cycles, trading latency for area savings. The design will:
- Extract inputs and detect special cases immediately.
- Use a small internal 48-bit multiplier unit iteratively if needed (here, direct 24x24 multiply since synthesizable multipliers are typical).
- Normalize and round in a dedicated sequential step.
- Handle special cases explicitly in FSM states.
- Use a 3-bit cycle counter as the FSM state.
- Perform rounding with round-to-nearest-even logic.
- Output valid results after the sequence completes, holding output stable until reset or new input.
This approach reduces area by avoiding multiple pipeline registers and wide parallel datapaths while maintaining IEEE correctness, suitable for area-constrained implementations with relaxed latency requirements.
