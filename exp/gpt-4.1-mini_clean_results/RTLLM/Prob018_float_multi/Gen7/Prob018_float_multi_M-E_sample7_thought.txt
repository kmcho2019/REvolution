Implement a fully sequential multi-cycle floating-point multiplier to reduce critical path and area complexity by breaking the operation into smaller steps across multiple clock cycles.

Key design points:
- Use a cycle counter (3 bits) to sequence through extraction, special case detection, multiplication, normalization, rounding, and final packaging.
- Extract and register input fields (sign, exponent, mantissa) on cycle 0.
- Detect special cases (zero, infinity, NaN) early to bypass multiplication when possible.
- Perform the mantissa multiplication using a sequential shift-and-add multiplier over multiple cycles instead of a big combinational 24x24 multiplier. This reduces area and critical path.
- Normalize the product by shifting left until MSB is 1 or exponent underflows.
- Implement IEEE-754 rounding (round to nearest even) using guard, round, and sticky bits.
- Adjust exponent for normalization shifts and rounding overflows.
- Package the final sign, exponent, and mantissa on the last cycle.
- Use internal registers to hold intermediate results and the cycle counter to synchronize steps.
- Output remains registered and valid only on the last cycle.
This approach drastically reduces combinational complexity and spreads the multiplication load over several clock cycles, making the design suitable for low-area or power-sensitive environments while remaining IEEE-754 compliant.
