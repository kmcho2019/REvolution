Design a fully combinational IEEE-754 single-precision floating-point multiplier with an explicit state machine to control multi-cycle operation, focusing on a clear stepwise operation rather than deep pipelining.

This approach uses a three-phase process controlled by a 3-bit cycle counter:
1. Cycle 0: Extract and preprocess inputs, detect special cases (zero, inf, NaN), prepare mantissas with implicit bits.
2. Cycle 1: Multiply mantissas and add exponents with bias adjustment; determine preliminary sign.
3. Cycle 2: Normalize the product mantissa, perform rounding with guard, round, sticky bits, adjust exponent; finally handle special cases and assemble IEEE-754 output.

By controlling the operation with a cycle counter, the design achieves a balance between resource use and timing without heavy pipelining.

Intermediate signals and results are registered at each cycle, ensuring stable data for the next operation and easy debugging.

The design addresses all special cases explicitly, supports gradual underflow by zeroing too-small exponents, and correctly rounds to nearest even.

All logic is synchronous to the clock with reset support for proper initialization.

This modular approach supports clear understanding, easier verification, and low latency (3 cycles per multiply) with moderate area and power use.

Note: This design avoids a full pipeline, focusing on a multi-cycle FSM approach rather than fully pipelined high-frequency operation.
