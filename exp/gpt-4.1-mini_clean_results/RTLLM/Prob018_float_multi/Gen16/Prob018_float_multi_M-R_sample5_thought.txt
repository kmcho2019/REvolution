Refactor the float_multi module by implementing a 3-stage pipelined design using a finite state machine (FSM) controlled by the counter to manage operation sequencing. The extraction and special case detection will be combinational logic driven directly from inputs using continuous assignments and registered at the first stage. Mantissa multiplication and exponent addition will be triggered by the FSM's first state, with registered results moved to the next stage. Normalization, sticky/round/guard bit extraction, rounding, and final assembly are handled in subsequent states.

This approach replaces multiple pipelined registers for each signal with a centralized counter FSM and a mixture of combinational and sequential logic. It uses non-blocking registers for pipeline stages and keeps the arithmetic and rounding logic similar for correctness.

The benefits:
- More explicit control flow using FSM.
- Use of continuous assignments reduces combinational logic replication.
- Clear separation of pipeline stages using counter states.
- Simplified signal naming and grouping.
- The rounding and normalization steps remain explicit and separated.

Special cases are handled combinationally but the final output register updates only in the final FSM state, maintaining synchronization.

Overall, the design matches functionality with a different structural organization and improved clarity in control flow.
