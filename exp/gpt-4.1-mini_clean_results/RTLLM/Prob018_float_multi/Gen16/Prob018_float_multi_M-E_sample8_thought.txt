New Architectural Idea:

To implement an IEEE-754 single-precision floating-point multiplier with a novel architecture, I propose a fully combinational approach with a single cycle latency (no pipelining), but using a carefully modularized structure inside the always block. This approach targets simplicity and ease of formal verification rather than high throughput, suitable for designs where a single-cycle multiplier is acceptable.

Key points of this approach:

1. Fully combinational logic for multiplier output computed on the rising edge of clk, controlled by rst.
2. Extract sign, exponent, mantissa immediately.
3. Handle all special cases explicitly: zero, infinity, NaN, denormal.
4. Multiply mantissas as 24x24 bit unsigned integers (including the implicit leading bit for normalized numbers, or zero for denormals).
5. Adjust exponent sum carefully, considering bias and normalization shifts.
6. Normalize the product mantissa depending on the top bits of the product.
7. Compute rounding bits (guard, round, sticky) from the lower bits of the product.
8. Round using round-to-nearest-even.
9. Handle overflow and underflow by saturating to infinity or zero respectively.
10. Provide the output as a synchronous register updated each clock.

Advantages:

- Single-cycle latency and simple control.
- Clear and understandable state machine: effectively combinational calculation each clock.
- No pipeline registers or counters needed, minimizing register overhead.
- Easier to debug and verify, suitable for a smaller-scale or control-centric design.

Drawbacks:

- Likely longer critical path than pipelined versions, so lower max frequency.
- Higher power consumption due to no clock gating or pipelining.
- No throughput improvement via pipelining.

Overall, this architecture offers a clean and educational demonstration of IEEE-754 multiplication with correctness as priority.
