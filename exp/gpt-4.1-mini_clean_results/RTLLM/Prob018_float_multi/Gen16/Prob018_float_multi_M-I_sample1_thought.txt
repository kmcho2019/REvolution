To optimize the original multi-cycle float multiplier for better performance, power, and area (PPA), the following strategies are applied:

1. **Pipelining Mantissa Multiplication:**  
   The 24x24 multiplier is the critical path and large combinational logic. Splitting the multiplication across two clock cycles (partial products + accumulation) will shorten critical path per cycle, enabling higher clock frequency.

2. **Early Special Case Detection and Bypass:**  
   Detect special cases (NaN, Inf, zero) immediately and skip multiplication and normalization if either input falls into these categories. This saves power by avoiding unnecessary operations.

3. **Use Nonblocking Assignments and Separate Combinational Logic:**  
   Replace any blocking assignments inside sequential blocks with nonblocking for proper synthesis and clarity. Move rounding logic into a combinational always block for better structure.

4. **Reduce Signal Widths and Align Types:**  
   Tighten the exponent_sum width to 9 bits since max sum after bias subtraction fits in 9 bits. Mantissa product uses 48 bits, which is accurate for 24x24.

5. **Operand Isolation:**  
   Only perform multiplication when inputs are normal and nonzero/non-NaN/Inf, reducing toggling in large multiplier units, lowering power.

6. **Explicit pipeline stages via `counter` states (now 5 states):**  
   0: Input capture & special case detection  
   1: Partial mantissa multiplication (high half)  
   2: Partial mantissa multiplication (low half) and partial product accumulation  
   3: Normalization, rounding decision logic  
   4: Final result assembly and output  

This splits multiplication into two cycles and isolates it from normalization and rounding to minimize critical path and switching. This will trade slightly increased latency for improved max frequency and power savings.

The interface remains the same, still synchronous reset and single multiplier throughput.

This approach should significantly improve timing (reduce critical path in any cycle), reduce power by skipping computation for special cases, and reduce logic complexity for rounding and normalization steps.
