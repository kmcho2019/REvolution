Refactor the floating-point multiplier by implementing a simple 3-stage pipeline controlled by a small FSM driven by the counter. On each clock cycle, the module progresses through extraction and special case detection (cycle 1), multiplication and normalization (cycle 2), rounding and final output packing (cycle 3). This breaks the long combinational path into shorter ones and allows reusing internal registers instead of large combinational logic.

Main changes:
- Use the 3-bit counter as FSM to sequence the stages.
- Extract inputs into internal registers on cycle 0.
- In cycle 1, detect special cases and perform mantissa multiplication.
- In cycle 2, normalize, calculate exponent, perform rounding.
- In cycle 3, pack and output the final result.
- Use synchronous registers and procedural blocks instead of a large combinational always block.
- The output z is registered at the last cycle of the pipeline.
- This structure reduces combinational path delay per cycle, improves timing, and makes internal signals visible per stage.
