To simplify the IEEE-754 single-precision floating-point multiplier while maintaining correct functionality, I will use a single always block synchronous to clk and rst, processing the multiplication in a small finite state machine with a simple cycle counter. This will reduce pipeline complexity and registers.

Key simplifications:

1. Use a 3-cycle operation controlled by a counter:
   - Cycle 0: Extract sign, exponent, mantissa, and special flags.
   - Cycle 1: Multiply mantissas, add exponents, adjust exponent bias, and normalize if needed.
   - Cycle 2: Perform rounding, handle overflow/underflow, special cases, and output the final result.

2. Use straightforward combinational logic within cycles rather than elaborate pipelining.

3. Keep internal exponent widths to 10 bits to safely hold sums and overflow info.

4. Handle special cases explicitly at the output stage (cycle 2).

5. Implement rounding to nearest even with guard, round, and sticky bits.

6. Keep only necessary intermediate registers, use non-blocking assignments in sequential blocks.

This approach trades latency (3 cycles per multiplication) for significantly simpler control and code, easier verification, and smaller register footprint while keeping IEEE-754 compliance including special case handling and rounding.
