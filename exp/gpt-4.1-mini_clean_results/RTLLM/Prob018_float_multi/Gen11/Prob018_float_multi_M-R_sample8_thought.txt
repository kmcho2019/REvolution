Refactor the floating-point multiplier as a multi-cycle sequential design controlled by a small state machine using the counter variable. This approach breaks down the multiplication into smaller steps over multiple clock cycles to reduce combinational path delay and improve timing closure.

Key points of the refactoring:
- Use the counter as a 3-bit state machine that advances each clock cycle when not in reset.
- On the first cycle, extract and store inputs' sign, exponent, and mantissa parts.
- Detect special cases early and flag them for quick output.
- On the second cycle, perform mantissa multiplication (using registers to store intermediate results).
- On the third cycle, normalize and adjust the product, set rounding bits.
- On the fourth cycle, perform rounding and final exponent adjustment.
- On the fifth cycle, generate the final IEEE-754 output.
- Keep all intermediate signals as registers updated sequentially.
- Reset output to zero when rst is high.
- Use non-blocking assignments consistently for registers in sequential logic.
- This sequential multi-cycle approach reduces combinational complexity per cycle, allowing higher clock speeds.

This preserves the original algorithmic flow but breaks it into discrete steps controlled by the counter, providing more timing-friendly hardware and clearer signal sequencing.
