Refactor the design by splitting the pipeline stages into separate always blocks driven by the clock and reset, and move combinational logic outside always blocks with continuous `assign` statements where possible.

Use a 3-bit `counter` as a synchronous cycle counter instead of a 2-bit counter to provide more granularity and clarity for stage separation. This also explicitly synchronizes data movement stage-by-stage.

Special case detection and input extraction are done combinationally and latched at the first stage.

Intermediate signals are held in separate registers per stage and transferred synchronously at each clock edge in individual always blocks per stage. This improves code clarity and modularity.

Rounding and normalization logic is also split into combinational parts and registered at the output stage, ensuring clear data flow.

Output muxing based on special cases and overflow/underflow is done combinationally and latched in the output register.

This structure improves readability and modularity, making debugging and timing optimization easier without changing the fundamental pipeline organization or functionality.
