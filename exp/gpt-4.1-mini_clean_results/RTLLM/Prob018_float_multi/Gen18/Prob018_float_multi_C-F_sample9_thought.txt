Fusion Strategy:

I will combine the simplicity and clear 3-cycle state machine control from Example 1 with the deeper pipelining and timing/power optimizations from Example 2 to create a balanced 4-stage pipelined floating-point multiplier.

Key points of the fusion:

1. Use a 4-stage pipeline for balanced stages:
   - Stage 1: Extract sign, exponent, mantissa, and special flags.
   - Stage 2: Prepare exponent sum, sign, and special cases, register mantissas for multiplication.
   - Stage 3: Perform 24x24 multiplication with registered inputs and normalize the result.
   - Stage 4: Round the result, handle special cases, overflow/underflow, and generate final output.

2. This pipeline depth balances latency and timing, improving max frequency while keeping area reasonable compared to a 5-stage pipeline.

3. Implement sticky bit calculation using hierarchical reduction for low fan-in, as in Example 2.

4. Use non-blocking assignments consistently and split logic cleanly across always blocks for clarity and synthesis friendliness.

5. Keep exponents in 10-bit to catch overflow and underflow easily.

6. Include complete IEEE-754 single precision handling: special cases (NaN, Inf, zero), normalization, rounding-to-nearest-even with guard/round/sticky bits, overflow and underflow handling.

7. Remove the simple 3-bit counter; instead rely on pipeline registers to track data through stages, improving modularity and making the design more amenable to high-frequency operation.

8. Output is registered at the last stage.

The result is a synthesizable, pipelined IEEE-754 single-precision float multiplier, balancing complexity, performance, power, and area.
