I will implement a sequential (iterative) IEEE-754 single-precision floating-point multiplier that operates over multiple clock cycles instead of fully pipelined combinational logic. This design trades latency for simpler hardware, reduced area, and potentially lower power. Key points:

1. **Sequential Iterative Mantissa Multiplication:**  
   Instead of using a 24x24 multiplier in one cycle, we'll multiply mantissas using a shift-and-add approach over 24 cycles. This is a classic "non-restoring" style multiplier suited for resource-limited designs.

2. **State Machine Control:**  
   A simple FSM will manage extraction, multiplication, normalization, rounding, and output packing over several cycles.

3. **Simplified Internal Widths:**  
   Exponents will be handled as 10-bit signed numbers to accommodate exponent addition and bias correction without overflow.

4. **Special Cases on Input Load:**  
   Special cases like zero, infinity, NaN will be detected immediately and handled separately without going through the multiplication steps.

5. **Normalization and Rounding:**  
   After multiplication completes, normalization will shift the result if needed. Rounding uses round to nearest even by inspecting guard, round, sticky bits.

6. **Output Ready at Multiplication End:**  
   The output is produced once the iterative multiplication and rounding finish (around 30-35 cycles). This suits systems where throughput is less critical than area/power.

This novel sequential design reduces combinational path and resource usage at cost of latency, providing an alternative architectural tradeoff compared to fully pipelined designs.
