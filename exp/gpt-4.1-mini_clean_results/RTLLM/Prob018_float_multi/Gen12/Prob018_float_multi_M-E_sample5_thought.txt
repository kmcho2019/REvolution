Implement a fully combinational 3-stage sequential IEEE-754 single-precision floating-point multiplier with minimal pipeline registers and built-in explicit state counter for multi-cycle sequencing. Instead of fully pipelined stages with extensive pipeline registers for all intermediate signals, use a simple FSM controlled by a 3-bit cycle counter that guides the operation through phases: input capture and decoding, mantissa multiplication and exponent addition, normalization and rounding, and final output assembly.

Key innovations:
- Use a single multiplier operation split over clock cycles controlled by the counter; multiplier itself can be combinational but outputs only latched at the right cycle.
- Maintain minimal internal state: just mantissas, exponents, sign bits, product, rounding bits, and special flags.
- Combine special case detection with the cycle counter steps to perform conditional flows.
- Use direct bitwise operations to calculate sticky bits for rounding, minimizing logic depth.
- Implement rounding logic and normalization within clocked steps controlled by the counter, minimizing combinational path.
- Output final IEEE-754 formatted result on the last cycle.
- Reset clears internal registers and counter.
- This approach trades off latency (3-4 cycles per multiplication) for simplicity and potentially better timing by breaking combinational paths across cycles.
- It avoids full pipelining and thus area overhead of multiple duplicated registers.
- The module is easy to understand and verify, relying on explicit cycle control.

This design fits well into sequential logic environments where throughput is less critical but timing and area are important, or where a minimal-control multi-cycle floating-point multiplier is preferred over fully pipelined multiplier arrays.
