We will implement a non-pipelined, combinationally-driven floating-point multiplier that completes the entire IEEE-754 single-precision multiplication in one clock cycle but uses a FSM to handle sequencing and manage special cases distinctly. The approach involves:

1. **FSM Control:**
   - Use a 3-bit counter to step through stages inside the same clock domain.
   - At each cycle, extract, process, multiply, normalize, round, and output sequentially.

2. **Simplification:**
   - Perform special cases checks explicitly before multiplication.
   - Use a 48-bit product register (24x24 multiplication) but perform multiplication only once at counter=1.
   - Maintain separate registers for mantissas, exponents, signs, and control bits updated each cycle.

3. **Normalization & Rounding:**
   - Normalize the product mantissa based on the leading bits.
   - Extract guard, round, and sticky bits for rounding at the proper step.
   - Implement round-to-nearest-even rounding.

4. **Output Handling:**
   - Generate the final IEEE 754 result after rounding.
   - Handle overflow (set to inf), underflow (set zero), NaNs and zeros correctly.

This approach trades off latency (multi-cycle latency) for simpler combinational blocks, easing timing closure and reducing peak power and area usage compared to a fully combinational single-cycle design. The small state machine and sequential operations also simplify sticky bit generation and rounding control.

The internal signals correspond to the requirements and state machine stages that process multiplication step by step within the module, synchronized by clk and reset.
