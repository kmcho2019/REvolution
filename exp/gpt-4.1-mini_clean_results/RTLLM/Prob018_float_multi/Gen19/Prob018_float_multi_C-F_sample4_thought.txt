Fusion Strategy:

I will merge the clarity and modularity of the FSM-based approach (Example 2) with the deeper pipelining and explicit stage registers from Example 1 to create a 5-stage pipelined IEEE-754 single-precision floating-point multiplier.

Key points of fusion:

1. Retain Example 1's detailed 5-stage pipeline to reduce critical path (especially isolating the large 24x24 multiplier in its own stage) and improve timing and throughput.

2. Adopt Example 2’s clear naming and state-like stages concept by mapping pipeline stages to named always blocks and segregating the logic clearly.

3. Use non-blocking assignments throughout with registers per pipeline stage to improve synthesis and timing.

4. Keep special cases (NaN, Inf, Zero) checking early in the pipeline for clean handling and maintain the canonical NaN output.

5. Normalize with shifting and exponent adjustment like Example 1, while also extracting guard, round, sticky bits for rounding logic.

6. Implement round-to-nearest-even rounding at the final stage, handling mantissa overflow and exponent overflow cleanly.

7. Use hierarchical sticky bit calculation (Example 1) for better power and timing, rather than Example 2’s loop.

8. Use 10-bit internal exponents for safe overflow/underflow detection as in both examples.

9. Use a 3-bit counter to track pipeline progress, primarily for control and debugging as in Example 1, but focus on pipeline stages rather than FSM transitions.

The result is a clean, well-pipelined design with high throughput, reduced critical path delay, improved timing, and clear modular stages.

This approach balances the modular, FSM clarity of Example 2 and the fine pipeline granularity of Example 1, delivering better PPA while maintaining full IEEE-754 compliance.
