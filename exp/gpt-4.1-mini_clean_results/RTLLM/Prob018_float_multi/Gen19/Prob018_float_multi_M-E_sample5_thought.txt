I will implement the float_multi module as a multi-cycle datapath controlled by a simple 3-bit counter to break down the multiplication into distinct pipeline-like stages without a complex FSM. Instead of a four-state FSM, the 3-bit counter sequences through all necessary steps, making the logic modular and easier to pipeline or stall later if needed.

The stages will be:

- Cycle 0: Capture inputs and extract sign, exponent, and mantissa. Identify special cases early.
- Cycle 1: Prepare mantissas (add implicit leading 1 or zero), determine sign result, and add exponents subtracting bias.
- Cycle 2: Perform 24x24 mantissa multiplication producing a 48-bit product.
- Cycle 3: Normalize the product, extracting guard, round, and sticky bits.
- Cycle 4: Round the mantissa using round-to-nearest-even, adjust exponent accordingly, handle overflow/underflow.
- Cycle 5: Handle special cases (NaN, infinity, zero), and register final output.

This pipeline approach breaks the long combinational path of multiplication and normalization across cycles, easing timing constraints. Registers between stages store intermediate results, preventing long combinational chains.

The design uses registers to hold all intermediate signals, ensuring synchronous operation with the clock. It handles all IEEE-754 corner cases: zero, subnormal, infinity, NaN. Rounding is round-to-nearest-even.

This approach is simple, uses a linear counting scheme instead of FSM encoding, and isolates complex operations to individual cycles, improving clarity and timing. It also facilitates future enhancements like stalling or extending pipeline depth.

I will define all internal signals with appropriate widths and implement the logic step-by-step driven by the counter. The output z is registered at the last stage. Reset zeroes all registers and sets counter to zero.

This design style is distinct from the FSM approach requested initially.
