Refactor the float_multi module to use a small FSM with explicit states for sequencing the operations, rather than a fixed 4-stage pipeline with separate stage registers. Use combinational `assign` statements for decoding inputs and extracting mantissas, signs, and exponents to simplify and clarify logic. Use sequential logic only for synchronization, multiplication, normalization, rounding, and output generation.

This approach emphasizes clear separation of control and data path, easier to follow state transitions, and reduces the number of pipeline registers required to track special flags across stages by storing them in a single state-register context.

Keep the multiplication in one cycle (assuming multiplier latency), then normalize and round in next cycles, updating exponent and mantissa accordingly. Rounding logic and special case handling are performed in dedicated states, ensuring combinational complexity is confined within cycles, which can help timing closure.

This FSM structure also facilitates expanding or modifying the multiplier functionality (e.g., adding latency states) and easier debug by tracing states.

The output `z` is updated only in the DONE state, ensuring all computations are complete.

The internal signals like `a_mantissa`, `b_mantissa`, `z_mantissa`, `a_exponent`, `b_exponent`, `z_exponent`, `a_sign`, `b_sign`, `z_sign`, and intermediate product and rounding bits are maintained as registers updated according to FSM state transitions.
