To simplify the IEEE-754 32-bit floating-point multiplier while maintaining functionality, the design will be implemented in a single sequential always block with a small FSM-like counter controlling the operation steps across multiple clock cycles. This approach reduces pipeline registers and complexity.

Key simplifications:
- Use a cycle counter to sequence operation stages: input decoding, multiplication, normalization, rounding, and output generation.
- Avoid multi-stage pipelining and large register arrays; use fewer internal signals updated in steps.
- Extract sign, exponent, and mantissa with implicit bit once.
- Detect special cases (NaN, Inf, zero) and handle them directly during output generation.
- Multiply mantissas using 24x24 multiplication producing 48-bit product.
- Normalize product by checking MSB and shifting mantissa and adjusting exponent accordingly.
- Implement round-to-nearest-even using guard, round, and sticky bits extracted from product.
- Handle exponent overflow (set to Inf) and underflow (flush to zero).
- Final output assembled as IEEE-754 format in 'z' register after all steps complete.
- The design uses simple registers and combinational logic inside sequential blocks only, making it clear and easy to verify.

This approach is easier to understand and maintain, and avoids complex pipelining while still providing correct IEEE-754 multiplication behavior, including handling special cases, normalization, rounding, and overflow/underflow.
