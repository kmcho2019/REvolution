Implement a sequential, multi-cycle IEEE-754 single-precision floating-point multiplier using a small FSM and a cycle counter. The multiplication of the 24-bit mantissas will be done using a shift-and-add iterative approach to reduce area and power compared to a full 24x24 combinational multiplier.

Key points:
- Use a 3-bit counter to step through stages:
  0: Input capture and special case detection.
  1: Mantissa multiplication using an iterative shift-and-add approach over several cycles (handle 24 bits of multiplier).
  2: Normalization and rounding.
  3: Output packaging and signaling completion.

- The mantissa multiplication iteratively shifts and adds the multiplicand based on multiplier bits over multiple cycles, avoiding a large multiplier.

- The exponent and sign are computed upfront, adjusted after multiplication and normalization.

- Rounding uses round-to-nearest-even, utilizing guard, round, and sticky bits extracted from the product.

- Special cases (zero, infinity, NaN) are prioritized and produce the correct output immediately.

- The sequential nature reduces peak combinational delay and power consumption at the cost of some latency.

- Output is registered and updated at the final stage.

This approach trades latency for reduced area and power, making it suitable for resource-constrained environments or lower clock frequency designs.
