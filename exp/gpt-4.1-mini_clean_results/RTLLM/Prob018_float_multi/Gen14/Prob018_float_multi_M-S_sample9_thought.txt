Simplify the floating-point multiplier by implementing a small multi-cycle FSM using a 3-bit counter to step through extraction, multiplication, normalization, rounding, and output assembly sequentially over multiple clock cycles. 

This approach breaks down complex combinational logic into smaller pieces per cycle, reducing combinational path length and easing synthesis. Use registers for internal signals updated at each step.

Key steps:

1. On reset, clear counter and output.
2. At cycle 1, extract signs, exponents, mantissas, and detect special cases.
3. At cycle 2, multiply mantissas and add exponents adjusting bias.
4. At cycle 3, normalize the product and adjust exponent.
5. At cycle 4, perform rounding and finalize exponent and mantissa.
6. At cycle 5, handle special cases, set the output and hold until reset or new inputs.

This design trades latency for simpler logic per cycle and registers internal states, making the critical path shorter per clock cycle and easier to meet timing, suitable for low-to-medium frequency and resource-limited designs.

It maintains full IEEE-754 compliance including NaN, infinity, zero, denormal handling, normalization, rounding to nearest even, overflow/underflow detection, and correct sign calculation.
