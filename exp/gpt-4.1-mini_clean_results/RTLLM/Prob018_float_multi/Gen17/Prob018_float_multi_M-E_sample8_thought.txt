Implement a fully combinational IEEE-754 single-precision floating-point multiplier that operates in one clock cycle and outputs the result registered on the clock edge. To keep latency low and avoid multi-cycle FSM complexity, perform all operations combinationally with registered inputs and outputs synchronized by the clock.

The design steps:
- On clock rising edge, capture inputs and decompose into sign, exponent, and mantissa.
- Immediately in combinational logic, determine special cases (NaN, Inf, zero, denormals).
- Compute sign XOR, add exponents minus bias, and multiply mantissas with implicit leading bits.
- Normalize the product mantissa and adjust exponent accordingly.
- Implement IEEE-754 round-to-nearest-even rounding using guard, round, and sticky bits.
- Handle overflow (set to Inf), underflow (set to zero), and special cases.
- Register the final assembled 32-bit IEEE-754 output.

This approach avoids multi-cycle state machines, reduces control complexity, and gives a single-clock-cycle latency multiplier suitable for high-performance datapaths. Internal signals like mantissa product and rounding bits are all combinational expressions. The pipeline boundary is the input and output registers clocked on clk.

While the combinational logic is large (e.g., 24x24-bit multiplier), this is common in high-performance floating-point units. For area/power tradeoffs, multiplier can be replaced by dedicated hardware or partial pipelining outside this module.

Internal signals a_mantissa, b_mantissa, product, exponents, signs, and rounding bits are registers to keep synchrony and clear code organization. No FSM or counters are used, simplifying control.

This design suits systems prioritizing throughput and low latency over area or multi-cycle sequencing.
