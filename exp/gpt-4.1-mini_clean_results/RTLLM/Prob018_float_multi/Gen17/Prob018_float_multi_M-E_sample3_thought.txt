Implement a multi-cycle IEEE-754 single-precision floating-point multiplier using a sequential FSM controlled by a 3-bit counter. The operation unfolds over several clock cycles, enabling simpler combinational logic per cycle and better timing performance.

Key features of this design:
- Inputs are latched at cycle 1, and internal registers store sign, exponent, and mantissa fields.
- Special cases (NaN, infinity, zero) are detected early and stored; if special cases are found, the module immediately prepares the final output without unnecessary computation.
- The mantissa multiplication (24x24 bits) is performed in one cycle, saving a cycle for normalization.
- Normalization and exponent adjustment happen in the next cycle, including rounding based on guard, round, and sticky bits.
- The sign is computed once at the beginning and maintained.
- The output is produced on the last cycle and held until next operation or reset.
- The counter advances each clock unless reset, providing a clear operation pipeline with a 4-cycle latency.
- The design uses explicit state sequencing and registers for each step, trading latency for better frequency and lower combinational complexity per cycle.
- Rounding uses round-to-nearest-even, the IEEE default.

This approach allows balancing between timing and resource use, facilitates debugging and testing, and supports future expansion (e.g., pipelining or extension to double precision).
