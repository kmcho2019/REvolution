To simplify the float_multi module while keeping full IEEE-754 compliance, I will:

1. Keep the 3-cycle operation controlled by a small cycle counter for clarity and ease of understanding.
2. Use direct field extraction and simple special case flags at cycle 0.
3. Perform the 24x24 mantissa multiplication and exponent addition at cycle 1.
4. At cycle 2, normalize the product, generate rounding bits, perform rounding-to-nearest-even, handle exponent overflow/underflow, and form the final IEEE-754 output.
5. Handle all special cases (NaN, infinity, zero, zero*inf) explicitly at cycle 2 with simple if-else checks.
6. Use concise and straightforward registers and logic without extra intermediate signals to reduce code complexity.
7. Use non-blocking assignments only in the sequential always block.
8. Avoid unnecessary wider signals and keep exponent and mantissa widths minimal but sufficient.
9. Keep rounding implementation basic but correct using guard, round, sticky bits.

This approach balances simplicity and correctness without adding pipeline stages or additional control logic, preserving the functional requirements.
