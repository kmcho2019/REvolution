I will design a fully combinational 32-bit IEEE-754 single-precision floating-point multiplier that completes the entire multiplication in one clock cycle (no multi-cycle FSM). To manage complexity and timing, the design modularizes each step combinationally:

- Extract sign, exponent, mantissa (with implicit bit) directly from inputs.
- Detect special cases (zero, infinity, NaN) using combinational logic.
- Compute sign as XOR of inputs.
- Compute exponent sum with bias correction and handle exponent overflow/underflow.
- Multiply mantissas (24x24 bits) combinationally producing 48 bits.
- Normalize the mantissa by detecting the highest bit and adjusting exponent accordingly.
- Implement IEEE-754 round-to-nearest-even rounding using guard, round, sticky bits.
- Handle special cases to output NaN/Inf/Zero correctly.
- Output the final 32-bit IEEE-754 floating-point number immediately.

This single-cycle, combinational approach emphasizes simplicity in control by eliminating FSM and counters, making it suitable for a combinational multiplier stage or short-path combinational block in a pipelined processor.

This design sacrifices latency (must meet timing on a single cycle) but gains simplicity and directness, ideal for high-frequency multipliers with pipelining at higher levels.

All intermediate signals are wires for purely combinational processing, and the clock and reset are used only for registering the output. The output register ensures synchronous output availability.

This approach enables easy insertion into synchronous designs with guaranteed correct IEEE-754 multiplication results in one cycle, relying on synthesis tools to optimize combinational delay.
