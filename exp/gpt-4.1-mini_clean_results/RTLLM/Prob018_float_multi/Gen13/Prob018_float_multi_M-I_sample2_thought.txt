To improve the original fully combinational design, which has a long critical path due to a large 24x24 multiplier and extensive combinational logic, I will implement a multi-cycle sequential floating-point multiplier using a simple FSM and a cycle counter. 

Key improvements:

- Split the multiplication into separate stages clocked across several cycles to shorten critical path per cycle.
- Use internal registers for mantissa, exponent, and sign extraction on the first cycle.
- Use a 24x24 multiplier once, store the product, and then do normalization and rounding in subsequent cycles.
- Use a 3-bit cycle counter for sequencing the operation steps.
- Handle special cases at the start and produce output at the end of the operation.
- The shorter combinational logic per cycle allows for a higher clock frequency.
- Reset logic will clear state and outputs properly.
- Output is updated only after the final cycle.

This approach trades some latency for improved timing, power, and area characteristics by breaking the logic into smaller, manageable steps synchronized by the clock and counter.

I will keep signals as per the user's request: a_mantissa, b_mantissa, z_mantissa as 24 bits; exponents as 10 bits internally to avoid overflow; product as 50 bits to hold multiplication results; guard, round, sticky bits for rounding; counter as 3 bits for sequencing. 

Special cases (NaN, Inf, Zero) will be detected and handled early to avoid unnecessary operations.

Normalization and rounding steps are done in later cycles to reduce combinational path.

Final output will be formed at the last cycle, and output register updated accordingly.

This design should improve max frequency and reduce power by limiting heavy operations to a single cycle rather than one big combinational block.
