Implement a fully combinational IEEE-754 single-precision floating-point multiplier with a single-cycle latency (besides input/output registers), optimizing for minimal pipeline complexity and clean modular arithmetic. 

Key points:
- Inputs are registered only once at the clock edge to reduce metastability.
- All calculations (special case detection, mantissa multiplication, exponent adjustment, normalization, rounding) occur combinationally within the clock cycle.
- Use a 25x25 multiplier (adding 1 explicit bit to mantissa for implicit leading one and an additional bit for safe rounding margin) to directly get a 50-bit product.
- Handle special cases (NaN, infinity, zero, denormals) upfront combinationally to generate correct output immediately.
- Normalize the product based on the highest two bits of the product (bit 49 and 48) to determine shift needed.
- Perform round-to-nearest-even rounding using guard, round, sticky bits derived from the product.
- Output register updates synchronously, minimizing latency to one clock cycle after inputs are stable and rst is inactive.
- Avoid FSM and pipelining states; instead, use combinational logic and registered outputs for simplicity and lower control overhead.
- Use extra exponent width internally (10 bits) for overflow/underflow detection.
- This architecture favors area and latency at the cost of combinational path depth, suitable when targeting moderately fast clock rates or when a single-cycle result is preferred.

This approach is conceptually simpler, easily testable, and cleanly handles special cases and rounding within one combinational block, producing the output on the next clock cycle.
