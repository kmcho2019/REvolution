The original design correctly implements a pipelined IEEE-754 single-precision floating-point multiplier with comprehensive handling of special cases, normalization, rounding, and overflow/underflow scenarios. However, the reported PPA metrics suggest moderate timing margin (WNS ~ -1.56 ns), decent power, and relatively large area due to wide intermediate buses and propagation of many flag signals across all pipeline stages.

To optimize for better Power, Performance, and Area (PPA), the following improvements will be made:

1. **Reduce internal exponent width from 10 bits to 9 bits**:
   - The exponent sum range fits within 9 bits (max ~ 254 + overflow), reducing register width and combinational logic cost.
   - Keep bias and intermediate calculations consistent without unnecessary bit widening.

2. **Minimize propagation of status flags and sign bits**:
   - Use a single pipeline register to hold combined special case indicators instead of propagating multiple individual flags.
   - This reduces flip-flop count and switching activity.

3. **Move rounding combinational logic into sequential block (Stage 3)**:
   - Instead of an always @(*) block, implement rounding logic within the clocked always block, breaking the long combinational path.
   - This improves timing and timing predictability.

4. **Optimize sticky bit calculation**:
   - Reduce width of sticky bit computation by truncating product bits beyond precision needed for rounding, decreasing switching and logic depth.

5. **Use non-blocking assignments consistently in sequential blocks**:
   - To follow best synthesis and simulation practices.

6. **Compact the pipeline into 3 stages if possible**:
   - Combine certain operations to reduce pipeline overhead, balancing latency and throughput.

7. **Clarify naming and code structure for maintainability**.

The improved design retains full IEEE-754 compliance and special case handling while improving timing margins and reducing area and power through signal width reductions, clocked rounding, and simplified flag propagation.

This approach will yield better clock frequency potential, lower power through fewer toggling signals and registers, and reduced area from narrower buses and simplified control logic.
