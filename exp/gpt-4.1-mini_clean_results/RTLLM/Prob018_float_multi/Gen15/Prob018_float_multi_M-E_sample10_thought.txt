I will implement a non-pipelined, fully combinational 32-bit IEEE-754 single-precision floating-point multiplier with synchronous output register. The design proceeds in a single cycle after inputs are latched. This architecture trades latency for simplicity and minimal pipeline control overhead.

Key ideas:
- Input operands are latched synchronously.
- Operand fields (sign, exponent, mantissa) are extracted combinationally.
- Special cases (zero, infinity, NaN) are detected and handled combinationally.
- Mantissas are expanded with implicit leading 1 for normalized numbers, or zero for denormals.
- Mantissas are multiplied using a single 24x24 multiplier.
- The exponent sum is calculated with bias adjustment.
- Normalization is performed by checking the MSB of the product.
- Rounding uses round-to-nearest-even, extracting guard, round, and sticky bits directly.
- Overflow and underflow are handled by saturating to infinity or zero, respectively.
- The final result is assembled combinationally and registered synchronously.
- A small counter is used to track the single-cycle process for clarity.
  
This approach has the advantage of a simple control path, easier verification, and smaller register overhead. The main critical path includes the 24x24 mantissa multiplication and normalization. Since it's single-cycle, timing optimization is critical, but it can be used in lower frequency or with retiming/logic optimization. It also simplifies debug since no multiple pipeline stages or flags propagation are required.

The outputs update synchronously on the clock after inputs are stable. Reset clears the output register.

This design uses minimal internal registers:
- Counter (3-bit) for state machine with idle (wait for valid inputs) and compute states.
- Registers for latched inputs and final output.
- Combinational logic for all the calculations.

This novel approach contrasts with deeply pipelined or staged designs by focusing on simplicity and direct calculation with registered inputs and outputs.
