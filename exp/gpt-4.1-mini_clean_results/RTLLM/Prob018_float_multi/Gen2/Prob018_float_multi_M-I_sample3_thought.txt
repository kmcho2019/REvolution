To meet the problem requirements strictly, including the internal counter and cycle-by-cycle operation, and to handle all IEEE 754 special cases with proper normalization, rounding, and gradual underflow, I will implement a multi-cycle design controlled by a 3-bit counter. The process will be:

- Cycle 0: Reset or idle, waiting for inputs.
- Cycle 1: Extract sign, exponent, mantissa; detect special cases.
- Cycle 2: Multiply mantissas, add exponents, combine signs.
- Cycle 3: Normalize and adjust exponent accordingly.
- Cycle 4: Perform rounding (round-to-nearest-even) with guard, round, sticky bits.
- Cycle 5: Final adjustments, handle overflow, underflow, and special cases; prepare output.
- Cycle 6: Output the result, hold until reset or new input.

This pipeline-like staged approach fits the internal signals and counter requirement, and allows stable registered outputs.

Additionally, the design will handle gradual underflow producing subnormal numbers if exponent after rounding underflows but is still >= -23 (mantissa bits), otherwise zero.

Sticky bit calculation will be exact. Sign will be registered and synchronized.

This approach splits the complexity into well-defined steps improving timing and correctness, and is fully synchronous.

I will define all internal regs for intermediate states and properly implement each stage per the IEEE 754 single-precision multiplication rules, carefully adjusting exponent biases and rounding to nearest even.

Reset sets counter and output zero.

This design fully matches the problem description and is robust for all corner cases.
