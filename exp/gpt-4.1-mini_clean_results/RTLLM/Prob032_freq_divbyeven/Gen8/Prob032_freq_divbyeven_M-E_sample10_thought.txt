A novel approach to the even frequency divider is to use a toggle flip-flop driven by a counter that counts up to NUM_DIV/2 - 1 and then toggles the output clock. Instead of using a single counter that resets and toggles clk_div, we can create a two-stage process:

1. A counter that counts from 0 up to (NUM_DIV/2)-1.
2. When the counter reaches this value, it resets to zero and toggles a toggle flip-flop (clk_div).

This approach explicitly separates counting and output toggling, making it clearer and easy to modify. Also, I will implement the counter with an explicit width derived from NUM_DIV, and I will use a parameter check at synthesis time to ensure NUM_DIV is even and >= 2.

The output clk_div will be initialized to 0 on reset.

The counter width is dynamically calculated to minimize register size.

This design differs from the given example by explicitly using a toggle flip-flop register separate from the counter register, emphasizing clarity and modularity. Also, the counter is always reset on reaching the terminal count rather than being conditionally incremented, which simplifies timing and reduces combinational logic.

No assertions or generate blocks will be used, for simplicity and compatibility with older synthesis tools.
