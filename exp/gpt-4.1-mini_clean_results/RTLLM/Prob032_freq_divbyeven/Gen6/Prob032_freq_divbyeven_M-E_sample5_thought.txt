To implement an even-frequency divider in a new architectural style, I will use a modulo counter that counts from 0 up to NUM_DIV - 1 and directly derive the output clock from the MSB (most significant bit) of this counter.

Since NUM_DIV is even, dividing the input frequency by NUM_DIV can be achieved by counting through NUM_DIV clock cycles and toggling the output every NUM_DIV/2 cycles, which means the MSB of a counter that counts modulo NUM_DIV toggles at exactly half the period.

Therefore, by using a binary counter that counts 0 to NUM_DIV-1, and setting clk_div as the MSB bit of this counter, I obtain the desired frequency division without explicit toggling or manual toggling logic.

This approach simplifies logic, reduces flip-flops required, and inherently guarantees a 50% duty cycle output clock.

The design features:
- A parameterizable NUM_DIV (even number).
- A synchronous active-low reset.
- A counter of sufficient width to count 0 to NUM_DIV-1.
- clk_div output assigned as the MSB of the counter.
- Compile-time check ensures NUM_DIV is even.

This architecture is efficient, clean, and uses the natural binary counting scheme to generate the divided clock.
