Instead of storing only the previous value of 'a' and detecting edges by comparing current and previous states in a single cycle, use a small two-stage shift register to sample 'a' across two consecutive clock cycles. The edge detection logic then compares these two delayed samples to identify rising and falling edges. This approach slightly decouples sampling from edge detection, potentially improving timing by spreading logic across registers. The outputs rise and down are registered signals that pulse high for one clock cycle following the detected edge, then return low until the next edge. The asynchronous reset initializes all registers. Non-blocking assignments ensure synchronous behavior and prevent race conditions.
