The original design uses a 2-stage synchronizer for input 'a' and combinational logic for edge detection, producing single-cycle pulses on rise and down signals. However, the problem statement specifies that after detecting an edge, the rise or down signal should be set to 1 on the next clock and then remain at 1 until the corresponding edge appears again (i.e., outputs are held until next edge).

To meet this requirement more precisely, I will modify the design to use flip-flops to store the output signals rise and down, setting them to 1 when the respective edge is detected and clearing them only when the opposite edge appears. This ensures that rise and down signals are latched after the detected edge and remain asserted until cleared by the opposite edge, meeting the user's specification.

For PPA optimization:

- The synchronizer remains a 2-stage flip-flop chain for metastability protection.
- The rise and down outputs are registered and hold their state as required.
- The edge detection logic remains simple.
- The design avoids unnecessary toggling by only changing rise/down when edges occur.
- This method trades off slightly more area and power due to the output registers but complies exactly with the user's requirement.

This modification ensures correctness according to the problem statement while keeping the logic minimal and synchronous.
