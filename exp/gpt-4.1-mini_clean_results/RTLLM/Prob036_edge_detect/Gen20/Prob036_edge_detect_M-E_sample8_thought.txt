Instead of just delaying the input signal once and comparing the current and previous values, this design uses a two-stage shift register to delay the input 'a' by two clock cycles. By comparing the two delayed signals, we can detect edges with a cycle latency of one clock cycle, and then latch the detected edge in a simple stateful way to ensure rise or down outputs remain asserted for exactly one clock cycle after the edge is detected.

The idea is:
- Use two flip-flops to shift the input 'a', obtaining a_d1 (1-cycle delayed) and a_d2 (2-cycle delayed).
- Detect edges by comparing a_d1 and a_d2.
- When an edge is detected, set rise or down registers to 1.
- rise and down outputs are registered signals that stay high for exactly one clock cycle after detection and then clear automatically.
- This approach adds a minimal pipeline stage and state, which can improve timing and provides a clear pulse-generation mechanism, avoiding purely combinational outputs.

The reset is synchronous active low to simplify timing and synthesis.  
This design is fully synchronous and meets the requirement of one clock-cycle output pulse synchronized to clk.
