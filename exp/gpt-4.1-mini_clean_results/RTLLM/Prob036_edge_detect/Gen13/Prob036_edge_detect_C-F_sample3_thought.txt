To fuse the best ideas from both examples, I will:

1. Register the input signal 'a' to synchronize it to the clock domain and avoid metastability.
2. Generate rising and falling edges by comparing the current and previous values of 'a' (i.e., a_reg and a).
3. Unlike the examples which drive 'rise' and 'down' as combinational wires, I will register these outputs. This ensures:
   - 'rise' and 'down' signals are asserted synchronously with the clock.
   - They remain high for exactly one full clock cycle after the edge is detected, avoiding glitches.
   - Better timing closure and output signal stability.
4. On reset, initialize all registers to zero.
5. Overall, this approach still uses minimal logic and registers (three flip-flops: one for input sampling and two for outputs), keeping the design clear, efficient, and robust.

This fusion satisfies the requirement that rise and down signals are set to 1 on the clock following an edge and remain 1 for exactly one cycle, then return to 0 until the next edge appears. This matches the spec more strictly than combinational outputs in the examples.
