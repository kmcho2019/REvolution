Fuse the two previous designs by registering the input signal 'a' on the positive clock edge with asynchronous active-low reset, storing it in 'a_prev'. Then generate the rise and down signals as registered outputs that assert for exactly one clock cycle upon detecting the respective edges. This approach eliminates potential glitches caused by combinational outputs directly derived from 'a' and 'a_prev', ensuring synchronous and stable one-clock-cycle pulses for rise and down. It also enhances power efficiency by avoiding glitches on outputs, and improves clarity by explicitly registering outputs rather than relying on combinational signals. The tradeoff is a small increase in area (two extra flip-flops for rise and down), but this improves robustness, power, and timing quality.

Implementation details:
- On reset, 'a_prev', 'rise', and 'down' are cleared.
- On each clock, 'a_prev' captures 'a'.
- If a rising edge is detected (a_prev=0 and a=1), 'rise' is set to 1; otherwise, 'rise' clears to 0.
- If a falling edge is detected (a_prev=1 and a=0), 'down' is set to 1; otherwise, 'down' clears to 0.

This synchronous pulse output design ensures that rise and down are each asserted for one clock cycle immediately after the edge is detected, meeting the specification of outputs being "set to 1 on the next clock when the corresponding edge appears, and then return to 0 until the next edge".

This solution is superior by avoiding combinational output glitches, maintaining precise one-cycle pulses, and keeping the design simple and fully synchronous.
