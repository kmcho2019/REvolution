Instead of assigning outputs as wires, register the outputs rise and down to ensure they are set to 1 for exactly one clock cycle following the detected edge, and then automatically cleared to 0 in the next cycle without requiring external intervention. This slightly increases the number of registers but reduces combinational logic on outputs and aligns outputs as registered synchronous pulses, which is often preferred in FPGA or ASIC designs for timing and glitch avoidance. The edge detection logic is straightforward: compare delayed and current 'a' to detect rising or falling edges.
