Instead of directly comparing the current input 'a' with the previous value stored in a register, use a two-stage shift register to delay the input signal by two clock cycles. This allows edge detection by comparing two consecutive delayed versions of 'a'. Specifically, rising edge is detected when the first delayed sample is 0 and the second delayed sample is 1, and falling edge when the first delayed sample is 1 and the second is 0. 

The outputs rise and down are registered and updated synchronously with clk, and held high for one clock cycle only when their corresponding edge is detected. This design leverages simple pipelining, avoids combinational logic on outputs, and may provide better timing and glitch immunity.

Reset initializes the delay registers to 0 to ensure a known start state.
