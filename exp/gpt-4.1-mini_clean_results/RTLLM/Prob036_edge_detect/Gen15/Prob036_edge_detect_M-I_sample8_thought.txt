The original design is already minimal and synthesizable, detecting edges via a registered delayed input and simple combinational logic, producing single-cycle pulses synchronized to clk. 

To optimize for better PPA while preserving functionality, consider:

- Changing asynchronous reset to synchronous reset: This can improve timing closure and reduce complexity on FPGA/ASIC flows since asynchronous resets add reset routing and glitch risks. A synchronous reset is typically better for timing, especially with slow input signals.

- Use nonblocking assignment consistently.

- Add a registered output stage for 'rise' and 'down' signals, instead of purely combinational outputs from logic, to avoid any glitches and ensure clean timing at outputs. This reduces potential glitches and improves timing/power by better synchronizing output transitions.

- The registered outputs will be set to 1 on the clock cycle after detecting edges and cleared on the next cycle if no new edge is detected, still providing one-cycle pulses.

- This modification trades minimal increase in registers for improved robustness and timing.

- The rest of the logic remains the same.

This strategy should improve timing and glitch power without increasing area noticeably and keep the outputs synchronous and glitch-free.
