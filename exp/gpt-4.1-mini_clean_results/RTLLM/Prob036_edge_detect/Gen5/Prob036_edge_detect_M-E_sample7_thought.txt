Instead of using a delayed version of 'a' and detecting edges by comparing the current and delayed signals, this design will implement a two-stage synchronizer and edge detector together.

The approach is:

- Use two flip-flops in series to sample and synchronize the asynchronous input 'a' (useful if 'a' might be asynchronous to 'clk').

- Detect rising and falling edges by comparing these two synchronized signals: when the lower stage flips from 0 to 1 (rising edge) or from 1 to 0 (falling edge).

- Generate one-cycle pulses 'rise' and 'down' for those edges.

- Use a synchronous active-low reset for better timing and simpler reset logic.

This method improves metastability robustness for asynchronous 'a' and isolates the synchronization and edge detection, making the design modular and clean.

It also avoids having separate registers for delayed 'a' and output signals; instead, the output signals are directly derived from the two-stage synchronized signals in the same sequential always block, making it structurally different from typical single-delay approaches.
