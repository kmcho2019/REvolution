The original design is already minimal and efficient for edge detection, producing clean one-cycle pulses synchronized to clk. To further improve robustness and avoid any potential glitches on 'rise' and 'down' outputs, explicitly registering these outputs can be done. This will make both outputs fully synchronous to clk with outputs set to 1 for exactly one clock cycle after the corresponding edge detection, then returning to 0 until the next edge.

This approach adds two flip-flops but improves signal integrity and timing closure, which can be beneficial in noisy or high-frequency environments. The trade-off is a slight increase in area and power, but with more reliable output signals. This is a good practice in professional synchronous designs.

The improvement is to:
- Register the 'rise' and 'down' signals inside an always block synchronized with clk and rst_n.
- Keep the previous value of 'a' in a register as before.
- Generate combinational signals for edge detection and latch them to the registered outputs.

This change slightly increases area and power but enhances timing robustness and signal stability.
