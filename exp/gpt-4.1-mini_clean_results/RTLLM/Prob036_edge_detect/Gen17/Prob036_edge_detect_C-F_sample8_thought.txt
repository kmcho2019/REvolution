To meet the specification that 'rise' and 'down' signals are set to 1 on the clock cycle after the edge appears and remain at 1 until the next corresponding edge, we should explicitly register the outputs. This removes any potential combinational glitches and ensures stable synchronous outputs.

The fusion strategy is:
1. Register the input 'a' to detect edges by comparing the current and previous value.
2. Generate combinational signals for rising and falling edges based on the registered previous state.
3. Use these combinational signals to update registered outputs 'rise' and 'down'.
4. On reset, all registers are cleared.
5. When a rising edge is detected, set 'rise' to 1 and clear 'down'.
6. When a falling edge is detected, set 'down' to 1 and clear 'rise'.
7. If no edge is detected, maintain the previous output states ('rise' and 'down'), holding them at 1 until the next edge event clears them or toggles the other.

This approach combines:
- The simplicity of storing previous 'a' to detect edges.
- The glitch-free and stable outputs by registering 'rise' and 'down'.
- The output signals remain high until the corresponding next edge appears, fulfilling the problem statement.

This design slightly increases area and complexity by adding two output registers but greatly improves output stability and matches the functional spec exactly.
