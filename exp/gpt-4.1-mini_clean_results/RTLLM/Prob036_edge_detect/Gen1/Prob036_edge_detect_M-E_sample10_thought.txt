Instead of directly detecting edges by comparing the current and previous 'a' values within the same always block, use a two-stage shift register to capture two successive samples of 'a'. Then detect edges by comparing these two delayed signals. Generate and hold the rise or down output signals high from the clock cycle after the edge until the opposite edge occurs or a reset is asserted. This means rise and down act as level indicators rather than pulses. This approach also eliminates combinational logic inside the sequential block for edge detection and uses simple level-hold outputs.
