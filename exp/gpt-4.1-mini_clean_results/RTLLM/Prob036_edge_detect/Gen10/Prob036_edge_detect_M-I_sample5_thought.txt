The initial design is minimal and functionally correct, generating one-cycle pulses for rising and falling edges synchronized to clk. To better align with the specification that "rise or down will be set to 1 on the next clock when the corresponding edge appears, and then return to 0 until the corresponding edge appears again," it's beneficial to register the outputs rise and down themselves. This approach explicitly ensures rise and down are outputs registered on the clock and held for exactly one clock cycle when an edge occurs.

By introducing rise_reg and down_reg registers clocked by clk and cleared on reset, we can:

- Store the edge detection result from combinational logic.
- Generate outputs rise and down as registered signals.
- Maintain outputs asserted for exactly one cycle.
- Possibly enable better timing and glitch prevention in downstream logic.

This small enhancement clarifies output timing and better fits synchronous design best practices with minimal area and power overhead.
