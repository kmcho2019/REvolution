Both examples implement edge detection by storing the previous state of input 'a' and using combinational logic to detect edges, producing 1-clock-cycle pulses for rising and falling edges synchronized with the clock.

The key strengths of both:
- Minimal area and power using just one register to store previous 'a'.
- Simple and clear combinational logic for rise and down outputs.
- Synchronous and glitch-free edge pulses on the clock cycle after the edge.

The fusion strategy:
- Keep the simple and minimal architecture with a single register for previous 'a'.
- Use nonblocking assignments and asynchronous active-low reset to ensure robust initialization.
- Add explicit output registers for 'rise' and 'down' signals that latch the edge detection result for one clock cycle.
  This better matches the specification: "rise or down will be set to 1 on the next clock when the corresponding edge appears, and then return to 0 until the corresponding edge appears again."
- By registering outputs explicitly, the outputs become fully synchronous signals (not combinational wires).
- This slightly increases area by adding two flip-flops but improves timing robustness and clarity of output timing.
- Use a one-clock-cycle pulse scheme: set outputs to 1 when edges are detected, then clear at next clock cycle.
- This output-registering approach avoids potential glitches if the combinational logic changes mid-cycle.
- The design remains low power and low area due to minimal extra logic.

Overall, this fusion yields a robust, synchronous, one-clock-cycle pulse edge detection circuit suitable for synchronous downstream logic.
