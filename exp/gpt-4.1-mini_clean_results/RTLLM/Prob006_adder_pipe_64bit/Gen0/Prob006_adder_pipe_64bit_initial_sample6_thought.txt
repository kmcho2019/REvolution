To design a 64-bit ripple carry adder with pipeline registers, we can split the 64-bit addition into multiple stages, each handling a subset of bits. For simplicity, and to have balanced pipeline stages, we can split into 4 pipeline stages of 16 bits each.

In each stage:
- Input operands' corresponding bits (16 bits) and a carry-in from the previous stage are added.
- The sum for that segment and the carry-out to the next stage are computed.
- The sums are registered to synchronize with the clock.
- The carry-out is also registered and passed to the next stage.

The enable signal i_en is also pipelined through registers to generate o_en at the output, indicating when the output result is valid.

At the end, the full 65-bit result (64 bits sum + final carry-out) is assembled from the registered sums and carry out.

Reset (active low) initializes pipeline registers and o_en to zero.

This approach balances pipeline registers and keeps ripple-carry logic confined within each stage, allowing partial parallelism and synchronization.

The module ports:
- clk, rst_n, i_en, adda[63:0], addb[63:0]
- result[64:0], o_en
