Implement a 64-bit ripple carry adder with a 4-stage pipeline, each stage handling 16 bits. Unlike the classical ripple carry adder that computes all bits sequentially, here each stage computes its 16-bit slice addition plus carry in registered from the previous stage's carry out. 

Pipeline registers hold operand slices, carries, partial sums, and enable signals. The carry out from each stage is registered and passed to the next stage as carry in.

To maintain simplicity and resource efficiency:
- Use one always block to update all pipeline registers synchronously.
- At each clock cycle, stage i computes sum and carry out based on registered operands and carry in.
- The input enable (i_en) is pipelined through each stage to produce the output enable (o_en) when the result is ready.
- The final 65-bit sum is constructed by concatenating the partial sums and the final carry out.

This architecture reduces pipeline stages compared to bit-sliced implementations, improving latency, yet enables high throughput by pipelining. It balances complexity and register usage.
