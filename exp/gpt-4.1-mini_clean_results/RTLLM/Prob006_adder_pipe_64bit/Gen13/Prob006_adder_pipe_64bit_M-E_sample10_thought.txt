To implement a pipelined 64-bit ripple carry adder with several registers, I propose a novel approach using an 8-stage pipeline where each stage processes 8 bits of the addition with carry propagation between stages.

Key points of this approach:
1. Divide the 64-bit addition into 8 segments of 8 bits each.
2. Each pipeline stage performs an 8-bit addition with a carry-in coming from the previous stage.
3. Input operands and enable signals are registered and propagated through the pipeline stages.
4. Each stage generates a partial sum (8 bits) and a carry-out.
5. The carry-out is pipelined and fed into the next stage's adder.
6. The output result is assembled by concatenating the partial sums from all stages and the final carry-out.
7. The output enable signal is synchronized and delayed through the pipeline registers to indicate when the final result is valid.
8. All registers and logic are synchronous to `clk` with asynchronous active-low reset (`rst_n`).
9. Temporary variables inside always blocks are avoided by declaring all necessary regs/wires at module scope.
10. This 8-stage approach balances latency (8 cycles), register count, and manageable combinational logic per stage (8-bit addition).

This architecture enables a steady pipeline throughput of 1 addition per cycle once filled and achieves a clean, modular design with clear stage boundaries and carry propagation.
