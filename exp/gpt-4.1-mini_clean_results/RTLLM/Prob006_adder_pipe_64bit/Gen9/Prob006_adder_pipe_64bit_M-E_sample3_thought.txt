Implement the 64-bit ripple carry adder pipelined by partitioning the 64-bit inputs into four 16-bit blocks, each forming one pipeline stage. Each stage computes the partial sum and carry-out from its 16-bit operands plus incoming carry-in. Registers store intermediate sums and carry signals to synchronize the pipeline. The enable signal (i_en) is also pipelined along with the data to generate the output enable (o_en).

This approach balances pipeline depth and complexity: fewer stages than an 8-bit slice approach but still pipelined to improve throughput over a purely combinational 64-bit adder. The carry ripple is localized within 16-bit blocks per stage, propagating carry sequentially through the pipeline stages.

The pipeline works as follows:
- On i_en, inputs are latched and stage 0 adds bits [15:0] plus carry_in=0.
- The carry-out of stage 0 is registered and passed as carry-in to stage 1 which adds bits [31:16].
- Similarly, carry-out of stage 1 goes to stage 2 (bits [47:32]), and then to stage 3 (bits [63:48]).
- Each stage registers its sum and carry-out.
- The final 65-bit sum is assembled from stage sums and the carry-out of stage 3.
- The enable signal is pipelined in four stages; when the last stage is valid, o_en=1.

This architecture uses nonblocking assignments to ensure correct sequential logic behavior, and synchronous active-low reset to initialize registers. The output and o_en are registered at the final stage, ensuring timing synchronization.
