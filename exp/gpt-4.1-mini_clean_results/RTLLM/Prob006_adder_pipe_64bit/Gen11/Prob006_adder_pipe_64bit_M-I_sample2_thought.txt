The original design approach of pipelining the 64-bit ripple carry adder into 8 stages of 8 bits is good. However, the syntax error stems from declaring a reg array inside an always block, which is not legal in Verilog. To fix this, I will:

1. Move the temporary sum and carry variables outside the always block or convert the logic to purely synchronous with intermediate variables declared as local variables inside the always block without the `reg` keyword.

2. Alternatively, since we need to compute sum and carry for each stage sequentially in the same clock cycle (for the registered pipeline), I will compute them sequentially with separate local variables (e.g., integers or 9-bit wire regs) declared before usage inside the always block, or better yet, use generate loops or simply update pipeline registers with the sum and carry outputs using intermediate variables in a clean way.

3. To keep logic synchronous and straightforward, I will implement each stage's addition sequentially using separate wires for combinational sum and carry, and then register these outputs synchronously on the clock. However, since this is a pipelined design, each stage processes its own slice per clock, and the carry propagates from stage to stage in subsequent clocks.

4. Since each stage depends on the carry of the previous stage from the prior clock cycle, the carry_in to stage `i` is registered from carry_out of stage `i-1`. This allows carry propagation over cycles, forming a ripple carry pipeline.

5. The enable (`en_pipe`) is also shifted through pipeline stages to indicate validity of the result.

6. The final stage registers the complete 64-bit sum slices and the final carry_out bit to form the 65-bit sum output.

This approach ensures no combinational loops, easy to synthesize, and clean pipelined ripple carry addition.
