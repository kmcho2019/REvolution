The original solution is logically correct and succinct, using an index and a shifted one-hot output. However, it triggers a tool error due to the assignment of the entire vector via shifting. To avoid this, I will implement the ring counter using a shift register style update inside a clocked always block, assigning directly to the reg output vector. On reset, 'out' is initialized with 1 at LSB, then on every clock edge, the bits are rotated left by 1, maintaining one-hot. This avoids variable shift operations in assignments and should be supported by all tools. This approach is also very clear and synthesizable, making it portable and simple.
