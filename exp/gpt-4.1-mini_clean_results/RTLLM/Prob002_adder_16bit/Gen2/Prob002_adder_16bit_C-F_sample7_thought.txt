Fuse the strengths of both examples by using two 8-bit adders for the 16-bit adder to reduce the carry chain length (compared to four 4-bit adders) and maintain modular reuse of a small adder. Additionally, implement the 8-bit adder itself as two 4-bit ripple carry adders internally, combining the 8-bit block-level carry optimization with a finer 4-bit sub-module hierarchy. This hierarchical design balances modularity, reduces carry chain depth, and simplifies verification. It improves timing by limiting the carry propagation path from 16 bits directly to two 4-bit stages in series. This fusion keeps the overall carry ripple limited to three stages per 16-bit addition: two 8-bit adders, each internally with two 4-bit adders.

So the structure is:
- Define a 4-bit adder module.
- Define an 8-bit adder module that instantiates two 4-bit adders internally.
- Define a 16-bit adder module that instantiates two 8-bit adders externally.

This leverages the smaller building block (4-bit adder) used inside the 8-bit adder, and the 8-bit adder is reused twice for the 16-bit adder, which reduces carry propagation delay relative to a pure 4-bit ripple chain across 16 bits and improves modular clarity over a monolithic 8-bit adder.

This hierarchical approach enhances timing and maintains design clarity, modularity, and reuse, resulting in better balanced PPA.
