This time, I'll implement the 16-bit adder using a 4-bit carry lookahead adder (CLA) block as the building unit instead of 8-bit ripple carry adders. Each 4-bit CLA block will internally generate propagate and generate signals, which allows faster carry computation within the block, significantly reducing the carry delay compared to ripple carry. Then I will instantiate four such 4-bit CLA blocks to build the 16-bit adder, connecting carry-outs and carry-ins between blocks using a top-level carry lookahead logic.

Specifically, the 4-bit CLA block produces its carry outputs by combining propagate and generate signals for the 4 bits, enabling carry signals to be computed in parallel rather than sequentially. The top-level 16-bit adder will compute the carries between 4-bit blocks in a carry lookahead manner, further accelerating the carry propagation across the whole 16-bit word.

This hierarchical CLA architecture balances complexity, speed, and modularity well. It is more performant than ripple carry due to parallel carry generation at the 4-bit block level and top-level carry logic. The smaller 4-bit blocks keep the logic manageable and suitable for synthesis. This design is purely combinational and parameterizable for easy extension.

The implementation will include:
- A 1-bit full adder module for sum calculation.
- A 4-bit CLA module computing sum and carry outputs using propagate/generate and carry lookahead logic.
- The top 16-bit adder module instantiates four 4-bit CLA blocks and calculates block carries at the top level using block propagate/generate signals.

This approach improves the critical path delay while maintaining a modular design and relatively low area.
