Implement the ALU using a modular, function-based approach that isolates each operation into a separate function, improving readability and potential for reuse. Use a 33-bit extended bus for addition and subtraction to capture carry-out without mixing signed and unsigned arithmetic in a single operation. Use separate signed and unsigned versions of operands where needed. For shift operations, use a unified variable shift amount source that depends on the opcode (immediate shifts use b[4:0], variable shifts use a[4:0]) to distinguish shift source. The flag output is driven by a combinational expression and defaulted to 0 otherwise (no high-impedance), while zero and negative are wire outputs assigned continuously to help synthesis and timing. Carry and overflow signals are only asserted for arithmetic operations, zero otherwise. The module defaults outputs explicitly for all operations to avoid latch inference. The LUI instruction uses b (not a) as the immediate, shifted left 16 bits as per MIPS convention. This decoupling of input roles for shift amount and immediates increases design clarity. Default case sets outputs to zero. This function-driven approach aids in formal verification and isolates logic, potentially easing debugging and maintenance.
