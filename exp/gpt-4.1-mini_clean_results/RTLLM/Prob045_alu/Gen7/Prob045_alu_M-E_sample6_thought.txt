Implement a combinational 32-bit MIPS ALU that selects the operation based on the 6-bit control input `aluc`. To differentiate from the example, the design uses a multi-step approach inside the combinational block: first precompute basic operations (add, subtract, bitwise ops, shifts) with wires and variables to avoid repetitive calculations. The addition and subtraction are computed with 33-bit width to capture carry/borrow and overflow. Overflow detection uses explicit logic based on sign bits of inputs and outputs.

The shifts are implemented using variable shift amounts from either `a[4:0]` or the immediate shift amount as per opcode. Instead of a case statement, the design uses if-else branches to clearly separate arithmetic, logic, shifts, and special instructions (LUI, SLT, SLTU), improving clarity. The `flag` output is only driven for SLT and SLTU instructions, otherwise driven to 0 to avoid high-impedance which is synthesis-unfriendly.

All output flags (`zero`, `carry`, `negative`, `overflow`, `flag`) are assigned combinationally outside the main combinational block for immediate updates with the result `r`. The `negative` flag is the MSB of `r`. The `zero` flag is high when result is zero. This modular approach ensures clean separation of logic and eases extensibility.

This approach aims for readability and clarity, with precomputation of results and explicit flag assignments, and avoids driving outputs to high-impedance, suitable for synthesis and easy debugging.
