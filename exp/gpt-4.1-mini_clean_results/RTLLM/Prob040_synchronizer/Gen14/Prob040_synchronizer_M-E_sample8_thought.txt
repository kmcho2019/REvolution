To provide a different architectural approach, use a handshake-style data transfer combined with a pulse synchronization to transfer the enable signal and stable data safely across clock domains. The design will implement:

1. In clk_a domain: latch data_in into data_reg when data_en is high (ensuring data_in is stable during data_en asserted).
2. Generate a single-cycle pulse (data_en_pulse) when data_en goes high to indicate new data is ready.
3. Synchronize this pulse signal into clk_b domain via a two-flip-flop synchronizer to produce a clean pulse in clk_b.
4. In clk_b domain: detect rising edge of the synchronized pulse to trigger loading the latched data_reg into output register dataout.
5. Use an output enable flag in clk_b to hold the output stable until the next new pulse arrives.

This handshake and pulse synchronization scheme ensures safe data transfer across asynchronous domains and makes the dataout update only on explicit new data ready events, reducing glitch risks. It also avoids direct enable signal transfer delays, instead passing a pulse event.

Reset signals are asynchronous as requested. All registers use non-blocking assignments. The module remains fully self-contained.

This approach decouples data capture and data transfer event signaling, improving robustness and reducing potential metastability issues.
