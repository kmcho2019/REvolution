Implement a multi-bit MUX-based synchronizer with a handshake-like mechanism and a sample-hold register to safely transfer data across asynchronous clock domains with different resets. In clk_a domain, latch data_in into a data_reg only when data_en is high, ensuring data stability during enable assertion. Also, generate a pulse signal indicating new data ready by edge-detecting data_en. Then, send a 2-bit control handshake signal across clock domains via a two-stage synchronizer in clk_b domain, encoding the new data ready event and its acknowledgment.

In clk_b domain, detect new data by decoding the handshake signal and update dataout only when the handshake indicates new valid data is available. This ensures the data output updates only after proper synchronization and handshake acknowledgment, avoiding metastability and data glitches.

Key differences from the given approach:
- Use a handshake approach to reliably detect new data arrival and acknowledge it, not just delay enable signals.
- Edge-detect data_en in clk_a to generate pulses indicating data update events.
- Synchronize handshake signals via double-flip-flop stages for metastability protection.
- Only update dataout on clk_b after handshake confirms data validity, holding previous output otherwise.
- This handshake protocol avoids any ambiguous enable duration crossing and ensures a clean, glitch-free transfer of multi-bit data from clk_a to clk_b domains.
