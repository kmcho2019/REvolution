The original design is functionally correct and well structured, but there are opportunities to optimize power and area while maintaining reliability:

1. **Clock Gating for clk_a Domain Registers**: Since `data_en` is low most of the time (data changes are infrequent and held constant during `data_en` high), clock gating the `data_reg` and `en_data_reg` registers to only clock-enable when `data_en` is high can reduce unnecessary toggling and dynamic power consumption.

2. **Reducing Switching on Enable Synchronizer**: The enable synchronizer in `clk_b` domain (two flip-flops) updates every cycle with `en_data_reg`, potentially toggling unnecessarily when `data_en` is stable low. Adding a simple registered handshake or detect rising edge of enable to only update dataout when enable transitions from 0 to 1 would further reduce unnecessary toggling in `dataout`.

3. **Avoiding Redundant Data Updates in clk_b Domain**: By updating `dataout` only on the rising edge of the synchronized enable signal (when it becomes '1' from '0'), we avoid re-assigning the same data every clock cycle, reducing switching activity and potentially improving timing by lowering dataout toggling.

4. **Maintain Reset and Domain Crossing Integrity**: Keep asynchronous reset on `clk_a` domain and synchronous reset on `clk_b` domain as specified. The synchronization registers remain intact for metastability protection.

5. **Synthesis-friendly Code Style**: Maintain non-blocking assignments and synchronous resets with minimal combinational logic.

This strategy reduces unnecessary toggling on both clock domains, improving power and possibly timing while keeping the logic minimal and area efficient.
