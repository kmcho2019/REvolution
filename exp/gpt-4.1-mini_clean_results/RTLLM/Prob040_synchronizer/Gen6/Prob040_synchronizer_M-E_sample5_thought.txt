Instead of capturing data_in and data_en separately in the clk_a domain and then synchronizing data_en into clk_b domain, this design uses a handshake-style approach to transfer data from clk_a to clk_b domain.

The idea:

1. In clk_a domain, when data_en is asserted, latch data_in into a register (data_reg_a) and assert a pulse signal (send_data_a) for one clk_a cycle.

2. The pulse send_data_a is then synchronized into clk_b domain via a 2-stage synchronizer (send_data_b_sync) to avoid metastability.

3. In clk_b domain, detect the rising edge of synchronized send_data_b_sync to load the data from a clk_a domain register into a clk_b domain register (data_reg_b). Since data_in is stable during data_en, and data_en is active at least 3 clk_b cycles, this guarantees proper capture.

4. The output dataout is driven by data_reg_b.

5. Reset signals are handled asynchronously, data_reg_a and send_data_a reset on arstn, data_reg_b and synchronizers reset on brstn.

This approach allows a pulse handshake to transfer data, avoiding direct data_en synchronization; instead, the pulse crossing acts as a valid signal to transfer data, reducing the need to hold enable signals across clock domains and simplifying control logic.

This new synchronizer also decouples data and enable paths, minimizing chances of glitches and improving timing closure due to shorter control paths in clk_b domain.

The design relies on stable input data_in during data_en asserted window, and the minimum intervals between changes (at least 10 clk_b cycles) provide sufficient time to transfer and latch data.
