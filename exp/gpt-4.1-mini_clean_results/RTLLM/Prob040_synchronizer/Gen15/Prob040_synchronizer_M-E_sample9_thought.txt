Implement a multi-bit synchronizer by capturing the stable data_in and data_en signals in the clk_a domain whenever data_en is asserted, holding that data steady in a register. Then, instead of directly transferring data_reg to the clk_b domain, use a pulse generator in clk_a domain that produces a short enable pulse synchronized to the data_en rising edge. This pulse is then synchronized across clk domains using a double flip-flop synchronizer on clk_b, creating a strobed load enable signal. When the load enable is detected in clk_b domain, data from a small FIFO-like register bank (with simple handshake) is transferred to clk_b domain data registers, ensuring glitch-free capture of multi-bit data. This method decouples data transfer from the enable level, reduces risk of metastability on multi-bit buses, and allows clean capturing of data in clk_b domain on enable pulse. Reset signals are handled asynchronously in clk_a domain and synchronously in clk_b domain per spec. The output dataout is updated only when the synchronized load enable pulse is detected, otherwise holding its previous value, ensuring stable output. This approach improves reliability of multi-bit data crossing clock domains by using pulse-based handshake and avoiding direct muxing based on level crossing.
