Use a handshake-style synchronizer with explicit data-valid latching to reduce timing and logic.  
1. In clk_a domain, data and data_en are latched into a staging register only when data_en is asserted, holding data stable.  
2. The data_valid signal (derived from data_en) is synchronized into clk_b domain via a 2-stage flip-flop synchronizer.  
3. A data_ready register in clk_b domain captures the synchronized data_valid signal's rising edge (indicating new data availability).  
4. On data_ready event, data from clk_a domain (held in staging register) is transferred asynchronously via a simple dual-clock FIFO-like handshake: data is sampled into clk_b domain register.  
5. The output dataout register holds the sampled data, updating only when new data is ready, otherwise holding previous value.  
6. Reset signals are handled asynchronously as per requirements but designed carefully to avoid glitches.  
7. The core idea is to treat data_valid as a handshake signal that crosses clock domains with a clear detect of new data pulses, minimizing data hazards and allowing safe data transfer with minimal logic.  
This approach ensures data is only captured and propagated when a new data item is indicated by the data_valid transition synchronized into clk_b domain, improving reliability and timing closure in multi-bit CDC data synchronizer designs.
