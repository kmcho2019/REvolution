Fuse the best aspects of both examples to achieve a clean, modular, and efficient multi-bit MUX-based synchronizer. From Example 1, we keep the modular two-stage enable synchronizer logic, improving clarity and reusability. From Example 2, we incorporate conditional latching of data_reg only when data_en is high in clk_a domain to reduce unnecessary toggling, and carefully update dataout only when the synchronized enable is active.

To reduce toggling further on clk_b domain registers, data_sync updates only when the synchronized enable signal is high, preventing redundant data latching. The output register dataout updates every clk_b cycle but driven by a mux selecting either new data or holding previous value, ensuring glitch-free stable output. We keep asynchronous resets for simplicity and correctness in two clock domains.

This fusion balances modularity, clarity, power reduction, and performance, while fully meeting the problem specification, including the MUX-based output update gated by synchronized enable.

The overall structure:

1. In clk_a domain: data_in and data_en are registered; data_reg latches data_in only if data_en is high.
2. Enable signal (en_data_reg) is synchronized into clk_b domain by the two-stage en_sync module.
3. In clk_b domain: data_sync register updates only when synchronized enable is high to reduce toggling.
4. dataout register updates every clk_b clock, taking the muxed value of either new synchronized data_sync (when enable is high) or the previous dataout, implementing the MUX behavior cleanly.

This improves power by minimizing unnecessary toggling, maintains performance via proper synchronization stages, and retains clean structure for area efficiency.
