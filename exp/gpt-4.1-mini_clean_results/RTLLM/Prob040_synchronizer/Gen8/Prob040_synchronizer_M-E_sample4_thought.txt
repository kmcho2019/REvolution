Instead of directly registering data_in and data_en in clk_a domain and passing the enable signal through two flip-flops in clk_b domain, this design uses a small FIFO style buffer with level indication to synchronize data safely. 

- On clk_a domain, when data_en is asserted, data_in is captured into a 2-entry FIFO register (depth=2) to tolerate any slight timing variation or jitter.
- A 'write pointer' toggles to indicate new data is available.
- The write pointer and data are transferred asynchronously to clk_b domain via a 2-stage synchronizer (for pointer only).
- In clk_b domain, the synchronized pointer compares with a read pointer to know when new data is ready.
- When new data is detected, the read pointer increments and outputs the corresponding data.
- This approach provides a handshake-like mechanism ensuring robust multi-bit data transfer across asynchronous clock domains.
- As data_en is high for at least 3 cycles of clk_b and data changes slowly, this approach safely avoids metastability and glitch issues, providing a clean synchronized dataout.

This FIFO-based synchronizer differs from the MUX-based approach by using pointer synchronization and buffered data storage, thus offering increased reliability especially for multi-bit wide signals.
