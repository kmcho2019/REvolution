To improve the PPA (Performance, Power, Area) metrics, I will optimize the FSM by using one-hot encoding instead of binary encoding. One-hot encoding often leads to faster state transitions and simpler combinational logic, reducing critical path delay (improving performance). It can also reduce power by minimizing logic toggling since only one flip-flop is active at a time. 

For area, while one-hot encoding uses more flip-flops, the combinational logic complexity is lower, typically leading to simpler synthesis results and better timing.

Additionally, I will implement the next state and output logic purely combinationally using a case statement for clarity and faster synthesis. The MATCH output will remain combinational (Mealy) and directly inferred from current state and input.

I will keep synchronous reset and state update on the clock's positive edge, ensuring a robust design.

This one-hot FSM will have six states corresponding to the prefix matches of "10011" (including the initial state). The encoding:

- S0: no match (000001)
- S1: matched '1' (000010)
- S2: matched '10' (000100)
- S3: matched '100' (001000)
- S4: matched '1001' (010000)
- S5: matched '10011' (100000) - final matched state, output MATCH=1 when transitioning to S5

MATCH will be asserted when transitioning into S5 with the final input bit.

This approach reduces combinational depth in the next-state logic, improving clock frequency and potentially lowering power while keeping functionality intact.
