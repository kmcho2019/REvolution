To optimize the given FSM for better PPA (power, performance, area), the improvement focuses on reducing combinational logic delay and minimizing unnecessary state transitions.

1. Use one-hot encoding for the states instead of binary encoding: One-hot FSMs often lead to simpler next-state logic, shorter critical paths, and better performance at a modest area cost, which is acceptable given the small number of states.

2. Compute MATCH output using a registered output instead of pure combinational Mealy output, but still keep the Mealy behavior by updating MATCH immediately on the clock edge based on the current input and state.

3. Combine the next state logic and output logic inside the synchronous always block to reduce combinational logic depth, avoiding a combinational function for next state, which may reduce delay.

4. Use non-blocking assignments for all sequential logic for safe synthesis.

5. Maintain synchronous reset.

This approach trades a slight increase in area (due to one-hot states) for a shorter critical path and potentially reduced power due to simpler combinational logic and fewer glitches, which should improve WNS and potentially lower dynamic power.

The FSM has 5 meaningful states (including reset state), so 6 states total to represent no match and progress, encoded in 6 bits (one-hot). The outputs will be registered but updated immediately on clock edge to emulate Mealy behavior.

We still maintain continuous detection and overlapping matches (loop detection), as required.
