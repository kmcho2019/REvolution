The original one-hot FSM is clear and correct but uses 5 flip-flops and multiple conditional if-statements, increasing area and power. To optimize PPA, I will:

1. Convert the FSM encoding from one-hot to binary (3 bits needed for 5 states). This reduces flip-flop count from 5 to 3, lowering area and dynamic power due to fewer toggling FFs.

2. Use a case statement for next-state logic, which synthesis tools can optimize better than multiple if-else chains.

3. Retain the Mealy output logic dependent on current state and input.

4. Keep synchronous reset and non-blocking assignment to ensure safe design.

This binary FSM approach will save area and power, with a slight increase in combinational logic complexity that should still meet timing. Overall, this tradeoff improves PPA metrics for the small FSM.

State encoding (binary):
S0 = 3'b000 (no match)
S1 = 3'b001 (matched '1')
S2 = 3'b010 (matched '10')
S3 = 3'b011 (matched '100')
S4 = 3'b100 (matched '1001')

This will be easier for synthesis and cause fewer toggles.
