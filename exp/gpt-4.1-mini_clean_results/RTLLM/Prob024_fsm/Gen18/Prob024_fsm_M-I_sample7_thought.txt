To optimize the existing one-hot FSM for better PPA (timing, power, area), I will:

1. Re-encode the FSM states using a binary encoding instead of one-hot. This reduces the number of flip-flops from 5 to 3 and cuts down the overall area and power consumption.
2. Simplify next-state logic with a case statement on binary states, improving combinational logic area and power.
3. Keep the Mealy output logic combinational and directly derived from current state and input for low latency.
4. Retain synchronous reset and clean separation of next state and state registers for robustness.
5. Ensure overlapping sequence detection remains intact by properly encoding partial matches in states and handling transitions accordingly.

The tradeoff is a slightly more complex combinational logic for next state and output, but given the sequence length (5), the binary FSM has only 5 states plus an unused state, which is manageable.

This approach reduces flip-flops and gating, improving area and power, while maintaining or potentially improving timing due to simpler state register and reduced switching activity.
