I will implement the Mealy FSM detection circuit using a one-hot encoded state machine with 6 states representing the progression through the sequence "10011". This state encoding simplifies the next-state logic by activating exactly one state bit at a time. The FSM transitions will be driven by the input bit and current active state. To support continuous and overlapping detection, the next states will allow proper rollback to previous states if partial matches remain.

The MATCH output is generated combinationally as a Mealy output when the FSM reaches the final state and the input is '1' on that clock cycle, indicating the full sequence has been matched with overlap support.

State updates and reset are synchronous on the clock's rising edge, with synchronous reset to ensure initialization to the idle state.

This approach differs from binary encoding by leveraging one-hot state encoding, which often leads to simpler and faster next-state logic and allows easy observation of individual states for debugging and tracing.
