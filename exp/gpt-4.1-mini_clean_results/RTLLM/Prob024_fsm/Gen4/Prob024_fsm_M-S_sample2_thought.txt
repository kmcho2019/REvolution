To simplify the FSM detection circuit, I will use a binary encoded FSM with 6 states representing how much of the "10011" pattern has been matched so far. This reduces the number of flip-flops from 6 (one-hot) to 3 (binary encoding), simplifying the design and reducing area.

The Mealy output MATCH is asserted combinationally when the next input completes the pattern; i.e., at the transition that reaches the final state. MATCH is registered synchronously with the clock to avoid glitches.

The next-state logic will consider overlap by carefully choosing transitions based on the pattern prefix to ensure continuous detection.

Synchronous reset initializes the FSM to state 0.

This simplified binary FSM reduces complexity and area while maintaining full functionality.
