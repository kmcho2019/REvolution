To simplify the FSM, I will use a binary-encoded Mealy FSM with 6 states representing the progress of matching the sequence "10011". Each state corresponds to how many bits of the sequence have been matched so far. On each clock cycle, the FSM transitions based on the input IN and current state. MATCH will be asserted combinationally when the input completes the full sequence in the proper state.

This binary-encoded FSM reduces flip-flops to 3 bits instead of one-hot encoding 5 bits, and the next state logic uses a simple case statement, improving clarity and maintainability without losing functionality. Overlapping detection is supported naturally by proper transitions back to earlier states when needed.
