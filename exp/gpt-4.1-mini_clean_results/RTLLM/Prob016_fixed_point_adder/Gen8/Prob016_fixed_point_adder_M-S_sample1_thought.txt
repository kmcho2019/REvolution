Simplify the fixed-point adder by leveraging direct two's complement addition and subtraction, avoiding explicit sign-magnitude conversion. Since inputs and outputs are in two's complement fixed-point format, addition can be performed directly. The key steps are:

1. Perform signed addition of inputs `a` and `b` directly, using a wider intermediate register (`N+1` bits) to detect overflow.
2. Assign the lower `N` bits of the result as the output.
3. Overflow handling: since fixed-point numbers in two's complement wrap naturally, and the problem states overflow is internally managed by MSB, no explicit saturation is needed here.
4. The fractional precision `Q` does not require special handling in addition because both operands share the same fixed-point format.
5. Internal register `res` stores the result (truncated to `N` bits).
6. This approach maintains the fixed-point addition semantics while significantly simplifying the design.

This simplification reduces complexity, improves clarity, and maintains correct fixed-point addition behavior consistent with two's complement arithmetic.
