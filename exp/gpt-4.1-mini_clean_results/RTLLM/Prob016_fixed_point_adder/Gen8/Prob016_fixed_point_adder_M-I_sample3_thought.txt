3. Determine the sign bits of `a` and `b` (MSB).

4. If signs are the same:
   - Add the absolute values of `a` and `b`.
   - The result sign bit is the common sign.
   - Handle overflow by saturating or wrapping (here wrapping is natural with two's complement).

   - Compare absolute values of `a` and `b`.
   - Subtract smaller abs from larger abs.
   - Result sign bit is the sign of the operand with larger absolute value.
   - If result is zero, sign bit is zero (positive zero).

6. Use two's complement arithmetic to implement absolute values and subtraction.
   - Absolute value can be computed as `a_sign ? (~a + 1) : a`.

7. Store result in an internal register `res` and assign to output.
