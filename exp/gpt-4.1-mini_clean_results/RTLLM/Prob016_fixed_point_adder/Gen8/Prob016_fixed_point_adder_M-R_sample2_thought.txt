Refactor the fixed-point adder to use purely combinational continuous assignments with intermediate signed wires instead of an always block and registers. This eliminates the explicit internal register and uses internal wire variables to perform the addition, overflow detection, and saturation. This approach uses two's complement arithmetic for signed addition, matches signs to detect overflow, and applies saturation accordingly.

Using continuous assignments simplifies the design and makes it more concise and clear, relying entirely on combinational logic without registers, while maintaining the same functionality and interface.

The parameter `Q` is kept for fixed-point precision indication but unused in arithmetic, ensuring consistent interface.

Outputs are driven by a combinational assign statement from intermediate logic.
