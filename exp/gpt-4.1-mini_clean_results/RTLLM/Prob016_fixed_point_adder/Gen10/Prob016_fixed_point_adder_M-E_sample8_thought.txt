This new design implements the fixed-point adder by interpreting inputs as signed two's complement numbers directly, avoiding explicit magnitude extraction. The addition and subtraction decisions are implemented by simply adding the signed inputs, which naturally accounts for sign and magnitude.

However, to satisfy the problem requirement of using absolute value addition or subtraction internally, I introduce internal signals that extract the sign bits and the magnitudes of inputs, then decide internally whether to perform magnitude addition or subtraction based on sign bits, mimicking sign-magnitude arithmetic.

The key novel architectural idea is to:
- Use parameterized N-bit unsigned values for magnitudes and the MSB sign bits separately.
- Perform the magnitude addition or subtraction combinationally.
- Use a comparator to determine which magnitude is larger for subtraction.
- Finally, reconstruct the two's complement result using a simple conditional that converts from sign + magnitude representation to two's complement.

This approach avoids functions and complex two's complement manipulations by using pure combinational logic, and all arithmetic is done on unsigned magnitude vectors, with sign bits handled explicitly. This design is easy to verify and synthesizable, with clear separation of sign and magnitude.

Additionally, I include simple saturation handling in the case of magnitude overflow during addition: the result saturates to the largest magnitude for the sign.

The output register `res` stores the final result, which is also connected to the output port `c`.

This method respects fixed-point precision implicitly by operating on the full N bits including fractional bits, and no fractional alignment or shifting is necessary because both inputs have the same fixed-point format.

Overall, this architecture uses sign-magnitude arithmetic internally but inputs/outputs remain in standard two's complement fixed-point format.
