Refactor the fixed-point adder by replacing the combinational always block with purely combinational logic implemented through continuous `assign` statements. This removes the internal register `res` and directly computes the output `c` as a wire. The absolute values and operations are computed with intermediate wires, and a mux-like structure selects the final output based on the sign bits of inputs and magnitude comparisons. This approach reduces the internal state, avoids blocking assignments in always blocks, and expresses the functionality more declaratively and succinctly, while preserving the same logic of sign handling, addition, and subtraction with magnitude comparison.
