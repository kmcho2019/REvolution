Since the inputs and output are signed fixed-point numbers in two's complement form, their addition can be directly done with a simple signed addition without needing to separately handle absolute values or sign bits explicitly. Two's complement arithmetic naturally accounts for sign and magnitude, simplifying the design significantly.

Thus, the fixed_point_adder can be implemented as a straightforward signed adder with parameters defining the bit widths. This approach automatically handles addition and subtraction, sign, and overflow within the N-bit result.

No separate sign or absolute value logic is necessary, maintaining the fixed-point precision defined by parameters Q (fractional bits) and N (total bits).

This simplification reduces code complexity and potential errors while fulfilling the fixed-point addition requirements.
