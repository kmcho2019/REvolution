Simplify the fixed-point adder by focusing on clear, straightforward sign and magnitude operations:

1. Extract sign bits of inputs `a` and `b`.
2. Compute absolute values using conditional negation.
3. When signs are equal, add absolute values and assign the common sign; if overflow occurs, saturate to max/min.
4. When signs differ, subtract the smaller absolute from the larger; sign of the larger magnitude operand dominates; zero result is positive zero.
5. Compose the final two's complement output from the sign and magnitude.
6. Avoid complex width mismatches by using consistent vector sizes.
7. Implement all logic combinationally with blocking assignments for clarity.
8. Use parameters for `N` and `Q` but `Q` is not directly used in arithmetic here since addition works on full fixed-point word.
9. No internal registers are needed except for intermediate combinational variables.

This simplification maintains correctness while improving readability and synthesis friendliness.
