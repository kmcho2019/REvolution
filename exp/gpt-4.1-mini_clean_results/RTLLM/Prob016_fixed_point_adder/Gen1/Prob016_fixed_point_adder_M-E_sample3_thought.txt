Design a parameterized fixed-point adder treating inputs as signed two's complement fixed-point numbers with total bit-width N and fractional bits Q. Use signed arithmetic directly by extending inputs to (N+1) bits to detect overflow and correctly perform addition or subtraction regardless of signs.

The key insight is to treat inputs `a` and `b` as signed numbers; simply sign-extend each to N+1 bits, add them, and then handle saturation and trimming back to N bits.

The steps:

1. Sign-extend inputs `a` and `b` to N+1 bits to capture potential overflow.
2. Add extended values to produce a sum with N+1 bits.
3. Check for overflow by detecting when the sum cannot be represented in N bits (e.g., sign bit of sum differs from next MSB).
4. Saturate the result to the maximum or minimum representable value if overflow occurs.
5. Trim the saturated or normal sum back to N bits to produce output `c`.
6. Because inputs are in two's complement fixed-point, addition preserves fractional format and precision `Q`.

This approach eliminates the need to separately handle absolute values or manually reconstruct sign and magnitude. Overflow handling ensures no wrap-around, which can cause errors.

Internal register `res` holds the intermediate sum (N+1 bits). Output is assigned from `res` after saturation logic.

This method is concise, synthesizable, robust, and fully parameterized for any N and Q.
