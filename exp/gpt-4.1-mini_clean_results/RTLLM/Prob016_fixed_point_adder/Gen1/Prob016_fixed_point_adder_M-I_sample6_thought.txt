To improve the original design, I will maintain the correct functional behavior but enhance synthesis friendliness and clarity:

1. Use blocking assignments in the combinational always block to better indicate combinational logic.

2. Avoid repeated assignments to the same variable inside conditions by first computing the result sign and magnitude separately, then combine.

3. Explicitly handle the sign and magnitude separately: 
   - Extract sign bits.
   - Compute absolute values via a dedicated function.
   - Compute sum or difference of absolute values accordingly.
   - Determine the sign of the result.
   - Recombine sign and magnitude into two's complement representation.

4. Return the result in two's complement signed format rather than manually forcing the MSB, to avoid incorrect sign bit manipulation that may lead to incorrect results.

5. This also better handles overflow because arithmetic in two's complement naturally wraps.

6. Use a helper function to convert magnitude and sign back into two's complement format.

7. Keep parameterized sizes and avoid redundant truncations.

This approach reduces subtle errors related to manual sign bit assignment and improves readability and maintainability without changing the intended functionality.

The fixed-point precision Q remains a parameter defining the fractional bits, which don't require special arithmetic treatment during addition or subtraction.

Overall, this redesign enhances the correctness and synthesizability of the fixed-point adder.
