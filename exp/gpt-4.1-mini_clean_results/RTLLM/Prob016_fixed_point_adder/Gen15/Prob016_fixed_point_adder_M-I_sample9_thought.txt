The original solution correctly implements the fixed-point adder per specification but has some inefficiencies: it unconditionally computes both add and subtract magnitudes, increasing power and area, and uses a two-step sign-magnitude recombination that can be streamlined.

To optimize power and area, I propose:
- Computing only the needed operation (add or subtract) based on the sign bits of the inputs. This conditional arithmetic saves unnecessary toggling.
- Simplify the sign assignment by always assigning the result sign as the sign of the operand with larger magnitude when signs differ.
- Remove the intermediate res_sign register by directly recomputing the final two's complement result after deciding magnitude and sign.
- Use localparams and packed wires to avoid large reg arrays and reduce complexity.
- Keep the combinational always block for clarity, but do only minimal arithmetic required.

This approach reduces redundant arithmetic logic, slightly improves power efficiency by avoiding unnecessary addition/subtraction computations, and keeps the design fully combinational for low latency. Overflow is still managed by truncation as before.

The precision Q parameter is maintained for completeness but does not directly affect logic because addition is bitwise.

Overall, these changes improve power and area without compromising correctness or precision.
