This new approach converts the inputs `a` and `b` from two's complement fixed-point to signed integers and performs direct addition. Since both inputs are signed fixed-point numbers with the same fractional width `Q`, a simple two's complement addition suffices for accurate fixed-point addition without separate absolute value handling.

Key points:
- Inputs and output are two's complement fixed-point numbers.
- Addition is direct two's complement addition, naturally handling signs and overflow within N bits.
- The result is truncated to N bits to fit the output width.
- The `res` register holds the intermediate signed sum.
- This approach simplifies the design, avoids explicit magnitude and sign extraction, and leverages native two's complement arithmetic for correct fixed-point addition.
- Overflow effects wrap around as in standard two's complement addition; if saturation or other overflow handling is desired, further logic could be added (not required here).
