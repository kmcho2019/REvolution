Implement the fixed_point_adder module by explicitly extracting the sign bit and magnitude of each input operand. Use conditional logic to differentiate two cases:

1. When both inputs have the same sign, perform magnitude addition, and assign the common sign to the result.
2. When the inputs have different signs, perform magnitude subtraction between the larger and smaller magnitude, and assign the sign of the larger magnitude operand to the result.

To handle fixed-point representation, treat the entire N-bit input as a signed number but work internally with absolute values as unsigned magnitude vectors for addition and subtraction. Finally, reconstruct the result by combining the result sign bit with the computed magnitude.

An internal register `res` stores the final N-bit result synchronously with combinational logic. Overflow is implicitly handled by limiting the magnitude width to N-1 bits and saturating or wrapping is not explicitly done; the MSB (sign bit) reflects sign and overflow is naturally constrained by the bit-width.

This approach directly follows the problem statement, differentiating addition and subtraction by sign, working on magnitudes, and setting the sign of the output correctly.

Advantages:
- Explicit sign and magnitude processing.
- Clear control flow for addition/subtraction based on sign bits.
- Parameterized for bit width and fractional precision.

Drawbacks:
- Slightly more combinational logic than simple signed addition.
