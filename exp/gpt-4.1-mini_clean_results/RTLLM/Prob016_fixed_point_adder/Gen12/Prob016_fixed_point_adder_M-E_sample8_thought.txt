Implement the fixed-point adder by explicitly converting the two's complement inputs into sign-magnitude form internally, then performing magnitude addition or subtraction based on sign comparison, and finally reconstructing the result back into two's complement form.

Specifically:

1. Extract sign bits (MSB) of inputs `a` and `b`.

2. Convert `a` and `b` to their magnitudes by taking two's complement if negative.

3. If the signs are the same:
   - Add magnitudes.
   - Result sign = input sign.
   - Saturate if addition overflow occurs (magnitude exceeds max positive value).

4. If signs differ:
   - Compare magnitudes.
   - Subtract smaller magnitude from larger magnitude.
   - Result sign = sign of operand with larger magnitude.
   - If result magnitude is zero, sign = 0.
   - No overflow possible here but check for zero.

5. Convert result sign and magnitude back to two's complement form.

6. Use a register `res` to hold final result `c`.

7. Ensure fractional precision Q is consistent by performing arithmetic on full N bits and treating the number as fixed-point with Q fractional bits (implicitly handled since all arithmetic is done on integers with same Q).

8. Handle overflow on magnitude addition by saturating result to maximum or minimum fixed-point value.
