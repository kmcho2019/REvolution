Instead of manually extracting magnitudes and separately handling addition and subtraction of absolute values, a cleaner approach is to interpret inputs directly as signed two's complement fixed-point numbers and use Verilog's signed arithmetic features.

We can declare inputs and outputs as signed vectors of width N, with Q fractional bits implied in fixed-point interpretation.

Then:

- Perform signed addition on the entire N-bit inputs using signed addition, which inherently manages sign, magnitude, and overflow in two's complement.

- Since fixed-point addition of two Q-bit fractional numbers is the same as integer addition on the entire word, no extra scaling is needed.

- The result can be truncated or capped to N bits; overflow wraps in two's complement which matches fixed-point arithmetic modulo behavior.

This architectural shift reduces complexity, leverages language features, and ensures efficient synthesis with minimal logic.

If overflow or saturation is needed, that can be added later, but here we strictly do addition modulo 2^N in fixed-point format.

The internal register 'res' will store the addition result.

This approach naturally respects sign bits and fixed-point format without explicit magnitude extraction or sign manipulations.

Hence, the module is parameterized, supports signed fixed-point addition, uses signed types, and has a simple, elegant implementation.

This satisfies all problem requirements with clean, synthesizable code.
