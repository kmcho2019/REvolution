I will implement the fixed-point adder using a two's complement arithmetic approach directly, exploiting that adding two two's complement numbers inherently handles sign and magnitude. The difference here is to handle the addition straightforwardly, then check for overflow to saturate results if needed.

Key points of this novel approach:
1. Inputs and output are in two's complement fixed-point format.
2. Addition is performed directly on the N-bit vectors as signed integers.
3. After addition, overflow detection is done by checking the sign bits of inputs and result:
   - Positive overflow: when inputs are both positive but result is negative.
   - Negative overflow: when inputs are both negative but result is positive.
4. In case of overflow, saturate the result to the max positive or min negative value.
5. Output `c` is assigned combinationally.
6. Parameters `N` and `Q` define total bits and fractional bits but internally handled as signed fixed-point numbers.
7. No explicit absolute value or separate magnitude computations are needed, leveraging native two's complement properties.
8. This reduces complexity, improves performance and synthesis friendliness.

This novel idea avoids explicit sign and magnitude manipulation by relying on two's complement arithmetic properties and overflow detection logic.
