I will implement the fixed-point adder using a two's complement arithmetic approach with sign extension to handle potential overflow safely. Instead of converting inputs to sign-magnitude format, I'll directly add the two operands as signed values (taking advantage of Verilog's signed arithmetic).

Key points:
- Inputs and output are treated as signed fixed-point numbers with total width N and Q fractional bits.
- To safely handle overflow, inputs are sign-extended by 1 bit before addition, resulting in (N+1)-bit signed operands.
- After addition, the result is truncated back to N bits, effectively saturating or wrapping depending on the fixed width.
- This approach leverages native signed arithmetic and avoids explicit magnitude extraction and conditional logic.
- This reduces logic and improves clarity and synthesizability.
- Internally, a signed addition is performed, directly reflecting fixed-point addition semantics.
- Precision Q is a parameter but doesn't change internal logic; it just defines the fractional positioning.
- Overflow is implicitly handled by truncation, consistent with typical fixed-point arithmetic.
