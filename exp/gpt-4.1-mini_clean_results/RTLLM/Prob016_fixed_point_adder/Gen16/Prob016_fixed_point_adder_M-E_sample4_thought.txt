I will implement the fixed-point adder using signed arithmetic directly, leveraging SystemVerilog's signed data type for simplicity and clarity. This eliminates the need to manually handle absolute values and sign bits separately.

The approach:
- Define inputs and outputs as unsigned vectors but cast them to signed internally.
- Convert the inputs into signed integers representing fixed-point numbers in two's complement.
- Perform the addition as a signed operation.
- Assign the result back to an unsigned output vector representing the fixed-point result.
- This naturally handles sign, addition, subtraction, and overflow within the N-bit signed range.
- Since it's fixed-point with Q fractional bits, no special shifting is needed during addition.
- This approach simplifies the architecture, improves readability, and reduces manual bit manipulations, while being synthesis-friendly and parameterizable.

This also inherently implements the correct sign and overflow behavior of two's complement arithmetic without explicitly computing absolute values or comparing magnitudes.

Additionally:
- The internal calculation uses a wider signed register (N+1 bits) to detect overflow if needed, but here output is truncated back to N bits.
- No internal reg required; all combinational logic.
- The module is purely combinational with parameters N and Q for precision and total bit width.
