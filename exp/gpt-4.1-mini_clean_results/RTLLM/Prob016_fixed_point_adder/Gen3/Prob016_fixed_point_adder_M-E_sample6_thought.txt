A different architectural approach is to manually handle fixed-point addition by explicitly separating sign and magnitude, then performing addition or subtraction on magnitudes, and reconstructing the signed fixed-point result.

Key steps:

1. Extract sign bits of inputs `a` and `b`.

2. Calculate magnitudes (absolute values) as unsigned numbers by conditionally inverting and adding one if negative (manual two's complement absolute).

3. Based on sign bits:
   - If same sign: sum magnitudes; assign sign of inputs to result.
   - If different signs: subtract smaller magnitude from larger magnitude; assign sign of operand with larger magnitude.

4. Since the numbers are fixed-point in two's complement with Q fractional bits, magnitude calculations ignore the fraction/integer split—they treat all bits as a unit.

5. The final magnitude is then converted back to signed two's complement form by applying sign and producing the N-bit output.

6. Overflow is naturally limited by N bits; no saturation logic is added.

7. Implementation uses purely combinational logic with local variables (regs) to hold magnitudes and intermediate sums/differences.

This approach explicitly implements two's complement absolute value logic and conditional addition/subtraction on unsigned magnitudes, clarifying the fixed-point adder behavior at the bit level without relying on signed arithmetic operators.

This meets the problem requirement for conditional addition or subtraction based on sign bits, and sets the result sign accordingly.
