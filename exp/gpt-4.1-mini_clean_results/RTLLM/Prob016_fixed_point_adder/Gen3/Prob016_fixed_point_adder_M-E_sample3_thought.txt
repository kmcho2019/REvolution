A different architectural approach is to explicitly compute the fixed-point addition using unsigned magnitude arithmetic combined with explicit sign handling, rather than relying on signed types.

Key points:

1. Interpret inputs `a` and `b` as signed fixed-point numbers in two's complement with N bits and Q fractional bits.

2. Extract sign bits, and compute absolute values as unsigned numbers (N-1 bits).

3. Perform addition or subtraction on the unsigned magnitudes, depending on sign bits:

   - If signs are equal, add magnitudes.

   - If signs differ, subtract smaller magnitude from larger magnitude.

4. Determine the sign of the result by:

   - For equal signs: same as operands' sign.

   - For different signs: sign of operand with larger magnitude; if magnitudes equal, output zero.

5. Construct the final result by concatenating the sign bit and the magnitude result.

6. Use registers and combinational always block for clarity.

7. This method explicitly works at the magnitude level, avoiding signed arithmetic and provides clear control over the sign and magnitude logic.

8. Overflow behavior: For addition of equal signs, magnitude addition can overflow N-1 bits (e.g., 15 bits if N=16). The MSB of the magnitude sum could overflow, which indicates overflow in two's complement fixed-point.

   - Here, we discard overflow beyond N-1 bits (typical fixed-width wraparound).

9. Fractional precision Q is inherent since we treat inputs as fixed-point values with Q fractional bits, and addition doesn't change Q.

This architecture fully respects the fixed-point addition semantics via magnitude + sign processing, cleanly separates sign from magnitude arithmetic, and uses parameterizable widths with no reliance on signed data types.
