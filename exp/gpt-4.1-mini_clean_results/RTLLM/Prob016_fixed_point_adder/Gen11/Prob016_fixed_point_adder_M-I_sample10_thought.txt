To improve the original solution for better performance, power, and area (PPA), the main optimization targets are:

1. **Reduce redundant arithmetic units**: The original code performs three full N-bit additions/subtractions (`mag_sum`, `mag_diff_ab`, `mag_diff_ba`). We can eliminate one subtraction by computing only one difference and then selecting the magnitude based on comparison. This saves area.

2. **Avoid multiple two's complement conversions**: By carefully managing sign and magnitude logic, minimize number of negations and additions.

3. **Simplify sign and zero checks**: Use minimal logic for zero detection and sign output.

4. **Maintain purely combinational logic**: Retain clarity and synthesis friendliness.

5. **Parameterization remains**: Keeps flexibility for arbitrary fixed-point widths and fractional bits.

The approach is:

- Extract absolute magnitudes for `a` and `b`.
- Compare magnitudes once to decide ordering.
- For same-sign inputs, just add magnitudes.
- For different-sign inputs, subtract smaller from larger magnitude.
- Determine output sign based on which magnitude is greater and original signs.
- Reconstruct result in two's complement from sign and magnitude.
- When result magnitude is zero, force sign to zero (positive).

By computing only one subtraction and one comparison, we save on hardware. This slightly improves area and power.

This optimization maintains the original functional behavior and fixed-point arithmetic consistency.
