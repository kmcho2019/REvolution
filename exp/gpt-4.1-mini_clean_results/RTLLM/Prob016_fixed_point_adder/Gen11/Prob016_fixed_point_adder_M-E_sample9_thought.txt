A novel approach is to treat the fixed-point inputs as signed numbers directly using Verilog's signed types, leveraging built-in signed arithmetic to handle addition and subtraction, which naturally manages sign, overflow, and two's complement representation without explicitly computing absolute values.

Key points:

- Declare inputs and outputs as signed vectors of width N.

- Perform signed addition directly on inputs, resulting in an (N+1)-bit signed intermediate sum to catch overflow.

- Saturate the output to the maximum/minimum representable fixed-point value if overflow occurs (based on the sign of the overflowed result).

- This approach maintains consistent fixed-point Q fractional bits since the binary point doesn't move.

- By relying on the language's signed addition and saturation logic, the design simplifies arithmetic logic, reduces logic and power overhead, and eliminates manual absolute value manipulations.

- The res register stores the saturated result.

This method robustly supports fixed-point addition with parameterized precision and bit width, providing straightforward extension or reuse.
