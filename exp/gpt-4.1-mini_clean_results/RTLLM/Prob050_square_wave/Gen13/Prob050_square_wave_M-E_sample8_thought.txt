Use a comparator-based edge detection approach where the count increments continuously from zero to its maximum (255), wrapping around. On each clock cycle, compare count to freq/2 (half of the freq input) to determine the output wave level. The wave_out is high when count is less than freq/2 and low otherwise, creating a variable duty cycle square wave with 50% duty when freq is even.

This method eliminates toggling logic and explicit flip-flops for wave_out, instead generating wave_out combinationally from count and freq. The freq input controls the period by changing the width of the high portion of the wave.

To handle freq=0 (no toggling), wave_out will be constantly low (or could be defined as constant). The design uses a free-running 8-bit counter with automatic wrapping, producing a periodic waveform with period proportional to freq.

This approach reduces state registers, replaces toggling with combinational logic, and may improve timing by removing conditional toggling and simplifying logic paths. It also naturally creates a 50% duty cycle when freq is even, approximating the desired square wave.
