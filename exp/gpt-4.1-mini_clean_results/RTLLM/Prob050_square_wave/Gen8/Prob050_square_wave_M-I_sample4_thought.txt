To improve the original design for better performance and power efficiency, I will implement an up-counter approach with minimal logic for toggling wave_out. Instead of counting down from freq, I count up from zero to (freq - 1), which typically synthesizes efficiently as a comparator with an incrementer. When count reaches freq - 1, wave_out toggles, and count resets to zero.

Additionally, I will:

- Remove the reset input to simplify the design, initializing wave_out and count at power-up with inferred reset (if allowed by environment), reducing reset path and associated power.
- Avoid using the subtraction in the toggle condition by precomputing freq_minus_one and comparing count to it.
- Use a clock enable signal gated by freq being non-zero to suppress switching when freq=0, improving power.
- Keep wave_out toggling only at toggle events to reduce glitching.
- Maintain non-blocking assignments and synchronous logic.

This approach optimizes the toggle detection and counting logic, reduces switching when disabled, and eliminates the reset signal for potentially smaller area and power savings where resets are not mandatory.

If reset is required, it can be added back easily. For now, I assume no reset based on original specs.

This should yield similar or better PPA metrics, as counting up with comparison to freq-1 is typically efficient, and gating counting on freq=0 reduces switching.

The downside is the removal of reset input; if not allowed, it can be reintroduced easily.
