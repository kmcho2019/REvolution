The original solution uses a 9-bit adder and overflow detection to replace a comparator, which can add unnecessary delay and area for such a small counter and comparator size. Also, the count register resets to zero when freq ≤ 1, causing unnecessary switching; better to hold count steady in this condition to save power. Removing the synchronous reset (rst_n) simplifies the design since it wasn't specified, reducing area and complexity. Instead, implement power and timing optimization by directly comparing count with freq - 1 to toggle wave_out, which synthesis tools optimize well for 8-bit signals. Keep counting and toggling disabled (count holds, wave_out holds) when freq is 0, preventing invalid toggling. This results in simpler, smaller, lower power design with better timing margins.
