The original solution uses an initial block to generate the clock, which is appropriate for testbench clock generators but may cause timing mismatches if the PERIOD parameter is odd, leading to truncation in `(PERIOD/2)`. Also, initializing `clk` directly in an initial block is correct, but to ensure even PERIOD and precise timing, I will explicitly compute half period as a real value to avoid rounding/truncation errors and use a `localparam` to represent the half period delay.

Since synthesis is not the goal (clock generator for simulation), using an initial forever loop is appropriate.

To further improve correctness:

- Use a localparam `HALF_PERIOD` computed as `PERIOD/2.0` to avoid integer truncation.

- Make sure the initial block sets the initial value to 0, then toggles every half period precisely.

- This will guarantee a clock with exact PERIOD length, even for odd values.

- The module remains purely a clock generator suitable for simulation.

This improves simulation accuracy and prevents failures due to timing mismatches caused by integer division truncation.
