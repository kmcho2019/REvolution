A different approach to generate a periodic clock signal without using delays or infinite loops in an initial block is to exploit Verilog's built-in `forever` loop inside an initial block together with a parameterized `#(period/2)` delay. Although this method is also simulation-only (since no input clock is given), it offers a clean and concise implementation with clear timing control and no counters. 

The key idea is:
- Initialize `clk` to 0 at the start.
- Use an initial block with a `forever` loop that waits for half the PERIOD using `#(PERIOD/2)` and toggles `clk`.
- The toggling repeats indefinitely, producing a square wave of the desired period.

This approach directly expresses the toggling timing without internal counters or manual increments and is commonly used in testbenches and simulation clock generators. The module is simple and the parameterization straightforward.

Since the task specifies an initial block and the output `clk`, this method perfectly fits those requirements while being distinct from the counter-based approach.
