Instead of using an initial block with delays to toggle the clock, use a continuous procedural block that toggles the clock on a timed event triggered by a forever loop inside an always block combined with a timer variable.

Specifically, implement a free-running clock with an always block and a counter variable incremented by a time unit (e.g., #1), toggling the clock every half period.

This approach avoids reliance on delays inside an initial block and models the clock toggling more structurally.

It also provides clear cycle timing and can be easily modified for synthesis or simulation.

The clock is initialized to 0, and an integer counter counts time steps until half_period, at which point the clock toggles and counter resets.

This method ensures integer granularity timing and good simulation compatibility.

It is also easy to extend with a clock enable or reset if needed later.

This design is novel relative to the original initial+forever+delay approach.
