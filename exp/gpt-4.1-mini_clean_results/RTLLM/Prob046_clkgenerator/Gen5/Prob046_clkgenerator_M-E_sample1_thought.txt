Instead of using an initial block with delays, this design generates the clock using a simple counter driven by an internal time unit (e.g., a simulator time tick) or using a local generated always block triggered by a dummy clock or simulation time progression. 

Here, since we want a standalone module, I will implement a clock generator using an always block with an integer counter that increments on a dummy event (like every time unit), and toggles clk when the count reaches half the PERIOD. This approach models the clock period explicitly in terms of cycles or time units and avoids delays with real numbers.

Specifically, I will use an always block triggered by the simulation time unit (using `#1` in initial to generate a "tick") and count ticks internally. When the count reaches half of PERIOD, toggle the clk and reset count.

This structure does not rely on delays with real numbers; it uses only integer counters and an initial block to generate a "tick" signal internally. This approach provides explicit control over timing and is portable for simulation.

The module interface remains simple with only an output clk. The internal counter and the "tick" generator are hidden inside.

This method also lets the PERIOD be an even or odd integer without truncation issues, as counting is integer-based.
