A novel approach to this problem is to use a shift register that shifts data in from the LSB side rather than MSB, but to assign the output in reverse order, ensuring that the final parallel output places the first serial bit received at the MSB and the last at the LSB as required.

Specifically, on each valid input bit, we shift the existing register left by 1 and insert the new bit at the LSB. We use a 4-bit counter to track the number of bits received. When the counter reaches 8, indicating 8 bits have been collected, we reverse the bits of the shift register to produce the output such that the first received bit is the MSB and the last received bit is the LSB, matching the requirement that bits appear sequentially from MSB to LSB in the parallel output.

To implement bit reversal efficiently, a simple bit rearrangement is done combinationally when the counter hits 8. The dout_valid is asserted for a single cycle immediately after the 8th bit is received.

Reset clears all registers and output signals. The design is synchronous and all signals use nonblocking assignments.

This approach separates the data collection (shift register accumulating bits from LSB) from the output formatting (reversing bit order), enhancing modularity and making the design's intent clear. The bit reversal is straightforward for 8 bits and avoids complex shifting logic during data capture.

The timing guarantees that the output data is stable and valid right after receiving 8 serial bits, with dout_valid pulsed for one cycle.

This technique is simple, resource-light, and easy to verify.
