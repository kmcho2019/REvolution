To implement an 8-bit radix-2 divider supporting signed and unsigned division, I will design a sequential FSM that performs restoring division algorithm over 8 clock cycles. The approach is:

1. **Input Capture and Preprocessing:**
   - On opn_valid, capture dividend and divisor, and determine their absolute values if sign is set.
   - Record sign flags to apply at the end.
   - Handle divisor zero case (optional: here we assume divisor != 0).

2. **Restoring Division Algorithm with 8 Iterations:**
   - Use a 17-bit shift register combining remainder (9 bits: including one extra bit for sign) and quotient (8 bits).
   - Initialize remainder = 0, quotient = dividend_abs.
   - On each iteration:
     - Shift left remainder and next dividend bit (from quotient).
     - Subtract divisor_abs from remainder.
     - If remainder >= 0 after subtraction:
       - Set quotient bit to 1.
     - Else:
       - Restore remainder (add back divisor_abs).
       - Set quotient bit to 0.

3. **Sign Correction:**
   - After division completes, apply sign correction to quotient and remainder.
   - Quotient sign is XOR of dividend and divisor sign.
   - Remainder sign is the same as dividend sign.

4. **Output Result:**
   - The result concatenates remainder (8 bits) in upper half and quotient (8 bits) in lower half.
   - res_valid asserted to signal result ready.

5. **Handshake:**
   - Wait for res_ready to clear res_valid and ready for next operation.

This design differs by explicitly implementing restoring division with a dedicated remainder and quotient register, clear iteration steps, and explicit restoring of remainder when subtraction is negative. This is simpler and more intuitive, easier to verify and debug.

The pipeline is fully synchronous with FSM states: IDLE, RUN, DONE.

This approach ensures correct handling of signed and unsigned division with clear separation of steps.
