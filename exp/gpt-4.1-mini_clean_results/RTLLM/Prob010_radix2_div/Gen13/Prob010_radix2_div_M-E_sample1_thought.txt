Use a radix-2 non-restoring division algorithm implemented as an iterative FSM with synchronous logic.  
On opn_valid, capture inputs and determine signs if signed division is requested.  
Compute absolute values of dividend and divisor for the division process.  
Use a 17-bit shift register SR to hold a 9-bit partial remainder in the upper bits and 8-bit quotient in the lower bits.  
Initialize SR with the dividend (absolute) shifted left by 1 (to provide an extra bit for partial remainder).  
At each clock cycle when running, shift left SR by 1, then add or subtract the divisor depending on the sign of the partial remainder:  
- If partial remainder was positive, subtract divisor.  
- If partial remainder was negative, add divisor.  
Set the current quotient bit based on the sign of the updated partial remainder after addition/subtraction.  
After 8 iterations, the division finishes, and if the partial remainder is negative, add back the divisor to correct the remainder.  
Apply sign corrections to the quotient and remainder based on the original signs of the dividend and divisor for signed division.  
Output the 16-bit result with remainder in upper 8 bits and quotient in lower 8 bits, and assert res_valid.  
The design uses a simple counter and start signal to control the FSM and res_valid signal to indicate completion.  
Reset clears internal state.  
This approach is different from restoring division by implementing non-restoring division, which avoids restoring partial remainders, potentially increasing efficiency.
