Use a synchronous iterative radix-2 divider with a state machine to process the division over 8 cycles. 

On opn_valid, capture and sign-extend the dividend and divisor, converting to absolute values if signed. Use a 17-bit shift register holding the partial remainder (upper 9 bits) and partial quotient (lower 8 bits). Initialize the remainder with the absolute dividend shifted left by 1 bit (to provide room for the next bit during shifts).

On each clock cycle, shift the remainder and quotient left by one bit, then subtract the divisor from the remainder part. If subtraction succeeds (remainder >= divisor), set the current quotient bit to 1 and update remainder with subtraction result; otherwise, leave the remainder as is and quotient bit 0.

After 8 iterations, fix signs of quotient and remainder based on the input signs if signed division is enabled.

Output the combined 16-bit result with remainder in upper 8 bits and quotient in lower 8 bits, and raise res_valid to indicate result ready.

This method avoids complex 9-bit subtraction and borrow detection by carefully managing the remainder and quotient in the shift register and uses simple combinational logic.

The FSM has three states: IDLE (waiting for opn_valid), DIVIDE (performing iterations), and DONE (output ready). This clean separation eases timing and control.
