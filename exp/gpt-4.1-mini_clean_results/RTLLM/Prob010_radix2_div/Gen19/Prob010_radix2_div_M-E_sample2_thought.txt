I will implement a synchronous radix-2 divider with an explicit 8-cycle iterative process controlled by a counter.

Key design points:

- Inputs are latched on a valid pulse (opn_valid) when the divider is idle and not busy.
- The divider supports signed and unsigned division by first computing absolute values of dividend and divisor.
- The division process uses a 17-bit shift register SR that holds the remainder in upper 9 bits and the partial quotient in lower 8 bits. Initially, SR loads dividend shifted left by 1 bit (to accommodate subtraction and shift).
- At each cycle, the top 9 bits (remainder portion) are subtracted by divisor. If subtraction result is non-negative, set quotient bit to 1 and update remainder to subtraction result. Otherwise, quotient bit is 0 and remainder unchanged.
- The quotient bit is shifted into the LSB of SR after shifting left by 1.
- After 8 iterations, the division is complete.
- If signed operation, quotient and remainder signs are corrected.
- Division by zero outputs a saturated quotient (e.g., 0xFF for quotient) and the dividend as remainder.
- Output is valid (res_valid = 1) after completion until acknowledged by res_ready input.
- This design includes an input res_ready to handshake output validity, allowing back-to-back division operations.
- The code uses a state machine with three states: IDLE, BUSY, DONE.
- All signals updated on the clock with synchronous reset.
- No typedefs for maximum tool compatibility.
- The division logic is clean and avoids complex recomputations.
- Proper start and done handshakes avoid stalls.

This approach guarantees:

- Correct iterative division with explicit cycle count.
- Simple and clear shift register updates.
- Correct signed division handling.
- Proper handling of division by zero.
- Robust handshake interface (opn_valid, res_ready, res_valid).
