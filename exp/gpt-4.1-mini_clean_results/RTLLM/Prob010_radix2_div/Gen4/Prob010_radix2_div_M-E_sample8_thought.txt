Implement a radix-2 divider using a classic non-restoring division algorithm with an explicit iterative shift-subtract approach controlled by a finite state machine (FSM). The key novel architectural ideas are:

- Separate sign processing at the start and end to handle signed division by converting inputs to absolute values and applying sign corrections on quotient and remainder after division.
- Use a single 17-bit shift register `SR` to hold remainder in the upper 9 bits and quotient in the lower 8 bits, shifted left every cycle.
- At each iteration, subtract the divisor absolute from the upper 9 bits of `SR`. If subtraction yields no borrow, shift in a quotient bit of 1; else restore remainder and shift in quotient bit 0.
- Use a simple FSM with three states: IDLE, DIVIDE, and DONE, with clear separation of control and data path.
- Result composed as `{remainder[7:0], quotient[7:0]}` after sign correction.
- res_valid signals when the result is ready and held until next operation starts or reset.

This architecture cleanly separates sign handling, subtraction logic, and FSM control, enabling clean and readable synchronous design suitable for synthesis and simulation.
