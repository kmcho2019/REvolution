Refactor the radix-2 divider by separating combinational and sequential logic more cleanly and restructuring the FSM as a one-hot encoded style for clarity. Use an explicit division datapath with combinational subtraction and next-state calculation via assign statements, and keep state registers simple. Also, instead of holding the entire remainder and quotient in a single shift register, split them into separate registers for clearer datapath control. This structure enhances clarity and may improve synthesis tool optimization.

Key points of refactoring:
1. One-hot FSM states (`IDLE`, `RUN`, `DONE`) implemented as separate signals.
2. Use separate registers for quotient and remainder instead of a 16-bit shift register.
3. Perform subtraction combinationally using remainder and divisor.
4. Use a next-state logic with continuous assignment.
5. Register inputs on operation start, including sign and absolute values.
6. Control `res_valid` as a simple registered pulse after operation completes.
7. Result formation and sign correction performed combinationally after done.
8. Handle divisor zero case by outputting zero quotient and remainder.
9. Make the design fully synchronous, only combinational logic for subtraction and corrections.
10. Clear comments and organized logic for easy understanding and maintenance.

This refactor maintains the radix-2 division principle and signed/unsigned support but offers a cleaner, more modular code structure improving readability and potential tool friendliness.
