Implement a radix-2 divider using a sequential iterative subtract-and-shift approach with an explicit FSM controlling the division phases. On opn_valid, inputs are latched, and absolute values are taken based on sign. The dividend is loaded into a 9-bit remainder register (shifted left by one for the algorithm) and quotient is initially zero. Each cycle, the remainder is shifted left by one, and the divisor is conditionally subtracted from the remainder. If subtraction is successful (non-negative result), the corresponding quotient bit is set to 1; otherwise, the remainder is restored and quotient bit set to 0. After 8 iterations, if the operation is signed, quotient and remainder signs are corrected. The result is then packed as {remainder[7:0], quotient[7:0]} and res_valid asserted. FSM states include IDLE, RUN, and DONE to control operation sequencing, improving clarity and extensibility. The design cleanly separates the subtraction step and sign correction, and handles division by zero by outputting zero quotient and remainder immediately.
