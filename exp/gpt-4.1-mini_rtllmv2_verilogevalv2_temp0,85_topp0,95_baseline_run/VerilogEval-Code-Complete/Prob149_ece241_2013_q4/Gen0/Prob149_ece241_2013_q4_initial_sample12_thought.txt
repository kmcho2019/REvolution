The design implements a finite state machine (FSM) that tracks the water level based on the three sensors s[1], s[2], s[3]. The flow control outputs fr1, fr2, fr3 correspond to nominal flow rates at each level, and dfr controls the supplemental flow valve, enabled when the water level is rising from a lower to a higher level zone.

Key points:
- The water level zones are defined by the sensors:
  - Above s[3]: s[1], s[2], s[3] all asserted, flow rate zero (all outputs off).
  - Between s[3] and s[2]: s[1], s[2] asserted, fr1 on.
  - Between s[2] and s[1]: only s[1] asserted, fr1 and fr2 on.
  - Below s[1]: no sensors asserted, fr1, fr2, fr3 all on.
- dfr (supplemental valve) is enabled when the water level rises from a lower zone to a higher zone (previous zone number less than current).
- Synchronous active-high reset initializes the FSM to the lowest water level (no sensors asserted) with all flow valves open and dfr asserted.
- We'll encode the zones as 2-bit states:
  00 = below s[1]
  01 = between s[2] and s[1]
  10 = between s[3] and s[2]
  11 = above s[3]
- At each clock, determine the current zone from sensors, compare with previous zone to determine if water level is rising, update outputs accordingly.

This approach simplifies logic and cleanly implements the required behavior.
