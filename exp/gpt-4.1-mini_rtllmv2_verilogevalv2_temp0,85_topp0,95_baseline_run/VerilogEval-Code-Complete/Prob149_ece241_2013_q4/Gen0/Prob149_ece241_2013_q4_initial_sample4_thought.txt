The design is a synchronous FSM that monitors the water level via three sensors s[1], s[2], s[3]. Based on the sensor inputs, the water level falls into one of four states: above s[3], between s[3] and s[2], between s[2] and s[1], or below s[1]. Each state corresponds to a specific nominal flow valve output combination (fr1, fr2, fr3), and the supplemental flow valve dfr depends on whether the water level is rising compared to the previous state.

We will encode states based on water level ranges:
- STATE_ABOVE_S3: s[1], s[2], s[3] all asserted
- STATE_BETWEEN_S3_S2: s[1], s[2] asserted, s[3] deasserted
- STATE_BETWEEN_S2_S1: s[1] asserted, s[2], s[3] deasserted
- STATE_BELOW_S1: none asserted

We will keep track of the previous state to detect if the water level is rising (previous state lower in water level order than current) to control dfr.

Reset sets the FSM to the lowest water level (below s[1]) and all outputs asserted.

On each clock, inputs s are sampled, next state is determined. Outputs are assigned based on the current water level state and whether the level is rising.

Flow outputs according to the table:
- Above s[3]: no flow valves (fr1=0, fr2=0, fr3=0, dfr=0)
- Between s[3] and s[2]: fr1=1, others 0, dfr depends on rising
- Between s[2] and s[1]: fr1=1, fr2=1, fr3=0, dfr depends on rising
- Below s[1]: fr1=1, fr2=1, fr3=1, dfr depends on rising

We will implement this in a synchronous always block with reset, store previous water level state for comparison, and output the signals accordingly.
