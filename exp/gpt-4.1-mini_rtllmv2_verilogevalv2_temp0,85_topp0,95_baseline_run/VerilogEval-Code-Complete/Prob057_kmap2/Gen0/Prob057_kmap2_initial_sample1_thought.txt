First, let's write the minterms from the Karnaugh map. The inputs are a, b, c, d where 'ab' is the column index and 'cd' is the row index. The K-map layout is:

      ab
cd  00 01 11 10
00 | 1 |1 |0 |1 |
01 | 1 |0 |0 |1 |
11 | 0 |1 |1 |1 |
10 | 1 |1 |0 |0 |

For each cell that is '1', write down the minterm (a,b,c,d) as per the row and column:

Indexing variables as:
- a,b : columns (00=ab=00, 01=01, 11=11, 10=10)
- c,d : rows (00=cd=00, 01=01, 11=11, 10=10)

For example:
- cd=00, ab=00: c=0, d=0, a=0, b=0 => minterm = !a & !b & !c & !d

List all minterms with '1':
(Using notation: a,b,c,d)
1) cd=00, ab=00: !a & !b & !c & !d
2) cd=00, ab=01: !a & b & !c & !d
3) cd=00, ab=10: a & !b & !c & !d
4) cd=01, ab=00: !a & !b & c & !d
5) cd=01, ab=10: a & !b & c & !d
6) cd=11, ab=01: !a & b & c & d
7) cd=11, ab=11: a & b & c & d
8) cd=11, ab=10: a & !b & c & d
9) cd=10, ab=00: !a & !b & c & d
10) cd=10, ab=01: !a & b & c & d

Next, simplify the function using these minterms or by grouping from the K-map:

Looking for groups:

- Column ab=00 (a=0,b=0): rows 00,01,10 have 1s: c,d=00,01,10 => group of 3, but let's group 2's

- Group 1: cells (00,00),(01,00),(10,00) = all with ab=00, c=00,01,10, d varies. This is !a & !b & !d (since d=0 or 1), but since d varies, we can't simplify d. But let's look for bigger groups.

- Group 2: cells (10,00) and (10,01) are 1s: ab=00 and 01, cd=10 => c=1, d=0 - so group of two: (a=0,b=0 or 1), c=1,d=0 => c & !d & !a (since a=0 in 00, and a=0 in 01? No, in 01 b=1, a=0) So group is !a & c & !d (covers both)

- Group 3: cells (11,11),(11,10),(11,01) have 1s at ab=11,10,01, cd=11. That is a=1 for ab=11 and 10, a=0,b=1 for 01. So probably smaller groups.

- Let's write SOP expression from minterms (for clarity):

out = (!a & !b & !c & !d) + (!a & b & !c & !d) + (a & !b & !c & !d) + (!a & !b & c & !d) + (a & !b & c & !d) + (!a & b & c & d) + (a & b & c & d) + (a & !b & c & d) + (!a & !b & c & d) + (!a & b & c & d)

Try to factor common terms:

- For !c & !d terms:
  (!a & !b) + (!a & b) + (a & !b) = !a & (!b + b) + a & !b = !a + a & !b = !a + a & !b = !a + a & !b
  Actually, better: all terms with !c & !d and a,b combinations: 
  The terms (!a & !b), (!a & b), (a & !b) with !c & !d cover all except a=1,b=1.

- So sum of these three terms simplifies to (!a + !b)

- So part 1: (!a + !b) & !c & !d

- For c & !d terms:
  (!a & !b & c & !d) + (a & !b & c & !d) = (!b & c & !d) & (!a + a) = !b & c & !d

- For c & d terms:
  (!a & b & c & d) + (a & b & c & d) + (a & !b & c & d) + (!a & !b & c & d) + (!a & b & c & d) 
  Actually, repeated terms (!a & b & c & d) twice, so let's count once.

  The combinations are:
  - !a & b & c & d
  - a & b & c & d
  - a & !b & c & d
  - !a & !b & c & d

  Sum these:

  (c & d) & (all combinations of a,b) except (a=1,b=0) and (a=0,b=1) ?

  Actually, all combinations of a,b except (a=1,b=1) ?

  Checking:

  - !a & !b
  - !a & b
  - a & b
  - a & !b

  These are all combinations of a,b. So this means c & d (full a,b combinations) => c & d

So overall simplified expression:

out = (!a + !b) & !c & !d + !b & c & !d + c & d

We can factor further:

out = (!c & !d & (!a + !b)) + (!b & c & !d) + (c & d)

Alternatively, write as:

out = c & d + !c & !d & (!a + !b) + !b & c & !d

This is good enough for implementation.
