Analyze the Karnaugh map to find the minimal sum-of-products expression for the output. The map is 4-variable with variables a,b on columns and c,d on rows.

K-map:

       ab
  cd  00 01 11 10
  00 |1 |1 |0 |1 |
  01 |1 |0 |0 |1 |
  11 |0 |1 |1 |1 |
  10 |1 |1 |0 |0 |

We label variables as: c,d rows; a,b columns.

Let's write the minterms where output = 1:

Indices (cd,ab) and values:

cd=00 (c=0,d=0):
  ab=00 (a=0,b=0): 1 -> minterm m0 (a=0,b=0,c=0,d=0)
  ab=01 (a=0,b=1): 1 -> m4 (a=0,b=1,c=0,d=0)
  ab=11 (a=1,b=1): 0
  ab=10 (a=1,b=0): 1 -> m2 (a=1,b=0,c=0,d=0)

cd=01 (c=0,d=1):
  ab=00: 1 -> m1 (a=0,b=0,c=0,d=1)
  ab=01: 0
  ab=11: 0
  ab=10: 1 -> m3 (a=1,b=0,c=0,d=1)

cd=11 (c=1,d=1):
  ab=00: 0
  ab=01: 1 -> m13 (a=0,b=1,c=1,d=1)
  ab=11: 1 -> m15 (a=1,b=1,c=1,d=1)
  ab=10: 1 -> m14 (a=1,b=0,c=1,d=1)

cd=10 (c=1,d=0):
  ab=00: 1 -> m8 (a=0,b=0,c=1,d=0)
  ab=01: 1 -> m12 (a=0,b=1,c=1,d=0)
  ab=11: 0
  ab=10: 0

Now, group the ones to simplify:

Observing groups:

1) Group of four in top-left corner: m0(0000), m1(0001), m4(0100), m5(0101)?
m5 is 0, so no.

2) Group of four in left two columns and top two rows: m0, m1, m4, m? m4=1, m0=1, m1=1, but m5=0 so no.

3) Group of four in first column (ab=00) and all cd rows where output=1 at cd=00 (m0), cd=01(m1), cd=10(m8), cd=11(m9=0)? m9=0 no.

But m0,m1,m8 are 1; m9 is 0. So max group size is 2 or 3.

4) Group of four in bottom left corner: m8(1000), m12(1010), m? No, m9=0 and m13=1, but not in same columns.

Let's try another approach: directly write down the sum-of-products.

Minterms where output=1:

m0: a=0,b=0,c=0,d=0 -> !a & !b & !c & !d
m1: !a & !b & !c & d
m2: !a & b & !c & !d (wait check m2 = cd=00 ab=10)
m2 is at cd=00 (c=0,d=0), ab=10 (a=1,b=0)
So m2: a=1,b=0,c=0,d=0
m3: a=1,b=0,c=0,d=1
m8: a=0,b=0,c=1,d=0
m12: a=0,b=1,c=1,d=0
m13: a=0,b=1,c=1,d=1
m14: a=1,b=0,c=1,d=1
m15: a=1,b=1,c=1,d=1
m4: a=0,b=1,c=0,d=0
m10: 0
m9:0
m11:0

Writing all minterms with output=1:

m0: !a & !b & !c & !d
m1: !a & !b & !c & d
m2: a & !b & !c & !d
m3: a & !b & !c & d
m4: !a & b & !c & !d
m8: !a & !b & c & !d
m12: !a & b & c & !d
m13: !a & b & c & d
m14: a & !b & c & d
m15: a & b & c & d

Looking for groups:

Group A (m0,m1): !a & !b & !c (d changes)

Group B (m2,m3): a & !b & !c (d changes)

Group C (m4): !a & b & !c & !d (single)

Group D (m8, m12): !a & !d & c (b changes from 0 to 1, a=0)

Group E (m13,m15): b & c & d & !a or a

m13: !a & b & c & d

m15: a & b & c & d

We can group m13 and m15 as b & c & d (a changes)

Similarly m14: a & !b & c & d

Group F (m14): a & !b & c & d

m4 and m12: both have !a & b & !d (c changes between 0 and 1)

m4: !a & b & !c & !d

m12: !a & b & c & !d

So group G: !a & b & !d (c changes)

Similarly, m14 and m15 differ in b:

m14: a & !b & c & d

m15: a & b & c & d

So group H: a & c & d (b changes)

Sum of products:

out = (!a & !b & !c) // group A (m0,m1)

    + (a & !b & !c)  // group B (m2,m3)

    + (!a & b & !d)  // group G (m4,m12)

    + (!a & !d & c)  // group D (m8,m12)

    + (a & c & d)    // group H (m14,m15)

    + (!a & b & c & d) // m13 (not grouped with others except m15 but a differs)

But m13 and m15 group H covers m15 but not m13 (since m13 is !a,b,c,d), m15 is a,b,c,d

But group H is a & c & d (b changes): covers m14,m15 but not m13

So m13 needs to be covered separately.

Putting it all together:

out = (!a & !b & !c) + (a & !b & !c) + (!a & b & !d) + (!a & !d & c) + (a & c & d) + (!a & b & c & d)

Try to factor some terms:

(!a & !b & !c) + (a & !b & !c) = !b & !c

Similarly (!a & b & !d) + (!a & !d & c) = !a & !d & (b + c)

Sum of products final:

out = (!b & !c) + (!a & !d & (b + c)) + (a & c & d) + (!a & b & c & d)

This is a minimal and clear expression.
