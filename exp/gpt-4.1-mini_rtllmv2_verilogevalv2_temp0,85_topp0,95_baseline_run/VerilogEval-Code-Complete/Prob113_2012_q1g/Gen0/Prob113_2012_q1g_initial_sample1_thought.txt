The Karnaugh map rows and columns correspond to x[1]x[2] and x[3]x[4] respectively. The K-map is:

       x3x4
       00  01  11  10
x1x2  ----------------
00  | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
01  | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
11  | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
10  | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 |

From the map, express f as sum of minterms or simplified SOP.

Minterms where f=1 (x1x2x3x4):

x1x2=00(0,0):
 - 00: f=1  -> 0000
 - 10: f=1  -> 0010

x1x2=01(0,1):
 - all 0

x1x2=11(1,1):
 - 00:1 -> 1100
 - 01:1 ->1101
 - 11:1 ->1111

x1x2=10(1,0):
 - 00:1 -> 1000
 - 01:1 -> 1001
 - 10:1 -> 1010

Now simplify groups:

Group1: x1x2=11, x3x4=00,01,11 (1100,1101,1111) -> x1=1, x2=1, x3=don't care, x4=don't care except for 11 and 01, which differ in x4; so group includes 1100 and 1101 and 1111, but 1110 is 0, so cannot group 1111 with 1100/1101 fully; however, 1100 and 1101 differ in x4 only, so group x1=1, x2=1, x3=0 and x4=don't care for 1100 and 1101 (but 1100 and 1101 differ in x4). Also 1111 is separate.

Group2: x1x2=10: 1000, 1001, 1010; 1011 is 0,  so can group 1000 and 1001 (differ in x4), and 1000 and 1010 differ in x3, so 1000,1001,1010 can be grouped as x1=1, x2=0, x4=don't care, x3=don't care?

No, 1001 and 1010 differ in both x3 and x4; 1000 and 1001 differ in x4, so group (1000,1001) is x1=1,x2=0,x3=0,x4=don't care

Group3: 0000 and 0010: differ in x4 only: group x1=0,x2=0,x3=0,x4=don't care

Similarly 1111 is single; 1010 is single; 1111 is 1111, which is x1=1,x2=1,x3=1,x4=1

So the groups:

- G1: x1=1,x2=1,x3=0,x4=don't care: covers 1100,1101

- G2: x1=1,x2=0,x3=0,x4=don't care: covers 1000,1001

- G3: x1=0,x2=0,x3=0,x4=don't care: covers 0000,0010

- Single 1111: x1=1,x2=1,x3=1,x4=1

- Single 1010: x1=1,x2=0,x3=1,x4=0

F can be written as:

f = (x1 & x2 & ~x3) | (x1 & ~x2 & ~x3) | (~x1 & ~x2 & ~x3) | (x1 & x2 & x3 & x4) | (x1 & ~x2 & x3 & ~x4)

Alternatively, factor:

f = ~x3 & ( (x1 & x2) | (x1 & ~x2) | (~x1 & ~x2) ) | (x1 & x2 & x3 & x4) | (x1 & ~x2 & x3 & ~x4)

Note that (x1 & x2) | (x1 & ~x2) | (~x1 & ~x2) = x1 | ~x2 (since (x1 & x2) | (x1 & ~x2) = x1, and together with ~x1 & ~x2 is ~x1 & ~x2)
