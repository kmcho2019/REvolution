The design intent is to detect three conditions in an HDLC bit stream by counting consecutive 1s and decoding the patterns that lead to discarding a bit, flagging frame boundaries, or signaling an error. The FSM is a Moore machine with outputs asserted exactly one clock cycle after the event.

The code encodes states for counting 0 to 6 consecutive 1s (ST_S0 through ST_S6), an error state (ST_ERR), and separate output assertion states (ST_DISC, ST_FLAG, ST_ERR) that hold the output signals high for one cycle.

The key problematic areas revealed by the simulation log are the 'disc' and 'err' outputs, both showing multiple mismatches, while 'flag' is clean.

   - The FSM logic moves from ST_S5 to ST_DISC on zero input, which asserts disc output for that cycle.
   - Then on the next cycle, based on input, it goes to ST_S0 or ST_S1 accordingly.
   - Despite this, the simulation shows 6 mismatches on 'disc'. This likely indicates that the FSM outputs disc at the wrong time or misses asserting disc for a full cycle.
   - This problem may stem from the way the outputs depend solely on the current state, but the event triggering disc depends on the previous input and state.
   - The timing relationship between input, state transition, and output assertion may not match the expected behavior, causing output misalignment.

2. **'err' output mismatches:**

   - In ST_ERR, the err output is asserted continuously (sticky error), as designed.
   - However, the simulation log shows 248 mismatches on 'err', indicating the err output is asserted incorrectly or not asserted when expected.
   - Since ST_ERR is a sticky error state, it should remain once entered until reset.
   - This suggests the FSM may be incorrectly transitioning out of ST_ERR or failing to enter it correctly.
   - Another cause might be the asynchronous reset usage or the interpretation of the error condition, but the code has synchronous reset.

   - Possibly the input sequence or timing assumptions in the testbench differ from the FSM's assumptions, causing the 'err' output to mismatch.
   - Alternatively, the FSM states and transitions may not correctly track the number of consecutive 1s, leading to incorrect or delayed error detection.

3. **Correct 'flag' output:**
