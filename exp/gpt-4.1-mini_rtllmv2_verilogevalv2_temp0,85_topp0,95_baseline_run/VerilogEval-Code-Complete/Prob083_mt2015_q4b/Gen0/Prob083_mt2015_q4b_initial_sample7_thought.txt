From the waveform, the output z is 1 only for specific input combinations and times. Observing the input-output patterns:

- Initially (time 0 to 20ns) when x=0, y=0, z=1
- At 25ns, x=1, y=0, z=0
- At 35-40ns, x=0, y=1, z=0
- At 45-50ns, x=1, y=1, z=1
- At 55ns, x=0, y=0, z=1 again
- At 60-65ns, x=0, y=1, z=0
- At 70ns, x=1, y=1, z=1
- At 75-85ns, x=0, y=1, z=0
- At 90ns, x=1, y=0, z=0

From this, z=1 when:
- x=0 and y=0
- x=1 and y=1

So z = (x & y) | (~x & ~y) which is XNOR of x and y.

We can implement this using a simple assign statement for z as the XNOR of inputs x and y.
