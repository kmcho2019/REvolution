The output z is high at initial state (0ns to 20ns), also at times when both x and y are 1 (45ns, 50ns, 70ns), and again at 55ns when both x and y are 0. z is low otherwise. Observing the waveform:
- z = 1 when (x=0 and y=0) or (x=1 and y=1)
- z = 0 otherwise
Thus, z behaves like the XNOR of x and y.
