From the waveform, observe that z is mostly 1 when both x and y are 0, and 0 otherwise, except some cases when x and y are both 1 and z is 1. Let's analyze the waveform:

At time 0 to 20ns: x=0, y=0 => z=1
At 25ns, x=1, y=0 => z=0
At 35-40ns, x=0, y=1 => z=0
At 45-50ns, x=1, y=1 => z=1
At 55ns, x=0, y=0 => z=1
At 60-65ns, x=0, y=1 => z=0
At 70ns, x=1, y=1 => z=1
At 75-85ns, x=0, y=1 => z=0
At 90ns, x=1, y=0 => z=0

From this, z is 1 if (x and y are both 0) or (both are 1), else 0. So, z = (x & y) | (~x & ~y), which is x XNOR y.

Therefore, implement z as x XNOR y.
