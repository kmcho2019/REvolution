The output z is high only when both x and y are low (x=0, y=0) as seen at times 0-20ns and at 55ns. At all other times, z is low except at times where x=1 and y=1 (45ns, 50ns, 70ns) where z is high again. So the z output is high either when x and y are both zero or when both are one. This can be implemented as z = ~(x ^ y), i.e., z is high when x equals y.
