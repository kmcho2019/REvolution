To create a dual-edge triggered flip-flop using only single-edge triggered flip-flops (which is what FPGAs provide), we can use two flip-flops: one triggered on the rising edge and one on the falling edge of the clock. Each flip-flop samples the input `d` on its respective clock edge. Then, we combine their outputs to update the final output `q` on both edges.

A common approach is:
- Use a flip-flop triggered on the rising edge of `clk` that samples `d` and stores it in `q_rise`.
- Use another flip-flop triggered on the falling edge of `clk` that samples `d` and stores it in `q_fall`.
- Use a simple logic to update `q` from either `q_rise` or `q_fall` on both edges. Since `q` is a `reg`, it can be updated in an always block sensitive to both edges by combining the two registers.

Because an always block sensitive to both edges is not synthesizable, we can instead create two always blocks triggered separately on posedge and negedge that update `q` from their respective sampled values.

This will functionally behave like a dual-edge triggered flip-flop.

The implementation:
- Two registers `q_rise` and `q_fall` hold sampled values of `d` at posedge and negedge.
- Two always blocks triggered at posedge and negedge clk update `q_rise` and `q_fall` respectively.
- Two more always blocks (or combined logic) update `q` from `q_rise` and `q_fall` on their respective edges.

Alternatively, we can simply assign `q` directly in the posedge and negedge always blocks.

This is valid and synthesizable.
