First, interpret the Karnaugh map with variables ab as columns and cd as rows. The map is:

       ab
cd   01  00  10  11
00 | d | 0 | 1 | 1 |
01 | 0 | 0 | d | d |
11 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
10 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 |

Mapping ab and cd to values:

- ab = 00, 01, 10, 11 correspond to columns 1, 0, 2, 3 respectively.
- cd = 00, 01, 11, 10 correspond to rows 0, 1, 3, 2 respectively.

We rewrite the K-map with standard order (rows cd: 00,01,11,10; cols ab: 00,01,11,10) for clarity:

cd\ab | 00 | 01 | 11 | 10
-------------------------
00    | 0  | d  | 1  | 1
01    | 0  | 0  | d  | d
11    | 1  | 0  | 1  | 1
10    | 1  | 0  | 1  | 1

Note: The original map uses ab columns as 01, 00, 10, 11. We reorder to standard binary for clarity:
- column 00: ab=00
- column 01: ab=01
- column 10: ab=10
- column 11: ab=11

From the problem statement, the columns are ordered as 01, 00, 10, 11. To maintain consistency, let's map the input variables accordingly.

Assign column indices to ab:
col0: 01 -> a=0,b=1
col1: 00 -> a=0,b=0
col2: 10 -> a=1,b=0
col3: 11 -> a=1,b=1

Similarly, rows cd:
row0: 00 -> c=0,d=0
row1: 01 -> c=0,d=1
row2: 11 -> c=1,d=1
row3: 10 -> c=1,d=0

We will build the function based on this exact mapping.

Fill the function with the values (d=don't-care, can be chosen 0 or 1 for simplification):

Row\Col | 01(ab=01) | 00(ab=00) | 10(ab=10) | 11(ab=11)
--------------------------------------------------------
00(cd=00) | d | 0 | 1 | 1
01(cd=01) | 0 | 0 | d | d
11(cd=11) | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1
10(cd=10) | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1

Let's choose don't-cares to 1 or 0 to simplify:

- Row0, col0: d (cd=00, ab=01) → set to 1 to help grouping
- Row1, col2 and col3: d (cd=01, ab=10 and 11) → set to 0 to avoid complexity

Now, analyze groups:

Group 1: All entries in columns 10 and 11 (ab=10 or 11) for rows 00,11,10 are 1. This is a block where ab[1] = 1 (a=1). This suggests a term a=1.

Group 2: For row 11 (cd=11), columns 00,10,11 are 1. So for c=1, d=1 and b=0 or 1 and a=0 or 1 except for col01 (ab=01) which is 0. So likely c=1 and d=1 with some conditions.

Group 3: Row 10 (cd=10), columns 00,10,11 are 1.

Simplify:

- The big group of all 1's in columns 10 and 11 (ab=10 or 11) for rows 00,11,10 corresponds to a=1.

- For column 00 (ab=00), row 11 and 10 is 1, which is c=1,d=1 and c=1,d=0.

So c=1 and a=0,b=0 (ab=00) → a=0,b=0,c=1. So one product term is ~a & ~b & c.

- For column 00 (ab=00) row 00 is 0, row01 is 0.

- For column 01 (ab=01), row00 set to 1 (chosen from don't-care), rest zeros.

So another product term is ~a & b & ~c & ~d (for cd=00, ab=01).

So the final expression is:

out = a | (~a & ~b & c) | (~a & b & ~c & ~d)

This can be implemented in Verilog.
