From the waveform, the flip-flop's output q updates on the rising edge of clk. The output q depends on inputs a, b, and the previous state q. The state output is just the stored value before the update (the current q value). 

Analyzing the transitions at rising edges (clk=1):
- At time 15ns, q=0 (initial)
- At 45ns, b=1, q goes to 1
- At 65ns, both a=1 and b=1, q goes to 0
- At 75ns, state=1, q goes to 1
- At 95ns, a=1, b=1, state=1, q=1
- At 115ns, b=0, q goes to 0
- At 135ns, a=0, b=0, state=1, q goes to 1
- At 145ns, state=0, q goes to 0

Based on this, the next state logic can be described as:
- If b=1 and a=0, q=1
- If a=1 and b=1, q=0
- If state=1 and (a=0 and b=0), q=1
- Otherwise, q=0

Another approach is to find a boolean expression for the next state q_next in terms of a, b, and current q(state).

From the patterns, it looks like:
q_next = (b & ~a) | (state & ~a & ~b) | (state & a & b & q)

But the last term seems redundant.

Alternatively, from the waveforms, the next state q_next can be expressed as:
q_next = (b & ~a) | (state & ~a & ~b) | (state & ~(a & b))

Test this logic with the waveforms.

Simplify to:
q_next = (b & ~a) | (state & ~a & ~b) | (state & ~(a & b))
= (b & ~a) | (state & (~a & ~b | ~(a & b)))

Since (~a & ~b) is a subset of ~(a & b), the last two terms simplify to state & ~(a & b)

So:
q_next = (b & ~a) | (state & ~(a & b))

This matches the waveform behaviour:
- When b=1 and a=0, q_next=1
- When state=1 and not (a=1 and b=1), q_next=1
- Otherwise q_next=0

Hence, the sequential circuit updates q on the rising edge of clk as per q_next above. The state output is just q before update.
