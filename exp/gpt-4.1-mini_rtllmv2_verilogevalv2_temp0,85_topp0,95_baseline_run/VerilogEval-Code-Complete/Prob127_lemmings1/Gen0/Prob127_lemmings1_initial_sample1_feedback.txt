ANALYSIS:
The design intent is a Moore FSM with two states: walk_left and walk_right, switching directions when bumped on either side, and asynchronously resetting to walk_left. The Verilog code closely follows this intent by encoding states as a single bit, using an asynchronous reset in the clocked always block, and combinational next state logic.

However, the simulation log indicates 83 mismatches in outputs walk_left and walk_right, first occurring at time 120 ps, which is very early in the simulation. This suggests the outputs are not stable or correct immediately after reset or at some early clock edges.

A key observation is that the asynchronous reset is triggered on the posedge of areset. If areset is deasserted asynchronously, or if bump inputs change near the asynchronous reset release, the FSM may compute next_state and outputs based on transient or non-synchronized signals, causing glitches or incorrect outputs. The mismatch count (83 out of 229 samples) also points to possible timing issues or incorrect initial states during or shortly after reset.

Another subtlety is that the outputs are assigned directly from the state register, which should be stable in a Moore machine. Since walk_left and walk_right are assigned combinationally from 'state', any metastability or timing hazards in 'state' (due to asynchronous reset or input timing) can propagate to outputs.

In summary, while the code structurally matches the problem statement, the observed simulation errors likely arise from asynchronous reset handling and the resulting timing of state and outputs, causing mismatches at early simulation times.
