To implement a 64-bit ripple carry adder with pipelining, I will split the 64-bit addition into multiple pipeline stages. Each stage will add a portion of the operands along with the carry from the previous stage and register the partial sum and carry for the next stage. This pipelining enables throughput improvements by overlapping operations in different stages.

I will divide the 64-bit addition into 8 stages of 8 bits each. Each stage will add 8 bits of adda and addb plus the carry-in from the previous stage. Each stage will register its sum and carry-out. The carry-out of one stage feeds the carry-in of the next stage.

The enable signal (i_en) is also pipelined through registers to synchronize with the pipeline stages, producing the final output enable (o_en).

The output result is the concatenation of all partial sums plus the last carry-out bit, making a 65-bit sum.

On reset, all pipeline registers clear. On each clock, if i_en is asserted, the pipeline registers update; otherwise, they hold their values.

This approach balances logic complexity and pipeline depth, providing a good throughput for a large adder.
