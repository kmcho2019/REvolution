To achieve a 3.5x frequency division using double-edge clocking, we divide the input clock by 7 cycles (MUL2_DIV_CLK = 7) producing a divided clock period of 7 input clock cycles. We create two intermediate clocks: one toggles after 4 cycles and the other after 3 cycles, thus generating uneven clock periods. By phase shifting one intermediate clock by half a clock period (using the input clock's edges) and OR-ing both clocks, we create a fractional frequency division output with improved duty cycle and smoother waveform.

The design uses a 3-bit counter that counts from 0 to 6 (7 cycles total). The divided clock toggles on specific counts (4 and 0 for one phase, 3 and 0 for the other) to generate the two intermediate clocks. The phase shifted clocks are generated by triggering on opposite clock edges (posedge and negedge of clk). The output is the OR of these two intermediate signals.
