To achieve a fractional frequency division of 3.5 using double-edge clocking, we count through 7 input clock cycles (twice the desired division) and generate two intermediate clocks with uneven high/low periods (4 cycles high, 3 cycles low, then 3 cycles high, 4 cycles low). By creating two clocks that are phase shifted by half a clock period relative to each other (one delayed, one advanced), and then OR-ing them, the final output clock achieves a uniform frequency at 1/3.5 the input clock frequency with a balanced duty cycle. The design uses a 3-bit counter cycling from 0 to 6, two toggling signals to generate the intermediate clocks, and edge alignment logic for phase shifting. Reset asynchronously initializes all registers.
