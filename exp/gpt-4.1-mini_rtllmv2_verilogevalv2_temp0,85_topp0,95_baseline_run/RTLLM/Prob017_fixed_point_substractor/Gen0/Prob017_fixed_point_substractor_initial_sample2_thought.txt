To implement the fixed-point subtractor with parameters Q and N, we'll treat inputs a and b as signed fixed-point numbers represented in two's complement format. 

The subtraction will be done by analyzing the signs of a and b (MSB bit):
- If both have the same sign, we subtract their magnitudes and keep the sign the same.
- If they have different signs, we add their absolute values, and assign the sign based on the relative magnitude of a and b as per the instructions.

We will:
- Extract sign bits.
- Compute absolute values of inputs.
- Perform the appropriate addition or subtraction.
- Determine the sign of the result.
- Reapply the sign to the result.
- Set the output to zero with sign bit 0 if the result is zero.

Using a register `res` for the intermediate result and output `c` to hold the final signed fixed-point subtraction result.

This approach preserves the fixed-point format because we treat the entire bit-vector as a signed number. Adding or subtracting these signed numbers will maintain the fixed-point scaling implicitly. The parameter Q is informational here to clarify the fixed-point position but does not require special scaling manipulation during subtraction.

The module will be combinational for immediate subtraction.

We will use $signed conversions to handle signed arithmetic properly in Verilog.
