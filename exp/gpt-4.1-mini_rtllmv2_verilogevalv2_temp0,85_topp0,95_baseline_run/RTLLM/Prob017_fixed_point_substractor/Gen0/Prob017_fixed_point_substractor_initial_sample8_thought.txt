To implement the fixed-point subtractor with parameters Q and N, the inputs a and b are signed fixed-point numbers with N bits, where the top bit is the sign and the lower Q bits are fractional.

The subtraction behavior depends on the signs of a and b:

- If the signs are the same, subtract b from a directly. The result sign matches the inputs' sign.

- If the signs differ, effectively we perform an addition of the absolute values and determine the sign by comparing absolute values.

Because the inputs are in two's complement, we can perform subtraction directly and then handle the sign logic to enforce the described behavior.

We will:
- Extract signs of a and b.
- Compute absolute values of a and b.
- According to the sign combination, perform either subtraction or addition of absolute values.
- Determine the sign of the result accordingly.
- Handle the zero result case by forcing sign bit to zero.
- Store the final signed fixed-point result into 'res' register.
- Assign 'res' to output c.

This approach respects fixed-point representation as the bits are kept intact, and operations are done on the entire N-bit vectors.
