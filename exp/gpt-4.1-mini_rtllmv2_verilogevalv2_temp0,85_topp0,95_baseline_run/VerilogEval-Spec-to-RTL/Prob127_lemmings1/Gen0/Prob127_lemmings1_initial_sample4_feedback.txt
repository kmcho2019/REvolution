The design intent is to model a two-state Moore FSM representing a Lemming walking left or right, switching directions if bumped on either side. The outputs walk_left and walk_right are combinationally assigned based solely on the state, consistent with a Moore machine.

Despite this, the simulation log reports 83 mismatches in walk_left and walk_right outputs out of 229 samples, with the first mismatch at time 120 ps. This suggests the outputs do not match the expected values in about a third of the test samples.

Examining the code:

1. The next state logic correctly switches direction whenever either bump_left or bump_right is asserted, which matches the problem requirements ("If it's bumped on both sides at the same time, it will still switch directions").

2. The state register block uses posedge clk and posedge areset for asynchronous reset, setting state to WALK_LEFT on reset, which meets the spec.

3. The outputs are purely combinational and depend only on the current state, as required for a Moore machine.

Possible causes of the mismatch include:

- The testbench may be driving bump_left and bump_right signals asynchronously and expecting immediate output changes, but since the FSM is synchronous, outputs only update on clock edges after inputs are registered. This discrepancy can cause output mismatches if the testbench expects combinational response.

- The design uses SystemVerilog "typedef enum logic" which may be unsupported or differently interpreted by some simulators or synthesis tools, but the compilation log shows no syntax errors, so this is less likely.

- The FSM resets asynchronously on areset's posedge but the testbench may not respect reset timing properly, causing transient states and output glitches.

- The simulation mismatch starting at time 120 ps suggests a timing or initialization issue: the FSM might be entering unexpected states early in simulation or not properly synchronized with the testbench clock and input stimulus.

In summary, while the FSM logic matches the problem statement, the simulation mismatches indicate that the outputs sometimes do not reflect the intended state transitions, likely due to timing expectations or initial conditions differing between the design and the testbench scenario.
