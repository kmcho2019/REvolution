From the waveform, the sequential circuit has one bit of state memory. The output 'state' is the stored flip-flop value. The output 'q' depends combinationally on inputs a, b, and the state. Observing the pattern:

- 'state' is updated on the positive edge of clk.
- 'q' appears to be a function of a, b, and state.
- When state=0 and (a,b)=(0,1), q=1 (e.g., at 45ns).
- When state=0 and (a,b)=(1,0), q=1 (e.g., at 55ns).
- When state=1 and (a,b)=(0,0), q=1 (e.g., at 75ns).
- Otherwise, q=0.

Checking q more closely, q = (state & ~a & ~b) | (~state & ((a & ~b) | (~a & b))) which is the XOR of a and b when state=0, and q=1 when state=1 and a=0,b=0.

The next state on the clock edge equals q.

So, the flip-flop updates state <= q at posedge clk, and q is combinationally computed as above.

This behavior implements a one bit memory with output and next state logic defined by inputs and current state.
