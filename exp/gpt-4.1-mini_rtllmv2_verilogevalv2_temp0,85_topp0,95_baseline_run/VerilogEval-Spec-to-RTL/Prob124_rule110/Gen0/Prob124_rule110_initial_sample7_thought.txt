Implement a 512-bit register q to hold the current state of the cellular automaton. On each rising edge of clk:

- If load is high, load q with the input data.
- Otherwise, update q according to Rule 110.

To compute the next state for each cell i, consider q[i+1] as left neighbor, q[i] as center, and q[i-1] as right neighbor. For out-of-bound indices, treat as zero. 

Implement Rule 110 using the truth table given, which can be represented by the boolean function:

next = (left & center & ~right) ? 0 :
       (left & center & right) ? 0 :
       (left & ~center & right) ? 1 :
       (left & ~center & ~right) ? 0 :
       (~left & center & right) ? 1 :
       (~left & center & ~right) ? 1 :
       (~left & ~center & right) ? 1 :
       (~left & ~center & ~right) ? 0 :

This can be simplified as next = (left & center & right) ? 0 :
                                  (left & center & ~right) ? 1 :
                                  (left & ~center & right) ? 1 :
                                  (left & ~center & ~right) ? 0 :
                                  (~left & center & right) ? 1 :
                                  (~left & center & ~right) ? 1 :
                                  (~left & ~center & right) ? 1 :
                                  0;

But easier to implement as the truth table in boolean expression or a small combinational logic.

Alternatively, Rule 110's next state bit can be computed as:

next = (left & center & ~right) ? 0 :
       (left & center & right) ? 0 :
       (left & ~center & right) ? 1 :
       (left & ~center & ~right) ? 0 :
       (~left & center & right) ? 1 :
       (~left & center & ~right) ? 1 :
       (~left & ~center & right) ? 1 :
       (~left & ~center & ~right) ? 0;

Which matches the boolean function: next = (left & center & ~right) ? 0 :...

Alternatively, it is easier to implement by noticing Rule 110's binary representation is 01101110 (in binary), which corresponds to the next state bits indexed by the neighborhood bits (left, center, right):

Neighborhood (left center right) -> next state (bit from LSB to MSB)
111 (7) -> 0
110 (6) -> 1
101 (5) -> 1
100 (4) -> 0
011 (3) -> 1
010 (2) -> 1
001 (1) -> 1
000 (0) -> 0

So, next = rule110[{left, center, right}] where rule110 = 8'b01101110.

This is most convenient to implement by concatenating left, center, right as a 3-bit index into the rule vector.

For boundaries q[-1] and q[512], treat as 0.

Implement combinational logic for next state q_next, then on clock edge, q <= q_next or q <= data if load is asserted.
