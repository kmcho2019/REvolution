By analyzing the waveform, the output z is 1 only when both inputs x and y are 0 (except some cases) or when both are 1. Specifically, z is 1 when (x & y) == 1 or (x == 0 & y == 0) except when (x == 0 & y == 0) at times 35ns, 40ns, 60ns, 65ns, 75ns, 80ns, 85ns where z=0 if y=1 and x=0. Looking carefully at the waveform, when x=0 and y=0, z=1; when x=1 and y=0, z=0; when x=0 and y=1, z=0; and when x=1 and y=1, z=1. So the output is the XNOR of inputs x and y.
