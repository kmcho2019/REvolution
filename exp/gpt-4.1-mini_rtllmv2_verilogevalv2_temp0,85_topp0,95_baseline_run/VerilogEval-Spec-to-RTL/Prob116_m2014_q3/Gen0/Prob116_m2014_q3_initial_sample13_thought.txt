First, map the inputs x[3:0] to the Karnaugh map variables as specified. The K-map is indexed by x[3]x[4] for rows and x[1]x[2] for columns. For clarity, let's assume x[3] = x[3], x[4] = x[0] (since only x[3:0] are available and the problem uses 1-based indexing for x, but in Verilog indexing is zero-based, so to match the problem statement let's map x[3] to x[3], x[4] to x[0], x[1] to x[1], and x[2] to x[2]). But the problem says all inputs are one bit and x is 4 bits, so x[3] is the MSB and x[0] is the LSB.

From the Karnaugh map:

Rows (x[3]x[4]) = x[3]x[0]
Columns (x[1]x[2]) = x[1]x[2]

K-map cells (row, column):

Row 00 (x[3]=0, x[0]=0):
  Col 00 (x[1]=0, x[2]=0): d
  Col 01 (x[1]=0, x[2]=1): 0
  Col 11 (x[1]=1, x[2]=1): d
  Col 10 (x[1]=1, x[2]=0): d

Row 01 (x[3]=0, x[0]=1):
  Col 00: 0
  Col 01: d
  Col 11: 1
  Col 10: 0

Row 11 (x[3]=1, x[0]=1):
  Col 00: 1
  Col 01: 1
  Col 11: d
  Col 10: d

Row 10 (x[3]=1, x[0]=0):
  Col 00: 1
  Col 01: 1
  Col 11: 0
  Col 10: d

Since d means don't care, we can choose convenient values.

The goal is to write a minimized expression for f in terms of x[3:0]. I will write the minterms for the cells with 1, ignoring don't cares:

Minterms:

Row 01, Col 11: x[3]=0, x[0]=1, x[1]=1, x[2]=1 => 0 1 1 1 => x = 4'b0111 = 7

Row 11, Col 00: 1 1 0 0 => x=4'b1100 = 12

Row 11, Col 01: 1 1 0 1 => x=4'b1101 = 13

Row 10, Col 00: 1 0 0 0 => x=4'b1000 = 8

Row 10, Col 01: 1 0 0 1 => x=4'b1001 = 9

So the ones are at 7, 8, 9, 12, 13 and also from row 01 col 11 = 7.

Also from row 11 col 00 and 01: 12 and 13.

From row 10 col 00 and 01: 8 and 9.

From row 01 col 11: 7.

Also row 11 col 11 and 10 are don't cares, so can be chosen.

Similarly for other cells with 0, we avoid those minterms.

One approach is to write the sum of minterms for these ones and simplify.

Alternatively, let's write the expression directly by inspecting the K-map and grouping:

Group 1: row 11 and 10, columns 00 and 01 (cells 8,9,12,13) are 1.

Row 11: x[3]=1, x[0]=1

Row 10: x[3]=1, x[0]=0

Columns 00 and 01: x[1]=0, x[2]=0 or x[2]=1

So columns 00 and 01 imply x[1]=0 (since col 00=0 0 and col 01=0 1).

Hence, group 1 covers x[3]=1, x[1]=0 (since x[0] varies 0 or 1, and x[2] varies 0 or 1).

Expression for group 1: x[3] & ~x[1]

Group 2: row 01 col 11 = 1 (x=7), no adjacent 1s in that row or col, but d in adjacent cells.

We can merge cell 7 (0111) with don't cares in row 00 col 11 or row 01 col 10 or row 11 col 11 or row 10 col 10.

Cells with d near 7:

row 00 col 11 = d

row 01 col 10 = 0 (not d)

row 11 col 11 = d

row 10 col 10 = d

If we treat those d as 1, we can make groupings.

Group 3: columns 11 in rows 01, 11, and 10 can be grouped if we consider don't cares as 1.

Row 01 col 11: 1

Row 11 col 11: d (chosen as 1)

Row 10 col 11: 0 (no, so can't group all three)

So only row 01 and 11 col 11 can be grouped.

Row 01: 0 1 1 1 => x=7

Row 11: 1 1 1 1 => x=15 (d cell)

Group 3: column 11, rows 01 and 11: x[1]=1, x[2]=1, x[0] varies 1 or 1, x[3] varies 0 or 1.

Expression: x[1] & x[2] & x[0]

Group 4: row 11 and 10, col 01, cells 13 and 9 are 1.

Row 11 col 01: 1

Row 10 col 01: 1

Columns 01: x[1]=0, x[2]=1

Rows 11 and 10: x[3]=1, x[0]=1 or 0

So expression: x[3] & x[2] & ~x[1]

But this is included in group 1 (x[3] & ~x[1]) already.

Group 1 covers 8,9,12,13.

Group 3 covers 7, and possibly 15 (don't care).

Other 1s at 12 and 13 are in group 1.

So the final simplified expression:

f = x[3] & ~x[1] | x[1] & x[2] & x[0]
