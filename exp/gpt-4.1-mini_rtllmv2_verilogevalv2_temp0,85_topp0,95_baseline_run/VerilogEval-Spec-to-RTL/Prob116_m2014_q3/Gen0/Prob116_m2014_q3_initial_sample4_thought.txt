The inputs are 4 bits: x[3:0]. The Karnaugh map is organized with x[3]x[4] as row and x[1]x[2] as column. We need to map these bits properly. 
Assuming x[3] is the most significant bit and x[0] the least, the map uses x[3]x[4] but we only have x[3:0], so x[4] is ambiguous.

Since only 4 bits are given and the map labels x[1]x[2] and x[3]x[4], it's likely that "x" bits are numbered x[1], x[2], x[3], x[4], indexed 1-based, meaning the input vector is 4 bits representing x[1], x[2], x[3], x[4].

Therefore, map inputs: x[1], x[2], x[3], x[4] with indices from 1 to 4 (1-based). We should remap to zero-based indices:

- input vector x[3:0] corresponds to x[4] = x[3], x[3]=x[2], x[2]=x[1], x[1]=x[0] (or similar). To be consistent, we can say:

x[3] = x4  
x[2] = x3  
x[1] = x2  
x[0] = x1

Mapping Karnaugh map rows = x[3]x[4] = x4 x3 = x[3] x[2]  
Columns = x[1]x[2] = x2 x1 = x[1] x[0]

So rows (x[3]x[2]), columns (x[1]x[0]).

The Karnaugh map:

       x[1]x[0]
x[3]x[2] 00  01  11  10
00        d   0   d   d
01        0   d   1   0
11        1   1   d   d
10        1   1   0   d

We can implement the function by either writing the sum of minterms for 1s or simplifying using don’t cares.

First, list all cells with value 1:

- (x[3]x[2], x[1]x[0]) = (01, 11) = row=1 col=3 (0-based) → f=1
- (11, 00) = row=3 col=0 → f=1
- (11, 01) = row=3 col=1 → f=1
- (10, 00) = row=2 col=0 → f=1
- (10, 01) = row=2 col=1 → f=1

Cells with 0:

- (00, 01) = 0
- (01, 00) = 0
- (01, 10) = 0
- (10, 11) = 0

Cells with d (don’t care):

- (00, 00), (00, 11), (00, 10), (01, 01), (11, 11), (11, 10), (10, 10)

Let's express the inputs as:

row = x[3]x[2]  
col = x[1]x[0]

We can write the minterms for the 1s:

Positions with 1:

1) row=01 col=11 → x[3]=0, x[2]=1, x[1]=1, x[0]=1  
Minterm: ~x[3] & x[2] & x[1] & x[0]

2) row=11 col=00 → x[3]=1, x[2]=1, x[1]=0, x[0]=0  
Minterm: x[3] & x[2] & ~x[1] & ~x[0]

3) row=11 col=01 → x[3]=1, x[2]=1, x[1]=0, x[0]=1  
Minterm: x[3] & x[2] & ~x[1] & x[0]

4) row=10 col=00 → x[3]=1, x[2]=0, x[1]=0, x[0]=0  
Minterm: x[3] & ~x[2] & ~x[1] & ~x[0]

5) row=10 col=01 → x[3]=1, x[2]=0, x[1]=0, x[0]=1  
Minterm: x[3] & ~x[2] & ~x[1] & x[0]

Try to simplify:

Group 1: positions (10,00) and (10,01) → x[3]=1, x[2]=0, x[1]=0, x[0]=x (don't care)  
Group 2: positions (11,00) and (11,01) → x[3]=1, x[2]=1, x[1]=0, x[0]=x  
Group 3: position (01,11) → unique minterm: ~x[3] & x[2] & x[1] & x[0]

So simplified expression:

f = x[3] & ~x[2] & ~x[1] | x[3] & x[2] & ~x[1] | ~x[3] & x[2] & x[1] & x[0]

Combine the first two terms:

x[3] & ~x[1] & (~x[2] | x[2]) = x[3] & ~x[1] (since x[2] can be 0 or 1)

So:

f = x[3] & ~x[1] | ~x[3] & x[2] & x[1] & x[0]

This is the minimal function.
