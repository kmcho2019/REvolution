The state machine uses a one-hot encoding with states A to F assigned to y[0] to y[5]. The transitions depend on input w and the current state y. We want to implement next-state signals Y1 and Y3 which correspond to next state bits y[1] and y[3] respectively.

By inspection of the state transitions and one-hot encoding:

- Y1 corresponds to state B (y[1])
- Y3 corresponds to state D (y[3])

Let's write down the next state logic for y[1] (B) and y[3] (D):

For y[1] (B):

From the transitions:

- A(0) --0--> B: when in A (y[0]) and w=0, next state is B -> so Y1 = y[0] & ~w
- Other transitions do not lead to B.

So, Y1 = y[0] & ~w

For y[3] (D):

From the transitions:

- B(0) --1--> D: y[1] & w
- C(0) --1--> D: y[2] & w
- E(1) --1--> D: y[4] & w
- F(1) --1--> D: y[5] & w
- D(0) --1--> A (doesn't affect y[3])
  
So, Y3 = (y[1] | y[2] | y[4] | y[5]) & w

Implementing these equations directly in combinational logic will produce next-state signals Y1 and Y3.

The module will have inputs y[5:0] and w, outputs Y1, Y3.

No clock or reset needed here as this is combinational next state logic.
