The state machine uses a one-hot encoding for states A-F on y[5:0], where each bit corresponds to a state. The transitions depend on input w.

We need to implement next-state signals Y1 and Y3 corresponding to y[1] and y[3], i.e., the next values of bits y[1] and y[3] in the next cycle.

By inspection of the given state transitions and one-hot encoding:

- y[0] = A
- y[1] = B
- y[2] = C
- y[3] = D
- y[4] = E
- y[5] = F

We derive next Y1 (next y[1]):

States that transition into B (y[1]):

From A(0) with w=0: A(0)→B(1) => y[0] & ~w → y[1] next
From C(2) with w=1: C(0)→D(3), so no
From B(1) with w=0: B(0)→C(2), no
From D(3) with w=1: D(1)→A(0), no
From E(4) and F(5) no transition to B
So only from A with w=0 leads to B

But actually we want the next y[1]:

y[1]' = (y[0] & ~w)

Y3 (next y[3]):

States that transition to D(3) (y[3]):

From B(1) with w=1: B(0)→D(3) => y[1] & w
From C(2) with w=1: C(0)→D(3) => y[2] & w
From E(4) with w=1: E(1)→D(3) => y[4] & w
From F(5) with w=1: F(1)→D(3) => y[5] & w

So, next y[3] = w & (y[1] + y[2] + y[4] + y[5])

We implement these as combinational outputs Y1 and Y3.

Since the module has inputs y[5:0] and w, outputs Y1 and Y3 are combinational functions of y and w.
