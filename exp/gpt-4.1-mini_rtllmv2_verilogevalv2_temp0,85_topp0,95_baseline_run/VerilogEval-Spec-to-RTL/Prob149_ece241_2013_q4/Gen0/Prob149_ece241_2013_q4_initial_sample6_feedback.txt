The major issue lies with the dfr output, which should assert (go high) when the water level is rising compared to the previous level. The code attempts this by defining:

```verilog

...
dfr <= rising ? 1'b1 : 1'b0;
```

Because `prev_level` might not consistently hold the actual previous water level at the correct clock cycle, the comparison to determine rising level is unreliable, resulting in dfr toggling incorrectly or remaining incorrect, as reflected by the 1658 mismatches in the simulation.

Furthermore, the reset logic sets all outputs to 1, aligning with the requirement for low water level, but does not explicitly initialize `prev_level` and `curr_level` in a way that guarantees consistent rising detection on the first few cycles after reset.

In summary, the fundamental problem is the logic and timing of how the previous water level is stored and compared to the current level to generate the dfr signal. This discrepancy between design intent and implementation leads to the dfr output failing simulation extensively, while the nominal flow outputs behave correctly.
