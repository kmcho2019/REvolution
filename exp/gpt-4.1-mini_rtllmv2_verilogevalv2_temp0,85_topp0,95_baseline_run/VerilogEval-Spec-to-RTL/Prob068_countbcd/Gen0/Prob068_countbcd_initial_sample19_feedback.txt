The design intent is to implement a 4-digit BCD counter with enable signals controlling increments of the tens, hundreds, and thousands digits, and synchronous reset behavior. The issues stem primarily from the way the sequential logic updates the register `q` and the enable signals `ena` within a single always block.

1. **Multiple procedural assignments to overlapping bits of `q` within the same clock cycle:**  
   The code tries to assign to different 4-bit slices of the `q` register (`q[3:0]`, `q[7:4]`, `q[11:8]`, `q[15:12]`) in sequence based on intermediate enable signals `ena`. However, `ena` is also assigned in the same clock cycle, leading to combinational dependencies within the synchronous block that do not reflect the correct ripple carry effect. For example, `ena[0]` is set when ones roll over, and then immediately used in the same clock cycle to decide tens increment. Because all assignments happen in the same clock cycle, `ena` updates are not yet visible for conditional evaluation, causing incorrect enables and increments.

2. **Incorrect handling of enable signals inside the same clock cycle:**  
   The enables `ena[0]`, `ena[1]`, and `ena[2]` are set and consumed within the same always block and clock edge. In Verilog, non-blocking assignments (`<=`) to `ena` will not take effect until after the entire block completes, so the conditional statements depending on `ena` values do not behave as intended. This results in upper digits not being incremented correctly and enables being out of sync with the count.

3. **Partial updates and potential overwriting of `q`:**  
   Because `q` is assigned piecewise in non-blocking assignments during the same clock edge, and the control flow depends on intermediate enable values that do not update immediately, some updates to digit slices may be lost or overwritten, causing mismatches in the counter output.

4. **Reset behavior is correct but does not address increment logic flaws:**  
   The synchronous reset correctly clears both `q` and `ena`, but the problems lie in the increment and enable propagation logic, which does not align with the sequential nature of the design.

The simulation mismatch counts (8372 mismatches on `ena` and 4400 on `q`) highlight that the enable signals and counter outputs are substantially different from the expected waveform, reinforcing that the logic sequencing and signal dependencies are incorrectly managed in the code.
