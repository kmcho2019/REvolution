However, this implementation causes the ena signals to be asserted only in the very cycle where the lower digit rolls over. If the simulation expects the ena signals to be held high for longer or to reflect enable status in a different timing manner, this would cause mismatches.

Because ena is reset to zero at every clock and only set when a rollover occurs, ena signals might not align correctly with when the upper digits actually increment, especially since the increments for the upper digits also occur conditionally inside nested if statements. This tight coupling of ena and increment logic within one synchronous block could cause delays or misalignments.

The lack of explicit combinational assignments for ena or separate pipelining stages might also contribute to the mismatch if the testbench expects ena to behave as a combinational carry-out signal or to be registered differently.

In summary, the cause of the ena mismatch lies in the way ena signals are assigned synchronously and only asserted briefly in the rollover cycle without consideration of timing or persistent enable signaling. This discrepancy between the design intent for enable signals and their timing in code causes the simulation mismatches, despite correct counting in q.
