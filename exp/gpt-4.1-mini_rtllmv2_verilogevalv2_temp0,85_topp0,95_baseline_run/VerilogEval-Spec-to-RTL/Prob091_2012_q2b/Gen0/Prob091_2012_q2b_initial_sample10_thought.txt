To implement Y1 and Y3 as the inputs of the state flip-flops y[1] and y[3] respectively, we analyze the next-state transitions for states B (y[1]) and D (y[3]):

- Y1 corresponds to next state B:
  From the FSM:
    - A (y[0]) --1--> B (y[1])
    - B (y[1]) --0--> D (y[3]) (so not going to B)
    - C (y[2]) --1--> E (y[4])
    - D (y[3]) --1--> F (y[5])
    - E (y[4]) -- ?
    - F (y[5]) -- ?

  But more directly from the FSM transitions, states that go to B are only from A with input w=1.
  So Y1 = y[0] & w

- Y3 corresponds to next state D:
  From the FSM:
    - B (y[1]) --0--> D (y[3])
    - C (y[2]) --0--> D (y[3])
    - D (y[3]) --0--> A (y[0]) (not D)
    - E (y[4]) --0--> D (y[3])
    - F (y[5]) --0--> D (y[3])

  So Y3 = (y[1] & ~w) | (y[2] & ~w) | (y[4] & ~w) | (y[5] & ~w)

In summary:
Y1 = y[0] & w
Y3 = (~w) & (y[1] | y[2] | y[4] | y[5])
