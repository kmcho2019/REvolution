The output is a 25-bit vector representing pairwise comparisons of the five 1-bit inputs (a,b,c,d,e). Each bit in the output corresponds to the equality of one pair of inputs. The pairs are ordered with 'a' compared to all inputs, then 'b', etc., down to 'e'. The bit is 1 if the two bits are equal, which can be computed as the XNOR of the two bits. The bits in the output vector start from the comparison of e and e at out[0] up to a and a at out[24], matching the example given. We'll implement this by explicitly assigning each bit of the output as the XNOR of the two corresponding inputs.
