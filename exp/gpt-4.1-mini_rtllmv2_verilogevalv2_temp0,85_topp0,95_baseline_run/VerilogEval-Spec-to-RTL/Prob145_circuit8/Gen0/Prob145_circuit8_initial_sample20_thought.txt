From the waveform, p and q are outputs that depend on the clock edges and input a with some state memory.

- At the first rising edge of clock (25ns), p = 0, q unknown (x).
- At the next falling edge (55ns), q = 0 and p = 0.
- On the following clock low cycles, when a toggles, p stays 0, q stays 0.
- On rising clock edges with a=1, p goes to 1, q remains 0.
- When clock goes low at 115ns, p=1 and q=1.
- Later on, at clock high cycles with a=1, p=1 and q=1.
- When clock goes low at 175ns, both p and q reset to 0.

Summary:

- p is set to the current value of a at the rising edge of clock.
- q is set to p's previous value at the falling edge of clock.
- On reset or initial, outputs start unknown but eventually zero.
- At falling edge, q <= p.
- At rising edge, p <= a.

Implement a sequential circuit with two flip-flops:

- p captures input a on rising edge of clock.
- q captures p on falling edge of clock.

This matches the observed behavior.

Note: Outputs are initially unknown and become defined after clock edges.

We'll use non-blocking assignments in always blocks triggered by clock edges.

Outputs p and q are registered outputs.

Implement with two always blocks, one on posedge clock for p, one on negedge clock for q.
