The given waveform suggests a sequential circuit with two registers p and q updated on the rising edge of clock.

- At the first rising clock edge (around 25ns), p is initialized to 0, q is unknown.
- p updates to 1 only when a is 1 at the rising clock edge, otherwise stays 0.
- q updates on the falling edge of clock:
  - At falling edges (e.g. 115ns), q becomes equal to p.
  - After that, q remains at the last assigned value until next falling edge.
- p updates only on rising edges of clock, q only on falling edges.
- On rising edge, p = p OR a (meaning if a=1, p becomes 1)
- On falling edge, q = p.
- Both p and q start unknown and then get initialized accordingly.

This matches the waveform.

So the circuit has two registers:
- p updates on posedge clock: p <= p | a
- q updates on negedge clock: q <= p

Outputs p and q are the values of those registers.

Implementation:
- Use two always blocks sensitive to posedge and negedge clock respectively.
- Registers p_reg and q_reg hold internal values assigned to outputs p and q.

This approach matches the behavior observed in the waveform.
