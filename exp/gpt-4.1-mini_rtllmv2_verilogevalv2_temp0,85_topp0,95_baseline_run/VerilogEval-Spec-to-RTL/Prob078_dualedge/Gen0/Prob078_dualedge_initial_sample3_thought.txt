To implement a dual-edge triggered flip-flop on an FPGA that only supports edge-triggered flip-flops on a single clock edge, we can use two flip-flops: one triggered on the positive edge of the clock, and one triggered on the negative edge of the clock. Then, we combine their outputs to produce the final output. 

Since the output should update on both edges, we can sample the input `d` on the positive edge into one register, and on the negative edge into another register, then use a multiplexer or logic to produce `q`. However, to ensure `q` updates on both edges, a common approach is:

- Register q_pos captures `d` on the positive edge.
- Register q_neg captures `d` on the negative edge.
- Output `q` is assigned from either register, chosen based on clock phase.

To implement this:

1. Create two flip-flops: `q_pos` on posedge clk, and `q_neg` on negedge clk.
2. Use a clock phase signal or simply assign `q` to the appropriate register based on the clock level.
   Since outputs only change at clock edges, and `q_pos` updates on posedge, `q_neg` on negedge, we can use `q = clk ? q_pos : q_neg;`

This way, `q` updates on both edges of the clock.

This is a commonly used technique to implement a dual-edge triggered flip-flop in FPGAs.
