To implement a dual-edge triggered flip-flop using only single-edge triggered flip-flops (because FPGAs don't support dual-edge triggering directly), we can use two flip-flops: one triggered on the positive edge of the clock, and one triggered on the negative edge of the clock. Both flip-flops sample the same input 'd'. Then, we combine their outputs using a multiplexer controlled by the clock to produce the final output 'q'. This effectively captures the input on both edges.

Specifically:
- On the rising edge of clk, the first flip-flop samples 'd' and updates q_pos.
- On the falling edge of clk, the second flip-flop samples 'd' and updates q_neg.
- The output 'q' is selected from q_pos when clk is high, and q_neg when clk is low.

This technique mimics a dual-edge flip-flop by combining two single-edge flip-flops.
