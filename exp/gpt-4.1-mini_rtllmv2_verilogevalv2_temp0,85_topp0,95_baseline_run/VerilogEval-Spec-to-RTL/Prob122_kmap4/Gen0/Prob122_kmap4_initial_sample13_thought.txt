The Karnaugh map pattern shows that output toggles between 0 and 1 for every adjacent cell in both row and column directions. Observing the pattern, the output is 1 when the two-bit inputs (cd) and (ab) differ in parity or complement each other in a checkerboard pattern. This can be interpreted as the output being the XOR of the first pair (c, d) and the second pair (a, b). Specifically, the pattern corresponds to the XOR of the XOR of a and b, and the XOR of c and d. So out = (a ^ b) ^ (c ^ d).
