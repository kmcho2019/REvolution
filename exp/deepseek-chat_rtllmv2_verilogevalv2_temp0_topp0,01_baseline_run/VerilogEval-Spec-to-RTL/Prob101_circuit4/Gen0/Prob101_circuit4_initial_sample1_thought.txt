Analyzing the simulation waveforms, we can observe that the output q is 1 in all cases except when:
1. All inputs (a, b, c, d) are 0
2. When a=1 and b=0 and c=0 (regardless of d)

This suggests the logic function is: q = (b OR c) AND NOT (a AND NOT b AND NOT c)
Alternatively, we can see that q is 1 whenever either b or c is 1, except when a=1 and b=0 and c=0.
This can be simplified to: q = b | c | (a & d)
But looking at the waveform, when a=1, b=0, c=0, d=0, q=0 (so the a&d term doesn't cover this)
A better expression is: q = b | c | (a & d & (b | c))
But this seems too complex.

Looking again, the simplest pattern is:
q is 0 only when (a=0 and b=0 and c=0 and d=0) OR (a=1 and b=0 and c=0)
Otherwise q is 1.

This can be implemented as:
q = !( (!a & !b & !c & !d) | (a & !b & !c) )
