To implement a 4-bit priority encoder, we need to prioritize the input bits from left to right (most significant bit to least significant bit). If the most significant bit is high, we output its position. If not, we move to the next bit and so on. This can be achieved using a combination of logical AND and OR operations to check for the priority condition. We can use case statements or if-else conditions to prioritize the bits. However, to keep the design more combinational and straightforward, we can directly use conditional operators in the assignment of output bits. The output 'pos' is assigned based on the condition of the input 'in'. For the given 4-bit input, we will have 4 conditions to check, and the output will be 2 bits to represent the position of the first 1 bit (00 for no 1's found, 01 for the least significant bit, 10 for the second bit, and 11 for the most significant bit).
