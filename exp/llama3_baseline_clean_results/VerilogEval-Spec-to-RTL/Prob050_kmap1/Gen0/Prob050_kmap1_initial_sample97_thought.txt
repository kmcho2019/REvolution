The Karnaugh map can be simplified into a Boolean expression that describes the output out in terms of the inputs a, b, and c. Looking at the map, we see that the output is 1 whenever a is 1 (regardless of the values of b and c), and it's also 1 when a is 0 and both b and c are 1. This can be expressed as out = a + (b * c). However, we notice that when a is 0, the output is 1 for all combinations of b and c except when both are 0. Therefore, the expression can also be seen as out = a + (b & c). Considering the K-map's structure and applying the rules of Boolean algebra, we see the output is actually the result of a OR operation with the AND operation of b and c, which matches the initial observation. Thus, the Verilog module can directly implement this logic equation.
