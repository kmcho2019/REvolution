For each cell in the map, if the output is '1', it means that the combination of inputs represented by the cell's position results in an output of '1'. 

Looking at the map:
- When b=0 and c=0 (bc=00), the output is '0' for a=0 and '1' for a=1.
- For all other combinations of b and c (bc=01, bc=11, bc=10), the output is '1' regardless of the value of a.

Thus, the logic can be simplified as follows: The output is '1' whenever a is '1' (since for a=1, all combinations of bc result in an output of '1'), or whenever b and c are not both '0' (since for bc=01, bc=10, and bc=11, the output is '1' regardless of a).

This simplification can be represented by the Boolean expression: out = a + (b | c).
