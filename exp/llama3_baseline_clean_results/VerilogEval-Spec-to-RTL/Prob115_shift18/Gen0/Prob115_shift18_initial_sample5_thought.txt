To implement the 64-bit arithmetic shift register, we first need to understand the different operations based on the 'amount' signal. For left shifts, we simply shift the data to the left by 1 or 8 bits based on 'amount'. For right shifts, we perform an arithmetic right shift, which involves shifting the data to the right and filling the leftmost bits with the sign bit (the most significant bit) of the data. We use the 'load' signal to decide whether to load the data into the shift register or to shift the existing data. If 'ena' is high, we perform the shift operation; otherwise, the shift register remains unchanged. We can use Verilog's built-in shift operators (<<, >>) for left and right shifts. For arithmetic right shift, we can use the division operator (/) or implement it manually.
