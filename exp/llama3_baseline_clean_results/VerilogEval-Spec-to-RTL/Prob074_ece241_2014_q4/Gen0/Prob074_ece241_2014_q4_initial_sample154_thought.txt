To implement the described finite state machine in Verilog, we first need to break down the problem into smaller components and then integrate them. The machine consists of three main parts: 
1. Three two-input gates (XOR, AND, OR) with specific connections to the input `x` and the outputs of three D flip-flops.
2. Three D flip-flops, each connected to the output of one of the gates and whose outputs feed back into the gates.
3. A three-input NOR gate that takes the outputs of the three D flip-flops and produces the final output `z`.

We will use the `always` block for the sequential logic of the D flip-flops, triggered on the positive edge of `clk`. For the combinational logic (the gates), we'll use continuous assignments.
