The problem can be solved by implementing a Moore state machine with three states: walking left, walking right, and falling. 
The machine transitions from walking left to walking right when bumped on the left and from walking right to walking left when bumped on the right. 
When the ground disappears, the machine transitions to the falling state, where it remains until the ground reappears. 
The machine also has a digging state that it enters when dig is high and the Lemming is walking on the ground. 
The digging state continues until the ground disappears, at which point the machine transitions to the falling state.
To keep track of the direction the Lemming was walking before it fell or started digging, we will also store the walking direction along with the state.
We will also need to handle the case where the Lemming is bumped at the same time as it starts or stops falling, or when it is told to dig while falling.
This can be achieved by adding more states to the machine to handle these cases, or by using a more complex next state logic.
However, for simplicity, we will assume that these cases do not affect the walking direction.
