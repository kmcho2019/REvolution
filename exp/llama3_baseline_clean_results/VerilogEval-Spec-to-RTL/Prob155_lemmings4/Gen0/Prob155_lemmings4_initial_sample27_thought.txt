To model the behavior of a Lemming in the game Lemmings using a Moore state machine, we first identify the states the Lemming can be in. These states include walking left, walking right, falling, and digging. We also consider the conditions under which the Lemming transitions between these states, such as hitting obstacles, losing ground, being told to dig, and potentially splattering upon hitting the ground after a long fall.

The state machine can be designed with states like IDLE_LEFT, IDLE_RIGHT, FALLING, and DIGGING. However, to accurately model the behavior, including the conditions for transitioning between states and the constraints related to falling, digging, and splattering, we may need additional states or counters to track the duration of the fall.

We use a finite state machine (FSM) with the following approach:
- Start in the IDLE_LEFT state upon reset, with walk_left = 1.
- Transition to IDLE_RIGHT upon receiving a bump_left signal.
- Transition to IDLE_LEFT upon receiving a bump_right signal.
- If ground = 0, transition to FALLING, setting aaah = 1 and resetting walk_left and walk_right.
- While in FALLING, increment a fall_counter until ground = 1, at which point, transition back to the previous walking state (IDLE_LEFT or IDLE_RIGHT) unless the fall_counter exceeds 20, in which case, transition to a SPLATTERED state.
- If dig = 1 and ground = 1, transition to DIGGING, setting digging = 1.
- In DIGGING, continue until ground = 0, at which point, transition to FALLING.
- Ensure that being bumped while falling or digging does not change the walking direction, and that being told to dig while falling or without ground is ignored.

The outputs walk_left, walk_right, aaah, and digging are determined by the current state of the FSM.
