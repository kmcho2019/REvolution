The problem can be solved by designing a Moore state machine with the following states: 
- WALK_LEFT: The Lemming is walking left.
- WALK_RIGHT: The Lemming is walking right.
- FALLING: The Lemming is falling.
- DIGGING: The Lemming is digging.
- SPLATTERED: The Lemming has splattered and is no longer walking, falling, or digging.

The state machine transitions between these states based on the input signals. 
When the ground disappears (ground=0), the Lemming will transition from WALK_LEFT or WALK_RIGHT to FALLING.
When the ground reappears (ground=1) while the Lemming is falling, it will transition back to WALK_LEFT or WALK_RIGHT, depending on its previous direction.
When the Lemming is told to dig (dig=1) while walking on ground, it will transition to DIGGING.
When the ground disappears while the Lemming is digging, it will transition to FALLING.
If the Lemming falls for more than 20 clock cycles and hits the ground, it will transition to SPLATTERED.

To keep track of the number of clock cycles the Lemming has been falling, a counter can be used. This counter will be incremented every clock cycle while the Lemming is in the FALLING state, and reset when the Lemming transitions out of the FALLING state.

The output signals (walk_left, walk_right, aaah, digging) will be determined by the current state of the state machine.
