To implement a dual-edge triggered flip-flop on an FPGA, we can use two single-edge triggered flip-flops. One flip-flop is triggered on the positive edge of the clock (posedge), and the other is triggered on the negative edge (negedge). However, the latter requires creating a derived clock signal that is the inverse of the original clock. We can then use these two flip-flops to capture the input 'd' on both edges of the clock. The output 'q' of our dual-edge triggered flip-flop can be the output of a multiplexer that selects between the outputs of the two single-edge triggered flip-flops based on the current edge of the clock. This approach ensures that our circuit behaves like a dual-edge triggered flip-flop without directly using an always @(posedge clk or negedge clk) block, which is not synthesizable.
