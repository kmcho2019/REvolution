To create a dual-edge triggered flip-flop, we can use two D-type flip-flops. 
One flip-flop is triggered on the rising edge of the clock and the other 
on the falling edge. The outputs of these two flip-flops are then 
combined using an OR gate, with the output of the first flip-flop inverted 
and then ORed with the output of the second flip-flop. This way, 
the output of the circuit is set on both edges of the clock. 
However, this approach requires a way to delay the output of the first 
flip-flop by half a clock cycle. Instead, we can simply use two D-type 
flip-flops in a master-slave configuration where one is triggered on 
the rising edge and the other on the falling edge of the clock. 
The master flip-flop is triggered on the rising edge, and its output 
is connected to the input of the slave flip-flop, which is triggered 
on the falling edge. The output of the slave flip-flop is the output 
of the dual-edge triggered flip-flop. Alternatively, we can use a 
more straightforward approach of using two regular D-type flip-flops 
in a time-division configuration: one clocked on the rising edge and 
the other on the inverted clock (thus falling edge), and then 
selecting between their outputs based on the clock edge.
However, the simplest and most common approach is to use a single 
D-type flip-flop and a 2:1 multiplexer. The input to the flip-flop 
is the data input 'd'. The select input of the multiplexer is the 
clock 'clk'. The two inputs of the multiplexer are the output of the 
flip-flop and the inverted output of the flip-flop. When the clock 
is high, the flip-flop is clocked and its output is selected by the 
multiplexer. When the clock is low, the inverted output of the 
flip-flop is selected, which is equivalent to clocking the flip-flop 
on the falling edge.
This approach provides a simple and efficient way to implement a 
dual-edge triggered flip-flop using standard FPGA components.
