To implement a dual-edge triggered flip-flop, we can utilize two single-edge triggered flip-flops. One flip-flop will be triggered on the positive edge of the clock, and the other on the negative edge. The output of these two flip-flops will be combined using a multiplexer, which is controlled by the clock signal. This way, on the positive edge of the clock, the output of the positive edge flip-flop is selected, and on the negative edge, the output of the negative edge flip-flop is selected. However, a simpler approach involves using a single flip-flop and a latch. The flip-flop captures the input on the positive edge of the clock, and the latch captures the input on the negative edge of the clock. Since FPGAs don't allow always @(posedge clk or negedge clk), we use a different approach where we generate an internal clock that is twice the frequency of the original clock and has a phase shift, allowing us to capture data on both edges of the original clock.
