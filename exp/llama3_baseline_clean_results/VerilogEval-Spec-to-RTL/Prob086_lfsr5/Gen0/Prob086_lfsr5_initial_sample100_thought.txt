To implement a 5-bit maximal-length Galois LFSR, we need to define the LFSR's state and its next state logic. The LFSR's state will be represented by a 5-bit register, `q`. The next state logic will be determined by the tap positions. For a 5-bit LFSR with taps at positions 5 and 3, the next state of each bit position can be calculated as follows: 
- The most significant bit (MSB) `q[4]` will be the result of the XOR operation between `q[0]` and the current value of `q[4]` (since there's a tap at position 5).
- The bit at position 3 (`q[2]`) will be the result of the XOR operation between `q[0]` and the current value of `q[2]` (since there's a tap at position 3).
- The remaining bits will simply shift right.
On the positive edge of the clock, the LFSR will update its state to the next state.
In case of reset, the LFSR will reset to the state where `q[0]` is 1, and the rest of the bits are 0.
