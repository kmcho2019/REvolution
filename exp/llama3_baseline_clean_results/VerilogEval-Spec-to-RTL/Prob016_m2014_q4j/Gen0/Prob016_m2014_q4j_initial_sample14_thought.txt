To implement a 4-bit adder with full adders, we first need to understand the structure of a full adder. A full adder is a digital circuit that adds three one-bit inputs (A, B, and carry-in) and produces two outputs: sum and carry-out. We can use four full adders to implement a 4-bit adder. Each full adder will be responsible for adding corresponding bits of the two input numbers (x and y) and the carry from the previous full adder. The first full adder does not have a carry-in, so we set it to 0. The outputs of each full adder are the sum bit and the carry-out, which is used as the carry-in for the next full adder. The final carry-out from the last full adder will be the overflow bit and should be included as the most significant bit of the output sum.
