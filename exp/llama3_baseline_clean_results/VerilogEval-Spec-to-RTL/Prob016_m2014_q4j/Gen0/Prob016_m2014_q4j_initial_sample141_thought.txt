To implement a 4-bit adder, we can chain four full adders together. The full adder is the basic building block of digital adders. It takes three one-bit inputs: two for the numbers being added (x and y) and one for the carry from the previous addition. Each full adder will produce a sum and a carry. We start with the least significant bits of x and y, and the carry-in for the first full adder is 0 (since there's no carry to consider for the least significant bit). The carry-out from each full adder becomes the carry-in for the next full adder. The final carry-out from the most significant bit's full adder serves as the overflow bit for our 4-bit adder.
