To determine the behavior of the sequential circuit, we observe the output values of p and q at different times based on the input signals clock and a. 

The output p is high (1) whenever the input a is high (1) and the clock is high (1). 
The output q seems to be dependent on the previous state of the outputs and the current input a and clock signal. 
Specifically, q goes high (1) when the clock is high (1) and a is low (0) for the first time after it was previously low (0) for at least one clock cycle. 
After that, q remains high (1) even if a goes high (1) or low (0) as long as the clock remains high (1).

Based on this observation, we can implement the module TopModule as a sequential circuit using a finite state machine or simple flip-flops to store the state of the circuit.

However, upon closer inspection, a simpler interpretation is possible. It appears that p simply follows the input a when the clock is high. For q, it seems that once a transition from low to high clock is detected (indicating the start of a high clock period), q will go high after a is low during the high clock period, and it remains high until the clock goes low again. 

This suggests a simpler implementation focusing on the transitions of the clock and the state of a during those transitions.
