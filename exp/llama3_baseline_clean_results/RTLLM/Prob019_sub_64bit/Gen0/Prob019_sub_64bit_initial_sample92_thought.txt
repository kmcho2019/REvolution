To implement the 64-bit subtractor with overflow checking, we first need to understand how binary subtraction and overflow detection work. The subtraction will be performed using the built-in Verilog subtraction operator. For overflow detection, we will check the sign bits of the operands and the result. If the signs of the operands are different and the sign of the result does not match the sign of the first operand (A), an overflow has occurred. This can be achieved by comparing the most significant bits (MSBs) of A, B, and the result. The MSB is 1 for negative numbers and 0 for positive numbers in two's complement representation.
