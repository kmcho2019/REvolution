To implement the 64-bit subtractor with overflow checking, we need to perform the following steps:
1. Perform binary subtraction of the two input operands A and B.
2. Check for overflow conditions based on the sign bits of A, B, and the result.
3. Set the overflow flag accordingly.

The key idea here is to understand how overflow occurs in signed binary subtraction. 
For two's complement representation, the most significant bit (MSB) represents the sign (0 for positive, 1 for negative).
We can check for overflow by examining the sign bits of A, B, and the result.
If the signs of A and the result are different when B is negative, or if the signs of A and the result are the same when B is positive, an overflow has occurred.
