To implement the design of an unsigned 16-bit multiplier, we will create a Verilog module named `multi_16bit`. This module will utilize shift and accumulate operations to generate the product output (`yout`). The control signals will include a clock (`clk`), an active-low reset (`rst_n`), and a start signal (`start`), along with a completion flag (`done`) indicating the end of the multiplication operation.

We will use a finite state machine (FSM) approach to manage the different states of the multiplier, which are essentially: idle, running, and done. However, in this design, the state transitions are managed directly through the `start` signal and the `i` counter, simplifying the logic.

The shift count register (`i`) will be updated based on the clock edge or reset condition. It increments when the `start` signal is active and `i` is less than 17, resets to 0 when `start` is inactive or during reset, and its value determines when the multiplication is complete.

The multiplication completion flag (`done_r`) is set when `i` equals 16, indicating the end of the multiplication, and reset when `i` reaches 17 or during a reset.

The shift and accumulate operation is performed on every clock edge, with the multiplicand (`ain`) and multiplier (`bin`) being loaded into registers (`areg` and `breg`) at the start of the operation. The product (`yout_r`) is accumulated based on the bits of the multiplicand, shifting the multiplier accordingly.

Finally, the output (`yout`) is assigned the value of the product register (`yout_r`).
