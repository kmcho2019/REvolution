To design an ALU for a 32-bit MIPS-ISA CPU, we'll use a module-based approach with parameters defining the control signals for various operations. The ALU will take two 32-bit operands ("a" and "b"), a 6-bit control signal ("aluc"), and produce a 32-bit result ("r") along with several flags indicating conditions such as zero result, carry, negative result, overflow, and a general flag for SLT and SLTU operations. We'll utilize a case statement within an always block to perform the operation specified by the control signal, handling arithmetic, logical, shift, and other operations as defined by the MIPS instruction set architecture.
