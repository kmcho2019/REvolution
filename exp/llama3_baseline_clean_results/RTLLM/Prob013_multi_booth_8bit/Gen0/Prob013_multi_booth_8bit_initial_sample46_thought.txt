To implement the 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier, we first need to understand the Booth algorithm and how it applies to Radix-4 multiplication. The Booth algorithm is a method for multiplying two signed binary numbers in two's complement representation. Radix-4 Booth multiplication is a variation of the standard Booth algorithm that allows for the multiplication of two bits of the multiplier in each clock cycle, effectively reducing the number of cycles required for the multiplication.

In our implementation, we will use a counter (ctr) to keep track of the current bit pair of the multiplier being processed. We will utilize two 16-bit registers, multiplier and multiplicand, to store the extended multiplicand and the multiplier. The multiplicand is extended to 16 bits by sign-extending the 8-bit input (b).

On each clock cycle, we check if the current bit pair of the multiplier register indicates a positive, negative, or zero operation. Based on this, we either accumulate the multiplicand in the product register (p) or perform a subtraction. We use the ready signal (rdy) to indicate the completion of the multiplication process.

1. Reset handling: On the positive edge of the reset signal, we reset the multiplier and multiplicand registers.

3. Multiplication process: On each clock cycle, we check the current bit pair of the multiplier register and perform the corresponding operation (addition or subtraction).
