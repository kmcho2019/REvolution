The implementation of the 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier involves utilizing the Booth algorithm to perform the multiplication of two 8-bit inputs (a and b). The Booth algorithm allows for the multiplication of two numbers by iteratively shifting and adding the multiplicand based on the multiplier bits. In this case, the multiplicand (b) is extended to 16 bits by sign-extending it. The multiplier (a) is also extended to 16 bits. We then initialize two 16-bit registers, multiplicand and multiplier, to store the extended inputs. We use a counter (ctr) to keep track of the current bit position in the multiplier. If the current bit of the multiplier is 1, we accumulate the value of the multiplicand in the product register (p). We then shift the multiplicand left by 1 bit and increment the counter. Once the counter reaches 16, we set the ready signal (rdy) to 1, indicating the completion of the multiplication process.
