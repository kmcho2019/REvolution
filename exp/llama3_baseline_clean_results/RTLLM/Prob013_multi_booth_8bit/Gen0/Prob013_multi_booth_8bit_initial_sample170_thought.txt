The Booth algorithm is an efficient method for multiplication that reduces the number of required additions by shifting and adding the multiplicand. We will use two 16-bit registers to hold the multiplier and multiplicand. On the positive edge of the clock signal, if the reset signal is high, the registers will be initialized. If the reset signal is low, the multiplication process will start. We will use a counter to keep track of the current bit being processed. If the current bit is 1, the multiplicand will be added to the product. After each operation, the multiplicand will be left-shifted by 1. Once all bits have been processed, the ready signal will be set to 1, indicating the completion of the multiplication process.
