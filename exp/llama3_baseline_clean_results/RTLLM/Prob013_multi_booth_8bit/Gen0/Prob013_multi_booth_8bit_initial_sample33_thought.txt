The implementation of the 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier involves several steps. First, the multiplicand and multiplier are stored in 16-bit registers, with the most significant 8 bits of each register initialized with the sign bit of the corresponding input. The multiplication process is performed over 16 clock cycles. In each cycle, the multiplicand register is left-shifted by 1 bit, and the product register is updated based on the current bit of the multiplier register. If the current bit is 1, the multiplicand register is added to the product register. The counter is incremented in each cycle, and when it reaches 16, the multiplication process is complete, and the ready signal is set to 1. This process is controlled by the clock signal and the reset signal. On the positive edge of the reset signal, the registers are initialized, and the counter is reset to 0.
