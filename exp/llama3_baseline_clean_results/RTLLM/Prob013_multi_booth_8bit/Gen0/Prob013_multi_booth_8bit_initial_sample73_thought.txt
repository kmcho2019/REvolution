To implement an 8-bit Radix-4 Booth multiplier, we first need to understand the Booth algorithm, which is a multiplication algorithm that works by shifting and adding the multiplicand to the product based on the bits of the multiplier. In this case, we are implementing the Radix-4 variant, which involves considering two bits of the multiplier at a time. 

The module multi_booth_8bit will be implemented with the following components: 
- Two 16-bit registers (multiplier and multiplicand) to hold the multiplicand and the multiplier extended to 16 bits with the sign bit replicated.
- A 5-bit counter (ctr) to keep track of the current bit being processed in the multiplier.
- A 16-bit register (p) to accumulate the product.
- A 1-bit register (rdy) to indicate when the multiplication is complete.

The multiplier and multiplicand registers will be initialized on the positive edge of the reset signal. During normal operation (reset low), the multiplicand will be left-shifted by 1 on each clock cycle, and the product will accumulate the multiplicand based on the current bit of the multiplier register. 

After 16 clock cycles, the counter will reach 16, and the ready signal will be set to 1, indicating the completion of the multiplication process.
