To implement the 8-bit Radix-4 booth multiplier, we will use a finite state machine (FSM) that utilizes a counter to track the number of shifts performed during the multiplication process. On the positive edge of the clock signal (clk) or the positive edge of the reset signal (reset), the module performs the multiplication process. The multiplicand and multiplier are first initialized as 16-bit registers. The multiplicand is then left-shifted by 1 in each clock cycle to simulate the Booth algorithm's shifting operation. The product is accumulated based on the current bit of the multiplier register. Once the counter reaches 16, the ready signal (rdy) is set to 1 to indicate the completion of the multiplication process.
