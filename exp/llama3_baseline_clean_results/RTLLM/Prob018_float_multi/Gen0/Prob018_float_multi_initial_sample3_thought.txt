To design a 32-bit floating-point multiplier module in Verilog, following the IEEE 754 standard, we first need to understand the structure of a 32-bit floating-point number. It consists of a sign bit, an 8-bit exponent, and a 23-bit mantissa. 

Our approach involves several steps:
1. Extract the sign, exponent, and mantissa from the input operands.
2. Check for special cases such as NaN (Not a Number) or infinity.
3. Normalize the mantissas if necessary, ensuring they are in the correct format for multiplication.
4. Multiply the mantissas, combine the signs, and adjust the exponents accordingly.
5. Round the result and adjust the exponent as needed to ensure accurate representation.
6. Format the final result according to the IEEE 754 standard, handling overflow and underflow scenarios.

Given the complexity of floating-point arithmetic, we will use a cycle counter to sequence the operations over multiple clock cycles.
