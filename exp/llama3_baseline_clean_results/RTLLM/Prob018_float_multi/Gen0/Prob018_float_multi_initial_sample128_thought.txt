To implement a 32-bit floating-point multiplier following the IEEE 754 standard, we first need to break down the floating-point numbers into their constituent parts: sign bit, exponent, and mantissa. Then, we handle special cases such as NaN and infinity, normalize the mantissas, perform the multiplication, round the result, and finally, adjust the exponent to ensure the result is in the correct format. The design involves a state machine to sequence these operations over several clock cycles. Given the complexity of floating-point operations and the requirements for precision and adherence to the IEEE 754 standard, careful attention must be paid to each step of the process.
