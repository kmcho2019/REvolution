To implement an 8-bit adder, we can use a series of full adders connected in a ripple-carry fashion. Each full adder takes two input bits and a carry-in, producing a sum bit and a carry-out. By connecting the carry-out of one full adder to the carry-in of the next, we can propagate the carry through the entire 8-bit addition. The first full adder will take the least significant bits of the input operands A and B, along with the external carry-in (cin). Each subsequent full adder will take the next bits of A and B, along with the carry-out from the previous full adder. The final carry-out from the most significant bit's full adder will be the overall carry-out (cout) of the 8-bit adder.
