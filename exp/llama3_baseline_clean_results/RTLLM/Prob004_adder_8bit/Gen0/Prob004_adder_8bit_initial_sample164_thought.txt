To design an 8-bit adder, we can use a series of bit-level adders, specifically full adders, since each bit of the sum depends on the corresponding bits of the operands and the carry from the previous bit. We'll chain these full adders together, with the carry output of one full adder serving as the carry input to the next. This way, we can handle the propagation of the carry across all bits.

The module `adder_8bit` will take two 8-bit inputs `a` and `b`, a carry-in `cin`, and produce an 8-bit sum `sum` and a carry-out `cout`. Each bit of the sum is calculated by a full adder that takes the corresponding bits of `a` and `b` and the carry from the previous bit (except for the least significant bit, which uses `cin` as its carry input).

The carry-out `cout` of the most significant bit's full adder will be the carry-out of the entire 8-bit adder, indicating whether the sum of `a` and `b` exceeded 8 bits.
