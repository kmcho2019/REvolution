To implement a 64-bit Johnson counter, we first need to understand the operation of a Johnson counter. A Johnson counter is a type of ring counter where the next state is determined by the current state and the rules of operation. For a 4-bit Johnson counter example given, the state transitions follow a specific pattern that can be generalized for a 64-bit implementation. The key operations are the shifting of bits and the addition of a new bit at the most significant position based on the value of the least significant bit (LSB) of the current state. This process will be controlled by a clock signal for synchronization and a reset signal to initialize the counter to a known state. We will use Verilog's always block to describe the behavior at the rising edge of the clock and the falling edge of the reset signal. The counter's state will be represented by a 64-bit register Q.
