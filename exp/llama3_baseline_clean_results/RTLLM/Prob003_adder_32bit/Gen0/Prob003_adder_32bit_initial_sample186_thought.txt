To design a 32-bit carry-lookahead adder, we can first create a 4-bit or 16-bit CLA block, then instantiate multiple instances of it to create the 32-bit adder. However, for simplicity and to leverage the benefits of carry-lookahead, we'll directly design a 16-bit CLA block and then use two instances of it to create the 32-bit adder. The key idea is to compute the generate (G) and propagate (P) signals for each bit position, which are then used to calculate the carry-in and sum for each bit. We'll use these signals to look ahead and calculate carries for blocks of bits, reducing the delay compared to a ripple-carry adder.
