To design a 32-bit carry-lookahead adder using the Carry-Lookahead Adder (CLA) architecture, we first need to create a 4-bit or 16-bit CLA block. In this case, we'll opt for a 16-bit CLA block for better modularity and efficiency. This block will be designed to compute the sum and carry out for 16-bit inputs. The 32-bit adder will then be constructed by instantiating two of these 16-bit blocks and appropriately handling the carry propagation between them.

The 16-bit CLA block itself will utilize the principles of carry lookahead to generate the sum and carry bits for each position based on the inputs and carry-in. The equations for generate (G) and propagate (P) signals will be used, where G_i = A_i * B_i and P_i = A_i + B_i, to determine the carry-in requirement for each bit position. The carry lookahead logic will compute the carry-in for each bit position based on the G and P signals of previous bits.

For the top-level 32-bit adder module, we'll instantiate two 16-bit CLA blocks. The first block will handle the least significant 16 bits, and the second block will handle the most significant 16 bits. The carry-out from the first block will serve as the carry-in for the second block, ensuring proper carry propagation.
