To implement a 32-bit carry-lookahead adder, we first need to design a basic 4-bit or 16-bit carry-lookahead adder block. The 16-bit block will be more efficient for the 32-bit adder as it reduces the number of instances needed. Each block will calculate the sum and carry-out for its portion of the input operands. The 32-bit adder will then be composed of two instances of the 16-bit block, with the carry-out from the first instance connected to the carry-in of the second instance. This approach simplifies the design and reduces the propagation delay compared to a ripple-carry adder.

We will first define a 1-bit full adder module to use within our carry-lookahead adder. Then, we will design the 16-bit carry-lookahead adder block. Finally, we will use two instances of this block to create the 32-bit adder, ensuring to correctly propagate the carry from the first 16 bits to the second 16 bits.

The key to a carry-lookahead adder is the generation of carry signals in advance, reducing the dependency between bits and hence reducing the overall propagation delay.
