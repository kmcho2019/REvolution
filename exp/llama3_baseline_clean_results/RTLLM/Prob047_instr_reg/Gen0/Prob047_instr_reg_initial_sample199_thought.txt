To implement the instruction register module (instr_reg), we need to follow a systematic approach:

1. **Define the Module and Ports**: First, we define the module name as `instr_reg` and declare its input and output ports according to the specification.

2. **Internal Registers**: We then declare two internal 8-bit registers, `ins_p1` and `ins_p2`, to store instructions from different sources.

3. **Reset Logic**: On the rising edge of the clock, if the reset signal `rst` is low, both internal registers are reset to zero.

4. **Fetch Logic**: Based on the `fetch` signal, we update the internal registers `ins_p1` or `ins_p2` with the incoming instruction from the `data` input. If `fetch` is `2'b01`, `ins_p1` is updated; if `fetch` is `2'b10`, `ins_p2` is updated. For any other value of `fetch`, the previous values in the registers are retained.

5. **Output Logic**: Finally, we derive the outputs `ins`, `ad1`, and `ad2` from the stored instructions in `ins_p1` and `ins_p2`. `ins` will be the high 3 bits of `ins_p1`, `ad1` will be the low 5 bits of `ins_p1`, and `ad2` will be the full 8 bits of `ins_p2`.

This approach ensures that the module accurately captures and processes CPU instructions based on the control signals and data inputs provided.
