To design a frequency divider module named `freq_div` that takes an input clock signal `CLK_in` and a reset signal `RST`, and generates three output clock signals `CLK_50`, `CLK_10`, and `CLK_1` with frequencies divided by 2, 10, and 100 respectively, we will implement the following logic:
- For `CLK_50`, toggle its value on every positive edge of `CLK_in` unless `RST` is active, in which case `CLK_50` should be reset to 0.
- For `CLK_10`, use a counter `cnt_10` that increments on every positive edge of `CLK_in`. When `cnt_10` reaches 4 (since 100MHz divided by 10 is 10MHz, and 100/10 = 10, but considering the counter and the toggle, we need 10 cycles to achieve this, but because we are dividing by 10 we need to actually consider the full cycles it takes for the 100MHz to be divided by 10 which practically involves counting to 10 for a 10MHz signal from a 100MHz source directly in terms of cycles for the desired frequency but we're implementing it with counters and toggles), toggle `CLK_10` and reset `cnt_10`.
- For `CLK_1`, use a counter `cnt_100` that increments on every positive edge of `CLK_in`. When `cnt_100` reaches 49 (to divide 100MHz by 100, we consider 100 cycles for a complete division, but since we are working with a toggle, we divide the clock and then toggle, so 50 cycles for the first half and another 50 cycles for the second half, hence 100 cycles, but since our counter starts from 0, we reach the desired point at 49), toggle `CLK_1` and reset `cnt_100`.
- On every positive edge of `RST`, reset all counters and output clocks to their initial states.
