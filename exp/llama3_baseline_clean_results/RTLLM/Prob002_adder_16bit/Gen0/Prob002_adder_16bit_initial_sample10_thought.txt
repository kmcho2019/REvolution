To implement a 16-bit full adder, we first need to create a smaller bit-width adder, such as an 8-bit adder. This 8-bit adder will then be instantiated multiple times to create the 16-bit full adder. The 8-bit adder itself can be composed of two 4-bit adders, and those can further be made from two 2-bit adders. However, for simplicity and to minimize propagation delay, we can directly implement the 8-bit adder and then use two of these to make the 16-bit adder. The key concept here is to handle the carry-in and carry-out properly between the adders. The 16-bit adder will take two 16-bit inputs and a carry-in, and produce a 16-bit output along with a carry-out.
