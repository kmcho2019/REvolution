To implement a 16-bit full adder, we can break down the problem into smaller parts. First, we design an 8-bit full adder, which will be used as a building block. This 8-bit adder will take two 8-bit inputs and a carry-in, producing an 8-bit output and a carry-out. Then, we use two instances of this 8-bit adder in the 16-bit adder module. The first 8-bit adder will handle the lower 8 bits of the inputs (a[7:0] and b[7:0]) with the carry-in (Cin) from the external input. The second 8-bit adder will handle the upper 8 bits of the inputs (a[15:8] and b[15:8]) with the carry-out from the first 8-bit adder as its carry-in. The carry-out from the second 8-bit adder will be the final carry-out (Co) of the 16-bit adder. This hierarchical approach simplifies the design and reduces the complexity of the combinational logic.
