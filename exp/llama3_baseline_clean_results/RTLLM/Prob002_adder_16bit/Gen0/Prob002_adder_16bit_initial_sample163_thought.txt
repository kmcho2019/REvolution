To design a 16-bit full adder in combinational logic, we first need to create a smaller, reusable adder module, such as an 8-bit adder, which can then be instantiated twice in the 16-bit adder module to handle the high and low bytes of the input operands separately. This approach simplifies the design and makes it more modular. The 8-bit adder itself can be composed of even smaller, 1-bit full adders. Each 1-bit full adder will take two input bits and a carry-in, producing a sum bit and a carry-out. By cascading these, we can build up to an 8-bit adder and then a 16-bit adder. The final carry-out of each adder becomes the carry-in for the next, ensuring that carries propagate correctly across the entire 16-bit width.
