To implement a fixed-point subtractor, we first need to understand how fixed-point arithmetic works. Fixed-point numbers are represented as integers with an implicit decimal point at a certain position. Here, the position of the decimal point is determined by the parameters Q (number of fractional bits) and N (total number of bits).

The module will first check the signs of the input operands a and b. Depending on whether the signs are the same or different, it will perform subtraction or addition. If the signs are the same, it simply subtracts b from a. If the signs are different, it adds the absolute values of a and b and determines the sign of the result based on the relative sizes of a and b.

For handling zero, we explicitly set the sign bit to 0. For precision, we use the parameters Q and N to ensure accurate fixed-point arithmetic.

We'll use Verilog's built-in operators for arithmetic and comparison, and utilize the parameters Q and N to adjust the positions of the fractional and integer parts during the operations.
