To implement a 3-bit comparator, we will use basic digital logic principles. The comparator module will have three output signals: A_greater, A_equal, and A_less. These signals will be determined based on the comparison between the two input operands A and B. The comparison can be achieved using a combination of equality checks and greater/less than checks. 

We can compare A and B bit by bit from the most significant bit (MSB) to the least significant bit (LSB) to determine the relationship between the two numbers. 

For equality (A == B), we simply compare each bit of A with the corresponding bit of B. If all bits match, then A_equal is set to 1.

For A_greater and A_less, we start by comparing the MSB of A and B. If the MSB of A is 1 and the MSB of B is 0, A is greater. If the MSB of A is 0 and the MSB of B is 1, A is less. If the MSBs are equal, we proceed to compare the next bit to the right and repeat the process until we find a difference or reach the LSB.

However, a more straightforward approach for implementing the comparator logic in Verilog can utilize the built-in comparison operators (> , < , ==) to directly compare the values of A and B, and then assign the results to the output ports accordingly.
