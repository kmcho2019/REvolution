The problem involves designing a multi-bit MUX-based synchronizer with two separate clocks, clk_a and clk_b, and two active-low reset signals, arstn and brstn. The input data signal, data_in, is referenced to clock A, while the enable signal, data_en, is delayed by two cycles using D flip-flops referenced to clock B. The output data signal, dataout, is referenced to clock B. To achieve this, we need to implement the following steps:

1. Design a register, data_reg, to store the input data signal, data_in, updated on the positive edge of clk_a or the falling edge of arstn.
2. Implement another register, en_data_reg, to store the input enable signal, data_en, updated on the positive edge of clk_a or the falling edge of arstn.
3. Create two registers, en_clap_one and en_clap_two, to control the selection of the input data, updated on the positive edge of clk_b or the falling edge of brstn.
4. Assign the output data value, dataout, on the positive edge of clk_b or the falling edge of brstn, based on the control signal en_clap_two.

To ensure proper synchronization and data transfer between the two clock domains, we need to carefully manage the clock and reset signals, as well as the enable and control signals.
