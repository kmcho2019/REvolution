To design the 4-bit Linear Feedback Shift Register (LFSR), we first need to understand the principle of its operation. The LFSR generates pseudo-random sequences by shifting the bits and applying a feedback mechanism. The feedback is calculated by XORing the most significant bit (MSB) and the second most significant bit, then inverting the result. This process is synchronized by a clock signal (clk) and can be reset using an active high reset signal (rst). When the reset is high, the register is initialized to zero. Otherwise, on the rising edge of the clock, the bits are shifted left, and the new feedback value is inserted at the least significant bit (LSB). We will implement this using Verilog, focusing on the synchronous reset and the specific feedback mechanism described.
