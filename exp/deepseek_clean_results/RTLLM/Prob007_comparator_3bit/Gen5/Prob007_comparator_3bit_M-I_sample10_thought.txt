The original solution is functionally correct but can be optimized for area by sharing comparison logic between the three outputs. Instead of implementing three separate comparators (>, ==, <), we can:
1. First compute equality (A == B) which is the simplest comparison
2. Then compute greater-than (A > B) only when equality is false
3. Derive less-than from the other two outputs since they are mutually exclusive
This reduces redundant logic and should save area while maintaining the same performance and power characteristics.
