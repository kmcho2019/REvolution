Here's a novel approach using a shift register instead of a traditional counter:
1. Instead of counting clock cycles, we'll use a shift register that circulates a single '1' bit
2. The shift register length is NUM_DIV/2 (since we toggle on every NUM_DIV/2 cycles)
3. When the '1' bit circulates back to position 0, we toggle the output clock
4. This eliminates the need for comparison logic (== operator)
5. The shift operation naturally handles the counting and wrap-around
6. Still maintains synchronous reset and even division validation

Advantages:
- No comparator needed (just look at LSB of shift register)
- Parallel load on reset makes initialization simple
- Shift operation is efficient in hardware
- Same functionality as original but different implementation
