The current implementation is correct but can be optimized for power and area by leveraging the following observations:

1. Symmetric comparisons: a~^b is identical to b~^a, so we can compute these once and reuse the result.
2. Self-comparisons (a~^a, b~^b, etc.) always evaluate to 1, so we can hardcode these bits.
3. The remaining unique comparisons can be computed once and reused in multiple positions.

Optimization strategy:
1. Pre-compute all unique non-self comparisons (10 total for 5 inputs)
2. Hardcode the 5 self-comparisons (always 1)
3. Reuse the pre-computed comparisons in the output vector
4. This reduces the number of XNOR operations from 25 to 10

Expected improvements:
- ~60% reduction in dynamic power (fewer switching gates)
- ~40% area reduction (fewer logic gates)
- Same timing characteristics (still single-level logic)
