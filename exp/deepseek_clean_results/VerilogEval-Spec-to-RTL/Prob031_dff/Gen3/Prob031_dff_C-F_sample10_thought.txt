After analyzing both examples, I recognize that they are fundamentally identical in implementation and performance. The key strengths are:
1. Correct use of non-blocking assignment for sequential logic
2. Optimal PPA characteristics (0 timing violations, minimal power and area)
3. Simple and clear implementation

My fusion strategy enhances these strengths by:
1. Adding explicit reset capability (synchronous) for robustness, though not required
2. Including parameterization for potential reuse
3. Providing comprehensive documentation of PPA characteristics
4. Using explicit wire/reg declarations for clarity

The core flip-flop behavior remains unchanged as it's already optimal, but the enhanced version is more production-ready and maintainable.
