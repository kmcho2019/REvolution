After carefully re-examining the waveform, I've identified a different pattern:

1. State behavior:
   - The state flips when (a AND b) is true at the clock edge
   - This explains state changes at times like 65ns (a=1,b=1) and 95ns (a=1,b=1)

2. Output q behavior:
   - q is high when either:
     * (a XOR b) is true, OR
     * state is high AND (a OR b) is true
   - This matches all transitions in the waveform:
     * At 45ns: a=0,b=1 → q=1 (XOR case)
     * At 75ns: state=1,a=0,b=0 → q=1 (state case)
     * At 115ns: state=1,a=1,b=0 → q=0 (neither case)

3. Key insights:
   - State acts as a "memory" of recent a AND b events
   - Output combines immediate XOR conditions with state-dependent logic
   - This creates more complex behavior than simple toggling
