Here's a novel approach using separate state machines for direction and action:

1. Split the design into two parallel FSMs:
   - Direction FSM: tracks left/right walking direction
   - Action FSM: handles walking/falling/digging/splattered states

2. The direction FSM only changes when:
   - Not falling/digging/splattered
   - Receives bump signals
   - Resets to left

3. The action FSM handles state transitions with priorities:
   - Falling takes precedence over digging
   - Digging takes precedence over walking
   - Splattered is terminal until reset

4. A separate counter tracks fall duration, reset when not falling

5. Outputs are derived from both FSMs' states

Benefits:
- Clear separation of concerns
- Direction changes are isolated from action logic
- Easier to modify/extend individual behaviors
