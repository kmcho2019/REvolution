Here's a simplified approach that reduces both area and power while maintaining the same functionality:
1. Use a 3-bit shift register instead of 4-bit since we only need to track the last 3 bits (plus current input)
2. Combine the shift and compare operations into a single step
3. Remove the separate 'found' register and make the output purely combinational
4. The shift register stops updating once the pattern is found, saving power

This reduces the storage elements from 4 to 3 bits and eliminates one register bit (the 'found' flag), while maintaining the same detection capability. The output becomes combinational but this is acceptable since it's just a status signal.
