Here's a novel approach using a T-flip-flop based implementation instead of a traditional state machine. The key insight is that the state transitions can be modeled as:
- When in OFF state (0) and j=1, toggle to ON (1)
- When in ON state (1) and k=1, toggle to OFF (0)
We can implement this using a T-flip-flop where the toggle input is (j & ~state) | (k & state). This eliminates explicit state encoding and reduces the logic to a simple combinational expression driving the flip-flop.
