After carefully analyzing the Karnaugh map, I notice that the output primarily depends on the values of a, b, and c, while d is truly a don't-care that doesn't affect the output pattern. The output is 1 when either:
1. c is 0 and (a or b is 1), or
2. c is 1 and (a and b are both 1)

This can be simplified to: out = (~c & (a | b)) | (c & a & b). However, looking at the map more closely, we can see that when c=1, the output is 1 whenever a=1 (regardless of b), except for one case (cd=01, ab=00). Therefore, an even simpler expression would be: out = a | (~c & b).
