After carefully analyzing the Karnaugh map, I notice that the output is primarily determined by the combination of inputs a, b, and c, while d is a don't-care that we can ignore. The output is 1 when:
1. c is 0 and (a is 1 or b is 1) - this covers the top-right 1s
2. c is 1 and (a is 0 or b is 1) - this covers the bottom three rows

This can be simplified to: out = (~c & (a | b)) | (c & (~a | b)). Notice that (~a | b) is common in both cases when c is 0 or 1, so we can factor it out. The minimal expression becomes: out = (~a | b) | (~c & a).
