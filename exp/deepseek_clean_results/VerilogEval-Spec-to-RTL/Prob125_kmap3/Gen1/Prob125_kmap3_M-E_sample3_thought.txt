A different approach would be to recognize that the output is primarily driven by the combination of b and c, with a playing a role only in specific cases. Looking at the Karnaugh map:

1. When c=1, out is 1 whenever b=1 (right two columns)
2. When c=0, out is 1 when (a=0 and b=1) or (a=1 and b=0 and cd=00)

This can be simplified to: out = (c & b) | (~c & ((~a & b) | (a & ~b & ~d)))

However, since d is don't-care, we can choose to ignore the d term in the second part, making it: out = (c & b) | (~c & ((~a & b) | (a & ~b)))
