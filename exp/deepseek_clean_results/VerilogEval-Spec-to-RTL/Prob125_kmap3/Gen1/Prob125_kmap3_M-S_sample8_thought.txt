After carefully analyzing the Karnaugh map, I notice that the output is primarily determined by the combination of a and b when c is high, and has specific patterns when c is low. The don't-care conditions can be used to simplify the logic further. The key observations are:
1. When c=0: out is 1 only when a≠b (XOR) except when ab=00 (which is 0)
2. When c=1: out is 1 in all cases except when cd=0100 (ab=00)
This can be simplified to: out = (c & (a | b)) | (~c & a & ~b)
This covers all required cases while optimally using don't-care conditions.
