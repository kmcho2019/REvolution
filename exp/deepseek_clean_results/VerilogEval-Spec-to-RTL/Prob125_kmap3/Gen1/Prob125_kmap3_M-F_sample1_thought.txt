After carefully re-examining the Karnaugh map, I'll approach the problem differently. The output is 1 in these cases:
1. When ab = 10 or 11 (a is high) and cd ≠ 00 (c or d is high)
2. When ab = 00 and cd = 10 (special case)
3. When ab = 01 and cd = 00 (special case, but this is actually 0 in the map - need to be careful)

The don't-cares (marked d) can be treated as 1s to simplify the logic. The minimal expression becomes:
out = (a & ~b) | (a & b) | (~a & b & c) | (~a & ~b & ~c & d)

But this can be further simplified to:
out = a | (b & c) | (~a & ~b & ~c & d)

However, looking at the map again, a simpler expression that covers all cases is:
out = a | (b & c) | (~a & b & d)

This covers:
- All cases where a=1 (first term)
- The cases where b=1 and c=1 (second term)
- The don't-care cases where we can choose d=1 (third term)
