After carefully analyzing the Karnaugh map, I propose a different approach using multiplexers for efficient implementation. The key observation is that the output depends heavily on the value of 'c', which suggests we can use 'c' as a selector for a 2:1 mux. 

For c=0 (top two rows of K-map):
- Output is 1 when (ab=10 or ab=11) and cd=00
- Output is 0 otherwise (with don't-cares in cd=01)

For c=1 (bottom two rows of K-map):
- Output is 1 for all cases except when (ab=01)
- Don't-cares in cd=01 can be treated as 1 to simplify

This leads to the expression: out = c ? (~(a & ~b)) : ((a ^ b) & ~d)
The don't-care 'd' is used to simplify the c=0 case by assuming d=0 when needed.
