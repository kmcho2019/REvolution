Looking at the Karnaugh map, we can observe that the output is 1 in two main cases:
1. When a is 1 (right half of the map)
2. When a is 0, b is 0, and c is 1 (top-left corner)

This leads to a very simple logical expression: out = a | (~a & ~b & c). The don't-care conditions can be ignored as they don't affect this minimal expression.

The implementation can be simplified to:
1. Directly compute the AND term (~a & ~b & c) without intermediate wires
2. OR this with input a
This reduces the number of explicit assignments while maintaining the same functionality.
