Here's a novel approach using a priority encoder concept:
1. Observation: The output is 1 in most cases except when (c=0 and b=1) or (c=1 and a=0 and b=1)
2. Key insight: We can treat this as a priority encoding problem where certain input combinations have higher priority for determining the output
3. Novel architecture:
   - First, detect the "must be 0" conditions (highest priority)
   - Then, detect the "must be 1" conditions (medium priority)
   - Finally, use a default value (lowest priority)
4. Benefits:
   - More scalable for larger truth tables
   - Clear priority structure makes it easy to modify conditions
   - Potentially better for synthesis as priority encoders are well-optimized in tools
5. Implementation:
   - Use nested conditional assignments to implement the priority scheme
   - Take advantage of Verilog's conditional operator precedence
