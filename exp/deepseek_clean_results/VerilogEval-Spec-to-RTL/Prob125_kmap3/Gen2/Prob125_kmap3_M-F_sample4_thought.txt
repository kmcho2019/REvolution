To correctly implement the Karnaugh map, I'll analyze it systematically:

1. First, I'll ignore d (don't-care) and focus on a, b, c combinations:
   - For c=0 (first row of K-map):
     - ab=00: out=0
     - ab=01: out=d (can choose 0)
     - ab=10: out=1
     - ab=11: out=1
   - For c=1 (remaining rows):
     - When ab=00: out=0 (from cd=01) or 1 (from cd=10/11) - but cd=01 shows 0, so we'll keep 0
     - When ab=01: out=0 (from cd=00) or 1 (from cd=11/10) - we'll choose 1 for minimal logic
     - When ab=10 or 11: out=1 in all cases

2. The minimal logic expression that covers this is:
   out = (~c & (a ^ b)) | (c & (a | b))
   This works because:
   - When c=0: output is 1 only when a≠b (XOR)
   - When c=1: output is 1 when either a or b is 1

3. We can further optimize by observing that (~c & (a ^ b)) | (c & (a | b)) is equivalent to:
   out = (a | b) & (a | c | ~b)
   But the first expression is simpler to implement.
