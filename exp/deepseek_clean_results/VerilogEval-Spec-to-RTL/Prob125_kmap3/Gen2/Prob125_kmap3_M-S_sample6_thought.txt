After carefully re-examining the Karnaugh map, I notice that the output can be expressed most simply by focusing on the patterns where the output is 1:
1. When c=0, output is 1 if either a or b is 1 (except for cd=00, ab=00 which should be 0)
2. When c=1, output is 1 if a is 1 (regardless of b, except for cd=01, ab=00 which should be 0)

The don't-care conditions (d) can be used to our advantage to simplify the logic. The simplest expression that covers all cases is: out = a | (b & ~c). This works because:
- When a=1, output is always 1 (covering all c=1 cases where a=1)
- When a=0, output is b & ~c (which handles the c=0 cases correctly)
- The don't-care conditions allow us to ignore d completely
- The only exception (cd=00, ab=00) is handled since a=0 and b=0 makes output 0
- The other exception (cd=01, ab=00) is handled since a=0 and b=0 makes output 0
