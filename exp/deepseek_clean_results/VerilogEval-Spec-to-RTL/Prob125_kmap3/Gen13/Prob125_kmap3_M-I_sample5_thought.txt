To optimize the solution further, I'll focus on reducing the gate count and logic depth while maintaining correct functionality. The key improvements are:
1. Notice that when c=1, the output is 1 when either (b=0) OR (b=1 AND a=1)
2. When c=0, the output is simply equal to a (taking advantage of don't-cares)
3. This can be implemented with just one OR gate and one AND gate, plus an inverter for b
4. The logic depth is reduced from 3 levels (AND-AND-OR) to 2 levels (AND-OR)
5. This eliminates one intermediate wire and reduces the total gate count
