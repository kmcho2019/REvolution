The key insight is that the output is primarily determined by the combination of inputs a and c:
1. When a=1, output is always 1 (as seen in both examples)
2. When c=1, output is 1 unless a=0 and b=1 (from Example 1)
3. When c=0, output equals a (which is already covered by point 1)

This can be simplified to a single logical expression:
out = a | (c & ~(a & ~b))
But since a=1 already forces output to 1, we can simplify further to:
out = a | (c & b)
This captures all cases while using minimal logic operations.
