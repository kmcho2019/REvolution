After carefully analyzing the Karnaugh map, I notice that the output primarily depends on the combination of a, b, and c, with d being a don't-care that we can ignore. The pattern shows:

1. When c=0:
   - Output is 0 when ab=00 or 01
   - Output is 1 when ab=10 or 11

2. When c=1:
   - Output is 0 only when ab=00
   - Output is 1 for all other ab combinations

This can be simplified to: out = (a & b) | (c & (a | b))
This expression covers all cases:
- When c=0: only a AND b gives 1 (matches ab=11 case)
- When c=1: any a OR b gives 1 (except ab=00 which gives 0)
The don't-cares are optimally used to support this simplification.
