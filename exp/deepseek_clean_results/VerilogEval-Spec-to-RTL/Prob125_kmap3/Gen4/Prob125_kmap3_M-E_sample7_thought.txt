After re-examining the Karnaugh map, I notice that the output is primarily determined by the combination of c and the other inputs, with d being a don't-care. Here's a new approach:

1. When c=0 (first two rows of K-map):
   - Output is 0 when ab=00 or 01
   - Output is 1 when ab=10 or 11
   This can be expressed as (a & ~b) | (a & b) = a

2. When c=1 (last two rows of K-map):
   - Output is 0 only when ab=00
   - Output is 1 for all other cases
   This can be expressed as a | b

Combining these observations, the output can be expressed as:
out = (c & (a | b)) | (~c & a)

This simplifies to: out = a | (c & b)

This solution:
- Correctly handles all specified cases
- Optimally uses don't-care conditions
- Is simpler than the original attempt (only 2 operations)
- Matches all required outputs while properly utilizing don't-cares
