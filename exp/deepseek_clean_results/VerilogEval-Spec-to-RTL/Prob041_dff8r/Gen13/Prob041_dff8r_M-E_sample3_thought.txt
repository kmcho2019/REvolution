Instead of using a single parameterized DFF module, I'll implement a distributed reset architecture where:
1. Each bit has its own dedicated DFF with local reset logic
2. Reset propagation is pipelined to reduce fanout and improve timing
3. The reset signal is synchronized through a chain of flip-flops
4. Each bit's reset is individually controlled by the propagating reset signal
5. This creates a wave-like reset propagation through the register

Benefits:
- Better reset timing due to reduced fanout
- Potential power savings from staggered reset transitions
- More scalable for larger bit widths
- Interesting side effect: bits reset sequentially rather than simultaneously
