Instead of directly comparing current and previous inputs, I'll implement a shift register that tracks the last 2 cycles of each input bit. This allows:
1. Each bit's history is maintained independently in a small shift register
2. Edge detection becomes a pattern match on the shift register contents (looking for "01")
3. The design is easily scalable to detect longer patterns if needed
4. The shift register approach could be more power-efficient for bits that don't change often
5. This method naturally extends to detecting other patterns (like negative edges) by changing the match pattern
