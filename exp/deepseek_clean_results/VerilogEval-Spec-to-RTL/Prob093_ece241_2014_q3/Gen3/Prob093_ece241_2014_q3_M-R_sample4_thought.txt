Let's analyze the K-map column by column to determine each mux_in bit:

1. mux_in[0] (ab=00):
   - Should be 1 when cd=01 or cd=11 (but not cd=10)
   - This is equivalent to (c AND d) OR (~c AND d) which simplifies to d
   - Wait no, looking at K-map: 00=0, 01=1, 11=1, 10=0
   - So it's actually d (matches 01 and 11 cases)

2. mux_in[1] (ab=01):
   - Always 0 as per K-map (all entries in this column are 0)

3. mux_in[2] (ab=11):
   - Should be 1 only when cd=11 (c AND d)

4. mux_in[3] (ab=10):
   - Should be 1 when cd=00 or cd=10 or cd=11
   - But looking at K-map: 00=1, 01=0, 11=1, 10=1
   - This is equivalent to ~d OR c (matches all required cases)

The correct implementation requires:
- mux_in[0]: Just d (matches 01 and 11 cases)
- mux_in[1]: constant 0
- mux_in[2]: c AND d
- mux_in[3]: ~d OR c
