After analyzing both examples, here's the optimal fusion strategy:

1. From Example 1: The insight that the output is only 0 when (a=0 AND b=0 AND c=0) is valuable, but the implementation uses a NOR gate followed by inversion, which is less direct.

2. From Example 2: The simple OR operation (a | b | c) is elegant and efficient, but we can potentially optimize further by recognizing that when a=1, the output is always 1 regardless of b and c.

3. Fusion Insight: The most efficient implementation would be (a | (b & c)) because:
   - When a=1: Output is immediately 1 (short-circuit evaluation possible)
   - When a=0: Only need to check if either b or c is 1 (equivalent to ~(b=0 AND c=0))
   - This reduces to a 2-input OR and a 2-input AND, which is more area-efficient than a 3-input OR in some technologies
   - Maintains the same functionality while potentially offering better PPA

This implementation combines the logical insight from Example 1 with the simplicity of Example 2, while potentially improving upon both in terms of power and area efficiency.
