Here's a novel approach using a shift register implementation:
1. Instead of direct concatenation, we'll use a sequential approach that shifts the input bits into a 32-bit register
2. The shifting happens in a single clock cycle (combinational logic)
3. The two LSBs are hardwired to 1's
4. The 32-bit register is then split into the four 8-bit outputs
5. This approach demonstrates an alternative way to organize the bits that could be useful in more complex scenarios where sequential processing is needed

The benefits of this approach:
- Shows a different architectural style
- Could be extended to multi-cycle implementations if needed
- Demonstrates bit manipulation through shifting
- Maintains the same functionality as the original
