This approach implements a bit-serial multiplexer using a shift register and counter:
1. Uses a 100-bit shift register to process inputs one bit at a time
2. A 7-bit counter (since 100 needs 7 bits) controls the selection
3. On each clock cycle, selects one bit from either a or b based on sel
4. Shifts the selected bit into the output register
5. Advantages:
   - Extremely low fanout (only 1 bit selection per cycle)
   - Minimal combinational logic
   - Can be easily pipelined or power-gated
   - Scales well to very wide buses
   - Lower dynamic power for infrequently changing data
6. Tradeoff: Requires multiple cycles to complete (100 cycles)
