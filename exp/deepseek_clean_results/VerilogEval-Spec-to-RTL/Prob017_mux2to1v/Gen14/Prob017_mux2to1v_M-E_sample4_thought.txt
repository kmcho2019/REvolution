Here's a novel approach using a hierarchical multiplexer structure with bitwise encoding:

1. Instead of a direct 100-bit mux, we'll implement a two-level hierarchy:
   - First level: Divide the 100 bits into 10 groups of 10 bits each
   - Second level: Each group has its own 10-bit mux controlled by sel

2. The key innovation is using a bitwise encoding scheme where:
   - Each output bit is computed as: (a_bit & ~sel) | (b_bit & sel)
   - This breaks the dependency chain and allows parallel computation

3. Benefits:
   - Better timing due to reduced fanout (only 10 bits per mux)
   - More regular structure that may map better to certain architectures
   - Clear physical hierarchy for placement and routing
   - Potential power savings from localized switching
