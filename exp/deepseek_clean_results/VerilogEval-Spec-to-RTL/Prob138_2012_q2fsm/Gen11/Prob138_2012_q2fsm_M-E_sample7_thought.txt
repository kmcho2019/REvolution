I'll implement a shift register-based FSM that encodes states as bit patterns in a shift register. This approach offers:

1. **Shift Register States**: Each state is represented by a unique bit pattern in a 3-bit shift register, where only one bit is '1' at a time (similar to one-hot but with shifting behavior).

2. **Transition Logic**: State transitions are implemented by shifting bits left or right based on input 'w', with special cases for wrap-around.

3. **Output Generation**: The output 'z' is derived directly from the MSB of the shift register when in specific states.

Advantages:
- Reduced combinational logic compared to traditional FSM
- Simple, predictable timing due to shift operations
- Natural state encoding that minimizes transitions
- Easy to extend by just increasing register size
