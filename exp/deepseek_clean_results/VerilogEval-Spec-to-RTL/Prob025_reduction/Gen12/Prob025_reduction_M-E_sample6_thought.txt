Here's a novel approach using a hierarchical tree structure with intermediate registers to potentially improve timing in larger designs where the parity calculation might be part of a critical path:

1. Instead of a direct XOR reduction, we'll use a 2-level hierarchical approach with registered intermediate results
2. First level: Compute XOR of bit pairs (4 parallel XOR operations)
3. Register these intermediate results (helps with timing closure in larger designs)
4. Second level: XOR the registered intermediate results in pairs
5. Final XOR to get the parity bit

Benefits:
- Better timing characteristics for large designs (can pipeline if needed)
- More regular structure that might map better to certain FPGA architectures
- Potential for better clock-to-q timing if parity is used in a synchronous system
- Easier to extend to wider data widths while maintaining good timing
