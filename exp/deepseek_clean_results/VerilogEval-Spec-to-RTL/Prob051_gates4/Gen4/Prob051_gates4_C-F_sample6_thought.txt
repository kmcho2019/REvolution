The optimal solution combines the conciseness of reduction operators with the balanced tree structure where it matters most. Here's the fusion strategy:

1. For AND and OR operations:
   - Use reduction operators (& and |) as they are:
     * Most concise and readable in Verilog
     * Synthesize efficiently to standard cells
     * Have clear semantics
     * Don't need tree structure since AND/OR operations are typically well-optimized in synthesis tools

2. For XOR operation:
   - Use explicit tree structure (two 2-input XORs followed by final XOR) because:
     * XOR trees have better timing characteristics than long XOR chains
     * Some technologies implement XOR more efficiently as trees
     * Maintains balance in critical paths
     * More predictable synthesis results across different technologies

3. Benefits of this hybrid approach:
   - Minimal and readable code for AND/OR operations
   - Optimal implementation for XOR operation
   - Best overall PPA characteristics
   - Maintains clarity while being implementation-aware
