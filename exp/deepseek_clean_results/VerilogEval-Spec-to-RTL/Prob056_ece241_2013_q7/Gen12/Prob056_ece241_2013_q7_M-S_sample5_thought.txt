To simplify the JK flip-flop implementation, I'll optimize the combinational logic by recognizing that:
1. When J=K=0, we keep the old value (Q)
2. When J≠K, we take the value of J
3. When J=K=1, we toggle the value (~Q)

This can be expressed more concisely as:
Q_next = (J & ~K) | (~J & K & Q) | (J & K & ~Q)
But we can further simplify by observing that:
- When J≠K, output follows J
- When J=K, output toggles
Thus, Q_next = (J ^ K) ? J : (J ^ Q)

This reduces the logic to a simple mux between J and the toggled Q, controlled by whether J equals K.
