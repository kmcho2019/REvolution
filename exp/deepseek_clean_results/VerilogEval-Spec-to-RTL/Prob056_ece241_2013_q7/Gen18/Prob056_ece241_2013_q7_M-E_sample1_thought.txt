I'll implement the JK flip-flop using a different approach that leverages the inherent properties of a D flip-flop with combinational logic. The key idea is:

1. Use a standard D flip-flop as the storage element
2. Create combinational logic that converts JK inputs to appropriate D input
3. The conversion logic will be:
   D = (J & ~Q) | (~K & Q)
   
This implementation is efficient because:
- It clearly separates combinational and sequential logic
- Uses minimal gates (2 ANDs, 1 OR, and inverters)
- The expression directly maps to the JK truth table requirements
- Easy to understand and verify behavior
- Naturally prevents race conditions by using edge-triggered flip-flop
