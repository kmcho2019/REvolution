The JK flip-flop can be implemented even more simply by recognizing that:
1. When J and K are different, Q takes J's value
2. When J and K are same, Q toggles if both are 1, or holds if both are 0
3. This can be expressed as: Q_next = (j & ~k) | (~j & k) ? j : (j ^ Q_reg)

But we can simplify further by observing that (j & ~k) | (~j & k) is just j XOR k, so:
Q_next = (j ^ k) ? j : (j ^ Q_reg)

This reduces the logic to one XOR gate and a multiplexer, making it very compact.
