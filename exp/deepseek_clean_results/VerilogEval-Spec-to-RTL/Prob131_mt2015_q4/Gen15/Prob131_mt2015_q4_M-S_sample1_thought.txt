Simplification Strategy:
1. Boolean Analysis:
   - ModuleA's function z = (x^y) & x simplifies to x & ~y (since x^y = x&~y | ~x&y, and AND with x leaves only x&~y)
   - ModuleB's truth table matches x NOR y (z=1 only when x=0 and y=0)

2. Structural Simplification:
   - Since all submodules share identical inputs, we can compute their outputs once and reuse
   - The original OR/AND/XOR combination simplifies to (a_out OR b_out) XOR (a_out AND b_out)
   - This further simplifies to a_out XOR b_out (since (A+B) XOR (AB) = A XOR B)

3. Final Implementation:
   - Implement ModuleA as x & ~y (1 gate)
   - Implement ModuleB as ~(x | y) (1 gate)
   - Final output is direct XOR of ModuleA and ModuleB outputs
   - Eliminate redundant submodules since inputs are identical

Key Benefits:
- Reduces gate count from original solution
- Maintains identical functionality
