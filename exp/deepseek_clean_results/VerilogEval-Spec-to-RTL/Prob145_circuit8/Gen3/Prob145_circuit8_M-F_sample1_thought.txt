After carefully re-examining the waveform and feedback, I realize the behavior is more complex than a simple AND gate and flip-flop. The key observations are:

1. Output p is indeed high only when both clock and a are high (p = clock & a)
2. Output q behaves differently - it toggles its state only after seeing three consecutive high p values (clock & a) during clock high phases
3. The waveform shows q changes from 0 to 1 only after three consecutive cycles where p=1 (at 90-110ns)
4. Similarly, q changes back to 0 after seeing a=1 during a clock low phase (at 175-185ns)

The solution requires:
1. Keeping the combinational p output (clock AND a)
2. Adding a counter to track consecutive p=1 occurrences
3. Implementing state logic for q that:
   - Sets q=1 after three consecutive p=1
   - Resets q=0 when a=1 during clock low
4. The counter should reset if p=0 during any clock high phase
