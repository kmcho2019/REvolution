The waveform analysis reveals a simpler behavior than initially modeled:
1. Output p is high only when both clock and input a are high (p = clock & a)
2. Output q captures p's value at the negative clock edge (q becomes what p was during the preceding high clock phase)

This can be implemented more directly without a state machine:
- Use combinational logic for p (AND of clock and a)
- Use a flip-flop for q that samples p on negative clock edges
- Remove the state machine and initialization since they're unnecessary
- Let outputs remain undefined (x) before first clock edge
