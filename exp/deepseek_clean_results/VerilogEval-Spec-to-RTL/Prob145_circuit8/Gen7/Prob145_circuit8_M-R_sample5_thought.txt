1. Output p is a synchronous version of input 'a' - it captures 'a' at the positive clock edge and holds it until the next edge
2. Output q acts as a delayed version of p, but only after p has been high for at least one full clock cycle
3. The key insight is that q toggles its state when:
   - p was high in the previous cycle AND
   - p is high in the current cycle
4. Need to track two states:
   - Current p value (synchronous with clock)
   - Whether p was high in the previous cycle (to trigger q toggle)
5. The outputs reset when specific patterns occur (like when a goes high during low clock)
