To simplify the solution, we can observe the Karnaugh map and derive a more optimized Boolean expression. The key insights are:

1. The output is 1 in most cases (10 out of 16), so it's more efficient to express when the output is 0 and invert that.

2. Looking at the K-map, the 0s occur in specific patterns:
   - When c&d is true and a&b is false (cd=11, ab=00)
   - When d is true and c is false and a&b is either 01 or 11
   - When c is true and d is false and a&b is either 11 or 10
   - When c&d are false and a&b is true (cd=00, ab=11)

3. We can combine these conditions more efficiently by factoring common terms:
   - (!a & !b & c & d) can be written as (c & d & !(a | b))
   - The d-related terms can be grouped as (!c & d & (a^b))
   - The c-related terms can be grouped as (c & !d & a)
   - The final term is (!c & !d & a & b)

This results in a more compact expression with fewer operations.
