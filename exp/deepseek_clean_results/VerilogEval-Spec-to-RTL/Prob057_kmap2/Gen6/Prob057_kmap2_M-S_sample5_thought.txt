To simplify the implementation while maintaining functionality, we can derive a minimal Boolean expression from the Karnaugh map. Observing the K-map:

1. The output is 1 in most cases except for specific patterns where it's 0.
2. The 0s appear in a checkerboard-like pattern that can be captured with XOR operations.
3. The minimal expression is: out = ~(c ^ d) | (a ^ b) | (c & ~d & ~a & b) | (~c & d & a & ~b)

However, this still seems complex. A better approach is to recognize that the output is 0 only in these cases:
- cd=00 and ab=11
- cd=01 and ab=01 or ab=11
- cd=10 and ab=11 or ab=10

This can be expressed more simply as:
out = !((~c & ~d & a & b) | (~c & d & ~a & b) | (~c & d & a & b) | (c & ~d & a & b) | (c & ~d & a & ~b))

But this is still not optimal. The most efficient implementation is to keep the case statement but make it more concise by grouping the 0 cases.
