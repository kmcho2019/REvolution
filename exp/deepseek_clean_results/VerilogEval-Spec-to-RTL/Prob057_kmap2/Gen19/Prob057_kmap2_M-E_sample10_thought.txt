Novel Architectural Idea: Majority Function with Selective Inversion

1. Key Insight:
   - The K-map shows symmetry when viewed diagonally (from ab=00,cd=00 to ab=11,cd=11)
   - The pattern resembles a modified majority function where we need 3/4 inputs to agree, but with selective inversion

2. Implementation Strategy:
   - Use XOR gates to create phase-controlled signals
   - Implement a weighted voting mechanism where:
     - a and b have higher weight when c=0
     - c and d have higher weight when a=1
   - Combine using a balanced OR-AND structure that naturally captures the K-map patterns

3. Advantages:
   - More regular structure than minterm-based approaches
   - Better scalability for similar patterns in larger K-maps
   - Potential for better timing due to balanced path delays

4. Verification:
   - The structure ensures all 1s in K-map are covered by:
     - When c=0, the a|b term dominates
     - When a=1, the c|d term dominates
     - The XOR terms handle the edge cases
   - All 0s in K-map are properly excluded
