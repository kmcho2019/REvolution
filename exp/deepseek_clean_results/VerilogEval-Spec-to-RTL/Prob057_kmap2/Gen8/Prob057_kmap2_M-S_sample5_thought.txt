To simplify the solution while maintaining functionality, we can observe that the output is 0 in only four specific cases. Instead of computing these cases separately and ORing them, we can directly express the output as 1 unless one of these four cases occurs. The four cases can be identified by their unique combinations of inputs:

1. cd=00 & ab=11 (~c & ~d & a & b)
2. cd=01 & b=1 (~c & d & b)
3. cd=10 & a=1 (c & ~d & a)
4. cd=11 & ab=00 (c & d & ~a & ~b)

The simplification comes from recognizing that we don't need to compute intermediate terms - we can directly check for these four conditions and invert the result. This maintains the same functionality while potentially reducing gate count.
