To refactor the implementation while maintaining the same functionality, we'll use Boolean equations derived from the Karnaugh map. This approach:

1. Identifies prime implicants from the K-map to create a minimized Boolean expression
2. Uses continuous assignments (assign statements) instead of a case statement
3. Groups the 1s in the K-map to form the simplest possible logic expression
4. The K-map shows four main groups of 1s that can be covered with:
   - Group 1: ~c & ~d (top two rows when ab=00 or ab=10)
   - Group 2: ~a & ~b (left two columns when cd=00 or cd=01)
   - Group 3: b & d (middle-right when cd=11)
   - Group 4: a & ~b & c (specific cells)

This implementation will be more efficient as it reduces the logic to essential terms and uses direct wiring rather than a multiplexer structure.
