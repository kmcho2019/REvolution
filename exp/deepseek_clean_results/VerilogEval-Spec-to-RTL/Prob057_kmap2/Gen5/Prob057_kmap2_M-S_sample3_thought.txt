After carefully analyzing the Karnaugh map again, I notice a simpler pattern emerges when we consider the following observations:

1. The output is 1 in most cases except when:
   - c=0 AND a=1 AND b=1 AND d=1 (top-right corner when cd=00)
   - c=1 AND a=0 AND b=0 (bottom-left corner when cd=11)
   - c=1 AND a=1 AND b=0 AND d=0 (bottom-right corner when cd=10)

2. This can be expressed more simply as:
   out = ~((~c & a & b & d) | (c & ~a & ~b) | (c & a & ~b & ~d))

3. Using De Morgan's laws, we can further simplify to:
   out = (~c | ~a | ~b | ~d) & (~c | a | b) & (~c | ~a | b | d)
   
But the simplest implementation comes from directly implementing the exception cases:
out = !( (!c && a && b && d) || (c && !a && !b) || (c && a && !b && !d) )
