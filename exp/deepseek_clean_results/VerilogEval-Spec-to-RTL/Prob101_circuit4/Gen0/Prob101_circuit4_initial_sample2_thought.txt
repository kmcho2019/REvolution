Analyzing the simulation waveforms, we can observe that the output q is 1 in the following cases:
1. When c is 1 (regardless of other inputs)
2. When b is 1 (regardless of other inputs except when a is 1 and d is 0)
3. The only exception is when a=1 and d=0, where q=0 even if b=1 or c=1

This behavior can be summarized as: q = (b & ~(a & ~d)) | c
In other words, q is true when either c is true, or when b is true and we're not in the case where a is true and d is false.
