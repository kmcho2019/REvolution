Analyzing the simulation waveforms, we can see that the output q is 1 in all cases except when:
1. All inputs are 0 (a=0, b=0, c=0, d=0)
2. When a=1 and both b=0 and c=0 (regardless of d)

This suggests the logic function is: q = (b OR c) OR (a AND (b OR c)). However, looking more closely, we can see it's actually equivalent to: q = b | c | (a & (b | c)), which simplifies to just q = b | c.

But wait - looking at the waveform:
- At 55ns (a=1,b=0,c=0,d=0) q=0
- At 60ns (a=1,b=0,c=0,d=1) q=0
This shows q is 0 when a=1 AND b=0 AND c=0, regardless of d.

At all other times, q=1. So the correct logic is: q = ~(a & ~b & ~c)
