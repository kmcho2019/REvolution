Analyzing the simulation waveforms, we can see that the output q is 1 in all cases except when:
1. a=0 and b=0 and c=0 and d=0 (all inputs are 0)
2. a=0 and b=0 and d=0 (regardless of c)
3. a=1 and b=0 and d=0 (when c=0)

This pattern suggests that q is 1 when either:
- b is 1 (regardless of other inputs), OR
- c is 1 and d is not 0 (but this is already covered by b=1 cases), OR
- More precisely, q is 0 only when (a=0 and b=0 and d=0) or (a=1 and b=0 and c=0 and d=0)

The simplest implementation is q = b | (c & d) | (a & c)
But looking at the truth table, it's actually equivalent to q = b | (c & (a | d))
