To simplify the implementation, let's analyze the Karnaugh map more carefully and find the most efficient grouping of 1's:

1. For x[3:2] = 00: f=1 only when x[1:0] is 00 or 10
   - This can be expressed as: ~x[3] & ~x[2] & ~x[0] & (x[1] ^ x[0])

2. For x[3:2] = 11: f=1 when x[1:0] is 00, 01, or 11
   - This is equivalent to x[3] & x[2] & (x[1] | ~x[0])

3. For x[3:2] = 10: f=1 when x[1:0] is 00, 01, or 10
   - This can be written as x[3] & ~x[2] & (~x[1] | ~x[0])

The x[3:2] = 01 case is always 0 and doesn't need any terms.

Combining these observations gives us a much simpler expression:
f = (~x[3] & ~x[2] & ~x[0]) | 
    (x[3] & x[2] & (x[1] | ~x[0])) | 
    (x[3] & ~x[2] & (~x[1] | ~x[0]))
