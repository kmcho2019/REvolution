Here's a novel approach using a hierarchical combination of smaller logic blocks that implement the K-map regions. Instead of a direct case statement, we'll:

1. Break down the K-map into its essential patterns:
   - The '1's form distinct groups that can be implemented as separate AND-OR terms
   - The top row (x[3:2]=00) has two '1's at x[1:0]=00 and 10
   - The bottom two rows (x[3:2]=11 and 10) have more complex patterns

2. Implement these patterns using basic logic gates:
   - First term covers x[3:2]=00 AND (x[1:0]=00 OR x[1:0]=10)
   - Second term covers x[3:2]=11 AND (x[1] OR x[0])
   - Third term covers x[3:2]=10 AND (x[1:0]!=11)

3. Combine these terms with OR gates to form the final output

This approach is more efficient than a case statement and demonstrates understanding of Boolean logic synthesis from K-maps.
