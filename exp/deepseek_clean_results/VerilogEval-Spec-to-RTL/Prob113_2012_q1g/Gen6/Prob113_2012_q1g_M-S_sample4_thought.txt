To simplify the implementation while maintaining correctness, I'll analyze the Karnaugh map differently:

1. Observe that the output is 1 in three main cases:
   - When x[3] and x[2] are both 1 (third row), except for x[1]x[0] = 10
   - When x[3] is 1 and x[2] is 0 (bottom row), except for x[1]x[0] = 11
   - When x[3] and x[2] are both 0 (first row), for x[0] = 0 or (x[1]x[0] = 00)

2. This leads to a more concise expression:
   f = (x[3] & x[2] & ~(x[1] & ~x[0])) |       // Third row cases except 1110
       (x[3] & ~x[2] & ~(x[1] & x[0])) |       // Bottom row cases except 1011
       (~x[3] & ~x[2] & (~x[0] | ~x[1]))       // First row cases

3. Further simplification:
   - For x[3]x[2] = 11: f is 1 unless x[1]x[0] = 10
   - For x[3]x[2] = 10: f is 1 unless x[1]x[0] = 11
   - For x[3]x[2] = 00: f is 1 when x[0] is 0 or x[1] is 0
   - For x[3]x[2] = 01: always 0 (second row)

Final simplified expression:
f = (x[3] & x[2] & ~(x[1] & ~x[0])) |
