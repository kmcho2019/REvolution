A more efficient approach would be to recognize patterns in the Karnaugh map that can be implemented using simpler logic expressions. Observing the K-map:

1. The output is 1 when x[3:2] is 00 and x[1:0] is 00 or 10 (top corners)
2. The output is 1 when x[3:2] is 11 and x[1] is 0 (left two columns)
3. The output is 1 when x[3:2] is 10 and x[1:0] is not 11 (all except bottom right)

This can be expressed as:
f = (~x[3] & ~x[2] & ~x[1] & ~x[0]) |  // 0000
    (~x[3] & ~x[2] & x[1] & ~x[0]) |   // 0010
    (x[3] & x[2] & ~x[1]) |            // 11x0
    (x[3] & ~x[2] & ~(x[1] & x[0]))    // 10 and not 11

This implementation reduces the number of terms by using pattern recognition and Boolean algebra simplification.
