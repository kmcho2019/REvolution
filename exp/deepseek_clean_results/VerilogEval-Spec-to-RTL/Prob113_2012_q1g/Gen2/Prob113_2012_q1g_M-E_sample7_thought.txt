Let me approach this differently by using a hierarchical design with smaller submodules. I'll break down the problem into two parts: row selection and column selection. 

1. First, I'll create a row decoder that identifies which of the four rows (based on x[3:2]) we're in.
2. Then, I'll create a column pattern matcher that checks the appropriate pattern for each row (based on x[1:0]).
3. Finally, I'll combine these results to produce the output f.

This approach makes the design more modular and easier to verify. For the row patterns:
- Row 00 (x[3:2]=00): Pattern is ~x[0] (matches 00 and 10)
- Row 01 (x[3:2]=01): Always 0
- Row 11 (x[3:2]=11): ~x[1] (matches 00 and 01)
- Row 10 (x[3:2]=10): ~x[1] | (x[1] & ~x[0]) (matches 00, 01, and 10)

The final output is the OR of these row-specific patterns when their row is active.
