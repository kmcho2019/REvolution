The fusion strategy combines the best aspects of both solutions:
1. From Example 1: The Boolean algebra simplification (z = x & ~y) provides the most minimal gate-level implementation, which is optimal for ASIC designs where discrete gates are preferred.
2. From Example 2: The MUX implementation makes the control logic explicit and may be more efficient in FPGA architectures where MUXes are fundamental building blocks.

- Maintain the Boolean algebra simplification (x & ~y) for minimal gate count
- Use a ternary operator (?:) for clear expression of the control flow
- Result in code that is both minimal and clearly expresses the logical relationship
- Be synthesis-friendly for both ASIC and FPGA targets
- Potentially enable better technology mapping depending on the target library
