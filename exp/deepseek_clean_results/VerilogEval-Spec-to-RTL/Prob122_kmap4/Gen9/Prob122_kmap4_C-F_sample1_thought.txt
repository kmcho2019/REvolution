The two examples provided both correctly implement the Karnaugh map using XOR operations, but they use slightly different formulations. After analyzing both:

1. Example 1 uses a direct XOR chain (a ^ b ^ c ^ d)
2. Example 2 groups XOR operations ((a ^ b) ^ (c ^ d))

Both implementations are functionally equivalent and have identical PPA metrics (same power, area, and timing). The choice between them comes down to:
- Readability: Example 2's grouped XORs might be slightly more readable as it shows the symmetry in the problem
- Potential synthesis optimization: Some synthesis tools might better optimize one form over the other, though in this case both produce identical results

Since both solutions are already optimal in terms of PPA, the best fusion is to maintain the same logic but choose the more readable form. The grouped XOR version (Example 2) better reflects the checkerboard pattern's symmetry in the Karnaugh map.
