After analyzing both examples, I recognize that they are fundamentally equivalent in terms of functionality and PPA metrics. The key insight is that the Karnaugh map represents a 4-input XOR function, which can be implemented in various equivalent ways. 

The fusion strategy focuses on:
1. Maintaining the optimal PPA metrics (which both examples achieve)
2. Choosing the most readable and maintainable implementation
3. Preserving the clear relationship to the original Karnaugh map pattern

The best fusion is to use the hierarchical parity approach from Example 2, as it:
- Clearly shows the relationship between row and column parity
- Makes the checkerboard pattern explicit
- Is more modular and easier to understand/maintain
- Has identical PPA to the flat XOR chain
- Could be more testable (individual row/column parities could be probed)

This maintains all the benefits while improving readability without sacrificing performance.
