I'll implement a novel solution using a priority encoder approach. The key observation is that the output q is only 1 when at least one of the following conditions is true:
1. Both b and d are 1
2. Both a and d are 1
3. Both b and c are 1
4. Both a and c are 1

We can implement this as a priority encoder that checks these conditions in sequence. The priority isn't actually important since the conditions are mutually exclusive in terms of output (they all result in q=1), but this approach demonstrates an alternative way to structure the logic.
