The original implementation uses a series of ternary operators to match specific input patterns. We can refactor this to use a more concise Boolean expression that captures the same logic. Observing the truth table, we can see that q is true when either:
1. b is high and either c or d is high (b & (c | d)), or
2. a is high and d is high (a & d)

This reduces the logic to a simple OR of two conditions, which is more efficient in terms of area and power while maintaining the same functionality.
