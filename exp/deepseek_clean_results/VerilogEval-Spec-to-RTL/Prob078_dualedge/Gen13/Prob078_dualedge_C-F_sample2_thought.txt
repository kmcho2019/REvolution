Fusion Strategy:
1. Maintain the core concept of using two flip-flops (positive and negative edge-triggered) as this is the most reliable way to emulate dual-edge triggering in FPGAs.
2. Eliminate any potential clock gating complexity while keeping the clean separation of edge detection.
3. Use non-blocking assignments for all sequential elements to ensure proper synchronous behavior.
4. Keep the output multiplexer simple and direct, controlled by the clock signal.
5. Use clear naming conventions for better readability and maintainability.
6. The solution should be technology-agnostic and portable across different FPGA/ASIC implementations.

The fused solution combines the reliability of both examples while maintaining simplicity. It uses two standard flip-flops (positive and negative edge) with a clock-controlled mux, providing a clean and efficient implementation that clearly shows the dual-edge triggering behavior without any unnecessary complexity.
