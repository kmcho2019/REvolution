Here's a simplified approach that maintains the exact same functionality:

1. Use a 2-bit counter (since we only need to count 4 cycles)
2. Initialize counter to 0 on reset (simpler than starting at 4)
3. Increment counter each cycle until it reaches 3 (4 cycles total)
4. shift_ena is simply the OR of all counter bits when not in reset
   - This means shift_ena is high for counter values 0-3 (4 cycles)
5. After 4 cycles, counter stays at 3 and shift_ena stays low

Key simplifications:
- Smaller 2-bit counter instead of 3-bit
- Simpler initialization (0 instead of 4)
- Simpler output logic (just OR of counter bits)
- Still maintains exact 4-cycle enable period
