To simplify the next-state logic for y[1], we'll:
1. Focus only on transitions where y[1] changes or stays 1 in next state
2. From the state machine:
   - y[1] becomes 1 when:
     * From B/C with w=1 (go to D)
     * From D with w=0 (go to F)
     * From E (stays E or goes to D)
     * From F with w=1 (go to D)
3. Notice that y[1] is 1 in next state when:
   - Current state is E (y=100) regardless of w
   - Current state is F (y=101) and w=1
   - Current state is B/C (y=001/010) and w=1
   - Current state is D (y=011) and w=0
4. This can be expressed as:
   next_y1 = (y == 3'b100) |                // State E
             (y == 3'b101 & w) |            // State F with w=1
             ((y == 3'b001 | y == 3'b010) & w) |  // States B/C with w=1
             (y == 3'b011 & ~w)             // State D with w=0
5. Simplify using state bit patterns:
   next_y1 = (~y[2] & ((y[1] & ~y[0] & w) | (~y[1] & y[0] & ~w))) |
             (y[2] & (y[0] | ~w))
