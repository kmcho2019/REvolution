The original implementation is already optimal in terms of functionality and minimal logic usage. However, we can explore minor optimizations by leveraging the logical relationships between some outputs to potentially reduce area and power:

1. out_nand is the inverse of out_and - we can derive it from out_and instead of recomputing a & b
2. out_nor is the inverse of out_or - can be derived from out_or
3. out_xnor is the inverse of out_xor - can be derived from out_xor
4. out_anotb can be expressed as (a & ~b) which is already optimal

This approach reduces the number of primitive operations from 7 to 4 (AND, OR, XOR, and NOT) while maintaining all functionality. The inverted outputs will now use the base outputs plus inverters, which might save some area in certain technology libraries where inverters are smaller than compound gates.
