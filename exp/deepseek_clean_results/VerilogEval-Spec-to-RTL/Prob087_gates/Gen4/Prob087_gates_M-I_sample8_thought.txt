The original implementation is already optimal in terms of functionality and minimal logic. However, we can explore minor optimizations by leveraging the relationship between complementary gates to potentially reduce area and power:

1. For complementary outputs (AND/NAND, OR/NOR, XOR/XNOR), we can compute one and invert it to get the other, reducing the number of primitive gates needed.
2. The 'out_anotb' (a AND NOT b) can be implemented using the same NOT gate as used for NAND/NOR outputs.
3. This approach reduces the total number of primitive gates from 7 to 4 (AND, OR, XOR, and one inverter shared across outputs).

This optimization maintains the same functionality while potentially reducing area and power consumption through gate sharing.
