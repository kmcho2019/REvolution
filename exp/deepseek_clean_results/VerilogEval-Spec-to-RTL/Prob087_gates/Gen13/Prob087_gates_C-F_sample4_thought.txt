The optimal solution combines the strengths of both examples while addressing their minor weaknesses:

1. Signal Sharing (from Example 1):
   - Keep shared inverted signals (a_not, b_not) for potential power optimization
   - But use them more effectively by applying them directly to inverted operations

2. Direct Computation (from Example 2):
   - Maintain direct computation of basic operations for clarity
   - But leverage shared signals for inverted operations to reduce redundant logic

3. Logical Grouping:
   - Group operations by their logical relationships (basic, inverted, special)
   - Maintain clean separation for readability and potential synthesis optimization

4. Power Optimization:
   - Compute inverted operations using shared inverted signals rather than inverting outputs
   - This reduces switching activity by eliminating redundant inverters

5. Maintainability:
   - Keep the code structure simple and well-commented
   - Use descriptive signal names for better understanding

This fusion results in a design that is both power-efficient and clear, with potential benefits for both ASIC and FPGA implementations.
