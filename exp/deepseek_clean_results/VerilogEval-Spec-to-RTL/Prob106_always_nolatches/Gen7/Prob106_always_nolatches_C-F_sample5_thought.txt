The optimal solution combines the best aspects of both examples while addressing their inefficiencies:

1. From Example 1's ROM approach, we recognize that a full ROM is overkill for just 4 mappings, but we keep the concept of centralized mapping.

2. From Example 2's parallel comparison approach, we maintain the efficiency of direct combinational logic but optimize it further.

3. Key improvements:
   - Use simple equality comparators for each arrow key case (like Example 2)
   - Add a shared prefix check (0xE0) to reduce unnecessary lower byte comparisons
   - Keep outputs mutually exclusive by design (only one can be active at a time)
   - Use direct assignments for minimal logic depth and best timing

This fusion results in:
- Better power efficiency than either example (only necessary comparisons)
- Minimal area (just 4 comparators and some AND gates)
- Optimal timing (single-level logic for each output)
- Maintained readability and scalability
