The key insight is that we can further simplify the logic by recognizing that:
1. next_state[0] is only active when in=0 AND (state A or C), which can be written as ~in & (state[0] | state[2])
2. next_state[1] is active when either:
   - in=1 AND state B (stays in B)
   - in=1 AND state D (transitions to B)
   - in=1 AND state A (transitions to B)
   This simplifies to just in & (state[0] | state[1] | state[3])
3. next_state[2] is active when in=0 AND (state B or D)
4. next_state[3] is only active when in=1 AND state C
5. The output remains simply state[3]

This reduces the number of logic operations while maintaining correctness.
