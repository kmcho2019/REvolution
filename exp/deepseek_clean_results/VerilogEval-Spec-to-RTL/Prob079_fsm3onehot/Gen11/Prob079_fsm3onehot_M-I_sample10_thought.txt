1. **Output Optimization**: Since the output is simply state[3], we can keep this as is - it's already minimal.

2. **State Transition Optimization**:
   - next_state[0]: Can be expressed as ~in & (state[0] | state[2]) - this is already optimal
   - next_state[1]: Current implementation (in & ~state[2]) is good but we can consider if in & (state[0] | state[1] | state[3]) might be more readable
   - next_state[2]: ~in & (state[1] | state[3]) is optimal
   - next_state[3]: in & state[2] is optimal

3. **Power Optimization**: We can use an always_comb block instead of continuous assignments to potentially give the synthesis tool more optimization opportunities while maintaining combinational behavior.
