To optimize the solution further, I'll focus on power reduction while maintaining the same functionality. The current implementation has two main terms:
1. (x[2] & ~x[0])
2. (~x[2] & x[3] & x[0] & x[1])

Looking at the second term, we can observe that when x[3] is high and x[2] is low (x[4]x[3]=01 in K-map), we only need x[1]x[2]=11 (x[0]x[1]=11) to output 1. However, the x[3] term in the second expression might be redundant because when x[2] is low and x[3] is high, we're already in the x[4]x[3]=01 case (since x[2] is x[3] in K-map and x[3] is x[4]).

We can simplify the second term to just (~x[2] & x[0] & x[1]), which reduces the gate count and power consumption while maintaining correct functionality. This eliminates one AND operation.

The improved expression becomes:
f = (x[2] & ~x[0]) | (~x[2] & x[0] & x[1])
