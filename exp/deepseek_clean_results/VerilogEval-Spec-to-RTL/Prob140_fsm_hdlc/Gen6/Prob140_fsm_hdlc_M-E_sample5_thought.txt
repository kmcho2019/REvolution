My novel approach uses a shift register combined with pattern matching to detect the sequences, rather than a traditional state machine. Here's the key insight:

1. **Shift Register Tracking**: Maintain a 7-bit shift register that tracks the last 7 input bits. This gives us a sliding window to detect patterns.

2. **Pattern Matching**: Compare the shift register contents against the target patterns:
   - 0111110 (5 ones followed by 0) for discard
   - 01111110 (6 ones followed by 0) for flag
   - 01111111 (7 ones) for error

3. **Edge Detection**: Use the shift register's MSB to detect when we've seen 7 consecutive 1s, which persists until a 0 breaks the sequence.

4. **Output Timing**: Register the pattern matches to ensure outputs are asserted on the cycle after detection.

This approach is more parallel and potentially more efficient than a state machine, as it can detect all patterns simultaneously in combinatorial logic.
