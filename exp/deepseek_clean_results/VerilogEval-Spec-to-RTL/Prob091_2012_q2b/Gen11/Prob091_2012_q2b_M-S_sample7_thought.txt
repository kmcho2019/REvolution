Simplification Strategy:
1. **Y1 Logic**: The original (y[0] & w) is already minimal - no simplification possible as it directly represents the transition from A to B when w=1.

2. **Y3 Logic**: 
   - Original: ~w & ~y[0] & ~y[3]
   - Observation: In one-hot encoding, only one y bit can be active at a time
   - Therefore, ~y[0] & ~y[3] is equivalent to checking if we're in states B, C, E, or F
   - However, Y3 only needs to be active during transitions to D (y[3])
   - From state machine: Y3 should be 1 when:
     * From B with w=0
     * From C with w=0
     * From E with w=0
     * From F with w=0
   - This simplifies to: (y[1] | y[2] | y[4] | y[5]) & ~w
   - But since one-hot, can use OR reduction: (|y[5:1]) & ~w

3. **Final Simplification**:
   - Y1 remains y[0] & w
   - Y3 becomes (|y[5:1]) & ~w
   - This uses fewer gates (just one OR and one AND) while maintaining correctness
   - Functionally equivalent under one-hot encoding constraints
