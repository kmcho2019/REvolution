After analyzing the state machine and the one-hot encoding, we can simplify the logic further:

1. For Y1 (next state for y[1]):
   - Only transitions from state A (y[0]) to B (y[1]) when w=1
   - This is simply y[0] & w (no simplification possible)

2. For Y3 (next state for y[3]):
   - Looking at all transitions to state D (y[3]):
     - From B (y[1]) when w=0
     - From C (y[2]) when w=0
     - From E (y[4]) when w=0
     - From F (y[5]) when w=0
   - However, E (y[4]) and F (y[5]) are already output states (Y3 is their next state input)
   - We can express this as: (~w) & (y[1] | y[2] | y[4] | y[5])
   - But since y[4] and y[5] are output states, we can simplify to just (~w) & (y[1] | y[2])

The previous solution used ~w & ~(y[0] | y[3]) which is equivalent but less intuitive. Our simplified version directly represents the state transitions to D.
